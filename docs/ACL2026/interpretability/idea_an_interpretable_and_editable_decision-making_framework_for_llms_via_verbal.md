# IDEA: An Interpretable and Editable Decision-Making Framework for LLMs via Verbal-to-Numeric Calibration

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2604.12573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12573)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/leonbig/IDEA](https://github.com/leonbig/IDEA)  
**Area**: Interpretability / LLM Decision Making  
**Keywords**: Interpretable Decision Making, Verbal Probability Calibration, EM Algorithm, Parameter Editing, Human-AI Collaboration

## TL;DR

The IDEA framework is proposed to extract decision-making knowledge from LLMs into an interpretable parametric model based on semantic factors. By using an EM algorithm to jointly learn the mapping from verbal probability expressions to numerical values alongside decision parameters, it achieves calibratable, editable, and interpretable LLM decision-making. Using Qwen-3-32B (78.6%), it outperforms DeepSeek R1 (68.1%) and GPT-5.2 (77.9%) across five datasets.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: LLMs are increasingly being deployed in automated decision-making scenarios. However, their application in high-risk areas such as financial investment and loan approval is still limited by a fundamental "trust deficit"—stakeholders cannot reliably verify, audit, or intervene in the decision-making process.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing methods fall short in three dimensions: (1) probability estimates generated by LLMs are overconfident and poorly calibrated; (2) generated explanations are often post-hoc rationalizations that do not reflect the internal reasoning process; (3) there is a lack of a quantitative framework to precisely integrate expert knowledge, as prompts alone cannot guarantee behavioral compliance. For example, ranking and scoring may yield inconsistent orders for the same options, and instructions to explicitly exclude a factor often fail to stop it from influencing predictions.

**Key Challenge**: There is a fundamental "internal-external misalignment" between the internal computation and the external output of LLMs. Logit-based methods conflate next-token confidence with decision uncertainty and remain black boxes; DeLLMa relies on LLMs to directly output precise numerical values, which is exactly what LLMs are poor at; BIRD assumes factor independence and uses fixed verbal-to-numeric mappings, losing calibration accuracy and natural correlations between factors.

**Goal**: To build a decision-making framework that simultaneously satisfies three properties: calibrated probability estimation, semantic interpretability, and quantitative human-AI collaboration (through precise parameter editing).

**Key Insight**: The authors made two key observations: (i) while LLMs cannot reliably output precise numerical probabilities, they can generate decision-relevant factors from their extensive knowledge; (ii) LLMs are more consistent when producing verbal probability expressions (e.g., "likely", "unlikely") than precise numbers, as such phrases are far more prevalent in training corpora than exact probability values.

**Core Idea**: Instead of making the internal reasoning process of the LLM transparent, its knowledge is extracted into an inherently transparent form—an interpretable parametric model over a semantic factor space. This is achieved through an EM algorithm that jointly learns verbal-to-numeric mappings and decision parameters.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The mechanism of IDEA is to decompose the target probability $P(O_i|Q)$ into two separable parts: a decision model $P(O_i|\mathbf{f})$ (mapping factor configurations to outcomes) and factor inference $P(\mathbf{f}|C)$ (inferring factor values from conditions). The entire workflow is divided into an offline training phase (Factor Identification → Behavior Probing → EM Joint Estimation) and an online inference phase (Factor Determination → Correlated Sampling → Marginalization Calculation), with additional support for parameter editing interventions by experts.

### Key Designs

1.  **Verbal-to-Numeric Calibration via EM Joint Estimation**:
    - **Function**: Simultaneously learns the mapping from verbal probability expressions to numerical values and the decision model parameters.
    - **Mechanism**: To resolve the "chicken-and-egg" problem—where learning the decision model requires numerical labels, but determining what numerical values verbal expressions represent requires the decision model—the E-step computes the posterior expectation of each latent probability (a precision-weighted combination of model predictions and verbal mappings), and the M-step updates model parameters (using MSE + ranking consistency loss + Elastic Net regularization) and verbal mappings (with monotonicity constraints). The decision model uses logistic regression with interaction terms, where main effects directly quantify the contribution of each factor.
    - **Design Motivation**: BIRD's use of fixed mappings from psychological literature leads to a -6.8% average F1 drop; joint learning adapts to specific tasks and the LLM's language usage habits.

2.  **Correlated Sampling for Factor Inference**:
    - **Function**: Maintains natural correlations between factors during marginalization when uncertain factors exist.
    - **Mechanism**: Factors are divided into observed and uncertain sets. For uncertain factors, the LLM performs conditional sampling of $T=50$ joint configurations (using high temperature to ensure diversity), followed by Monte Carlo methods to calculate the final probability. This estimator is unbiased with a standard error of $O(1/\sqrt{T})$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Previous methods (like BIRD) assume conditional independence of factors, ignoring natural correlations (e.g., between "high income" and "stable employment"), leading to inaccurate marginalization estimates.

3.  **Quantitative Parameter Editing**:
    - **Function**: Allows experts to edit the relative importance of factors in a mathematically precise manner.
    - **Mechanism**: Introduces Average Marginal Effect (AME) to convert the log-odds space coefficients of logistic regression into intuitive probability space changes. It supports structural editing (adding/removing factors) and quantitative editing (solving constrained optimization via sequential quadratic programming to satisfy expert-specified importance ratios while minimizing interference with other factors). For example, excluding a "credit history" factor simply requires setting its coefficient to zero, precisely changing the approval probability from 21.6% to 52.3%.
    - **Design Motivation**: Excluding factors via prompting is highly unreliable (ERR only 0.06-0.43), whereas parameter editing achieves perfect factor exclusion (ERR=1.00) and zero relative error.

### Loss & Training

The model parameter update in the M-step uses a compound loss: MSE reconstruction loss + ranking consistency hinge loss (ensuring the probability of "likely" is higher than "unlikely") + Elastic Net regularization (applied only to interaction terms, with L1 inducing sparsity and L2 ensuring numerical stability). Verbal mappings are initialized with values from psychological literature and iterated until the $Q$ function change is less than $1e-4$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Binary decision accuracy was evaluated on five datasets (Complex Decision: BIGDATA22 stock prediction, German Credit loan approval; Reasoning: COMMON2SENSE, PLASMA, TODAY):

| Model | Method | Avg. F1 (5 Datasets) | Three-class Ranking Macro F1 |
|------|------|----------------|------------------|
| Qwen-3-32B | IDEA | **78.6%** | **0.693** |
| Qwen-3-32B | CoT | 67.7% | 0.339 |
| Qwen-3-32B | BIRD | 71.4% | 0.521 |
| GPT-5.2 | CoT | 77.9% | 0.402 |
| DeepSeek R1 | CoT | 68.1% | 0.286 |
| Qwen-3-8B | IDEA | 73.2% | 0.697 |
| Qwen-3-4B | IDEA | 71.6% | 0.504 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration (Qwen-3-32B) | Avg. F1 | Ranking Macro F1 | Note |
|-------------------|---------|--------------|------|
| IDEA (Full) | 78.6% | 0.693 | Full model |
| w/o EM | 71.8% (-6.8%) | 0.632 | Fixed verbal mapping used |
| w/o Inter | 71.0% (-7.6%) | 0.644 | Interaction terms removed |
| w/o MC | 71.8% (-6.8%) | 0.617 | Deterministic factor assignment |

### Key Findings
- The three modules contribute roughly equally, each providing a 6-8% gain, indicating that EM calibration, interaction terms, and correlated sampling are all indispensable.
- IDEA achieves perfect factor exclusion (ERR=1.00) and zero calibration error, whereas prompt-based methods achieve at most an ERR of 0.43.
- Even on smaller models (Qwen-3-4B), IDEA significantly outperforms direct prompting, suggesting the framework is not highly sensitive to model scale.
- IDEA's advantage is even more pronounced in ranking tasks, particularly in its ability to identify "equivalent" categories, which far surpasses other methods.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Leveraging Verbal Probability Consistency**: Effectively utilizes the property that LLMs produce "likely/unlikely" more consistently than precise numbers, converting unreliable numerical outputs into reliable ordinal signals, which are then mapped to optimal numerical values via the EM algorithm. This approach can be generalized to any scenario requiring numerical information extraction from LLMs.
- **Mathematical Guarantees for Parameter Editing**: Achieves precise, predictable, and reversible behavioral intervention through AME and constrained optimization. This is the first framework to provide mathematical guarantees for LLM decision editing. This "extraction-modeling-editing" paradigm can be transferred to other scenarios requiring human intervention in AI decisions.
- **Elegance of Decoupled Design**: Decomposing decision-making into factor inference and a decision model leverages the LLM's breadth of knowledge (factor generation) while avoiding its numerical unreliability (parametric model), representing a sophisticated "play to strengths, avoid weaknesses" design.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Currently limited to binary decisions and binary factor settings; extending to multi-class decisions and continuous factors requires more complex parameterization.
- The assumption of factor completeness may be difficult to satisfy in open-domain decisions; omitting important factors would systematically degrade performance.
- EM only guarantees convergence to a local optimum, and sensitivity to initialization has not been fully discussed.
- The behavior probing phase requires a large number of LLM queries (up to 256 configurations), which may be restrictive for cost-sensitive API usage.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs BIRD**: BIRD assumes factor independence and uses fixed mappings; IDEA significantly outperforms it through EM joint learning and correlated sampling, with an average F1 increase of approximately 7%.
- **vs DeLLMa**: DeLLMa relies on LLMs to directly output numerical utilities, which is unreliable; IDEA bypasses this by using verbal probabilities as intermediaries.
- **vs Concept Bottleneck Models**: CBMs require task-specific training and assume concept independence; IDEA provides similar interpretability while additionally supporting parameter editing.
