# Anomaly-Preference Image Generation (APO)

**Conference**: ICML 2026  
**arXiv**: [2605.02439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02439)  
**Code**: Not explicitly released  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / Industrial Anomaly Detection / Preference Alignment  
**Keywords**: Few-shot Anomaly Generation, DPO, Implicit Preference, Time-aware LoRA, Hierarchical Sampling

## TL;DR
The authors reformulate "few-shot anomaly image generation" as a "preference optimization problem without human annotation." Real anomalies serve as positive samples, while the denoising bias of a reference model at the same timestep serves as implicit negative samples. Through a DPO-style loss, the diffusion model is aligned with the anomaly distribution. A Time-Aware Capacity Allocation (TACA) mechanism adjusts LoRA ranks by timestep to preserve structural diversity, and hierarchical Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) regulates text-anomaly alignment strength. This approach sets new state-of-the-art records for both realism and diversity on benchmarks like MVTec.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Industrial visual anomaly detection is constrained by "scarcity of defect samples + expensive annotation." A mainstream approach is using diffusion models to synthesize additional samples from a few real anomalies to augment detectors. Solutions are divided into fine-tuning methods (e.g., AnomalyDiffusion, DualAnoDiff, SeaS) and training-free methods (e.g., AnomalyAny).

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Fine-tuning methods often decouple the learning of appearance and location, leading to semantic inconsistency, while dual-stream architectures experience feature conflicts and gradient interference. Training-free methods compress all computation into inference time, resulting in high latency. Neither class explicitly constrains the "generated distribution to align with the real anomaly distribution," leading to either overfitting (poor diversity) or distribution drift (poor realism).

**Key Challenge**: There is a trade-off between realism and diversity. Directly applying DPO requires paired preference data (both positive and negative samples generated by the model), but in few-shot scenarios, human preference pairs are unavailable. The challenge is performing distribution alignment without manual preference signals.

**Goal**: (1) Construct a stable optimization target without human annotation for direct distribution alignment. (2) Maintain diversity during alignment. (3) Allow adjustable control between "base model coherence" and "anomaly pattern alignment" during inference.

**Key Insight**: The core of DPO is KL-regularized policy optimization, where the trick is re-parameterizing the reward as $\beta\log\frac{p_\theta}{p_\text{ref}}$. If real anomalies are used as positive samples and the "denoising error of the reference model on the same $\mathbf{z}_t$" is treated as an implicit baseline, preference gradients can be obtained without manual negative samples.

**Core Idea**: Replace the paired preference log-ratio in DPO with the denoising deviation $\Delta = \|\hat\epsilon_\theta-\epsilon\|^2 - \|\hat\epsilon_\text{ref}-\epsilon\|^2$ relative to the reference model. This transforms few-shot anomaly generation into an analytical and stable implicit preference optimization problem.

## Method
The APO framework consists of three components: Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO, which reformulates DPO for non-paired data), Time-Aware Capacity Allocation (TACA, which adjusts LoRA rank by diffusion timestep), and Hierarchical Sampling (tri-path CFG during inference). These components address "alignment," "diversity," and "controllability," respectively.

### Overall Architecture
- **Input**: A small set of $K$ real anomaly samples $\{(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{c}_i)\}$ + a pre-trained Stable Diffusion as the reference model $\epsilon_\text{ref}$.
- **Training Loop**: Sample real anomalies → add noise to arbitrary $t$ → denoise simultaneously using $\epsilon_\text{ref}$ and $\epsilon_\theta$ → calculate deviation $\Delta$ → update the policy using $\mathcal{L}_\text{APO} = -\log\sigma(-\beta_t\Delta)$ (updating only the TACA-style time-aware LoRA). No negative sample sampling or reward models are required.
- **Inference**: Decompose the denoising process into "unconditional + text-conditioned + anomaly-aligned" components, controlled by $s_\text{text}$ and $s_\text{align}$ weights (hierarchical sampling). Aggregate upsampled alignment deviations from each step to form an anomaly localization mask.

### Key Designs

1. **Implicit Preference Alignment CPO (DPO Rewrite without Paired Annotation)**:
    - **Function**: Reformulates the paired preference loss of DPO into a contrast between the "policy vs. reference model" denoising deviations on the same real sample, removing dependency on human labels.
    - **Mechanism**: Starting from constrained optimization $\max_\theta \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}] - \beta D_\text{KL}(p_\theta \| p_\text{ref})$, the optimal policy is $p^*_\theta \propto p_\text{ref}\exp(\mathcal{R}/\beta)$, with reward re-parameterized as $\mathcal{R} = \beta\log\frac{p_\theta}{p_\text{ref}}$. By simplifying the "KL difference along the diffusion trajectory" using variational ELBO, a closed-form $\delta = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_t[\lambda'_t(\|\hat\epsilon_\theta-\epsilon\|^2 - \|\hat\epsilon_\text{ref}-\epsilon\|^2)]$ is derived, where $\lambda'_t$ is the time derivative of the log-SNR. The final loss is $\mathcal{L}_\text{APO} = -\log\sigma(-\beta_t\Delta)$, with $\beta_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta\lambda'_t$ providing time-adaptive weights. $\Delta < 0$ indicates the policy model aligns better with the real anomaly distribution than the reference. The sigmoid transforms this "betterment" into preference probability; its monotonic log-sigmoid is more stable than a pure L2 loss and avoids overfitting to few samples.
    - **Design Motivation**: Traditional DPO requires $(\mathbf{z}^w, \mathbf{z}^l)$ pairs; these are unavailable in few-shot anomaly scenarios. Using the reference model's denoising on the same $\mathbf{z}_t$ as a natural baseline is equivalent to "preferring real anomalies over the reference model’s expected prediction at that noise level," retaining DPO's KL regularization stability without annotation costs.

2. **Time-Aware Capacity Allocation (TACA)**:
    - **Function**: Dynamically adjusts the trainable LoRA rank according to the diffusion timestep—small ranks at high-noise steps preserve structural diversity, while large ranks at low-noise steps learn fine-grained anomaly details.
    - **Mechanism**: Diffusion denoising determines global structure (layout/composition) at high-noise steps and texture details at low-noise steps. Uniform fine-tuning across all steps tends to overfit background patterns at high-noise levels, suppressing diversity. TACA defines LoRA weight updates as $\Delta W_t = B \cdot G_t \cdot A$, where $G_t$ is a diagonal mask selecting dimensions. The number of active dimensions is $k(t) = \lfloor k_\text{min} + (k_\text{max}-k_\text{min})(T-t)/T \rfloor$. As $t\to T$ (high noise), $k\to k_\text{min}$ (low adaptation); as $t\to 0$ (low noise), $k\to k_\text{max}$ (high adaptation).
    - **Design Motivation**: Allocating "model capacity" as a tunable resource along the time axis respects the physical meaning of the diffusion process. This results in the simultaneous preservation of structural diversity and anomaly detail, solving the realism vs. diversity trade-off.

3. **Hierarchical Sampling + Deviation-Guided Localization**:
    - **Function**: Decomposes denoising into three independently adjustable components during inference and upsamples alignment deviations into pixel-level anomaly masks for zero-cost localization.
    - **Mechanism**: Denoising prediction is written as $\hat\epsilon = \epsilon_\text{ref}(\mathbf{z}_t, t) + s_\text{text}\Delta_\text{text} + s_\text{align}\Delta_\text{align}$, where $\Delta_\text{text} = \epsilon_\text{ref}(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{c}, t) - \epsilon_\text{ref}(\mathbf{z}_t, t)$ controls text condition strength, and $\Delta_\text{align} = \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{c}, t) - \epsilon_\text{ref}(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{c}, t)$ controls anomaly alignment strength. These independent scales sample from a guided distribution $p_\text{ref} \cdot (p_\text{ref}^{\mathbf{c}}/p_\text{ref})^{s_\text{text}} \cdot (p_\theta^{\mathbf{c}}/p_\text{ref}^{\mathbf{c}})^{s_\text{align}}$. Summing $\|\Delta_\text{align}\|$ weighted by $k(t)$, followed by bilinear upsampling and Gaussian smoothing, generates $\mathbf{P}_\text{anomaly}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Traditional CFG uses a single scale, unable to separate "text semantics" from "anomaly patterns." The hierarchical form provides fine-grained control. The localization mask is a free byproduct since the deviation signal is the best indicator of anomaly saliency.

### Loss & Training
Only LoRA adapters are trained; the reference model $\epsilon_\text{ref}$ is frozen. The single loss term is $\mathcal{L}_\text{APO} = -\log\sigma(-\beta_t\Delta)$, with timesteps $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,T)$ sampled uniformly and noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$. $\beta$ controls KL strength, and $\beta_t$ scales automatically with log-SNR.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (MVTec Realism + Diversity)
Comparison against Crop&Paste, DFMGAN, AnomalyDiffusion, DualAnoDiff, AnomalyAny, and SeaS. Metrics: IS (Inception Score, higher is better realism/diversity) and IC-LPIPS (Intra-Class LPIPS, higher is better diversity).

| Category | Metric | AnomalyDiff | DualAnoDiff | SeaS | APO (Ours) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| cable | IS / IC-L | 2.13 / 0.41 | 2.15 / 0.43 | 2.09 / 0.42 | **2.20 / 0.45** |

APO improves both metrics simultaneously across almost all categories—realism and diversity improve together rather than being traded off.

### Ablation Study

| Config | Key Observation | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CPO only (no TACA) | Realism is high, but diversity drops | Validates TACA as the key to diversity |
| TACA only (no CPO) | Diversity is high, but distribution drifts | Validates CPO as the key to realism |
| Full APO | Balanced performance | The two modules are complementary and indispensable |
| Tuning $s_\text{text}, s_\text{align}$ | Smooth sliding on trade-off curve | Hierarchical sampling provides fine-grained controllability |

### Key Findings
- **Orthogonal Contributions**: CPO handles "target distribution alignment," while TACA manages "preservation of generation diversity." Both are required for dual-SOTA realism and diversity.
- **Free Localization Mask**: No explicit localization supervision is used, yet $\|\Delta_\text{align}\|$ naturally reflects anomalous regions, providing pseudo-labels for downstream detectors.
- **Time-Adaptive Scales**: The $\beta_t$ re-parameterization in DPO scales automatically with log-SNR, resulting in much more stable training than a fixed $\beta$—this time-adaptive weighting has general value for preference learning in diffusion models.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Generalizing DPO to Non-Paired Diffusion**: Using "reference model denoising error" instead of "model-generated negative samples" is a simple yet powerful preference signal design; it applies to any few-shot scenario requiring alignment with a target distribution.
- **Timed Capacity Allocation Philosophy**: TACA is a one-line code change (adjusting LoRA masks by $t$), but it significantly mitigates background overfitting at high-noise steps. This idea of "allocating trainable parameters based on the noise schedule" can be transferred to style transfer, customized generation, and other LoRA fine-tuning tasks.
- **Alignment Deviation as Saliency**: Training lacks localization supervision, yet inference yields pixel-level masks. This "alignment → implicit localization" byproduct simplifies deployment by removing the need for a separate segmentation head.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Validation was restricted to industrial anomalies (MVTec, VisA); transferability to medical anomalies or natural scene defects requires further study.
- Inference requires two forward passes (running both $\epsilon_\theta$ and $\epsilon_\text{ref}$), requiring model distillation for real-time applications.
- The $k_\text{min}, k_\text{max}$ schedule in TACA is hand-crafted; learning an optimal data-driven allocation schedule remains an open question.
- Stability in extreme few-shot cases ($K=1$ or $2$) was not explicitly explored; experiments used $K$ in the range of 5–10.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. AnomalyDiffusion / DualAnoDiff**: While those use textual inversion or dual-stream fine-tuning, APO uses preference learning + LoRA, making the framework lighter and more stable.
- **vs. SeaS**: SeaS follows a unified multi-task route; APO focuses on "aligning distribution first, then hierarchical sampling." APO offers a superior realism-diversity Pareto Frontier.
- **vs. DPO / Diffusion-DPO (Wallace 2024)**: Classic Diffusion-DPO requires human-ranked image pairs; APO uses real samples + reference model deviation, marking the first application of DPO to scenarios where no preference pairs are available.
- **vs. RLHF for Diffusion**: Traditional RLHF requires training a reward model followed by PPO; APO is a one-step, single-loss process with training costs comparable to standard fine-tuning.
