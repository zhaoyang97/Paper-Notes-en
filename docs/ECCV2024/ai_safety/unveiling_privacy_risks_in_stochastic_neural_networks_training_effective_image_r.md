# Unveiling Privacy Risks in Stochastic Neural Networks Training: Effective Image Reconstruction from Gradients

**Conference**: ECCV 2024  
**PDF**: [ECVA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/04439.pdf)
**Code**: None  
**Area**: AI Safety  
**Keywords**: Gradient Inversion Attack, Stochastic Neural Networks, Federated Learning, Privacy Leakage, Image Reconstruction

## TL;DR

This paper unveils that Stochastic Neural Networks (SNNs) in federated learning remain vulnerable to gradient inversion attacks. It proposes the ISG method, which reconstructs training data by formulating the stochastic training process of SNNs as an equivalent variant of traditional NN training, and introduces a feature constraint strategy to improve reconstruction fidelity.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Federated learning (FL) protects privacy by sharing model gradients instead of raw data. However, recent studies show that an adversarial server can reconstruct training data from the shared gradients through Gradient Inversion Attacks (GIA). Existing GIA methods mainly target traditional deterministic neural networks (such as CNNs and ResNets) by simulating the training process of clients to reconstruct the input data.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: It is widely believed in the academic community that Stochastic Neural Networks (SNNs), such as Bayesian Neural Networks and noise injection networks, possess inherent immunity to GIA because of the stochasticity introduced during their forward propagation, which makes it difficult for attackers to simulate the training process. Consequently, many works have adopted SNNs as a privacy-preserving mechanism.

**Key Challenge**: In truth, the gradients of SNNs contain information about the stochastic components. Although the forward propagation is stochastic, the backward propagation of gradients remains deterministic—given a specific sampled value of the stochastic parameters, the gradient is uniquely determined. Thus, attackers can deduce the stochastic components from the gradients and reconstruct the training data.

**Goal**: (1) Verify whether SNNs are truly immune to GIA; (2) design an effective gradient inversion method capable of attacking SNNs; (3) propose a feature constraint strategy to improve reconstruction quality.

**Key Insight**: The authors find that the stochastic training process of SNNs can be formulated as a variant of the traditional NN training process—where the stochastic components can be viewed as part of the network weights, thereby converting the gradient inversion problem of SNNs into that of traditional NNs.

**Core Idea**: Reparameterize the stochastic training process of SNNs into an equivalent form of traditional NNs to make existing GIA techniques directly applicable to SNNs, demonstrating that stochastic perturbations in forward propagation cannot protect privacy.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The ISG (Inverting Stochasticity from Gradients) framework consists of two stages. In the first stage, after receiving the gradients shared by the SNN client, the attacker reformulates the parameterization of the SNN, treating the stochastic components as optimizable variables. In the second stage, the virtual input image and the stochastic components are jointly optimized to align the gradients generated by the virtual image on the SNN with the observed real gradients. The input is the intercepted gradient, and the output is the reconstructed training image.

### Key Designs

1. **Inverting Stochasticity**:

    - **Function**: Transform the stochastic forward propagation of SNNs into a deterministically attackable form.
    - **Mechanism**: For stochastic layers in SNNs (e.g., Dropout, Gaussian noise layer), the forward propagation can be written as $y = f(x, \epsilon)$, where $\epsilon$ is a random sample. The gradient $\nabla_\theta L$ is a deterministic function with respect to a specific value of $\epsilon$. Therefore, the attacker can treat $\epsilon$ as an optimization variable and minimize $\|\nabla_\theta L(x^*, \epsilon^*) - \nabla_\theta^{obs}\|^2$ to jointly solve for $x^*$ and $\epsilon^*$.
    - **Design Motivation**: This reveals the fundamental weakness of SNNs: although practitioners inject randomness during forward propagation, the gradients leak information about the sampled values, allowing attackers to "invert" this stochasticity.

2. **Feature Constraint Strategy**:

    - **Function**: Enhance the semantic fidelity and visual quality of the reconstructed images.
    - **Mechanism**: In addition to the gradient matching loss, intermediate layer feature matching constraints are introduced. Utilizing the fact that SNN intermediate features can be partially recovered from gradients (via the chain rule), the distance between features generated by the virtual image and target features derived from gradients is computed as $L_{feat} = \sum_l \|h_l(x^*) - h_l^{target}\|^2$. This provides richer supervision signals for the optimization.
    - **Design Motivation**: Over-relying on gradient matching makes the optimization susceptible to local optima, especially for high-resolution images and larger batch sizes. Feature constraints offer additional constraints at the semantic level, narrowing the search space.

3. **Multi-stage Optimization**:

    - **Function**: Stabilize the optimization process and prevent divergence caused by inaccurate estimation of stochastic components in the early stages.
    - **Mechanism**: A three-stage optimization is adopted: first, $\epsilon$ is fixed to its mean value (eliminating stochasticity) to optimize the image for a coarse reconstruction; then, $\epsilon$ is unlocked to jointly optimize both the image and the stochastic components for fine adjustments; finally, feature constraints are utilized to further enhance the reconstruction quality.
    - **Design Motivation**: The joint optimization space is high-dimensional and highly non-convex. The multi-stage strategy provides a coarse-to-fine optimization path, significantly improving convergence stability.

### Loss & Training

The total loss function is $L_{total} = L_{grad} + \alpha L_{feat} + \beta L_{reg}$, where $L_{grad}$ is the cosine similarity loss of gradient matching, $L_{feat}$ is the intermediate feature matching loss, and $L_{reg}$ includes image prior regularization (such as total variation TV regularization). The optimization utilizes the Adam optimizer with the learning rate decaying step-by-step from 0.1.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| SNN Type | Dataset | PSNR ↑ | SSIM ↑ | LPIPS ↓ | Traditional NN Attack Quality |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|---------|----------------|
| Dropout NN | CIFAR-10 | **25.8** | **0.91** | **0.08** | 26.2 / 0.92 / 0.07 |
| Gaussian Noise NN | CIFAR-10 | **24.3** | **0.88** | **0.11** | 26.2 / 0.92 / 0.07 |
| Bayesian NN | CIFAR-10 | **22.7** | **0.84** | **0.14** | 26.2 / 0.92 / 0.07 |
| Dropout NN | ImageNet | **21.4** | **0.82** | **0.18** | 22.1 / 0.84 / 0.16 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | PSNR ↑ | SSIM ↑ | Description |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| ISG (Full) | **25.8** | **0.91** | Full attack method |
| w/o Stochasticity Inversion (fixed $\epsilon$=mean) | 21.3 | 0.79 | Heavy degradation without inverting stochasticity |
| w/o Feature Constraint | 23.9 | 0.86 | Feature constraint contributes approximately 1.9 dB |
| w/o Multi-stage Optimization | 23.1 | 0.83 | Multi-stage strategy contributes approximately 2.7 dB |
| Single-stage Joint Optimization | 22.4 | 0.81 | Direct joint optimization is unstable |

### Key Findings
- The privacy protection of SNNs falls far short of expectations: The attack quality of ISG on Dropout NN is close to that of traditional NNs (PSNR gap < 1 dB), indicating that Dropout offers almost no privacy protection.
- Bayesian NNs are harder to attack than Dropout NNs and Gaussian Noise NNs because their stochasticity is embedded in the parameters of the weight distribution, presenting a larger optimization space.
- The reconstruction quality degrades as the batch size increases, but ISG can still reconstruct recognizable images even at batch size = 8.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Revealing a major safety misconception**: The academic community generally believes that the stochastic forward propagation of SNNs protects privacy, but this paper proves it wrong—gradients leak info about the randomly sampled values. This discovery is highly significant for the safety evaluation of federated learning.
- **Reparameterization trick** converts SNN attacks into traditional NN attack problems. The concept is clean and straightforward, enabling direct reuse of existing GIA methods and improvements.
- The application of the multi-stage optimization strategy in high-dimensional non-convex optimization can be transferred to other scenarios requiring joint optimization of multiple parameter groups.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Experiments primarily use CIFAR-10 and subsets of ImageNet, without validation on larger datasets or more complex models (e.g., ViT).
- Only the scenario of a single gradient update is considered; the risk of accumulated gradient leakage over multiple training rounds is not analyzed.
- No effective defense strategy is proposed. The paper solely demonstrates that SNNs are insecure without providing solutions to truly secure them.
- Future research can explore attack performance when Differential Privacy (DP) is combined with SNNs to investigate genuinely effective privacy preservation schemes.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs IG (Inverting Gradients)**: IG is a classic GIA method but can only attack deterministic NNs. ISG extends the attack capability of IG to SNNs by introducing a stochasticity inversion module.
- **vs GradInversion**: GradInversion uses batch normalization statistics as extra constraints, whereas ISG employs a feature constraint strategy. The two methods can be complementary.
- **vs DPSGD**: Differential Privacy Stochastic Gradient Descent injects noise at the gradient level. The findings of ISG imply that adding randomness only in forward propagation is insufficient, and noise must be added at the gradient level to truly protect privacy.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It is the first to systematically reveal that SNNs are not immune to GIA, breaking common perceptions in the field.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Covers multiple SNN types with detailed ablation studies, though the dataset scope is relatively small.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Highly logical arguments with a professional perspective on security analysis.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Carries critical warning implications for privacy protection research in federated learning.
