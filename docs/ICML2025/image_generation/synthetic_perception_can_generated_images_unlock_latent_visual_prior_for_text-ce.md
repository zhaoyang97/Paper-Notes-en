# Synthetic Perception: Can Generated Images Unlock Latent Visual Prior for Text-Centric Reasoning?

**Conference**: ICML 2025  
**arXiv**: [2506.17623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17623)  
**Code**: To be released  
**Area**: Image Generation  
**Keywords**: synthetic perception, T2I augmentation, multimodal fusion, text classification, modality gap

## TL;DR

This work systematically investigates "synthetic perception"—utilizing T2I models to instantly generate synthetic images for text-only data as a complementary modality. Through a three-stage evaluation framework (generation $\rightarrow$ fusion $\rightarrow$ evaluation), it demonstrates that this strategy brings significant improvements (+3.9% Acc) to strong LLMs like Llama-3/Qwen-2.5 on challenging tasks such as sarcasm detection and implicit sentiment analysis, while yielding marginal gains on simple factual classification tasks.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: A vast amount of real-world data exists solely in text-only form, creating a "modality gap" that limits the direct application of multimodal models. Meanwhile, T2I synthesis technology has achieved unprecedented image quality and semantic fidelity.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:

1. Standard multimodal learning relies on high-quality image-text pairs, but text-only data inherently lacks visual correspondences.

2. Existing data augmentation methods utilize T2I as an offline data factory to expand training sets, but they fail to explore online, instance-level modality completion strategies.

3. Multimodal fine-tuning may cause "modality-induced forgetting"—the degradation of the model's original text-processing capabilities.

**Core Problem**: Under what conditions (if), through what mechanisms (how), and to what extent can synthetic images generated by T2I, acting as a dynamic new modality, enhance textual understanding?

## Method

### Overall Architecture

A three-stage evaluation pipeline:

1. **Synthetic Visual Modality Generation**: Original text $\rightarrow$ Prompt engineering strategies $\rightarrow$ T2I model $\rightarrow$ Synthetic images.

2. **Multimodal Representation and Fusion**: Text encoder extracts text features + Image encoder extracts visual features $\rightarrow$ Fusion module $\rightarrow$ Unified representation.

3. **Downstream Task Evaluation**: Unified representation $\rightarrow$ Classification head $\rightarrow$ Comparison against text-only baselines, textual expansion baselines, and knowledge retrieval baselines.

### Key Designs

1. **Multi-level Prompt Engineering Strategies**

    - P1 (Direct): Directly uses the original text as the T2I prompt.
    - P2 (Keyword-Enhanced): Extracts core semantic elements (nouns/adjectives/verbs) and inserts them into a template to improve the signal-to-noise ratio.
    - P3 (Task-Aligned Stylization): Injects task-related style keywords.
    - P4 (LLM-Elaborated): Uses Llama-3-8B as a "prompt engineer" to rewrite the text into rich visual descriptions.
    - Conclusion: P2 and P4 perform the best, with P2 being the most efficient and P4 winning on prompt rewriting quality enabled by the LLM.

2. **Comparison of Three Fusion Architectures**

    - F1 (Late Fusion): Simple feature concatenation.
    - F2 (Cross-Attention): Text features query visual features in the Transformer decoder layer—optimal.
    - F3 (Deep Fusion / MMBT): Early injection of visual tokens into the text encoder.
    - Key Findings: Cross-Attention is optimal under most configurations because it allows for the selective extraction of relevant visual information.

### Loss & Training

- Text encoders: Llama-3-8B-Instruct / Qwen-2.5-7B / Mistral-7B / BERT-base.
- Image encoders: SigLIP (optimal) > CLIP ViT-B/32 > DINOv2 (worst, due to lack of text alignment capability).
- T2I models: Flux.1-schnell (most efficient) / SDXL / DALL-E 3 (highest quality).
- AdamW optimizer, early stopping to prevent overfitting, NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**Overall performance across four datasets (Accuracy %)**

| Method | Amazon Reviews | AG News | SARC | Implicit Sentiment |
|------|---------------|---------|------|-------------------|
| Llama-3 Text-Only | 81.20 | 95.10 | 76.50 | 79.50 |
| + Textual Expansion | 81.65 | 95.15 | 77.10 | 80.10 |
| + Knowledge Retrieval | 81.40 | 95.30 | 76.80 | 79.80 |
| **+ Gen. Image (Ours)** | **82.90** | 95.25 | **80.40** | **83.20** |
| Qwen-2.5 Text-Only | 82.50 | 95.40 | 78.20 | 81.10 |
| **Qwen-2.5 + Gen. Image** | **84.10** | 95.55 | **82.10** | **84.80** |

### Ablation Study

**Comparison of T2I Models and Prompt Strategies (Amazon Reviews, Macro-F1 %)**

| T2I Model | Prompt Strategy | Ma-F1 | CLIP Score |
|---------|---------|-------|------------|
| SD1.5 | P2 | 76.24 | 0.28 |
| SDXL | P2 | 77.02 | 0.32 |
| SDXL | P4 | 77.35 | 0.33 |
| Flux.1-schnell | P2 | 77.15 | 0.34 |
| DALL-E 3 | P2 | 77.80 | 0.35 |

### Key Findings

- **Breaking the "textual expansion" ceiling**: On the SARC dataset, textual expansion only improves by 0.6%, whereas synthetic images bring a 3.9% enhancement—proving that the gain comes from the visual modality itself rather than "more text".
- **Task difficulty dictates gains**: Challenging tasks (sarcasm detection +3.9%, implicit sentiment +3.7%) observe significantly larger gains than simple tasks (AG News +0.15%).
- **SigLIP >> DINOv2**: Semantic alignment capability is far more critical than pure visual geometric features.
- **Flux.1-schnell**: Performance is close to SDXL (77.15% vs 77.35%) but with 10x faster inference speed (0.8s vs 8s).
- CLIP Score is highly correlated with downstream performance, serving as a proxy metric for synthetic image quality.

## Highlights & Insights

- Positioned T2I-generated images as "online instance-level modality completion" rather than offline data augmentation for the first time, introducing a novel cross-modality probing paradigm.
- Key Insight: Synthetic perception is most valuable for deep reasoning tasks that require visual grounding (e.g., sarcasm requires "seeing" the conflict between the literal text and the actual context).
- Designed three "critical baselines" (textual expansion/knowledge retrieval/Oracle) to systematically rule out confounding factors, ensuring methodological rigor.
- Efficiency analysis of Flux.1-schnell provides a feasibility validation for real-world deployment.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Only validated on text classification tasks, without involving generative tasks (e.g., summarization, QA).
- The computational overhead of synthetic images remains the main bottleneck for practical deployment, as even Flux.1-schnell requires 0.8s per sample.
- The issue of "modality-induced forgetting" is only briefly mentioned without in-depth quantitative analysis.
- Dataset selection is skewed toward English, lacking multilingual evaluation.
- The potential of utilizing video or multiple images as synthetic modalities remains unexplored.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs TTIDA/DIAGen**: These methods employ T2I as offline data augmentation to expand training sets, whereas this work performs online instance-level modality completion, differing in objectives and usage.
- **vs Missing Modality methods**: Traditional missing modality approaches passively counter the issue, while this work actively synthesizes visual modalities for text-only data.
- **vs CLIP/SigLIP**: The semantic alignment capability of the image encoder is a vital prerequisite for the success of synthetic perception.
- Insight: For tasks containing metaphor, sarcasm, or implication in text that require "seeing" the scenario to understand, synthetic vision serves as a valuable complement.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐ The research question is novel, but the methodological framework is majorly evaluative with no model architectural innovation.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Comprehensive ablations across four datasets, four types of encoders, five T2I models, and four prompt strategies.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The research question is well-defined, and the three-stage framework is clear.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐ Provides valuable reference for the systematic evaluation of the synthetic perception paradigm, though lacking in methodological innovation.
