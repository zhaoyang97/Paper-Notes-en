# Mixture-of-Expert Variational Autoencoders for Cross-Modality Embedding of Type Ia Supernova Data

**Conference**: ICML2025 (ML4Astro Workshop)  
**arXiv**: [2507.16817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16817)  
**Code**: [YunyiShen/VAESNe-dev](https://github.com/YunyiShen/VAESNe-dev)  
**Area**: Physics  
**Keywords**: Multimodal VAE, Supernovae, Cross-modality generation, Perceiver, Light curves, Spectra

## TL;DR

A multimodal Mixture-of-Experts VAE (MMVAE) based on the Perceiver-IO architecture is proposed to jointly embed light curves and spectra of Type Ia supernovae, enabling cross-modality probabilistic generation from light curves to spectra with reconstruction precision outperforming contrastive learning baselines.

## Background & Motivation

### Background
Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) are thermonuclear explosions of white dwarfs, widely used as standardizable candles for cosmological distances due to the Phillips relation between peak luminosity and decline rate. With the launch of the LSST survey at the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, approximately 1 million supernova light curves are expected annually, but spectroscopic follow-up resources are extremely scarce (with a coverage of only 0.1–1%).

### Key Challenge

**Modality Heterogeneity**: Light curves (photometric data) and spectra are two highly heterogeneous data modalities with irregular sampling and variable sequence lengths.

**Many-to-Many Mapping**: A single light curve can correspond to spectra at multiple different epochs, and the same spectrum can be compatible with multiple light curves.

**Missing Modality at Inference Time**: The vast majority of supernovae only have photometric data, requiring the model to perform inference under a single modality.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Contrastive learning (e.g., Maven) only learns aligned representations but does not support generation; diffusion models (e.g., Shen & Gagliano 2025) only perform conditional generation without providing a general representation.

### Goal
To design a unified framework that simultaneously achieves: (1) inferring time-varying spectral behavior from light curves, and (2) learning joint representations beneficial for downstream tasks.

## Method

### Data Encoding

**Light Curve Encoding**: Each observation point $(t_{n,i}, m_{n,i}, b_{n,i})$ contains time, magnitude, and band information. Time is encoded using sinusoidal encoding mapped by a learnable MLP; magnitude uses a linear embedding; band uses categorical embedding. The sum of these three yields an embedding sequence of $\mathbb{R}^{L_{\text{photo}} \times d}$, zero-padded to 10 observations per band (total length of 60).

**Spectra Encoding**: Each spectrum consists of wavelength-flux pairs $(\lambda_{n,i}, f_{n,i})$. Wavelength is encoded via sinusoidal encoding + MLP, flux uses linear embedding, and the two are summed. The spectral phase (time offset relative to peak brightness) is encoded as a special token appended to the end of the sequence, producing an embedding of $\mathbb{R}^{(L_{\text{spec}}+1) \times d}$.

### Transceiver Encoder-Decoder

Inspired by Perceiver-IO, a **transceiver** (transient perceiver) architecture is designed:
- **Encoder**: Uses a fixed-size latent variable sequence as queries to attend to input embeddings (keys/values) through cross-attention, outputting posterior mean and variance. Compared to standard Transformer encoders like Maven, it does not require fixed input lengths.
- **Decoder**: Similarly uses cross-attention to decode from latent variables back to target modalities.
- Each encoder/decoder has 4 layers of multi-head attention with 4 attention heads, model dimension $d=32$, and MLP dimension 32.

### Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts VAE (MMVAE)

**Core Idea**: Modeling the joint posterior as a mixture distribution of each modal posterior:

$$p(z_n | x_{n,1}, \dots, x_{n,M}) \approx \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} q_{\phi_m}(z_n | x_{n,m})$$

Each modality has an independent encoder $q_{\phi_m}$ and decoder $q_{\psi_m}$, with each modality conditionally independent during decoding:

$$p(x_{n,1,\dots,M} | z_n) \approx \prod_{m=1}^{M} q_{\psi_m}(x_{n,m} | z_n)$$

**Distribution Choice**: Both encoder and decoder parameterize the mean and variance of Laplace distributions; the prior $p(z)$ is a standard Laplace distribution, with a latent space dimension of $z \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$.

**Training Objective**: Evaluated using the Importance Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE) objective:

$$\mathcal{L}(x_{1,\dots,M}) = \mathbb{E}_{z^{1:K} \sim q_\phi} \left[ \log \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{1}{K} \frac{p(z) \prod_m q_{\psi_m}}{q_\phi} \right]$$

Using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, training is performed for 500 iterations.

### Baselines
1. **Uni-modal Spectral VAE**: Trained solely on spectra, utilizing the same transceiver architecture + standard ELBO loss.
2. **Contrastive Learning Transceiver**: Light curve and spectral encoders are jointly trained using the InfoNCE loss to minimize paired Frobenius distance in the $\mathbb{R}^{4 \times 8}$ space.
3. **Training Set Average Spectra**: The mean spectrum of the training set at each phase.

## Key Experimental Results

### Dataset
- **Source**: The Sedona radiative transfer simulation grid of Goldstein & Kasen (2018), containing 4,500 Type Ia supernovae.
- **Spectra**: Complete SED surfaces of each SN, with 1-day step size and ~30 Å wavelength intervals, extracting spectra from 10 days before peak to 30 days after peak (within a 10-day window).
- **Photometry**: Simulated LSST 6-band (ugrizy) photometry, requiring at least 10 valid observations.
- **Preprocessing**: Take the logarithm of spectral flux, with all values standardized to z-scores.
- **Split**: 80/10/10 training/validation/test.

### Cross-Modality Spectral Generation Results

| Method | Reconstruction Accuracy | CI Coverage | Remarks |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| MMVAE (Ours) | Comparable to Spectral VAE | Under-covered (under-calibrated) | Consistently outperforms contrastive learning across multiple phases |
| Spectral VAE (Upper bound) | Best | Better | Spectrometry only, no photometric information |
| Contrastive Learning + Nearest Neighbor | Poor | Incalculable | Essentially retrieves the average spectrum |
| Training Set Average | Worst | — | Unconditional generation |

**Key Findings**:
- The accuracy of MMVAE in reconstructing spectra from light curves approaches that of the VAE trained directly on spectra (upper bound), significantly outperforming the contrastive learning baseline.
- Contrastive learning methods essentially retrieve the "average supernova spectrum" for the given phase, failing to effectively utilize light curve information.
- In robustness testing with progressive masking of light curve inputs, MMVAE performance remains stable.
- **Calibration Issue**: Confidence interval coverage of posterior samples is insufficient, potentially originating from the encoder's limited capacity to approximate complex posteriors.

### Physical Parameter Regression
A small MLP is fitted using the encoded light curve embeddings to regress physical parameters from the simulation grid (kinetic energy $E_k$, ejecta mass $m_{\text{ej}}$, etc.). Compared to an end-to-end transceiver baseline, this demonstrates the utility of the latent representations for downstream tasks.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Application of Perceiver-IO in Astronomical Time Series**: The transceiver architecture naturally accommodates variable-length, irregularly sampled astronomical time series, avoiding the requirement of standard Transformers for fixed-length inputs.
2. **MMVAE Unifies Generation and Representation**: Compared to contrastive learning (representation only) and diffusion models (generation only), MMVAE simultaneously achieves both objectives within a single framework.
3. **Choice of Laplace Distribution**: Using Laplace distributions instead of Gaussian for both the prior and posterior might be better suited to the sparse/spiky characteristics of supernova data.
4. **Practical Value for LSST**: The model supports uni-modal input during inference, enabling real-time supernova classification and follow-up observation prioritization in scenarios where spectra are missing.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Insufficient Posterior Calibration**: The confidence interval coverage is low, and the model's uncertainty estimation is unreliable, limiting scientific applications.
2. **Validated Only on Simulated Data**: All experiments are based on radiative transfer simulations without testing generalization on real observational data.
3. **Low Intrinsic Diversity of Type Ia Supernovae**: SNe Ia have less intrinsic variation compared to core-collapse supernovae; whether the model is effective on more heterogeneous supernova classes remains uncertain.
4. **Small Training Scale**: With only 4,500 samples and 500 iterations, the model capacity and training adequacy remain questionable.
5. **Small Latent Space Dimension**: A $4 \times 4 = 16$-dimensional latent space may be insufficient to capture the fine structures of spectra.
6. **Lack of Direct Comparison with Diffusion Models**: No quantitative comparison was conducted against the prior diffusion-based methods from the same team.
7. **Only Two Modalities**: A third modality, such as host galaxy images, is not incorporated (the authors mention planning to extend this in the Discussion).

## Related Work & Insights

- **Maven** (Zhang et al., 2024): A multimodal foundation model for supernovae, using contrastive learning to align light curves and spectra, but does not support generation.
- **Variational Diffusion Transformers** (Shen & Gagliano, 2025): A conditional diffusion model that generates spectral posteriors from photometry, but does not provide general representations.
- **AstroCLIP** (Parker et al., 2024): An astronomical cross-modality foundation model utilizing contrastive learning to align galaxy images and spectra.
- **MMVAE/MMVAE+** (Shi et al., 2019; Palumbo et al., 2023): The theoretical foundation of multimodal VAEs on which this work builds directly.
- **Perceiver-IO** (Jaegle et al., 2021): A general architecture that handles arbitrary-length inputs through cross-attention.
