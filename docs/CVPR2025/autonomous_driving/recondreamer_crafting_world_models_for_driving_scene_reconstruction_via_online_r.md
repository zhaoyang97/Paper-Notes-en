# ReconDreamer: Crafting World Models for Driving Scene Reconstruction via Online Restoration

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2411.19548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19548)  
**Code**: [https://recondreamer.github.io](https://recondreamer.github.io)  
**Area**: Autonomous Driving / Scene Reconstruction / World Models  
**Keywords**: Driving Scene Reconstruction, World Models, 3D Gaussian Splatting, Online Restoration, Novel Trajectory Rendering

## TL;DR

This paper proposes ReconDreamer, which enhances driving scene reconstruction by incrementally integrating world model knowledge. The core components are DriveRestorer (a fine-tuned world model that restores rendering artifacts online) and a Progressive Data Updating Strategy (PDUS). It achieves high-quality novel trajectory rendering under large maneuvers (e.g., a multi-lane change of over 6 meters) for the first time, achieving a 24.87% improvement in NTA-IoU over the baseline.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Closed-loop simulation is crucial for end-to-end autonomous driving, requiring the reconstruction of sensor data from novel trajectory viewpoints. NeRF and 3DGS are the primary scene reconstruction techniques, but constrained by the density and diversity of training data, rendering quality declines sharply when deviating from the original trajectory.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Traditional 3DGS/NeRF methods (such as Street Gaussians, PVG) produce severe ghosting artifacts during novel trajectory rendering due to the lack of training data in novel viewpoint regions; (2) DriveDreamer4D proposes leveraging pre-trained world models to generate novel trajectory viewpoint data to expand the training set, but this training-free direct integration is only effective for simple, minor offsets (e.g., a 3-meter lane change) and still fails under large maneuvers (e.g., a 6-meter multi-lane change).

**Key Challenge**: Directly using data generated by world models introduces a significant domain gap (it is not fully consistent with real-world data). Moreover, as the trajectory offset increases, the rendering quality degrades more severely, and the world model struggles to restore artifacts of such substantial offsets in a single step.

**Goal**: To achieve high-quality driving scene reconstruction under large maneuvers by incrementally integrating world model knowledge.

**Key Insight**: Instead of letting the world model generate perfect videos of brand-new views in a single step, the world model is tasked with learning to restore artifacts produced by 3DGS rendering (rather than generating from scratch), thereby reducing the difficulty of each restoration step through the progressive expansion of the trajectory offset distance.

**Core Idea**: Fine-tune the world model as a "rendering restorer" (DriveRestorer) to restore ghosting artifacts in 3DGS novel trajectory rendering online; in conjunction with a Progressive Data Updating Strategy (PDUS), the trajectory offset is gradually expanded to keep the difficulty of each restoration step manageable.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The training of ReconDreamer is divided into an iterative loop: (1) render novel trajectory videos containing artifacts using the current 3DGS model; (2) use DriveRestorer to restore artifacts and recover video quality; (3) train the 3DGS model using the restored videos alongside the original trajectory videos; (4) progressively expand the novel trajectory offset distance via PDUS and repeat the above process. As iterations proceed, 3DGS gains the ability to render novel viewpoints with increasingly larger offsets.

### Key Designs

1. **DriveRestorer (Online Rendering Restorer)**:

    - **Function**: Restores artifact-ridden novel trajectory videos rendered by 3DGS to high-quality videos.
    - **Mechanism**: Fine-tuned based on an autonomous driving world model (e.g., DriveDreamer). The key challenge is the lack of paired datasets for rendering restoration. The solution is to utilize an **under-trained 3DGS model** to render artifact-containing videos along the original trajectory, pairing them with original ground-truth videos to form restoration data pairs $\{\hat{V}_{ori}^k, V_{ori}\}$ (sampled from different training stages $k$ to increase diversity). During training, a mask is applied to the degraded videos (focusing on distant areas and sky-scene boundaries), and optimization is performed using diffusion loss. During inference, structural conditions (3D bounding boxes and HD maps) are aligned to the novel trajectory through projective transformation.
    - **Design Motivation**: The restoration task is significantly easier than generation from scratch—rendered videos already contain a wealth of accurate appearance and structural information, meaning DriveRestorer only needs to handle the artifact regions. Utilizing renderings from under-trained models as degraded samples constitutes a clever self-supervised data construction method.

2. **Progressive Data Updating Strategy (PDUS)**:

    - **Function**: Keeps the restoration difficulty manageable by gradually expanding the trajectory offset, enabling rendering under large maneuvers.
    - **Mechanism**: During training, the novel trajectory is updated every $S$ steps. At the $k$-th update, the offset distance is $y = k \cdot \Delta y$ meters. The current 3DGS is used to render the novel trajectory video, which is then restored by DriveRestorer and added to the training set. The sampling weight for old vs. new data is $w = k / \sum_{j=1}^k j$ (assigning higher weight to later data), and the training set is formed as $D = 0.5 D_{ori} \cup 0.5 D_{novel}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Directly rendering large offsets (e.g., 6 meters) yields extremely poor quality, which DriveRestorer cannot restore fully in a single step. The progressive strategy breaks down large offsets into multiple small offset steps; at each step, 3DGS has already learned some novel viewpoint information from the restoration data of the previous step, resulting in better rendering quality and making restoration easier.

3. **Structure-Condition-Driven Spatial-Temporal Consistency**:

    - **Function**: Guarantees spatial-temporal consistency of traffic elements in the restored videos.
    - **Mechanism**: DriveRestorer uses 3D bounding boxes and HD maps as control conditions during inference, aligning these conditions to the novel trajectory via projective transformation. This ensures that the restored positions of vehicles, lane markings, etc., remain consistent with the actual scene layout.
    - **Design Motivation**: Purely visual restoration may alter the position or morphology of traffic elements; structural conditions provide hard constraints that are crucial for the fidelity of closed-loop simulation.

### Loss & Training

DriveRestorer uses the standard diffusion loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}} = \mathbb{E}[\|\epsilon_t - \epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, c)\|_2^2]$.

3DGS reconstruction employs a hybrid loss:
- Original data: $\mathcal{L}_{ori} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}^{RGB} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}^{Depth} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}^{SSIM}$
- Novel trajectory data (without depth GT): $\mathcal{L}_{novel} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}^{RGB} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}^{SSIM}$
- Total loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{ori} + \mathcal{L}_{novel}$

### Key Findings

- ReconDreamer is the first method capable of rendering effectively under a 6-meter offset. At a 6-meter offset, DriveDreamer4D achieves an NTA-IoU of only 0.121, whereas ReconDreamer reaches 0.358 (a 195.87% improvement).
- Progressive updating yields substantially better results than one-time generation, proving that the strategy of lowering restoration difficulty at each step is effective.
- The near-100% win rate in the user study demonstrates a significant leap in visual quality.
- DriveRestorer effectively eliminates ghosting artifacts, particularly in distant regions and sky-scene boundaries.
- The design of incrementally increasing late-stage data weights in PDUS ensures that the model continuously learns from the latest restored data.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Paradigm Shift from Generation to Restoration**: Tasking the world model to learn how to restore rendering artifacts (which already contain significant amount of accurate information) is much easier than generating novel-view videos from scratch. This concept has broad applicability.
- **Clever Utilization of Under-trained Models**: Utilizing the rendering outputs from different stages of the 3DGS training process as natural degraded-restoration data pairs, eliminating the need for manual annotation.
- **Curriculum Learning Philosophy in Progressive Strategy**: Decomposing large offsets into smaller, sequential steps to control the restoration difficulty per step is an elegant application of curriculum learning to rendering tasks.

## Limitations & Future Work

- It relies on the generation quality of the world model; limitations of the world model itself will propagate to the restoration quality.
- Requires 3D bounding boxes and HD maps as structural conditions, rendering it inapplicable in scenes lacking these annotations.
- The update step size Δy of PDUS needs to be set manually.
- The restoration process increases the overall training time.
- DriveRestorer could be extended into a more general rendering restoration tool, not limited to driving scenarios.
- The possibility of combining with feed-forward generative methods has not yet been explored.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs DriveDreamer4D**: Directly utilizes world models to augment data in a training-free manner, which works for minor offsets but fails at large offsets. ReconDreamer resolves this large-offset issue by fine-tuning the world model into a restorer combined with a progressive strategy.
- **vs Street Gaussians**: A standard 3DGS method for driving scenarios, limited by training data density. ReconDreamer augments data on top of it using world model knowledge, yielding an average improvement of 24.87% NTA-IoU.
- **vs SGD/GGS/MagicDrive3D**: These approaches utilize generative models to expand static backgrounds or sparse view points, but are insufficient to capture the complexity of dynamic driving environments.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The concepts of progressive world model knowledge integration and restorer design are highly novel.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Comparisons with multiple baselines and user studies are comprehensive, though verification across more diverse scenarios is lacking.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear methodological pipeline with high-quality illustrations.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Solves the rendering problem under large maneuvers for the first time, possessing high practical value for closed-loop simulation.
