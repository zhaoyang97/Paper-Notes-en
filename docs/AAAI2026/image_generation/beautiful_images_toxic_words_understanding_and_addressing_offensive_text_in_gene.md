# Beautiful Images, Toxic Words: Understanding and Addressing Offensive Text in Generated Images

**Conference**: AAAI2026
**arXiv**: [2502.05066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05066)  
**Code**: [sprintml/ToxicBench](https://github.com/sprintml/ToxicBench) / [sprintml/SafeTextGen](https://github.com/sprintml/SafeTextGen)  
**Area**: Image Generation
**Keywords**: Diffusion Models, NSFW Safety, Text-in-Image, Safety Fine-tuning, benchmark

## TL;DR
This paper identifies a novel threat of NSFW text embedded in diffusion-model-generated images, proposes NSFW-Intervention — a targeted LoRA fine-tuning method applied to text-rendering layers — and releases the ToxicBench benchmark.

## Background & Motivation
- Mainstream diffusion models (SD3, SDXL, Flux, DeepFloyd IF) can render not only realistic visual content but also legible text within generated images (e.g., signage, captions, typography).
- Prior NSFW safety work has focused primarily on **visual-level** harmful content (violence, nudity), successfully mitigated via safety filters and concept erasure techniques.
- However, **text rendered within generated images** can equally contain slurs, racial epithets, and sexually suggestive terms — constituting a previously overlooked safety threat.
- Such harmful text can escalate to hate speech or ideological propaganda, posing particular risk in the open-weight model setting.

## Core Problem
1. **Novel Threat Identification**: All mainstream diffusion models can embed NSFW text in generated images, and existing safety mechanisms offer no effective defense.
2. **Failure of Naïve Approaches**:
    - Input-side text filtering: lacks visual context (e.g., "Penetrating" carries different meanings in different scenes) and is ineffective against white-box access to open-source models.
    - Output-side OCR detection: models frequently generate misspelled text that humans can still interpret but OCR+toxicity detectors fail to flag (detection rate below 50% on SDXL).
3. **Insufficiency of Existing Methods**: Methods such as AURA, ESD, and Safe-CLIP suppress NSFW text but **equally degrade** benign text generation quality, lacking targeted precision.

## Method

### ToxicBench Benchmark
- **Dataset Construction**: 218 prompt templates (adapted from CreativeBench) × 437 NSFW terms (each paired with a semantically similar benign substitute generated by GPT-4).
    - Training split: 337 NSFW–benign word pairs → 73,466 prompt pairs.
    - Test split: 100 held-out word pairs → 21,800 prompt pairs, evaluating generalization to unseen NSFW terms.
- **Evaluation Pipeline**:
    - Image generation → EasyOCR text extraction → computation of text/image quality metrics.
    - Supports two modes: pre/post-mitigation comparison & standalone NSFW detection.
- **Novel Metric NGramLD** (N-gram Levenshtein Distance):
    - Extracts all k-gram substrings ($k \in [1, n+1]$) from OCR output and computes the minimum edit distance to the target word.
    - Addresses the over-penalization of long text outputs by standard Levenshtein distance, focusing on the most relevant substring match.

### NSFW-Intervention Method
**Step 1: Constructing the Safety Fine-tuning Dataset**

- For each NSFW prompt, generate an image $I_{\text{NSFW}}$ containing harmful text.
- Replace the NSFW term with its benign substitute and regenerate $I_{\text{benign}}$ using intermediate activation caching-based image editing.
- The two images differ only in the embedded text, with all other visual elements preserved.
- This yields training triplets $(x_{\text{NSFW}}, I_{\text{NSFW}}, I_{\text{benign}})$.

**Step 2: Targeted Safety Fine-tuning**

- Building on findings by Staniszewski et al. that text rendering in diffusion models is controlled by a small subset of attention layers:
    - SD3: joint attention layers.
    - SDXL / DeepFloyd IF: cross-attention layers.
- LoRA updates are applied exclusively to these text-rendering layers, substantially reducing the number of trainable parameters.
- Training objective: the model receives text encodings $\phi(x_{\text{NSFW}})$ from the NSFW prompt but learns to output the benign image $I_{\text{benign}}$.

**Training Loss**:

$$\mathcal{L} = \|w(t) \cdot (f_\theta(I_{\text{NSFW}}(t), t, \phi(x_{\text{NSFW}})) - I_{\text{benign}})\|^2$$

- $w(t)$ adopts logit-normal timestep weighting, prioritizing mid-to-late denoising steps where text is most legible.
- $I_{\text{NSFW}}(t)$ denotes the NSFW image with Gaussian noise added at timestep $t$.

**Core Advantage**: Directly modifies model weights, making it applicable to white-box/open-source settings and bypass-resistant to input/output filtering.

## Key Experimental Results

| Model | Benign NGramLD Δ↓ | NSFW NGramLD Δ↑ | Differential Gain | KID |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SD3 | 2.07 | 3.63 | +1.56 | 0.059/0.061 |
| DeepFloyd IF | 3.78 | 4.72 | +0.94 | 0.059/0.060 |
| SDXL | 4.75 | 5.69 | +0.94 | 0.063/0.065 |

- **Baseline Comparison**: AURA achieves a NSFW/benign NGramLD differential of only 0.36 (2.56 vs. 2.20); Safe-CLIP achieves 0.22 (2.87 vs. 2.65) — both far less targeted than the proposed method.
- **User Study**: The human recognition rate of harmful text in NSFW-prompted images decreases from 78.67% to 26.56%; misspelled NSFW variants drop from 76.41% to 11.45% (unseen during training), while benign text is maintained at 55.40%.
- **Ablation**: Fine-tuning all attention layers instead of only text-rendering layers yields a NGramLD improvement of only +0.49 on SD3 (vs. +3.63 for the targeted approach), validating the importance of layer selection.
- **Image Quality**: KID increases by at most 9%; FID degradation is minimal.

## Highlights & Insights
1. **Novel Threat Definition**: The paper is the first to systematically expose the safety blind spot of NSFW text embedded in generated images, with a clear and practically relevant problem formulation.
2. **Precise Layer-Level Intervention**: By exploiting the locality of text-rendering layers, LoRA fine-tuning is applied to a minimal set of parameters, balancing safety and generation quality.
3. **Ingenious Data Construction**: Activation-cache-based image editing produces image pairs differing only in rendered text, providing precise supervision signals for training.
4. **Complete Benchmark Ecosystem**: ToxicBench encompasses a dataset, evaluation metric (NGramLD), and pipeline, offering a standardized toolkit for future research.
5. **Strong Generalization**: The method is effective on unseen NSFW terms and spelling variants, and is extensible to VAR-based models (e.g., Infinity).

## Limitations & Future Work
- NSFW text suppression is not fully complete; some samples remain human-readable (e.g., highly sensitive terms such as "giant cocks").
- Benign text quality incurs some degradation (CLIP-Score on SD3 drops from 91.42 to 85.10), leaving room for optimization.
- The method relies on prior knowledge of text-rendering layers; new architectures require re-identification of these layers.
- Evaluation depends on OCR accuracy; artistic fonts or severely distorted text may evade detection.
- The dataset is based on English NSFW vocabulary; applicability to multilingual settings is not validated.
- Robustness against adversarial attacks (e.g., deliberately eliciting variant spellings) requires further investigation.

## Related Work & Insights

| Method | Type | NSFW Suppression | Benign Preservation | Applicable Setting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SLD | Inference guidance | Effective for visual NSFW, not for text | Good | API/black-box only |
| ESD | Full model fine-tuning | Moderate effect on text | Poor (overall quality degraded) | SD1.4 only |
| AURA | Neuron suppression | Effective but untargeted | Poor (equal harm to benign) | Requires text encoder |
| Safe-CLIP | Encoder fine-tuning | Effective but untargeted | Poor (equal harm to benign) | Requires CLIP architecture |
| **NSFW-Intervention** | **Targeted layer LoRA** | **Strong and generalizable** | **Good** | **General white-box/open-source** |

- The **layer-wise controllability** paradigm is transferable to targeted editing of other generative attributes (e.g., style, watermarks, copyright text).
- **Image–text alignment safety** is a universal concern for multimodal models; the benchmark design methodology proposed here is applicable to video generation models.
- This work is complementary to concept erasure methods (e.g., Selective Amnesia, UCE): the latter erase visual concepts, while this paper erases textual concepts.
- The NGramLD metric is broadly applicable to any task involving text rendering quality evaluation.
