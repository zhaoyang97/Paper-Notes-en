# The Biased Oracle: Assessing LLMs' Understandability and Empathy in Medical Diagnoses

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2511.00924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00924)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/Jeffateth/Biased_Oracle](https://github.com/Jeffateth/Biased_Oracle)  
**Area**: Medical AI / LLM Evaluation
**Keywords**: Medical Communication, Readability, Empathy Assessment, Demographic Bias, LLM-as-Judge

## TL;DR
This work systematically evaluates GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 on readability and empathy in medical diagnostic communication. Both models produce reading levels well above recommended standards (grades 9–13 vs. the recommended grades 6–8). Affective empathy varies significantly with diagnosis type and patient education level, and LLM-as-Judge exhibits severe self-serving bias (GPT inflates its own empathy scores by ~0.3 points).

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: LLMs have achieved diagnostic accuracy approaching or exceeding that of human physicians, yet the communicative quality of their diagnoses—whether patients can understand them and feel cared for—has rarely been systematically evaluated.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing evaluations focus on accuracy and safety, neglecting patient-centered communication quality. The readability of medical advice directly affects patient adherence; content above an eighth-grade reading level renders it inaccessible to a large portion of patients.

**Key Challenge**: LLM-generated medical text may be clear and useful for highly educated patients, yet pose comprehension barriers for those with lower literacy or specific cultural backgrounds. Whether different demographic groups receive equally high-quality communication remains unknown.

**Goal**: To systematically quantify the readability and empathy of LLM medical communication and detect biases along demographic dimensions (age, gender, region, and education level).

**Key Insight**: The study designs 156 synthetic prompts (demographic combinations × diagnostic scenarios), applies five readability metrics and a dual-dimension (affective/cognitive) empathy evaluation framework, and conducts cross-validation with LLM-as-Judge and human annotators.

**Core Idea**: Using 156 synthetic scenarios × a dual evaluation framework (readability + empathy) × LLM-as-Judge cross-validation, the study systematically exposes above-standard readability and empathy bias in LLM medical communication.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
**Design**: 156 prompts = demographic combinations (age/gender/region/education) × 10 diagnostic scenarios → responses generated by GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 → **Readability evaluation** (5 formulas) + **Empathy evaluation** (affective + cognitive, LLM-as-Judge + human annotation) → Statistical analysis (ANOVA/significance testing)

### Key Designs

1. **Readability Evaluation (5 Metrics)**:

    - Function: Quantify the reading difficulty level of generated text.
    - Mechanism: Five formulas—Flesch-Kincaid, SMOG, Gunning Fog, Coleman-Liau, and Dale-Chall—compute grade-level estimates from sentence length, word length, and syllable count.
    - Design Motivation: U.S. health literacy standards recommend a grades 6–8 reading level; cross-validating with multiple metrics avoids single-metric bias.

2. **Empathy Evaluation Framework (Affective + Cognitive)**:

    - Function: Assess the quality of empathy in LLM responses.
    - Mechanism: Affective empathy (1–3) measures emotional resonance; cognitive empathy (1–3) measures perspective-taking. GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 mutually evaluate each other (cross LLM-as-Judge), supplemented by 4 human annotators rating 40 samples.
    - Design Motivation: Empathy is central to effective patient–provider communication; distinguishing affective and cognitive dimensions yields greater precision.

3. **LLM-as-Judge Bias Detection**:

    - Function: Identify systematic biases in LLM self-evaluation.
    - Mechanism: GPT evaluating GPT vs. Claude evaluating GPT; analogously Claude evaluating Claude vs. GPT evaluating Claude. Score discrepancies are compared across conditions, with human annotation serving as a three-way cross-validation.
    - Design Motivation: GPT inflates its own affective empathy scores by ~0.3 points (2.5 vs. 2.2 by Claude); inter-annotator agreement is low (r < 0.5), indicating that LLM-as-Judge is unreliable for subjective assessments.

### Loss & Training
- This is an evaluation study; no model training is involved.
- Statistical analysis: ANOVA is used to test the significance of demographic factors.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Metric | GPT-4o | Claude-3.7 | Recommended Standard |
|--------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| Reading Grade (avg.) | Grades 9–13 | Grades 9–13 | Grades 6–8 |
| Affective Empathy (avg.) | 2.2–3.0 | 2.0–2.8 | — |
| Cognitive Empathy (avg.) | 2.8–3.0 | 2.8–3.0 | — |

### Ablation Study

| Dimension | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Diagnosis Type | Alzheimer's (2.2–3.0) >> Chronic Heart Disease (1.6–2.3) |
| Education Level | Medical degree ~0.5 points lower in affective empathy than high school |
| Age | U-shaped curve (under GPT evaluation) |
| Gender / Region | No significant difference |
| Self-evaluation Bias | GPT inflates own scores by ~0.3 points |

### Key Findings
- Both models substantially exceed recommended reading levels, posing comprehension barriers for patients with lower literacy.
- Affective empathy varies by diagnosis (higher for emotionally salient conditions), while cognitive empathy remains consistently high—indicating that LLMs can understand context but cannot consistently express care.
- LLM-as-Judge is unreliable for empathy assessment: inter-model agreement is low and deviates substantially from human annotation.
- Human annotators detected gender–ethnicity intersectional biases that models overlooked.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Above-standard readability is a universal problem**: All evaluated LLMs generate excessively complex medical text, constituting a serious equity concern for real-world deployment.
- **Self-serving bias in LLM-as-Judge is quantified**: An inflation of +0.3 points represents a documented systematic bias and serves as a warning for all evaluations that rely on LLM-as-Judge.
- **Dissociation between affective and cognitive empathy**: LLMs exhibit high cognitive empathy but unstable affective empathy, demonstrating that "understanding" does not equate to "caring."

## Limitations & Future Work
- The 156 scenarios provide limited coverage; rare diseases are not included.
- Human evaluation covers only 40 samples.
- Only text modality is assessed; multimodal scenarios are not evaluated.
- Synthetic scenarios lack ecological validity.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. MedPaLM**: MedPaLM evaluates diagnostic accuracy, whereas this work evaluates communication quality—the two are complementary.
- **vs. Health Literacy Research**: Traditional research assesses readability in human physicians; this work extends the analysis to LLMs.
