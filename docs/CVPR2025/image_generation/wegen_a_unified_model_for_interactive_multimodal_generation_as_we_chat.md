# WeGen: A Unified Model for Interactive Multimodal Generation as We Chat

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.01115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01115)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/hzphzp/WeGen)  
**Area**: Image Generation  
**Keywords**: Unified Multimodal Model, Interactive Generation, Instance Consistency, Generative Diversity, Design Assistant

## TL;DR

A unified framework named WeGen is proposed, integrating multimodal understanding and visual generation into a single model. By leveraging a Dynamic Instance Identity Consistency (DIIC) data pipeline and a Prompt Self-Rewriting (PSR) mechanism, it addresses the twin challenges of reference image consistency preservation and generation diversity, achieving an interactive experience akin to a conversational design assistant.

## Background & Motivation

### Limitations of Prior Work

**Limitations of Prior Work**: **Background**: Current visual generation tools face two fundamental issues: (1) each visual task requires a specialized model, demanding users to design complex workflows; (2) they are less intuitive than ChatGPT and have a steep learning curve. An ideal design assistant should unify various generation capabilities through natural dialogue.

Existing unified MLLM + diffusion model approaches (such as Emu, SEED-LLaMA) face two key challenges: (a) **Instance Identity Consistency**—users need to retain key attributes of reference images (such as vases or human faces) in generated outputs while allowing reasonable variations (such as pose or lighting), yet existing methods either perform simple copy-pasting or lose identity information; (b) **Insufficient Generative Diversity**—existing methods directly map conditions to continuous features of the diffusion model, lacking stochasticity during the sampling process, which leads different seeds to produce similar outputs.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

WeGen consists of three components: a CLIP encoder (converting reference images into 64 visual features), a Large Language Model (LLM) (handling interleaved text and visual inputs), and an SDXL decoder (translating generated features into final images). Two-stage training is employed: first training the SDXL decoder to reconstruct images from CLIP features, and then freezing CLIP and SDXL to fine-tune the LLM.

### Key Designs 1: Dynamic Instance Identity Consistency (DIIC) Data Pipeline

- **Function**: Build training data from videos, enabling the model to learn to preserve instance identity while allowing natural variations
- **Mechanism**: A four-step pipeline—(1) InternVL2 generates frame captions and extracts noun phrases to identify instances; (2) Grounding DINO localizes bounding boxes; (3) SAM2 tracks instance segmentation across frames; (4) Frame pairs sampled at a time interval $t_{ref}$ are used to construct training data. Compared to unnatural variations generated by artificial augmentation in single-image training, natural variations in videos are more beneficial for learning the/a balance between identity preservation and variation
- **Design Motivation**: Existing methods train on single images (input = output), which encourages simple copy-pasting. Video data naturally contains natural variations (pose, expression, context) of the same instance across different frames, making it an ideal data source for learning consistency-aware generation

### Key Designs 2: Prompt Self-Rewriting (PSR) Mechanism

- **Function**: Enhance the diversity of generation results, preventing different seeds from producing nearly identical outputs
- **Mechanism**: Before generating image features, the language capability of the MLLM is leveraged to rewrite detailed descriptions, introducing randomness brought by discrete text token sampling, which allows the model to explore different semantic interpretations
- **Design Motivation**: Directly mapping conditions to continuous visual features is a deterministic process and lacks natural diversity. Conversely, discretizing CLIP features causes severe information loss. PSR avoids this dilemma by introducing randomness at the semantic level

### Key Designs 3: Scaling Law of Large-Scale CLIP Encoders

- **Function**: Minimize information loss during the encoding-decoding process
- **Mechanism**: Explore the Scaling Law of CLIP when used as an encoder, adopting a larger-scaled CLIP model to reduce the loss of visual information
- **Design Motivation**: Information loss is one of the root causes of degraded identity consistency

### Loss & Training

In the first stage, the standard diffusion loss is used to train the SDXL decoder. In the second stage, when fine-tuning the LLM, classification loss is used for text tokens, and regression loss is used for visual features.

### Generation Diversity Comparison

The PSR mechanism makes the diversity of results generated by different seeds significantly higher than that of both the baseline without PSR and other unified models, while maintaining semantic alignment.

### Key Findings

- Video training data (DIIC) yields better performance in identity consistency compared to single-image training combined with data augmentation.
- A larger CLIP encoder significantly improves reconstruction quality and identity preservation.
- PSR produces genuinely diverse outputs under different seeds, rather than minor variations.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Interactive Design Assistant Paradigm**: Users progressively refine generation results through multi-round dialogue, which is more natural than single-turn instructions.
2. **Videos as Natural Data Sources for Consistency Learning**: Utilizing video sequences for consistency preservation training represents a simple yet effective approach.
3. **Unified Framework Covering a Wide Range of Tasks**: A single model supports text-to-image generation, subject-driven generation, conditional generation, inpainting, style transfer, etc.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Unified models may not match the performance of specialized models on a single task.
- The DIIC data pipeline relies on multiple foundation models, resulting in a complex processing workflow.
- Context management across multi-round interactions could become a bottleneck.

## Related Work & Insights

- The approach of using video data to learn object consistency can be extended to tasks such as video editing and virtual try-on.
- Introducing randomness at the semantic level with PSR may inspire other generative tasks that require diverse outputs.

## Rating

⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The direction of unifying multimodal generation and understanding is highly valuable, with both the DIIC data pipeline and the PSR mechanism holding practical significance. The positioning as a design assistant is clear, though the system complexity remains high.
