# Uncertainty Under the Curve: A Sequence-Level Entropy Area Metric for Reasoning LLMs

**Conference**: AAAI 2026
**arXiv**: [2508.20384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20384)
**Code**: Available
**Area**: LLM/NLP
**Keywords**: Entropy Area Score, uncertainty estimation, token-level entropy, training data selection, reasoning LLM

## TL;DR
This paper proposes the Entropy Area Score (EAS)—a method that quantifies uncertainty in reasoning LLMs by integrating token-level predictive entropy via a single forward pass. EAS requires neither external models nor repeated sampling, achieves strong correlation with answer entropy (Pearson $r=0.82$), and when applied to training data selection outperforms Pass Rate filtering by 1.2–2.3% Pass@1, making it an efficient and interpretable uncertainty estimation tool for LLMs.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Reasoning LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-QWQ) have achieved progress on mathematical and scientific reasoning, yet their outputs remain sensitive to evaluation conditions (seed, temperature, prompt), leading to substantial score variance. Uncertainty quantification is critical for reliable deployment.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
   - External methods (training separate uncertainty models) require additional data and computation.
   - Sampling-based methods (Self-Consistency) require multiple inference passes, incurring $K\times$ cost.
   - Token-level probability metrics (perplexity) serve only as proxies for the final answer and do not directly measure uncertainty within the reasoning process.

**Key Challenge**: There is a need for an uncertainty measure that operates in a single forward pass, has no external dependencies, and remains interpretable—yet existing methods are either expensive (multi-sample) or insufficiently fine-grained.

**Goal**: Design an efficient sequence-level uncertainty metric that extracts uncertainty signals from the model's own token-level entropy.

**Key Insight**: The token-level entropy trajectory of a reasoning LLM reflects the model's internal "degree of hesitation"—low entropy indicates confidence, high entropy indicates uncertainty. Integrating the full trajectory yields a sequence-level uncertainty estimate.

**Core Idea**: $\text{EAS}$ = area under the curve of token-level predictive entropy = a sequence-level uncertainty measure obtained from a single forward pass.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Given a sequence $S = (t_1, \ldots, t_T)$ generated by a reasoning LLM, the predictive entropy at each position $t$ is computed over the top-$K$ tokens as $\mathcal{H}_t = -\sum P_t(v)\log_2 P_t(v)$ (with $K=20$ covering 99.87% of the probability mass), and the score is defined as $\text{EAS}(S) = \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} \mathcal{H}_t$.

### Key Designs

1. **Token-Level Entropy Computation**:

    - **Function**: Captures the uncertainty at each generation step.
    - **Mechanism**: Approximates full-vocabulary entropy using the top-20 tokens, with an error below 4.70% (since probability mass is highly concentrated on a small number of tokens).
    - **Design Motivation**: Full-vocabulary (128K) entropy computation is costly; the top-$K$ approximation preserves accuracy while substantially reducing computation.

2. **Area-Under-the-Curve Integration**:

    - **Function**: Aggregates token-level signals into a sequence-level measure.
    - **Mechanism**: Directly sums entropy values across $T$ time steps—the area integral preserves the distinction between "sustained high entropy" and "transient high entropy."
    - **Design Motivation**: Sustained high entropy indicates overall uncertainty, whereas transient high entropy may reflect only localized difficulty; integration naturally distinguishes the two.

3. **Training Data Selection Application**:

    - **Function**: Uses EAS to identify high-potential training samples.
    - **Mechanism**: "High-potential" corresponds to moderate EAS values—the model exhibits internal struggle without being completely lost. Samples with extremely low or extremely high EAS are filtered out.
    - **Design Motivation**: More efficient than Pass Rate (which requires multiple sampling passes to judge correctness)—a single forward pass suffices.

### Loss & Training
- No training is required; EAS is a inference-time metric.
- When used for data selection, EAS outperforms Pass Rate under the same sample budget.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Model / Dataset | EAS Pearson $r$ | Best Baseline $r$ |
|----------------|----------------|------------------|
| Qwen-14B / AIME | **0.8237** | 0.65 |
| DS-R1-Qwen-14B / GPQA | **0.5968** | 0.42 |

### Ablation Study: Training Data Selection

| Method | Qwen-14B Pass@1 Gain | LLaMA-8B Pass@1 Gain |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Pass Rate filtering | Baseline | Baseline |
| **EAS filtering** | **+1.2–2.3%** | **+2.1–2.3%** |

### Key Findings
- **EAS strongly correlates with answer entropy** ($r=0.82$) while requiring only 1/4 of the computation cost (single pass vs. 4 inference passes).
- **Samples with moderate EAS are most valuable for training**—the model is engaged in "meaningful struggle."
- **Top-20 approximation is sufficiently accurate**: error <4.70%, covering 99.87% of the probability mass.
- **Not applicable to open-ended generation tasks**: EAS assumes answers can be binarized for correctness.

## Highlights & Insights
- **"The entropy trajectory is a window into the model's internal deliberation"**—low-entropy sequences indicate confidence; high-entropy sequences indicate hesitation. The intuition is elegant.
- The efficiency advantage of a single forward pass over 4 repeated sampling runs makes EAS practical for large-scale data pipelines.
- The concept of "high-potential" samples offers inspiration for curriculum learning and active learning.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Applicable only to tasks with well-defined correct answers (mathematical/scientific QA).
- Local entropy signals cannot capture global structural uncertainty.
- No comparison against theoretically grounded calibration methods such as conformal prediction.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Self-Consistency**: Requires multi-sample voting; EAS is more efficient with a single forward pass.
- **vs. P(True)**: Requires additional prompting; EAS is extracted directly from the generation process.
- EAS can serve as a reward signal for RL training, targeting high-EAS regions that require additional learning.

## Additional Notes
- Sequence-level entropy area metrics capture the dynamics of uncertainty throughout the reasoning process more effectively than single-point entropy, and can serve as an auxiliary signal in RL training.
- Integration with process reward models (PRMs) could enable finer-grained step-level uncertainty estimation.
