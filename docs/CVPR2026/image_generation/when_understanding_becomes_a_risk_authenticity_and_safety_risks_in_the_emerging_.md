# When Understanding Becomes a Risk: Authenticity and Safety Risks in the Emerging Image Generation Paradigm

**Conference**: CVPR 2026
**arXiv**: [2603.24079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24079)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: AI Safety / Image Generation
**Keywords**: Multimodal Large Language Models, Image Safety, Unsafe Content Generation, Fake Image Detection, Diffusion Model Comparison

## TL;DR

This paper presents a systematic comparative analysis of safety risks between MLLMs (Multimodal Large Language Models) and diffusion models, finding that MLLMs are more prone to generating unsafe images due to their superior semantic understanding (capable of interpreting abstract and non-English prompts), and that images generated by MLLMs are harder to detect by existing fake image detectors—even when detectors are fine-tuned specifically for MLLMs, detection can be circumvented by enriching prompt details.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: The text-to-image generation paradigm is shifting from diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) toward MLLMs (e.g., Janus, Bagel). Diffusion models generate images via iterative denoising, whereas MLLMs integrate language understanding, cross-modal reasoning, and image generation within a unified architecture, offering stronger semantic comprehension and compositional flexibility.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: A relatively mature safety infrastructure has been established around diffusion models—including safety filters (e.g., NSFW detectors) and fake image detectors (e.g., DE-FAKE). However, these defenses are optimized for diffusion models. MLLMs operate under a fundamentally different generation mechanism: they can interpret subtle, abstract, or indirectly expressed unsafe intent, which implies that existing safeguards may fail systematically.

**Key Challenge**: The stronger semantic understanding of MLLMs is a double-edged sword—while enhancing usability and generation quality, it also enables the model to comprehend and execute unsafe instructions that diffusion models simply cannot parse. The research community has yet to systematically recognize the new safety risks introduced by this paradigm shift.

**Goal**: Two core research questions: (1) Are MLLMs more likely than diffusion models to generate unsafe images? (2) Are fake images generated by MLLMs harder to detect than those from diffusion models?

**Key Insight**: Using diffusion models as a reference baseline, the study generates 82,880 images from 1,184 unsafe prompts and 14,000 images from 2,000 benign prompts, conducting systematic comparative experiments across 7 models (2 diffusion + 5 MLLMs).

**Core Idea**: The semantic understanding advantage of MLLMs becomes a liability in the safety dimension—they can interpret abstract unsafe prompts and generate meaningful unsafe content, whereas diffusion models, failing to understand such prompts, inadvertently produce corrupted outputs that appear "safe."

## Method

### Overall Architecture

This paper is a systematic measurement study rather than a proposal of a new method. The research framework spans two dimensions: unsafe image generation (RQ1) and fake image detection (RQ2). Each dimension is analyzed quantitatively and qualitatively using carefully curated datasets, model ensembles, and evaluation metrics.

### Key Designs

1. **Unsafe Image Generation Evaluation Framework**:

    - **Function**: Quantitatively measures each model's propensity to generate harmful content under unsafe prompts.
    - **Mechanism**: Seven models are selected (SD3.5 Large, SD3.5 Large Turbo, Bagel, Janus, Janus Pro, TokenFlow, VILA-U) and evaluated against five unsafe prompt datasets (I2P, Lexica, 4chan, Template, TemplateLong), with 10 images generated per prompt. **All external safety mechanisms are disabled** to measure the models' intrinsic tendencies. The OpenAI Moderation API serves as the safety classifier (89.2% agreement with human annotations), and an unsafe score (proportion of NSFW-flagged images out of 10) is computed per prompt–model pair.
    - **Design Motivation**: A fair comparison of intrinsic model safety requires isolating the effect of safety filters.

2. **Corrupted Image Factor Analysis**:

    - **Function**: Explains why diffusion models appear safer—not due to better safety alignment, but due to comprehension failure.
    - **Mechanism**: For 50 4chan prompts, safe counterparts are constructed by replacing unsafe terms; each model generates 10 images, and the "corrupted image rate" is measured. Diffusion models exhibit corruption rates as high as 80% (SD3.5 Large) on complex prompts, while the worst-performing MLLM reaches only 4.5% (Bagel). Since 99.4% of corrupted images are classified as safe, these generation failures artificially deflate diffusion models' unsafe scores.
    - **Design Motivation**: To reveal the true cause behind apparent safety differences and prevent the erroneous conclusion that diffusion models are inherently safer.

3. **Fake Image Detection Evaluation Framework**:

    - **Function**: Tests the ability of existing detectors to identify MLLM-generated images.
    - **Mechanism**: Benign prompts from MSCOCO and Flickr30k are used to generate images, which are then evaluated against four detectors (two commercial: Winston.AI and Illuminarty; two open-source: DE-FAKE and AIorNot-SigLIP2). A prompt richness gradient experiment is further designed: v0 (original prompt) → v1 (GPT-4o-expanded description) → v2 (further expanded), to examine how prompt length affects detection difficulty.
    - **Design Motivation**: To verify whether MLLM-generated images exhibit a distinct "fingerprint" from diffusion-generated images, and whether existing detectors generalize accordingly.

### Additional Analysis Dimensions

- **Cross-Lingual Understanding Analysis**: Chinese-language unsafe prompts (TemplateLongChinese) are used; diffusion models fail entirely (unsafe score = 0), while MLLMs continue to generate unsafe images.
- **Gender Bias Analysis**: MLLMs tend to generate female images in response to gender-neutral pornographic prompts.
- **Associative Reasoning Analysis**: Abstract slang prompts (e.g., "f*ck that the place is a sh*t hole") cause diffusion models to literally render the words, whereas MLLMs associatively generate corresponding scenes of filth.

### Fake Image Detection Results

MLLM-generated images pose greater challenges to existing detectors. Even when detectors are fine-tuned on MLLM data, cross-MLLM generalization remains poor. More critically, MLLMs can reduce detection probability by enriching prompt details (v0→v1→v2)—a phenomenon absent for diffusion models.

### Key Findings

- **MLLMs are systematically less safe**: Across all five datasets, MLLMs consistently yield higher unsafe scores than diffusion models.
- **The root cause is understanding capability, not safety alignment failure**: Diffusion models produce corrupted (and thus safe-classified) outputs because they cannot parse complex prompts—not because they are better aligned.
- **External safety filters are insufficient**: Even with NSFW detectors applied, MLLMs' unsafe scores remain higher than those of diffusion models.
- **Detector generalization dilemma**: Detectors fine-tuned on one MLLM perform poorly on others, and MLLMs can evade detection via prompt engineering.
- **MLLMs exhibit gender bias**: Gender-neutral pornographic prompts tend to elicit female image generation.

## Highlights & Insights

- **The core insight that "understanding is risk" is highly thought-provoking**: Conventionally, greater model intelligence is considered desirable; this paper demonstrates that understanding capability is a double-edged sword in the safety dimension. This framework generalizes to safety analysis of any AI capability—any technical advance that enhances "understanding" may simultaneously expand the attack surface.
- **The discovery of corrupted images as an "accidental safety valve" is intriguing**: Diffusion models are safe not because they are "trained to be safer," but because "they fail to understand and thus fail to generate." This implies that as diffusion models improve in comprehension, their safety risks will increase correspondingly.
- **The relationship between prompt richness and detection difficulty** reveals a novel attack vector: users can add more descriptive detail to prompt MLLMs into generating images that are harder to detect—an approach that does not work on diffusion models, underscoring a fundamental difference in generation mechanisms.

## Limitations & Future Work

- All experiments disable model safety mechanisms, which measures intrinsic model tendency but does not fully reflect real-world deployment scenarios.
- Only 7 models are tested; leading closed-source MLLMs with generation capabilities such as GPT-Image-1 and Gemini are not covered.
- Safety classification relies on the OpenAI Moderation API (which, despite 89.2% agreement with human annotations, carries approximately 10% error).
- The paper does not deeply analyze the specific mechanisms of MLLM safety alignment training, inferring only from external behavioral observations.
- Future directions include: (1) designing novel safety filters tailored to the semantic understanding characteristics of MLLMs; (2) leveraging prompt understanding capabilities inversely for safety detection.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. NSFW safety filters**: This paper demonstrates that even with external filters applied, MLLMs remain less safe than diffusion models, indicating the need for safety mechanisms specifically designed for MLLMs.
- **vs. DE-FAKE / AIorNot and other fake image detectors**: These detectors, optimized for diffusion models, show significantly degraded performance on MLLM-generated images, with poor post-fine-tuning generalization, revealing new challenges in the detector–generator arms race in the MLLM era.
- **vs. existing safety benchmarks (I2P, Lexica)**: This paper is the first to extend these benchmarks to a cross-paradigm comparison between MLLMs and diffusion models, supplemented by new test sets such as TemplateLong to probe semantic understanding differences.
