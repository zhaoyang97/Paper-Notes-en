# Coreference as an Indicator of Context Scope in Multimodal Narrative

**Conference**: ACL 2025 (GEM2 Workshop)  
**arXiv**: [2503.05298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05298)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/GU-CLASP/coreference-context-scope](https://github.com/GU-CLASP/coreference-context-scope)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM / Discourse Analysis  
**Keywords**: Coreference Resolution, Multimodal Storytelling, Visual Storytelling, Context Scope, Language Generation Evaluation  

## TL;DR

This paper reveals a significant divergence between human and large multimodal language models in the distribution of coreferential expressions within visual storytelling tasks. While humans dynamically interweave references to different entities and preserve textual-visual alignment, models exhibit limited capacity in tracking mixed references. Furthermore, a suite of quantitative metrics characterizing coreference patterns is proposed.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Visual Storytelling requires models to generate a coherent story narrative based on a sequence of images. With the rapid development of multimodal large language models (such as GPT-4V, LLaVA, etc.), the generated texts have shown substantial improvement in fluency and relevance. Coreference resolution—identifying different expressions referring to the same entity—remains a fundamental linguistic problem for measuring discourse coherence.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Although texts generated by multimodal models are increasingly successful in terms of surface quality (e.g., grammatical correctness, relevance), deep analysis of their underlying discourse structures is still lacking. Specifically, regarding the use of coreference: (1) **lack of quantitative metrics** to compare differences in coreference patterns between human and machine-generated texts; (2) **insufficient modeling of context scope**—humans naturally maintain coreferential chains across multiple paragraphs and images when writing a narrative, but can models do the same?

**Key Challenge**: The "perceived improvement" in generation quality does not equate to a "genuine enhancement in discourse capability." Models can mask their deficiencies in tracking entities across sentences and paragraphs through optimized vocabulary choices and syntactic structures. More granular analytical tools are required to expose these structural gaps.

**Goal**: (1) Propose a set of quantitative metrics to characterize the distribution of coreferential expressions in texts; (2) compare the coreference pattern differences between human and various multimodal models in visual storytelling tasks; (3) reveal the levels at which models fail in modeling context scope.

**Key Insight**: The authors leverage coreference as a probe for "context scope"—the distribution pattern of coreferential expressions in a text directly reflects the range of context the writer (human or model) can "see." If a model only processes local contexts, its coreference patterns will significantly differ from those of humans, who maintain global contextual awareness.

**Core Idea**: By analyzing features such as the length of coreferential chains, current degree of interleaving, and entity diversity, "context scope" is operationalized as quantifiable discourse metrics, thereby exposing the deep-seated deficiencies of multimodal models in narrative coherence.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The research workflow consists of three steps: (1) **Data Collection**—collecting human-written stories and stories generated by various multimodal models on visual storytelling datasets; (2) **Coreference Annotation & Extraction**—performing coreference resolution on both human and machine text, extracting all coreferential chains and related expressions; (3) **Metric Calculation & Analysis**—comparing the coreference patterns between human and machine texts utilizing the proposed quantitative metrics.

### Key Designs

1. **Quantitative Metric System for Coreference Patterns**:

    - Function: To provide a comprehensive suite of metrics to characterize the distribution patterns of coreferential expressions in text.
    - Mechanism: The authors design several complementary metrics: (a) distribution of coreference chain length—measuring the frequency of an entity being referenced in the text; (b) interleaving degree of references—measuring how often references to different entities cross-occur, where high interleaving indicates the narrator can track multiple entities simultaneously; (c) variety of referential forms—measuring whether diverse expressions are used for the same entity (e.g., "boy" $\rightarrow$ "he" $\rightarrow$ "the child"); (d) cross-image reference ratio—measuring if coreference chains span across descriptions of multiple images.
    - Design Motivation: A single metric cannot fully capture the complexity of coreferential behaviors. The multi-metric system captures characteristics from different dimensions, allowing a more fine-grained analysis of human-machine differences.

2. **Comparative Analysis Framework Across Multiple Models**:

    - Function: To systematically identify differences and commonalities in coreference behaviors across different multimodal models.
    - Mechanism: A representative selection of multimodal models (using diverse architectures and training strategies) generated texts under the same visual storytelling task. Coreference information was uniformly extracted to compute the metrics. Employing human-written texts as the "gold standard," the gap between each model and humans was quantified. The impact of model scale and architectural characteristics on coreference behaviors was also analyzed.
    - Design Motivation: Different models might show gaps with humans on different dimensions. Comparative analysis not only reveals common challenges (shared shortcomings among all models) but also identifies the advantages and disadvantages of specific architectures.

3. **Context Scope Probing Methodology**:

    - Function: To translate abstract "context modeling capability" into concrete, quantifiable discourse phenomena.
    - Mechanism: If a model's "effective context scope" is short, its performance on long-distance coreference will be poor—it will tend to repeatedly use explicit nouns instead of pronouns (as the model "forgets" who was previously mentioned), leading to shorter coreference chains and fewer cross-image references. Through these coreference features, one can indirectly measure the actual extent of context utilized by the model.
    - Design Motivation: Direct measurement of a model's context utilization capability is difficult, but coreference provides a natural "window"—coreference behaviors directly reflect memory and utilization of prior context.

### Loss & Training

As this is an analytical study, no new training methods are introduced. All analyses are based on the comparison between human-written texts and model-generated texts on existing datasets. Existing coreference resolution tools are utilized for coreference extraction.

### Human-Machine Difference Analysis

| Analysis Dimension | Human Characteristics | Machine Characteristics | Gap |
|---------|---------|---------|------|
| Interleaving of Entity References | Frequently interleaves references to different entities | Tends to describe entities sequentially with minimal interleaving | Significant |
| Pronoun Usage | Flexibly uses pronouns for anaphoric references | Relies more heavily on repeating full nouns | Obvious |
| Cross-Image Coherence | Naturally maintains coreferential chains across multiple images | High locality, with broken cross-image reference chains | Significant |
| Variety in Expression | Uses multiple referring expressions for the same entity | Relatively monotonous expression forms | Medium |
| Overall Story Consistency | Highly consistent coreference patterns across story narratives | High volatility in coreference patterns | Obvious |

### Key Findings

- **Models exhibit the largest gap in reference interleaving**: Humans naturally mention multiple entities alternately within a paragraph (e.g., "The boy ran towards the girl. He handed her a flower"), while models tend to use "block-like" descriptions (completely describing entity A before mentioning entity B), exposing a clear deficiency in tracking multiple entities concurrently.
- **Improvements in generation quality do not translate to coreference improvements**: Although newer models show significant progress in text fluency, their coreference patterns have not improved accordingly. This indicates that surface quality and deep discourse capability are two independent dimensions.
- **Model scale has limited impact**: Larger models show minor improvements in coreference behavior, but the gap remains substantial, suggesting coreference capabilities may not be resolved simply through scaling.
- **Cross-image reference is a major shortcoming for models**: This points to a fundamental deficiency in models' ability to integrate multi-image information to construct spanning narratives.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Using coreference as a probe for context scope is an elegant research design**: Rather than directly measuring "how much context the model understands," it indirectly quantifies it via the natural linguistic phenomenon of coreference. This has both linguistic foundations and engineering utility, offering a methodology that can be extended to other discourse phenomena (such as topic transitions, causal chains, etc.).
- **Exposing the decoupling of "perceived quality" and "structural quality"**: This serves as a reminder to the NLG community that automatic evaluation metrics (e.g., BLEU, BERTScore) might fail to capture deep discourse-level issues, underscoring the need for more evaluation dimensions tailored to discourse structure.
- **Highly generalizable metric design**: The proposed metrics for coreference patterns are applicable not only to visual storytelling but also to evaluating discourse coherence in any long-text generation task.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Data Scale and Diversity**: The scale and domain coverage of visual storytelling datasets are limited, which may not fully represent how the models perform in other narrative scenarios.
- **Accuracy of Coreference Tools**: Automated coreference resolution tools are prone to errors, especially on non-standard texts generated by models, which might introduce systematic biases.
- **Unclear Causal Relationships**: The paper reveals differences between humans and machines but does not explain the root causes—is it due to training data issues, attention mechanism limitations, or decoding strategy constraints?
- **Purely Analytical Work**: No concrete improvement methods are proposed to bridge the human-machine gap.
- **Depth Limitations of a Workshop Paper**: As a paper in the GEM2 Workshop, the scale of experiments and depth of analysis are somewhat limited compared to main conference papers.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. Traditional Coreference Resolution**: Traditional work focuses on enabling models to "perform" coreference resolution (as an NLU task). In contrast, this study shifts to analyzing "how models use" coreference (as an NLG evaluation dimension), representing an interesting pivot in research perspective.
- **vs. Visual Storytelling Evaluation**: Existing evaluations mainly focus on story relevance and fluency (e.g., VIST leaderboards), overlooking the quality of discourse structure. This paper fills that blank by incorporating the critical dimension of coreference.
- **vs. Discourse Analysis for LLM**: Similar works analyze LLM performance regarding discourse relations, topic coherence, etc. This work contributes a novel analysis angle centered on coreference.
- **Insights**: Coreference metrics can be integrated into automatic evaluation pipelines for multimodal narrative generation or used as training signals to improve discourse coherence.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The concept of using coreference as a context scope probe is novel, and the metric design has linguistic depth.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐ As a Workshop paper, the coverage of models and datasets is reasonable, but it lacks intervention experiments and causal analysis.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ With 19 pages of solid content, the analytical logic is clear, and linguistic concepts are well-explained.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exposes deep discourse-level defects in multimodal generative models. The proposed metrics possess practical value, though the lack of solutions slightly limits its impact.
