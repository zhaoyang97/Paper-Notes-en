# Can AI-Generated Persuasion Be Detected? Persuaficial Benchmark and AI vs. Human Linguistic Differences

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2601.04925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04925)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/ArkadiusDS/Persuaficial](https://github.com/ArkadiusDS/Persuaficial)  
**Area**: Robotics  
**Keywords**: Persuasion detection, AI-generated text, Multilingual benchmark, Linguistic difference analysis, Controllable generation

## TL;DR

This paper introduces Persuaficial—a high-quality multilingual benchmark for AI-generated persuasive text covering six languages. It systematically evaluates the differences in automatic detection difficulty between persuasive texts generated by LLMs and those written by humans. The study finds that subtle AI persuasion is harder to detect than human persuasion (F1 decreases by approximately 20%), while overly intensified persuasion is easier to identify.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: LLMs are capable of generating highly persuasive text, raising concerns about their misuse for propaganda, manipulation, and other purposes. Existing research has explored the ability of LLMs to identify persuasive language, but has not yet investigated whether AI-generated persuasion is more difficult to detect automatically than human-authored persuasion.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Lack of a systematic benchmark for AI-generated persuasive text; (2) Unknown impact of different generation strategies (paraphrasing, intensifying, weakening) on detection difficulty; (3) Systematic linguistic differences between AI and human persuasive texts have not been analyzed.

**Key Challenge**: If AI can generate persuasive text that is harder to detect than human writing, existing automatic detection systems will face severe threats, particularly in the fields of misinformation and political propaganda.

**Goal**: (1) Construct a multilingual benchmark for AI-generated persuasive text; (2) Evaluate the detectability of AI persuasion under different generation strategies; (3) Analyze the linguistic differences between AI and human persuasive texts.

**Key Insight**: Drawing on synthetic misinformation generation methods, four controllable persuasive text generation strategies are designed, combined with zero-shot LLM detection and 196-dimensional linguistic feature analysis.

**Core Idea**: The detectability of persuasive text depends on the generation strategy—subtle persuasion significantly increases detection difficulty, while intensified and open-ended generation make detection easier.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The research consists of three parts: (1) **Persuaficial dataset construction**—generating approximately 65K persuasive texts across 6 languages using 4 LLMs × 4 generation strategies × 3 source datasets; (2) **Detectability evaluation**—comparing the detection of human and AI-generated persuasive text using 4 LLMs in a zero-shot setting; (3) **Linguistic difference analysis**—performing a comparative analysis using the StyloMetrix tool to extract 196 linguistic features.

### Key Designs

1. **Four Controllable Persuasive Text Generation Strategies**:
    - Function: Systematically cover AI persuasion generation scenarios of varying degrees.
    - Mechanism: (a) Paraphrasing—semantically equivalent rewriting maintaining the original level of persuasion; (b) Subtle Rewriting—making persuasion more covert; (c) Intensified Rewriting—enhancing the persuasive effect; (d) Open-ended Generation—freely generating persuasive text based on factual summaries.
    - Design Motivation: Different strategies simulate various real-world scenarios of AI persuasion misuse, ranging from simple paraphrasing to fully autonomous creation.

2. **Multilingual and Multi-source Data Construction**:
    - Function: Ensure the diversity and generalization of the benchmark.
    - Mechanism: Sampling from three human persuasive datasets—SemEval 2023 Task 3 (News), DIPROMATS 2024 (Twitter), and ChangeMyView (Reddit). Generations are performed using four models: GPT-4.1 Mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemma 3 27B, and Llama 3.3 70B, covering English, German, Polish, Italian, French, and Russian.
    - Design Motivation: Multiple data sources avoid single-source bias, and multilingual coverage ensures cross-cultural applicability.

3. **196-Dimensional Linguistic Feature Analysis**:
    - Function: Reveal fine-grained linguistic differences between AI and human persuasive texts.
    - Mechanism: Using StyloMetrix to extract 196 interpretable linguistic features covering lexical, syntactic, and semantic dimensions, comparing the feature distributions of humans and various generation strategies.
    - Design Motivation: Provide interpretable insights beyond black-box detection to guide the development of more robust detection tools.

### Loss & Training

No model training is involved. Detection uses a zero-shot setting, with temperature set to 0 to ensure deterministic output.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**English Detection F1 Changes (Relative to Human Baseline, SemEval data, GPT 4.1 Mini)**

| Generation Strategy | F1 | Relative Change |
|---------|-----|--------|
| Human Authored | 0.740 | — |
| Paraphrasing | 0.701 | ↓5% |
| Subtle Rewriting | 0.403 | ↓46% |
| Intensified Rewriting | 0.815 | ↑10% |
| Open-ended Generation | 0.896 | ↑21% |

### Ablation Study

- The F1 for subtle generation dropped by an average of 20.42% across all detectors.
- Open-ended generation improved F1 by an average of 9.75%, and intensified generation improved it by an average of 5.33%.
- These patterns are highly consistent across the three source datasets and four detection models.
- Human quality assessment confirmed a generation accuracy of approximately 88.2%, with persuasion-related accuracy reaching 97.69%.

### Key Findings

- Subtle AI persuasion significantly degrades automatic detection performance, posing a real threat to current detection systems.
- Intensified and open-ended generation actually make detection easier, likely because the models over-express explicit persuasive cues.
- Patterns remain highly consistent across different detection models and source datasets, indicating that the results are generalizable.
- AI-generated texts exhibit systematic differences in linguistic features, which can serve as cues for improving detectors.

## Highlights & Insights

- The first systematic study of the detectability differences between AI persuasion and human persuasion.
- The finding that "subtlety makes detection harder, while intensification makes it easier" is intuitively sound but quantified and verified here for the first time.
- Multilingual coverage (6 languages) enhances the universality of the conclusions.
- The 196-dimensional linguistic feature analysis provides a foundation for interpretable detection.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Zero-shot detection may underestimate the capabilities of fine-tuned detectors.
- Generation quality depends on the instruction-following capabilities of the LLMs, which may vary between models.
- Adversarial scenarios (e.g., generation specifically optimized to evade detection) were not explored.
- Future work could combine linguistic feature analysis to develop more robust detectors for subtle persuasion.

## Related Work & Insights

- This work forms a counterpart in the persuasion domain to the synthetic misinformation generation methods of Chen and Shu (2023).
- The effectiveness of the StyloMetrix tool in persuasion detection has been further validated.
- This study provides important benchmark resources for research into AI safety and defense against information manipulation.
