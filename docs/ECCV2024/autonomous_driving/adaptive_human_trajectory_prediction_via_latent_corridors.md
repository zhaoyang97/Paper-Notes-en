# Adaptive Human Trajectory Prediction via Latent Corridors

**Conference**: ECCV 2024  
**arXiv**: [2312.06653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06653)  
**Code**: [https://neerja.me/atp_latent_corridors/](https://neerja.me/atp_latent_corridors/) (Project Page)  
**Area**: Autonomous Driving  
**Keywords**: Pedestrian trajectory prediction, adaptive prediction, test-time training, visual prompt tuning, low-rank representation  

## TL;DR
This paper introduces prompt tuning to pedestrian trajectory prediction. By adding learnable low-rank visual prompts (termed latent corridors) to the input of a pre-trained trajectory predictor, it achieves highly parameter-efficient adaptation to scene-specific behavioral patterns with less than 0.1% extra parameters, improving ADE by up to 23.9% and 26.8% on synthetic and real-world data, respectively.

## Background & Motivation
Pedestrian trajectory prediction is commonly formulated as a zero-shot generalization problem: training models on source datasets and directly deploying them to unseen environments. However, this paradigm implicitly assumes that human behavioral patterns in deployment scenarios remain stationary. In reality, pedestrian behavior varies dramatically due to changes in locations, times, and events (e.g., rush hours vs. weekends, day vs. night, new subway entrances altering pedestrian flows, or rainy days causing people to avoid puddles). When encountering these variations, the performance of existing predictors degrades rapidly.

Existing domain adaptation methods for trajectory prediction focus either on cross-domain transfer (e.g., New York to London) or rely on specialized architectures (e.g., memory bank matching). These methods lack a simple, lightweight, plug-and-play solution to rapidly adapt pre-trained models to specific behavioral trends of a deployed scene.

## Core Problem
How to rapidly adapt a pre-trained trajectory predictor to scene-specific human behavioral patterns using extremely scarce observations (e.g., observing 2 pedestrians for 30 seconds)? The key challenges lie in the extreme scarcity of data, the necessity to preserve the original capabilities of the model, parameter efficiency, and the capacity to capture spatial correlations between scene geometry and behavioral trends.

## Method
The general approach borrows the concept of prompt tuning from NLP: without modifying the primary parameters of the pre-trained model, a learnable "prompt" is added to the input to guide the model's output to adapt to the new scene.

### Overall Architecture
Input: Pedestrian past trajectory heatmaps $\mathbf{M}_{\tau-H:\tau}$ ($H$-step observation) + scene semantic segmentation map $S$ (generated by Mask2Former, compressed into 12 pedestrian-related categories).

The base predictor $\mathcal{P}$ utilizes the YNet architecture, divided into three parts: an encoder $\mathcal{P}_E$, a decoder $\mathcal{P}_D$, and a prediction head $\mathcal{P}_P$. Trajectory coordinates are converted into heatmaps of the same size as the scene image, predictions are made in the image space, and 2D coordinate predictions are finally obtained via soft-argmax sampling.

During adaptation, a scene-specific learnable prompt $p_k$ is element-wise added to the input heatmap. The encoder and decoder are frozen; only the prompt and (optionally) the prediction head are optimized.

### Key Designs
1. **Latent Corridor Prompt**: A learnable 2D visual prompt $p_k \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w}$ is introduced for each scene, which is element-wise added to the input trajectory heatmap: $\tilde{\mathbf{M}} = \mathbf{M} \oplus p$. This prompt is spatially aligned with the scene image, allowing it to encode spatial information on "where pedestrians tend to walk"—mimicking a non-physical "corridor" guiding human behavior.

2. **Low-Rank Parameterization**: Directly learning the prompt of size $h \times w$ (288×480 = 138K parameters) is structurally expensive. The paper proposes a rank-1 outer product formulation, parameterizing the prompt as the outer product of an $h$-dimensional vector and a $w$-dimensional vector. This requires only $h+w=768$ parameters (less than 0.1% model parameter growth). Experiments demonstrate that this rank-1 formulation matches the performance of the full-rank matrix.

3. **Three Deployment Configurations**:
    - **LC (Prompt-only)**: The entire predictor is completely frozen; only the prompt is learned. This offers the fastest adaptation, suitable for short-term transient events.
    - **LC + Joint FT**: Each of the $K$ scenes has an independent prompt, but they share a single fine-tuned prediction head, which is highly compact.
    - **LC + Per-Scene FT**: Each scene independently learns its prompt and prediction head, yielding the highest performance.

### Loss & Training
- Optimizes the same binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss used in pre-training: $\ell = \sum_\tau \text{BCE}(\mathcal{P}([\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\tau-H:\tau}, S]), \mathbf{M}_{\tau+1:\tau+T})$
- The prompt parameters are initialized using Kaiming initialization.
- Ultra-fast training: finished within a few minutes on a single 2080 Ti.
- Data scale is measured in "human-seconds" (number of humans $\times$ observation seconds). Effective adaptation is achieved even with less than 1 minute of human-seconds.

## Key Experimental Results

| Dataset | Metric | Ours (LC+PSF) | Scene-aware Baseline | Gain |
|--------|------|-------------|----------------|------|
| MOTSynth (437 scenes) | ADE | 42.6 (Joint FT) | 47.3 | 9.9% |
| MOT (4 scenes) | ADE | 37.4 | 44.2 | 15.4% |
| WildTrack (3 scenes) | ADE | 27.4 | 33.6 | 18.5% |
| EarthCam (4 scenes) | ADE | 17.5 | 23.9 | 26.8% |
| ETH/UCY | ADE | 21.8 | 32.3 | 32.5% |

- LC + Per-Scene FT yields an additional **11.2%** improvement compared to pure fine-tuning (without prompt) on MOT/WildTrack.
- For some MOTSynth scenes, ADE is improved by up to **63.4%** and FDE by up to **91.4%**.
- Generalizability is proven on the PECNet architecture (10.2% improvement in ADE).

### Ablation Study
- **Prompt Placement**: Adding the prompt to all input heatmaps yields the best performance (ADE 44.6), while adding it to semantic maps yields a weaker response (46.4).
- **Combination Function**: Element-wise addition outperforms multiplication.
- **Low-Rank vs. Full-Rank**: The rank-1 representation (768 parameters) performs comparably to the full-rank alternative (138K parameters).
- **Data Volume**: Performance continuously scales with increasing human-seconds, but significant adaptation gains are achieved even with minimal data.
- **Architecture Agnosticism**: Successfully applied to PECNet, validating the model-agnostic nature of the method.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Extreme Parameter Efficiency**: Less than 0.1% model parameter growth brings significant performance improvements; the rank-1 outer product formulation is an exceptionally elegant design choice.
- **Prompt Tuning for Domain Transfer**: For the first time, visual prompt tuning from NLP is successfully adapted for trajectory prediction, maintaining physical-spatial alignment with the scene via the visual prompt.
- **Emergent Scene Understanding**: Qualitative results indicate the model learns 3D ground plane awareness (avoiding predicting people "floating in the air"), staircase geometry, and popular routes.
- **High Practicality**: Prompts for different scenes can be swapped like config files, allowing easy toggling of distinct prompts for day/night or weekdays/weekends.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Only addresses **unimodal prediction** (deterministic future) without modeling multimodal future trajectory distributions.
- Assumes a **static camera** setup; not applicable to moving perspective views.
- Specifically validated on **outdoor pedestrian** scenarios, without extending to vehicle trajectories, indoor navigation, etc.
- While the rank-1 constraint is highly efficient, its representation capability for complex scene geometries remains to be explored (would rank-2 or rank-3 be better?).
- Has not been tested on more modern trajectory prediction paradigms (e.g., Transformer-based baselines).
- The acquisition of adaptation data relies on tracking algorithms, meaning tracking noise can impact adaptation performance.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Memory-based methods (MANTRA, MemoNet, etc.)**: Memory-bank approaches scale linearly in memory complexity with dataset size and require extra training-free lookup steps during inference. Latent Corridors uses a tiny, fixed-size prompt (768 parameters), making it more efficient and removing the matching bottleneck.
- **vs. Domain adaptation methods (Adaptive GNN, etc.)**: Domain adaptation techniques require specialized structural designs to separate domain-invariant and domain-specific features. Ours is model-agnostic and plug-and-play.
- **vs. Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT)**: VPT inserts prompts into the token sequence of Vision Transformers. This method adds the prompt directly in the pixel space to preserve spatial alignment, which is critical for spatial layout-aware tasks.

## Insights & Connections
- **Low-rank prompts can generalize to test-time adaptation of other vision tasks**: For instance, semantic segmentation or object detection models deployed in sensitive environments can utilize low-rank visual prompts for lightweight adaptation.
- The spatial alignment design and rank-1 parameterization are highly transferable techniques for other spatial-aware adaptation tasks.

- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Evaluated on synthetic, real-world, and in-the-wild datasets with rich ablation studies and insightful qualitative analysis.
