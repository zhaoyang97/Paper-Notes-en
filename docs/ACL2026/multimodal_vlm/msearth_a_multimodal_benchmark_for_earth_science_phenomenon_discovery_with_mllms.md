# MSEarth: A Multimodal Benchmark for Earth Science Phenomenon Discovery with MLLMs

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2505.20740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20740)  
**Code**: https://github.com/xiangyu-mm/MSEarth  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM / Scientific Benchmark / Earth Science  
**Keywords**: Earth science Benchmark, MLLM evaluation, Refined Caption, Graduate-level VQA, Multi-agent voting

## TL;DR
289K images were extracted from 64,560 CC-BY open-source earth science papers, and "refined captions" were synthesized using "raw caption + text context." Through 5-model multi-agent voting and three-stage PhD expert verification, a test set of 7,195 graduate-level questions (including captioning / MCQ / open-ended) was generated. This systematically reveals a 20+ point "perception >> reasoning" gap in SOTA MLLMs for multi-image earth science reasoning and provides a 441K training set that allows an open-source 7B model to match GPT-4o performance after GRPO.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: MLLMs already have domain benchmarks in single disciplines such as chemistry (ChemVLM), remote sensing (GeoChat), and meteorology (WeatherQA); general scientific benchmarks like ScienceQA, MMMU, and OlympiadBench are also expanding rapidly.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) General benchmarks are almost entirely at the **high school or undergraduate** level with synthetic or textbook problems, lacking graduate-level complexity; (2) Paper-based benchmarks (SciFIBench, ArxivQA, MMSci), while reaching graduate levels, **only use figure + caption pairs** to generate questions, losing the content that actually carries reasoning in the text (hypothesis, evidence, analysis, conclusion), which reduces the task to "caption matching"; (3) Questions generated by native LLMs lack support from paper text and cannot be verified; (4) Earth science (five spheres: atmosphere, cryosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, biosphere), a field combining **strong spatial observation and strong domain knowledge**, lacks even a single graduate-level multimodal benchmark.

**Key Challenge**: "Raw captions are too short to support complex scientific reasoning tasks"—averaging only 37.56 words and lacking the causal chains found in the main text; meanwhile, the main text is scattered across different paragraphs, requiring controllable retrieval-fusion methods to realign "figure-text-reasoning chains."

**Goal**: Construct the first graduate-level Earth-science multimodal benchmark to achieve: (a) question quality verifiable by paper text; (b) coverage of five spheres, 8 primary disciplines, and 66 sub-disciplines; (c) simultaneous support for captioning, MCQ, and open-ended tasks; (d) a supporting training set to allow open-source models to catch up.

**Key Insight**: Raw captions for each image are first regular-expression matched with all paragraphs in the text that cite the figure $\ge 2$ times. These are fed to GPT-4o to generate **refined captions** (averaging 136.29 words, including scientific reasoning). Refined captions then serve as "teacher signals" to drive VQA generation and automatic quality grading via 5-model multi-agent voting.

**Core Idea**: To **explicitly separate and then aggregate** the three elements: "image-reasoning-verification." Refined captions serve as the ground-truth reasoning chain. Multi-agent voting automatically categorizes questions into easy, specialized, hard, and discarded tiers based on "accuracy without refined caption / accuracy with refined caption / accuracy of large models only," combined with PhD manual cross-checking to form a high-fidelity test set.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
MSEarth follows a 4-stage pipeline (Figure 2):
(1) **Paper Collection and Classification**: 400K+ earth science PDFs were obtained from OpenDataLab and parsed into structured JSON using MinerU; cosine similarity between abstracts and keywords of the 5 spheres was calculated using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 for coarse classification, followed by a two-stage filter using Qwen-2.5-VL-72B to remove non-earth observation images, leaving 83K papers.
(2) **MSEarthCap Construction**: Figure labels were regular-expression matched to text paragraphs, retaining paragraphs with "$\ge 2$ citations" as context. Finally, GPT-4o was used to synthesize (figure, raw caption, context) into refined captions—producing 289,891 images from 64,560 papers.
(3) **MSEarthQA Generation**: GPT-4o, guided by refined captions, generated MCQ and open-ended questions, forcing questions to "utilize the differences between raw and refined captions" to ensure they are verifiable by paper content.
(4) **Multi-agent Voting + PhD Verification**: 5 models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/72B, InternVL2.5-7B/78B, GPT-4o) graded question difficulty through 3-stage filtering. Finally, PhDs were randomly sampled for annotation, removing invalid questions from 216 MCQs and 89 open-ended questions.

### Key Designs

1.  **Refined Caption: "Supplementing" Image Reasoning Chains from Text**:
    - **Function**: Expands image descriptions from a few lines of raw caption to scientific descriptions containing hypothesis, evidence, analysis, and conclusion, enabling questions to address actual reasoning.
    - **Mechanism**: First, figure labels (e.g., "Fig. 3") are searched in MinerU-parsed paragraphs using regular expressions, retaining all paragraphs citing the figure for $\ge 2$ sentences as context. Then, GPT-4o generates refined captions using (figure_image, raw_caption, paper_context) as input (prompt in Appendix E.6). Statistically, raw captions (37.56 words) $\rightarrow$ refined captions (136.29 words), a 3.6x increase in information density.
    - **Design Motivation**: Generating questions directly from raw captions (as in ArxivQA) only addresses surface-level information; questions about "why" cannot be handled. Refined captions bring text-based deduction alongside the image, making ground-truth answers supported by paper evidence—while also serving as "teacher signals" for subsequent multi-agent voting classification.

2.  **5-Model Multi-agent Voting + 3-Stage Difficulty Grading**:
    - **Function**: Automatically grades questions across "knowledge vs. perception" dimensions without the budget for full PhD annotation.
    - **Mechanism**: 5 MLLMs answer questions independently, with a 60% majority vote deciding the result: **Phase A**—given only raw caption + question; if $> 40\%$ of models fail, it moves to the next stage (approx. 70% of questions are discarded as "easy" / 20% retained for training); **Phase B**—refined caption is added; if $> 60\%$ of models then succeed, it is labeled "specialized" (requires domain knowledge, approx. 20%); **Phase C**—only models $\ge 70B$ vote; if $> 60\%$ succeed, it is labeled "hard" (requires strong perception, approx. 5%), and the remaining 5% are discarded as "flawed."
    - **Design Motivation**: Decomposing "why a question was missed" into two independent dimensions—`knowledge gap` (corrected by adding refined caption) and `perception gap` (corrected only by the largest models after adding refined caption)—allows the benchmark to diagnose model weaknesses.

3.  **Unified Interface + Field-based Annotation for Three Task Types**:
    - **Function**: Supports evaluation for captioning, MCQ, and open-ended tasks on the same image set to avoid distribution drift between different benchmarks.
    - **Mechanism**: Each data entry shares meta-fields like `question_id / images / classification / reasoning_chain` (Table 7), differing only in query and response: MCQ uses (raw_caption, question, options), open-ended uses (raw_caption, question), and captioning uses the question (guiding the model to generate a refined caption). Evaluation involves surface similarity (BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR/BERTScore) plus semantic evaluation using Qwen2.5-VL-72B as an LLM-judge (OE-Eval / Cap-Eval).
    - **Design Motivation**: Performing three tasks on a unified image set allows direct comparison of whether a model "fails to see the image / fails to describe / fails to select the answer"—this in-set decoupling is cleaner than cross-benchmark comparisons. Training can combine these tasks via instruction-tuning and GRPO.

### Loss & Training
**The benchmark itself involves no training**, but provides a training set of 441,785 questions (289,891 captioning + 102,753 MCQ + 49,141 open-ended). Ours performed instruction tuning on Intern-S1-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-7B for captioning and open-ended tasks, and GRPO RL for MCQ (correct answer = +1 reward).

### Ablation Study
Ablations were performed on raw caption input, CoT prompting, and multi-agent voting quality:

| Configuration | Reasoning | Perception | Overall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| InternVL3-78B w/o raw caption | 44.54 | 59.67 | 48.17 |
| InternVL3-78B + raw caption | 47.00 | 73.61 | **53.38** (+5.2) |
| Qwen2.5-VL-72B w/o raw caption | 41.43 | 57.72 | 45.33 |
| Qwen2.5-VL-72B + raw caption | 44.40 | 70.46 | **50.65** (+5.3) |
| GPT-o4-mini CoT-low | – | – | 52.0 |
| GPT-o4-mini Non-CoT high | – | – | **54.3** (CoT decreased score) |
| Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking + CoT | – | – | **52.0** (thinking model improved with CoT) |
| Gemini-2.5-Flash w/o think + CoT | – | – | **42.0** (base model improved +2 with CoT) |

**Multi-agent Voting Grading Quality**: 900 questions from Phase A were manually reviewed, showing 59/900 = 6.6% invalid; Phase B (1800 questions), 80/1800 = 4.4% invalid; Phase C (300 questions), 77/300 = 25.7% invalid—proving Phase B (specialized) has the highest quality, consistent with the expectation that "it requires a refined caption to answer correctly."

### Key Findings
- **The Perception-Reasoning Gap is Pervasive**: Gemini-2.5-Pro-Thinking has 77.06% perception vs 56.31% reasoning (a 20.75-point gap); almost all models reach a 75% perception ceiling before reasoning. This implies further improvements require domain knowledge and multi-step reasoning rather than just better vision.
- **Cross-image Reasoning is the Most Difficult**: All models drop most significantly in cross-image tasks (comparing/linking multiple images); open-source models score only 38-49, and the closed-source SOTA Gemini-3-Flash only reaching 66.85. Half of MSEarth questions are multi-image, reflecting real earth science scenarios.
- **MSEarth Training Data is Effective**: Qwen-7B improved +9.1 points on the test set, and Intern-S1-mini +5.4 points; the largest gains were in reasoning tasks rather than perception, indicating that GRPO primarily inputs domain knowledge.
- **Raw Captions are Key for Fair Input**: Adding raw captions improved perception by 8-12 points for all open-source models (as captions explain axes/symbols); reasoning is only meaningful after this. However, raw captions also carry a risk of "prompt leakage" and must be used carefully.
- **CoT can be Harmful to Models not Trained for Reasoning**: Gemini-2.5-Pro Non-CoT (52.33%) > CoT (50.67%), and o4-mini high-CoT performed worse than low-CoT, suggesting "only reasoning models should use CoT," consistent with recent findings in think-mode papers.
- **OE-Eval has the Highest Spearman Correlation with Human Judgment**: Krippendorff’s $\alpha = 0.695$, far exceeding traditional BLEU/ROUGE/BERTScore, validating LLM-judge as a more reasonable metric for open-ended scientific QA.

## Highlights & Insights
- **The "Refined Caption" is a Key Innovation for Benchmark Construction**: By "compressing" image and paragraph context back into a caption, all downstream VQA has verifiable ground truth; this technique can be migrated to any "image + paper" benchmarks in fields like Bio, Astro, or Chem.
- **Multi-agent Voting + PhD Verification is a High-Fidelity, Low-Budget Paradigm**: 5-model 3-stage voting automatically grades questions into "easy / specialized / hard / flawed" tiers; PhDs only conduct final verification on $\approx 5K$ questions, saving 80% in annotation costs while maintaining graduate-level quality.
- **Diagnostic Significance of Phase A/B/C**: Phase B contains the most data and highest quality—where "adding a refined caption makes the answer correct" perfectly represents questions where models "lack domain knowledge but have perception." Phase C represents the opposite—refined captions don't help, requiring larger models to "see" clearly. This orthogonal decomposition allows the benchmark to provide both total scores and precise diagonal results for weaknesses.
- **First Truly Multi-sphere / Multi-image / Graduate Earth Science Benchmark**: Covers 8 disciplines and 66 sub-disciplines, with 54.9% of questions being multi-image, filling gaps left by single-domain benchmarks like WeatherQA and GeoChat.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Authors' Acknowledgments**: (1) Too many earth science sub-fields exist, and niche directions (e.g., paleoclimatology subdivisions) are not fully covered; (2) most questions are still MCQs or short answers, lacking exploratory questions (e.g., hypothesis generation) requiring long-range reasoning.
- **Individual Observations**: (1) **Refined caption "quality" is capped by GPT-4o**—if GPT-4o is unfamiliar with a sub-field, the refined caption itself contains bias, which the paper did not quantify; (2) in multi-agent voting, GPT-4o participates in both generation and voting, introducing self-favoring bias; (3) the training set comes from the same set of papers, lacking a completely independent venue split from the test set, which might cause Qwen-7B-MSEarth to gain distribution-specific knowledge; (4) using raw captions as input significantly boosts perception, but in real research, the caption itself is the "answer," making the benchmark setting slightly loose; (5) evaluation relies on a single LLM-judge (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), whose preferences might not perfectly align with humans; while $\alpha=0.695$ was reported, it has not reached inter-PhD consistency levels.
- **Improvement Ideas**: Replace refined captions with an intersection of versions generated by different LLMs to reduce single-model bias; introduce hypothesis generation (allowing models to generate new observable hypotheses reproducible by papers); distinguish between in-paper and out-of-paper knowledge generalization during evaluation.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. MMMU / ScienceQA**: General science but only up to undergraduate level, lacking paper context; MSEarth provides graduate-level depth in a single discipline.
- **vs. ArxivQA / MMSci**: Also uses papers for graduate benchmarks, but ArxivQA only uses captions for generation (no paper context verification), and MMSci lacks verification; MSEarth implements "refined captions + multi-agent filtering + PhD verification" for higher quality.
- **vs. WeatherQA / GeoChat**: Single-domain (meteorology / remote sensing) benchmarks; MSEarth spans 5 spheres and 8 disciplines, closer to the multi-disciplinary nature of real Earth science.
- **vs. OlympiadBench (Olympiad-level)**: OlympiadBench uses a small number of manually annotated competition problems (8K) but only covers math and physics; MSEarth uses a paper-grounded automatic + voting pipeline to expand to 7K test and 441K training samples.
- **vs. G-Eval / LAVE (LLM-as-Judge Evaluation)**: Follows the LLM-judge evaluation but adds "expert cross-check" as final ground truth, providing evidence of high correlation between LLM-judge and humans (Spearman > traditional metrics) on graduate-level VQA.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ "Refined caption + multi-agent three-stage filtering" is a clear advancement in benchmark construction methodology; first multi-sphere Earth science graduate benchmark.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 21 MLLMs $\times$ 3 task types $\times$ 8 sub-disciplines + CoT / raw caption / training data ablations + PhD mini-set human evaluation + Spearman correlation + OE-Eval vs. traditional metric comparison; extremely comprehensive coverage.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The data pipeline is very clear, and the motivation for three-stage voting is clean; only a small pity that the main paper didn't illustrate the correspondence between Phase A/B/C and reasoning/perception in a cross-table.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Provides both "test + train" data sets for the earth science and multimodal communities; the refined caption paradigm can be reused by any paper-grounded benchmark; the story of the 7B model matching GPT-4o after GRPO encourages the path of small models + domain data.
