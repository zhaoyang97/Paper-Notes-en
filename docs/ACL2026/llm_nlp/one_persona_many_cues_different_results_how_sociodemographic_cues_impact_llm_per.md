# One Persona, Many Cues, Different Results: How Sociodemographic Cues Impact LLM Personalization

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2601.18572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18572)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/frawee/persona_cues)  
**Area**: LLM Fairness / Personalization  
**Keywords**: Persona Prompting, Socio-demographic Cues, LLM Personalization Bias, External Validity, Prompt Robustness

## TL;DR

This paper systematically compares the effects of 6 common persona prompting methods (two variants each for name, explicit mention, and dialogue history) across 7 LLMs and 4 tasks. It finds that while average responses are highly correlated across prompting methods, the differences between personas generated by different methods vary significantly. Overly explicit prompts lead to stronger personalization bias, warning against drawing bias conclusions based on a single prompting method.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Socio-demographic personalization in LLMs is increasingly common—adjusting responses by gender, race, or age has been proven to improve helpfulness. Researchers study biases in such personalization through "personas" (synthetic user profiles).

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Existing studies typically use only one prompting method to convey personas, ignoring LLM sensitivity to prompt variations; (2) The external validity of different prompting methods varies greatly—explicitly mentioning "You are talking to a woman" rarely occurs in real interactions; (3) It is unclear whether using different prompting methods leads to different conclusions regarding bias.

**Key Challenge**: The choice of prompting method may determine research findings—if one method shows bias while another does not, which is more credible?

**Goal**: Systematically evaluate the impact of prompting method choice on the discovery of personalization bias to provide methodological guidance for future research.

**Key Insight**: Design a flexible evaluation framework covering three major categories of prompting methods (Name, Explicit Mention, Dialogue History) with two variants each, comparing them across multiple models and tasks.

**Core Idea**: Prompting methods represent an implicit degree of freedom in personalization research. Although different methods correlate in average performance, they differ significantly in their degree of variance. Implicit prompting methods with high external validity should be prioritized.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

10 personas (3-4 values each for gender, race, and age) × 6 prompting methods × 7 LLMs × 4 evaluation tasks. Prompting methods are ranked by external validity: Dialogue History (highest) > Name > Explicit Mention (lowest). Main analysis dimensions: (a) consistency of average responses across methods; (b) variability in the degree of difference across methods; (c) alignment with real human dialogue history.

### Key Designs

1.  **Systematic Design of Six Prompting Methods**:
    *   Function: Covers the full spectrum from implicit to explicit prompting.
    *   Mechanism: Names (in system prompt/user prompt), Explicit Mentions (in system prompt/user prompt), Dialogue History (from real human interaction/synthetically generated). Each has different external validity—names are common in metadata, dialogue history is always present, explicit mentions are rare in real interactions.
    *   Design Motivation: If bias only appears under explicit mentions but not implicit methods, the "bias" might be a methodological artifact rather than a real issue.

2.  **Multidimensional Consistency Analysis**:
    *   Function: Distinguishes between "consistency in average levels" and "consistency in difference patterns."
    *   Mechanism: Spearman correlation analyzes average response consistency, but more critically, it analyzes which prompting method produces the largest inter-persona differences. Findings show explicit prompts lead to stronger personalization bias.
    *   Design Motivation: Highly correlated averages can mask important disagreements in difference patterns—two methods might agree that "GPT-4 is better than Claude" but disagree on "how much better it is for men than women."

3.  **Benchmark Comparison with Real Human Dialogue History**:
    *   Function: Evaluates which synthetic prompting method most closely approximates real-world personalization effects.
    *   Mechanism: Uses real human-LLM interaction data from Kearney et al. (2025) as a benchmark to compare the effects and bias patterns of various prompting methods against real interactions.
    *   Design Motivation: The ultimate concern is the bias actual users encounter, necessitating real interaction data as a reference.

### Loss & Training

No training involved. Evaluated 7 LLMs (GPT-4o/4.1, Gemma-3-27B, Claude 3.5 Haiku, Llama-3.1-70B, Mistral-Small, DeepSeek-V3).

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

*   Spearman correlations for average responses across prompting methods are typically $> 0.8$ (high consistency).
*   However, the inter-persona differences (bias magnitude) caused by explicit mentions are 2-3 times larger than those of implicit methods.
*   Disagreements exist among different prompting methods regarding which persona combinations produce the greatest differences.

### Key Findings

*   Explicit (but unnatural) prompting methods lead to stronger personalization bias—the more explicit the prompt, the more the model tends to treat personas differently.
*   The prompting method closest to real dialogue history depends on the specific dataset and demographic variable—there is no universal best.
*   The validity of using names as proxies faces ethical and methodological issues—the same name may span multiple demographic dimensions.
*   Explicit mentions in the system prompt vs. user prompt also produce different effects—bias in system prompts is typically larger.

## Highlights & Insights

*   This is an important methodological contribution, revealing an overlooked implicit degree of freedom in personalization bias research.
*   The finding that "more explicit equals more bias" has practical implications—researchers using high external validity (implicit) methods might underestimate bias, while those using low external validity (explicit) methods might overestimate it.
*   The flexible evaluation framework design can be directly reused by other researchers.

## Limitations & Future Work

*   Covers only English.
*   Limited persona dimensions (gender, race, age) without testing intersectional identities.
*   Limited benchmark data for dialogue history.
*   Mitigation strategies were not explored.

## Related Work & Insights

*   **vs. Kearney et al.**: They provide real interaction benchmark data; Ours compares synthetic prompting methods on this basis.
*   **vs. Durmus et al.**: They found differences between language vs. explicit mentions; Ours extends this to more prompt types and models.
