# Drama: Diverse Augmentation from Large Language Models to Smaller Dense Retrievers

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2502.18460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18460)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/facebookresearch/dpr-scale/tree/main/drama](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dpr-scale/tree/main/drama)  
**Area**: Information Retrieval / Text Embeddings  
**Keywords**: Dense Retrieval, Data Augmentation, Model Pruning, LLM Distillation, Multilingual Retrieval

## TL;DR

The Drama framework is proposed to systematically explore the combination of multiple LLM-based data augmentation strategies (cropped sentences + synthetic queries + LLM reranking preferences) with pruned LLM backbones. It trains small retriever models (0.1B-1B parameters) via single-stage contrastive learning, matching the 1B-parameter Gecko on BEIR with only 0.3B parameters, while exhibiting strong multilingual and long-context capabilities.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Fine-tuning 7B+ LLMs directly into dense retrievers (e.g., RepLlama) has demonstrated significantly better generalization capabilities than traditional BERT retrievers. However, the inference cost of LLM retrievers is approximately 40 times that of BERT retrievers, posing severe challenges in large-scale corpus encoding and online query latency.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Small retrievers (<1B parameters) generalize poorly under limited supervised data. Existing methods that leverage LLMs for data augmentation (e.g., InPars, Gecko, Mistral-E5) have developed independently, lacking fair comparison and systematic combination studies under a unified framework. Furthermore, small retrievers still rely on encoder architectures like BERT/XLM-R, preventing them from inheriting the advantages of LLMs in multilingual processing and long contexts.

**Key Challenge**: How to compress the retrieval capabilities of LLMs into efficient models with <1B parameters while maintaining high generalization and multilingual capabilities?

**Goal**: Comprehensively explore how to leverage LLMs to build small, universal retrievers from both data and model dimensions.

**Key Insight**: (1) Systematically compare and combine multiple LLM augmentation strategies at the data level; (2) Use LLM pruning to replace traditional encoder architectures at the model level.

**Core Idea**: Generate diverse training data using the retrieval preferences and generation capabilities of LLMs, while pruning LLMs to serve as backbones for small retrievers, achieving an end-to-end "LLM-to-LLM" knowledge transfer.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The Drama framework consists of two pillars: (1) diverse LLM data augmentation—using the Llama-3.1-8B retriever and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct generator to create augmented data via four methods; (2) LLM pruned backbones—structurally pruning Llama-3.2-1B to 0.1B/0.3B models for retriever initialization. The combination of these two is trained using single-stage contrastive learning.

### Key Designs

1. **Data Augmentation Method 1: 8B Retriever Mining (Low Cost)**:

    - Function: Randomly crop sentences from a large-scale corpus to serve as pseudo-queries, retrieve the top-50 candidates using an 8B LLM retriever, where the top-[1,10] are designated as positive examples, and the top-[30,50] as hard negative examples.
    - Mechanism: Enables the small retriever to learn from the relevance preferences of the 8B retriever. Pseudo-queries do not incur extra generation costs, and the volume of augmented data is equal to the corpus size $|C|$.
    - Design Motivation: The computational cost is limited to corpus encoding and retrieval, making it the most economical augmentation method.

2. **Data Augmentation Method 2: 70B Synthetic Queries (Medium Cost)**:

    - Function: For each document in the corpus, generate more realistic synthetic queries using Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, and then retrieve positive and negative examples using the 8B retriever.
    - Mechanism: Synthetic queries generated by LLMs align closer with the distribution of real user queries compared to randomly cropped sentences, thereby providing higher-quality training signals.
    - Design Motivation: The distribution of randomly cropped pseudo-queries deviates from real queries; synthetic queries can mitigate this gap.

3. **Data Augmentation Method 3: 70B Listwise Reranking Preferences (High Cost)**:

    - Function: For the top-20 retrieval results of synthetic queries, use 70B-Instruct to perform listwise reranking. The top-1 after reranking is used as the positive example, and top-[10,20] as hard negative examples.
    - Mechanism: Listwise reranking by LLMs simulates human judgment in selecting the most relevant document among multiple candidates, offering more precise relevance signals than the 8B retriever.
    - Design Motivation: The preferences of the 8B retriever are limited by its fine-tuning data, whereas the 70B Instruct model exhibits stronger zero-shot relevance judgment capabilities.

4. **LLM Pruning as Retriever Backbone**:

    - Function: Structurally prune Llama-3.2-1B (which itself is a pruned version of the 8B model) further down to 0.1B and 0.3B models.
    - Mechanism: Adopt the two-stage pruning of ShearedLlama: (1) learn parameter masks and constrain the target architecture via Lagrangian multipliers; (2) continue pre-training on 26B tokens to recover performance. Establish bidirectional attention during fine-tuning.
    - Design Motivation: Compared to training BERT/XLM-R from scratch, pruned LLMs naturally inherit multilingual capabilities and long-context support (8192 tokens) from LLMs, allowing flexible customization of model sizes.

### Loss & Training

- Uses standard InfoNCE contrastive learning loss, where each query is paired with 1 positive + 7 hard negatives (for 0.1B/0.3B) or 3 hard negatives (for 1B).
- Augmented data is sourced from English web crawls (25M) + multilingual Wikipedia + multilingual web crawls, with a sampling ratio of 2:1:1.
- Augmented data is mixed with E5 supervised fine-tuning data (approx. 2M) during training.
- Employs Matryoshka Representation Learning to support flexible embedding dimensions.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Model | Parameter Count | BEIR (13) | MIRACL (18) | MTEB-FR (5) | MTEB-ZH (8) |
|------|--------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| mE5-base | 86M | 50.2 | 60.1 | 45.4 | 61.6 |
| ArcticEmb-v2-M | 113M | 56.9 | 59.2 | 53.7 | 55.7 |
| **Drama-0.1B** | 113M | **56.9** | **70.4** | 52.1 | 61.7 |
| ArcticEmb-v2-L | 303M | 57.2 | 64.9 | 54.5 | 63.6 |
| **Drama-0.3B** | 265M | **58.0** | **71.4** | **54.8** | 63.0 |

| **Drama-1B** | 1B | **59.1** | **71.7** | **57.6** | 63.7 |

With 265M parameters, Drama-0.3B matches the 1B Gecko on BEIR while outperforming it by 15+ points on MIRACL.

Even without explicit long-context training data, Drama's zero-shot long-context performance significantly outperforms traditional encoder models.

### Key Findings

- Purely synthetic triplet data (Method 4) performs poorly, indicating that augmentation based on real corpora is more effective than pure synthesis—the diversity and detail of real documents are difficult to fully replace through LLM generation.
- Combining three augmentation methods works better than any single method, demonstrating that diversity is key.
- Pruned LLM backbones naturally inherit long-context extrapolation capabilities (128K tokens for 1B), whereas BERT models are capped at 512 tokens.
- Operating with bidirectional attention is a critical design choice for using pruned decoder-only models as retrievers.

## Highlights & Insights

- Systematically compares four LLM data augmentation strategies under a unified framework while controlling variables (same LLM, same corpus), filling a gap in fair evaluations in this domain.
- The concept of utilizing a pruned LLM as a retriever backbone is both novel and practical—different model sizes can be obtained in a single pruning run, naturally retaining multilingual and long-context capabilities.
- Achieves or exceeds the performance of traditional methods that require two-stage training using only single-stage training (no multi-round contrastive pre-training), simplifying the training pipeline.
- Drama-1B outperforms MistralE5 (7B) on BEIR with only 1B parameters, achieving a 7x compression in parameter size with better performance.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The pruning phase still requires 26B tokens for continuous pre-training, which incurs a non-trivial computational cost.
- The quality of the augmented data heavily depends on the capabilities of the 8B retriever and the 70B generator; updating the base models requires regenerating the entire dataset.
- Performance on MTEB-ZH is slightly inferior to ArcticEmb-v2, suggesting that language-specific optimizations might be needed.
- Integration with lexical embeddings (e.g., SPLADE) has not been explored.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs Gecko**: Both leverage LLMs for data augmentation to train small retrievers, but Drama systematically compares more augmentation strategies and utilizes a pruned LLM backbone rather than traditional encoders.
- **vs MistralE5**: MistralE5 directly uses LLMs to generate synthetic triplets to train a 7B retriever. Drama demonstrates that augmenting from a real corpus is more effective than pure synthesis, and successfully compresses these capabilities into much smaller models.
- **vs ArcticEmb-v2**: Both aim for unified English and multilingual performance, but Drama does not require physical two-stage contrastive pre-training, resulting in a more streamlined training pipeline.
