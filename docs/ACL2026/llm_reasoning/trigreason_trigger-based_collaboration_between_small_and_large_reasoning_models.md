# TrigReason: Trigger-Based Collaboration between Small and Large Reasoning Models

**Conference**: ACL 2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2604.14847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14847)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/QQQ-yi/TrigReason](https://github.com/QQQ-yi/TrigReason)  
**Area**: LLM Reasoning  
**Keywords**: Reasoning Acceleration, Small-Large Model Collaboration, Speculative Reasoning, Event Triggering, Reasoning Models

## TL;DR

TrigReason proposes an event-triggered collaboration framework between small and large reasoning models. By analyzing three types of small model reasoning risks (Path Deviation, Cognitive Overload, Recovery Failure), it designs strategy priming, cognitive offloading, and intervention request triggers to replace step-by-step polling verification. While maintaining LRM accuracy, it offloads 1.70-4.79x more reasoning steps to the small model, reducing latency by 43.9% and API costs by 73.3%.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Large Reasoning Models (LRM) such as DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ achieve powerful complex reasoning through extended thinking chains. However, the autoregressive generation of thousands of thinking tokens leads to significant reasoning latency. Recently, SpecReason proposed a speculative reasoning paradigm where a small reasoning model (SRM) generates reasoning steps and an LRM verifies them step-by-step.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: SpecReason faces two critical issues: (1) LRM-as-Judge is unreliable—experiments show scores from four different LRMs for the same reasoning trajectory varied widely from 1.87 to 8.93, with LRMs even rejecting 63.7% of their own generated steps; (2) Step-wise polling is inefficient—calling LRM for verification regardless of step difficulty increases latency by 22.44% and API costs by 42.31% in edge-cloud collaboration scenarios compared to using a pure LRM.

**Key Challenge**: Prior methods lack a systematic understanding of "when and why SRMs fail," necessitating frequent, blind verification to ensure quality. This results in the final output being mostly corrected and generated by the LRM, rendering speculative reasoning ineffective.

**Goal**: Systematically characterize the capability boundaries of SRM reasoning and design an "on-demand intervention" collaboration strategy instead of "step-wise verification."

**Key Insight**: By comparing reasoning trajectories of SRMs and LRMs, the authors identify three systematic risk patterns. A key discovery is that SRM failures are often accompanied by abnormally low token perplexity (overconfidence), which serves as an early warning signal for cognitive overload.

**Core Idea**: Transition LRM intervention from continuous polling to event-triggered—invoking the LRM only for initial strategic planning, detected abnormal overconfidence, and when reasoning enters a stagnant loop. This allows the SRM to perform the vast majority of reasoning steps autonomously.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

TrigReason delegates the reasoning process to the SRM for autonomous execution. The LRM intervenes only under three trigger conditions: generating strategic guidance in the initial phase (first $n$ steps); replacing the current step when a cognitive overload signal is detected during SRM reasoning; and taking over for $m$ steps to perform path correction when the SRM generates consecutive hesitation tokens. The entire process eliminates the need for LRM judgment at every step.

### Key Designs

1.  **Strategic Priming Trigger**:
    - **Function**: Resolves the risk of path deviation by ensuring the SRM starts from an effective reasoning path.
    - **Mechanism**: The LRM first generates the initial $n$ reasoning steps (default $n=20$) to complete problem decomposition and strategic planning. Control is then transferred to the SRM for subsequent steps: $y_{1:n} \sim p_L(y_{1:n}|x)$, followed by $y_t \sim p_S(y_t|y_{<t}, x)$ for $t > n$.
    - **Design Motivation**: SRMs lack strategic foresight and tend to jump directly into calculations or apply familiar but inappropriate methods. Ablation studies show that removing this trigger causes accuracy to drop by 25.4%.

2.  **Cognitive Offload Trigger**:
    - **Function**: Resolves the risk of cognitive overload by switching to the LRM during critical steps where the SRM's capability is insufficient.
    - **Mechanism**: The system monitors the ratio $r_s$ of token-level perplexity below a threshold $\tau$ in each reasoning step. An LRM replacement is triggered when $r_s > \rho$ (specifically when the proportion of tokens with PPL below $\tau=1.05$ exceeds $\rho$). Experiments found that 94.6% of SRM error steps are accompanied by overconfidence, while only 38.1% of all steps exhibit this phenomenon.
    - **Design Motivation**: Overconfidence is not a mark of capability but a symptom of mechanical pattern completion under cognitive overload. This signal is obtained directly from internal SRM states without external judgment.

3.  **Intervention Request Trigger**:
    - **Function**: Resolves the risk of recovery failure by requesting LRM correction when SRM reasoning becomes stagnant.
    - **Mechanism**: A hesitation word set $\mathcal{H}$ (e.g., "wait", "hmm", "alternatively") is maintained. When hesitation words appear in $k$ consecutive steps, the LRM is triggered to intervene for $m$ steps (default $m=1$) for path correction.
    - **Design Motivation**: SRMs lack self-reflection and error-correction mechanisms but implicitly produce hesitation signals. A single step of LRM correction is usually sufficient to realign the reasoning path.

### Loss & Training

TrigReason is entirely training-free and serves as a pure inference-time collaboration strategy. It utilizes the SGLang inference engine with a temperature of 0.6, top-p of 0.95, and a default token budget of 8192. Evaluation is conducted using pass@1 with k=16.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Configuration | AIME24 Accuracy | AIME25 Accuracy | GPQA-D Accuracy | SRM Token Ratio |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LRM only (QwQ-32B) | Baseline | Baseline | Baseline | 0% |
| SRM only (R1-1.5B) | Significantly Lower | Significantly Lower | Significantly Lower | 100% |
| SpecReason | ≈LRM | ≈LRM | ≈LRM | ~35.6% |
| **TrigReason** | **105.8% LRM** | **104.7% LRM** | **99.6% LRM** | **~61.4%** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Impact on AIME24 Accuracy | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Remove Priming (n=0) | -25.4% | Most critical module; initial planning is indispensable. |
| Remove Offload (ρ=1) | Significant Decrease | Prevents error accumulation. |
| Increase Correction (m=1→3) | Marginal Gain | 1-step correction is sufficient. |
| Increase Priming (n>30) | Diminishing Returns | Excessive priming wastes LRM resources. |

### Key Findings

- Under certain configurations, TrigReason even outperforms pure LRM performance (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B + Qwen3-30B reaches 119.3% of LRM on AIME24).
- The ratio of SRM token usage improved from ~35% in SpecReason to ~61%, with an efficiency gain of 1.70-4.79x.
- Latency was reduced by 43.9% and API costs by 73.3% in edge-cloud deployments.
- The method remains effective on BBH (logical reasoning) and ARC (common sense reasoning), proving triggers capture general reasoning difficulty signals.
- The cognitive overload threshold $\rho$ is model-dependent: optimal at 0.85 for DeepSeek-R1-1.5B and 0.75 for Qwen3-0.6B.

## Highlights & Insights

- The discovery that "overconfidence serves as a signal for cognitive overload" is profound—94.6% of SRM errors are accompanied by abnormally low perplexity. This replaces subjective LRM judgment with objective statistical signals, fundamentally solving the reliability issue of verification.
- The paradigm shift from continuous polling to event-triggering is noteworthy: instead of asking "is this step correct?", the system asks "when might it go wrong." This logic is applicable to all small-large model collaboration scenarios.
- The finding that only 1 step of LRM correction can recover the reasoning path suggests that LRM's value lies in "directional guidance" rather than "full participation."

## Limitations & Future Work

- Trigger designs are based on heuristic rules, and the causal relationship between overconfidence and actual reasoning errors is not fully understood.
- Similar to speculative decoding, it requires extra memory to run the small model, which may be restricted in memory-constrained environments.
- The gap with pure LRM slightly widens under high token budgets (32K), suggesting a need for optimization in ultra-long reasoning scenarios.
- The definition of the hesitation set $\mathcal{H}$ is empirical, and its cross-lingual generalization requires further validation.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs SpecReason**: SpecReason requires LRM verification at every step, yet verification itself is unreliable (rejection rates up to 80%). TrigReason reduces LRM calls to critical moments via precise triggers, increasing the SRM token contribution from 35% to 61%.
- **vs Reasoning Length Compression**: Methods like length-penalty RL directly compress the token budget, which may skip critical steps. TrigReason maintains the full reasoning process but uses the inexpensive SRM for the majority of steps without sacrificing quality.
