# FlareX: A Physics-Informed Dataset for Lens Flare Removal via 2D Synthesis and 3D Rendering

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2510.09995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09995)
**Code**: [https://github.com/qulishen/FlareX](https://github.com/qulishen/FlareX)
**Area**: 3D Vision / Image Restoration
**Keywords**: lens flare removal, dataset, physics-based simulation, 2D synthesis, 3D rendering

## TL;DR
This paper proposes the FlareX dataset, generated through three stages—parameterized template creation, illumination-law-guided 2D synthesis, and physics-engine-based 3D rendering—to produce physically realistic lens flare data. Models trained on FlareX significantly outperform those trained on all prior datasets on real-world test sets.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Lens flare produced under strong lighting conditions causes color shifts and information loss, degrading downstream vision tasks. Deep learning approaches rely on large-scale paired datasets to train flare removal models, yet acquiring paired data in the real world is extremely difficult.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing datasets (Wu et al., Flare7K, Flare7K++) primarily synthesize flare by overlaying handcrafted 2D flare templates onto background images, suffering from two core problems: (a) limited template variety (3–35 categories), insufficient to cover the diverse flare patterns encountered in the real world; and (b) physically ungrounded synthesis, where random overlaying yields unnatural flare brightness and position.

**Key Challenge**: The physical implausibility and insufficient pattern diversity of training data limit model generalization to real scenes, particularly for reflective flare and off-screen light sources.

**Goal**: (a) How to generate more diverse flare templates? (b) How to ensure that the brightness distribution of synthesized images conforms to physical laws? (c) How to obtain a reliable real-world test set?

**Key Insight**: The Blender 3D physics engine is employed to simulate flare formation, and the illumination law is introduced to quantify the relationship between the light source and the lens.

**Core Idea**: Combine parameterized templates, illumination-law-guided 2D synthesis, and 3D physics-based rendering to construct a diverse and physically realistic lens flare dataset.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
The FlareX generation pipeline consists of three stages: (1) parameterized creation of 9,500 flare templates in Blender (95 categories × 100 parameter variants); (2) compositing templates onto background images following the illumination law (Flare-2D); and (3) constructing 60 3D scenes to directly render 3,000 paired flare images (Flare-3D). The final dataset mixes 2D-synthesized and 3D-rendered data.

### Key Designs

1. **Parameterized Flare Template Creation**:

    - Function: Flares comprising components such as light sources, streaks, irises, and glare are manually created in Blender with physical constraints; diverse templates are then generated by varying parameters.
    - Mechanism: Using Blender's Flared plugin as the foundation, camera focal length and flare component parameters (position, color, size, shape) are manually adjusted. Light sources are bound to spatial points with inter-component constraints enforced. Flare templates are obtained by rendering on a black background; removing the flare components and re-rendering yields the light source annotations.
    - Design Motivation: Prior datasets contain only 3–35 flare types, insufficient to cover multi-reflection and non-spherical light source scenarios. The parameterized approach expands 95 base categories into 9,500 templates.

2. **Brightness Adjustment Module (BAM) Based on the Illumination Law**:

    - Function: Automatically adjusts the brightness of the overlaid flare according to the depth of the light source relative to the camera and the angle of incidence, following the illumination law.
    - Mechanism: A random affine transformation is first applied to each flare template: $\mathbf{F_i'} = \mathcal{T}_i(\mathbf{F})$. A pretrained monocular depth estimation model is used to obtain the depth map $\mathbf{D}$ of the background image. The light source depth $d_i = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \mathbf{D}_{(x_j,y_j)}$ and incident angle $\theta_i = \arctan(\frac{2r_i}{W} \cdot \tan\frac{\varphi}{2})$ are computed and substituted into the illumination formula $E = \frac{I \cdot \cos\theta}{d^2}$ to perform brightness adjustment: $\mathbf{F_i''} = \frac{\mathbf{F_i'} \cdot \bar{d}^2}{d_i^2 \cdot \sqrt{1+(\frac{2r_i}{W} \cdot \tan(\frac{\varphi}{2}))^2}}$
    - Design Motivation: Random overlaying in prior methods yields unnatural brightness (excessive brightness or oversized flare). Introducing the illumination law couples flare intensity with spatial position, while also supporting dataset customization for specific cameras by adjusting the field-of-view angle $\varphi$.

3. **3D Scene Rendering (Flare-3D)**:

    - Function: Sixty 3D scenes are constructed in Blender; flares are placed at physically plausible positions and paired images are rendered from multiple viewpoints.
    - Mechanism: Flare placement positions (near light sources, in the sky, outside windows) are customized within each 3D scene. Camera motion trajectory keyframes are set, and paired images with and without flare are rendered along the trajectory, yielding 3,000 training pairs.
    - Design Motivation: 2D depth estimation is not always accurate, whereas 3D rendering inherently obeys physical laws, with flare appearance automatically correlated to spatial position. This approach also avoids image distortion caused by additive overflow in 2D compositing.

4. **Real-World Test Set Construction (Masking Method)**:

    - Function: A blocker is used to occlude the light source to capture flare-free images; the occluded region is excluded during evaluation.
    - Mechanism: After capturing a flare image, a vision-test occluder is used to block the direct light source when capturing the flare-free image. The occluded region is annotated and excluded at evaluation time. A total of 100 high-resolution (3024×3024) image pairs are collected.
    - Design Motivation: Prior methods (lens wiping) cannot eliminate reflective flare, leaving residual flare in the ground truth and distorting evaluation results.

### Loss & Training
The training loss follows prior work: L1 loss + VGG-19 perceptual loss + reconstruction loss. Models are trained for 30,000 iterations on 512×512 cropped images.

### Key Findings
- Flare-2D and Flare-3D data are complementary; their combination yields the best performance. Using Flare-3D alone is weaker due to the limited scale of 3,000 pairs.
- The illumination law consistently improves performance on both datasets, demonstrating the general value of physical constraints.
- For off-screen flare removal (no-reference evaluation), models trained on FlareX achieve the best NIQE (3.967) and BRISQUE (28.354) scores.
- A user study confirms that the FlareX test set better discriminates performance differences between models than existing test sets.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Illumination Law in Data Synthesis**: Incorporating $E = I\cos\theta / d^2$ into the synthesis pipeline enables automatic adjustment of flare brightness using depth maps and incident angles. The key insight is that no modification to the model architecture is required—improved data alone drives performance gains.
- **Masking Test Methodology**: Physically occluding the light source with a blocker yields truly flare-free images, fundamentally resolving the problem that lens wiping cannot eliminate reflective flare.
- **2D+3D Hybrid Strategy**: 2D synthesis provides data volume while 3D rendering ensures physical consistency; the two are complementary. This hybrid strategy is transferable to other low-level vision tasks that rely on synthetic data.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Noticeable artifacts remain for extremely strong light sources and large-area flares.
- Only 60 3D scenes are used, limiting scene diversity.
- The possibility of directly embedding physical priors into the flare removal model architecture has not been explored.
- The illumination law assumes point light sources; its applicability to area light sources has not been validated.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Flare7K/Flare7K++**: The previously largest flare datasets, but with fewer template categories (35 vs. 95) and no physical constraints in synthesis. FlareX outperforms them across all models.
- **vs. SDFRD**: SDFRD focuses on DSLR cameras, whereas FlareX covers both daytime and nighttime scenes.
- The data-driven approach is transferable: physical laws can similarly be introduced to improve synthetic data in other image restoration tasks such as dehazing and deraining.
