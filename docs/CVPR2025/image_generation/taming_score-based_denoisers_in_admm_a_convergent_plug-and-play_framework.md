# Taming Score-Based Denoisers in ADMM: A Convergent Plug-and-Play Framework

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2603.10281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10281)  
**Code**: Provided in supplementary materials  
**Area**: Inverse Problem Solving / Diffusion Priors  
**Keywords**: ADMM-PnP, Diffusion Models, score function, manifold alignment, convergence analysis, AC-DC denoiser

## TL;DR

Proposes a three-stage AC-DC denoiser (Auto-Correction + Directional Correction + Score-Based Denoising) to address the manifold mismatch issue when embedding score-based diffusion priors into the ADMM-PnP framework. It establishes the first theoretical convergence guarantee for score-based denoisers in ADMM and consistently out-performs existing baselines in inverse problems such as denoising, inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution, phase retrieval, and HDR.

## Background & Motivation

**Rise of Diffusion Models as Inverse Problem Priors**: Pre-trained score functions can effectively approximate data distributions and have been widely used in posterior sampling (DPS, DDRM) and plug-and-play optimization (DiffPIR, RED-diff). However, integrating them into ADMM remains unnatural.

**Manifold Mismatch Issue**: The score function is trained on data manifolds perturbed by Gaussian noise $\mathcal{M}_{\sigma(t)}$, whereas the intermediate variables $\tilde{z}^{(k)} = x^{(k+1)} + u^{(k)}$ generated by ADMM iterations do not necessarily lie on these manifolds—with the dual variable $u^{(k)}$ further distorting the noise geometry.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: DiffPIR and SNORE attempt to pull the iterations back to the training manifold by adding Gaussian noise, but adding noise alone does not guarantee manifold alignment; purification schemes are also merely approximations.

**Gap in Convergence Theory**: Theoretical convergence guarantees for marrying score-based denoisers with ADMM are virtually non-existent—the dual variable complicates the noise distribution of iterations, rendering existing PnP convergence analyses (Ryu et al. 2019, Chan et al. 2016) not directly applicable.

**Unique Advantages of ADMM**: Compared to purely primal methods like gradient descent or proximal gradient descent, ADMM flexibly handles multiple regularization terms and constraints, offering greater practical value for inverse problems—making the integration with score denoisers a problem highly worth tackling.

**Core Idea**: Design a three-stage AC-DC denoiser—AC adds noise to pull the state toward the manifold neighborhood, DC performs fine correction via conditional Langevin dynamics, and Tweedie denoising lands the final projection—and prove that this denoiser satisfies weak non-expansiveness, thereby guaranteeing ADMM convergence.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

In the standard ADMM-PnP framework (with variable splitting $x = z$), the denoising step of the $z$-subproblem is replaced by the three-stage AC-DC denoiser:

$$\min_{x,z} \ell(y \| \mathcal{A}(x)) + \gamma h(z) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x = z$$

ADMM updates: (a) $x^{(k+1)}$ via minimizing the regularized likelihood, (b) $z^{(k+1)}$ via AC-DC denoising, (c) $u^{(k+1)} = u^{(k)} + (x^{(k+1)} - z^{(k+1)})$

### Key Designs

**Stage 1 — Auto-Correction (AC)**:
$$z_{\text{ac}}^{(k)} = \tilde{z}^{(k)} + \sigma^{(k)} n, \quad n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$
- Adds Gaussian noise to the current iteration, pulling $z_{\text{ac}}^{(k)}$ closer to the neighborhood of a certain trained noise manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{(k)}}$.
- Similar to the noise injection in DiffPIR, but this step alone is insufficient to guarantee manifold alignment.

**Stage 2 — Directional Correction (DC)**:
$$w^{(k,j+1)} = w^{(k,j)} + \eta^{(k)} \left( \frac{1}{\sigma_{s^{(k)}}^2} (z_{\text{ac}}^{(k)} - w^{(k,j)}) + s_\theta(w^{(k,j)}, \sigma^{(k)}) \right) + \sqrt{2\eta^{(k)}} n$$
- Runs $J$ steps of conditional Langevin dynamics with the target distribution being $p(z_{\sigma^{(k)}} | z_{\text{ac}}^{(k)})$.
- Key insight: $\text{supp}(z_{\sigma^{(k)}} | z_{\text{ac}}^{(k)}) \subseteq \mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{(k)}}$, so DC aligns the state back to the trained manifold while preserving measurement information.
- The conditional score is decomposed into the score function plus an approximation of the Gaussian likelihood gradient.

**Stage 3 — Score-Based Denoising**:
$$z_{\text{tw}}^{(k)} = z_{\text{dc}}^{(k)} + (\sigma^{(k)})^2 s_\theta(z_{\text{dc}}^{(k)}, \sigma^{(k)})$$
- After AC-DC correction, the iteration lies on the trained manifold, where applying the Tweedie formula for denoising becomes highly effective.
- Can also be replaced by an ODE solver (10 steps) to obtain finer reconstruction quality.

### Convergence Analysis

**Theorem 1 (Fixed step size, strongly convex loss)**: If the residual $R_\sigma = D_\sigma - I$ of the AC-DC denoiser satisfies the weak non-expansiveness $\|R_\sigma(\tilde{z}_1) - R_\sigma(\tilde{z}_2)\|^2 \leq \epsilon^2 \|\tilde{z}_1 - \tilde{z}_2\|^2 + \delta^2$, then ADMM converges to an $r$-neighborhood ball around the fixed point.

**Theorem 2**: Under the $M$-smoothness and coercivity conditions of $\log p_{\text{data}}$, the AC-DC denoiser satisfies weak non-expansiveness with probability $1 - 2e^{-\nu_k}$.

**Theorem 3 (Adaptive step size, no convexity required)**: Relaxing the convexity assumption, under the $\rho$-increasing rule of Chan et al. 2016, the AC-DC denoiser is a bounded denoiser, ensuring high-probability fixed-point convergence.

### Hyperparameter Strategy

- $\sigma^{(k)}$: Linear decay schedule $[0.1, 10]$, with a decay window of $W$ steps.
- Number of DC steps $J = 10$, learning rate $\eta^{(k)} = 5 \times 10^{-4} \sigma^{(k)}$.
- The $x$-subproblem is solved using the Adam optimizer (up to 1000 steps, with early stopping).

- The trends are consistent with FFHQ: Ours-tweedie yields the best PSNR/SSIM, while Ours-ode achieves the best LPIPS.

### Ablation Study

| Number of DC Steps $J$ | Performance |
|------------|------|
| $J = 0$ (Disable DC) | Severe artifacts, poor reconstruction quality |
| $J = 5$ | Significant improvement, but some remaining artifacts |
| $J = 10$ | Optimal trade-off point |
| $J > 10$ | Diminishing returns |

### Key Findings

- **AC-DC consistently outperforms all PnP baselines**: It significantly exceeds DiffPIR and RED-diff, validating the importance of manifold alignment.
- **Complementarity of Tweedie vs. ODE Denoisers**: The Tweedie variant is superior in PSNR/SSIM (pixel-level accuracy), whereas the ODE variant shines in LPIPS (perceptual quality), catering to different application requirements.
- **The DC step is crucial**: Disabling DC (only AC + Tweedie) fails to yield effective reconstruction, demonstrating that adding noise alone is insufficient for manifold alignment.
- **Effective even on non-convex problems (Phase Retrieval)**: Although the theoretical proof is strongest under strong convexity, experiments show stable convergence under non-convex settings as well.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Clear characterization of the manifold alignment block**: The analysis of how dual variables distort noise geometry is the core observation, explaining why combining score-based denoisers with ADMM has rarely been attempted before.
2. **Progressive logic of the three-stage design**: AC (coarse correction) $\rightarrow$ DC (fine alignment) $\rightarrow$ Denoising (projection). Each step is backed by clear geometric intuition and theoretical support.
3. **First convergence theory for score-based ADMM-PnP**: Extends the non-expansiveness framework of Ryu et al. (2019) to a weak non-expansiveness setting, making a solid theoretical contribution.
4. **Comprehensive task coverage**: Covers six inverse problems (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution, phase retrieval, HDR) rather than presenting evaluations solely under favorable settings.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **High computational overhead**: Each ADMM iteration requires multiple score evaluations ($J$ steps of DC + denoising), leading to an NFE significantly higher than DPS/DAPS.
- **Fixed-point vs. stationary-point convergence**: Theoretical results are limited to fixed-point convergence (which is weaker), failing to establish stationary-point convergence guarantees.
- **Adaptive step size required theoretically in non-convex scenarios**: The adaptive $\rho$ scheduling is less convenient in practice than a fixed step size, although experiments show that fixed step sizes also work well in non-convex settings.
- **Heuristic noise scheduling**: The decay strategies for $\sigma^{(k)}$ and $\sigma_{s^{(k)}}$ rely on empirical heuristics and lack adaptive mechanisms.
- **Validated only at 256×256 resolution**: Scalability to higher resolutions (e.g., 512, 1024) remains untested.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. DiffPIR**: DiffPIR uses variable splitting + proximal denoising (noise injection + Tweedie). However, adding noise alone cannot guarantee manifold alignment; AC-DC introduces the DC fine-correction step.
- **vs. DAPS**: DAPS uses annealed posterior sampling, belonging to the sampling paradigm. AC-DC operates in the deterministic optimization paradigm, allowing the integration of multiple regularization terms.
- **vs. RED-diff / SNORE**: These methods construct explicit regularizers and then apply gradient descent. They suit primal methods well but do not naturally adapt to ADMM.
- **Insights**: The conditional Langevin dynamics approach in the DC step—using a few MCMC steps to align the iterations back to the target manifold—serves as a general manifold projection strategy that can be extended to other frameworks requiring manifold alignment.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The three-stage AC-DC design is grounded in strong geometric intuition, and the convergence analysis is theoretically rigorous.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Comprehensive benchmarks across 6 inverse problems and 2 datasets, though lacking high-resolution experiments.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The theoretical proofs are rigorous and clear, though the notations are somewhat heavy.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐☆ — Highly effective, though the computational footprint limits actual deployment.
- **Overall Recommendation**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐
