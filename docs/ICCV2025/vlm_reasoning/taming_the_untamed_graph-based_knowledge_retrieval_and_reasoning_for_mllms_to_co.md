# Taming the Untamed: Graph-Based Knowledge Retrieval and Reasoning for MLLMs to Conquer the Unknown

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2506.17589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17589)  
**Code**: [github.com/wbw520/MH-MMKG](https://github.com/wbw520/MH-MMKG)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: Multimodal Knowledge Graph, Multi-Agent Retrieval, MLLM, Knowledge-Augmented Reasoning, Game Cognition

## TL;DR

Using *Monster Hunter: World* as a testbed, this paper constructs a multimodal knowledge graph (MH-MMKG) containing text, images, video, and complex entity relations, designs 238 complex queries along with a multi-agent knowledge retrieval method, and reveals the inadequacy of current MLLMs in domain-specific knowledge retrieval and reasoning tasks.

## Background & Motivation

### State of the Field

Recent MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude) achieve strong performance on general-purpose tasks such as commonsense VQA, but for domain-specific tasks involving rare data, relying solely on their perceptual capabilities and built-in knowledge is far from sufficient, and interpretability remains limited.

### Limitations of Prior Work

**Knowledge redundancy and low relevance in multimodal RAG**: Both heuristic and agent-based mRAG methods tend to retrieve imprecise knowledge with considerable information redundancy.

**Existing MMKG benchmarks lack challenge**: Most MMKGs are constructed from VQA datasets or web resources, knowledge that existing MLLMs have likely already learned, making it impossible to genuinely evaluate the augmentation effect of MMKGs.

**Modality limitations in existing MMKGs**: Prior work primarily integrates text and images, lacking richer modalities such as video.

**High cost of training retrievers**: Training effective graph retrievers in low-resource domains is impractical, and integrating new graphs into closed-source models is infeasible.

### Root Cause

There is a need for a domain in which MLLMs genuinely lack built-in knowledge to evaluate MMKG effectiveness, as well as a training-free retrieval method compatible with closed-source models.

### Starting Point

The video game *Monster Hunter: World* is selected as the testbed. The game's visual world is generated by computer graphics and differs substantially from the real world; the game world's knowledge is distinct from real-world knowledge, making the built-in knowledge of MLLMs nearly useless for answering domain-specific queries.

**Core Idea**: By constructing a multimodal knowledge graph for the game domain and pairing it with a multi-agent self-search retriever, MLLMs can retrieve relevant information from structured knowledge and enhance reasoning without any additional training.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Given an input query $Q$ (comprising question $q$, video/image $d$, and auxiliary information $z$), the system operates in three stages: (1) a perceiver converts visual references into textual descriptions; (2) a multi-agent retriever searches MH-MMKG for relevant knowledge subgraphs; (3) a summarizer generates the final answer using the retrieved knowledge.

### Key Designs

#### 1. **MH-MMKG Multimodal Knowledge Graph Construction**

- **Function**: Manually constructed by three experienced players as 22 subgraphs (one per monster), merged into a complete graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{V}, \mathcal{R})$.
- **Mechanism**: Entities are divided into monster entities $\mathcal{E}_o$ and other entities $\mathcal{E}_a$; edges $v = (e, r, e')$ represent relations. Each entity carries attributes $A(e) = (c, u)$, including video clips (60fps 4K recordings) and textual context, along with manually selected keyframes and human-authored captions.
- **Design Motivation**: Knowledge in the game world is highly structured (attack actions, combo conditions, weaknesses, etc.), making it well-suited for knowledge graph representation. Multimodal attributes (video + text + image) bring the MMKG closer to the demands of complex multimodal reasoning.

#### 2. **Multi-Agent Retriever**

- **Function**: Three agents collaborate to automatically retrieve the subgraph required to answer a given question from the knowledge graph.
- **Mechanism**:
    - **Topic Selection Agent** $L$: Analyzes the question to identify the root entity $e_0 = L(q, z, \mathcal{E}_o)$.
    - **Expansion Agent** $W$: Expands the subgraph via BFS, determining whether neighboring entities are useful for answering: $\mathcal{N}(e) = W(e, \mathcal{K}_t; Q, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{A})$.
    - **Verification Agent** $U$: Checks whether the path from the root to the current open entity provides sufficient knowledge: $o(e) = U(e, \mathcal{K}_{t+1}; Q, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{A})$.
    - Iteration continues until no open entities remain to be expanded: $\mathcal{O}_t = \emptyset$.
- **Design Motivation**: Leveraging MLLMs' self-search capability enables training-free navigation over the knowledge graph, making the method compatible with closed-source models.

#### 3. **Knowledge-Augmented Reasoning**

- **Function**: Paths over the retrieved subgraph $\hat{\mathcal{I}}$ are converted to text and fed into an MLLM to generate the final answer.
- **Core Formula**: $\hat{y} = \text{MLLM}(Q, \aleph(\hat{\mathcal{I}}, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{A}, \alpha))$
- **Design Motivation**: The model does not rely on built-in knowledge but only on pure reasoning ability. The number of paths is capped at $\alpha=5$ to prevent information overload.

### MH Benchmark Design

Six subtasks covering different cognitive levels:

| Subtask | Description | Samples |
|--------|------|--------|
| I: Individual Information | Retrieval of textual information about monsters (nicknames, habitats, etc.) | 24 |
| II: Attack Recognition | Identifying ongoing, imminent, or completed attack actions | 109 |
| III: Combo Prediction | Predicting subsequent attack sequences given the current action | 28 |
| IV: Condition Awareness | Detecting monster/environment states (enraged status, terrain changes, etc.) | 29 |
| V: Effect Insight | Analyzing the impact of environment and attacks on monster states | 35 |
| VI: Cross-Monster Analysis | Comparing attack patterns across different monsters | 13 |

Three task variants are defined: Knowledgeable (perfect knowledge + perfect perception), Perceptive (no knowledge but perfect perception), and Unaided (relying entirely on the model itself).

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Model | Vanilla Acc. | Know. Acc. | Unaided-Online Acc. | Online Pre. | Online Rec. |
|------|-------------|-----------|---------------------|-------------|-------------|
| GPT-4o | 0.312 | 0.856 | **0.510** | 0.276 | **0.563** |
| Claude 3.7 Sonnet | 0.283 | 0.899 | 0.439 | **0.291** | 0.403 |
| Claude 3.5 Sonnet | 0.287 | 0.878 | 0.397 | 0.233 | 0.327 |
| Gemini 1.5 Pro | 0.219 | 0.844 | 0.405 | 0.212 | 0.459 |
| Qwen2.5-VL-72B | 0.148 | 0.873 | 0.316 | 0.162 | 0.181 |
| InternVL2.5-78B | 0.160 | 0.865 | 0.308 | 0.156 | 0.238 |
| Human (Knowledgeable) | 0.525 | — | 0.903 | 0.921 | 0.854 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Acc. | Pre. | Rec. | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| 5 retrieved paths | **0.510** | 0.276 | 0.563 | GPT-4o optimal setting |
| 3 paths | ~0.48 | — | — | Insufficient information |
| 10 paths | ~0.49 | — | — | Information redundancy |
| BFS search (3 paths) | **Better** | — | — | Shorter paths more effective |
| DFS search (3 paths) | Worse | — | — | Long paths disadvantageous early on |
| Human keyframe selection | 0.510 | 0.276 | 0.563 | More informative |
| Uniform keyframe sampling | 0.484 | 0.239 | 0.525 | Small but non-negligible gap |

### Key Findings

- **All models perform extremely poorly in the Vanilla setting** (GPT-4o reaches only 0.31), confirming that the game domain is a genuine knowledge blind spot for MLLMs.
- **Accuracy reaches ~0.9 in the Knowledgeable setting**, demonstrating that MH-MMKG contains sufficient knowledge to answer the queries.
- **Online vs. Offline**: When the model knows "what to look for," visual perception improves substantially (GPT-4o gains 0.1).
- **Closed-source models consistently outperform open-source models**, suggesting richer game-related built-in knowledge in closed-source systems.
- **Models tend to retrieve excessive knowledge** (Recall > Precision), whereas human retrieval is substantially more precise.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Ingenious testbed selection**: The game world provides rich structured knowledge while ensuring MLLMs lack built-in domain knowledge, making it an ideal setting for evaluating knowledge-augmented reasoning.
2. **Multimodal KG with video integration**: Surpassing conventional text+image MMKGs by incorporating video attributes, better approximating complex multimodal task requirements.
3. **Training-free retrieval**: Multi-agent self-search requires no embedding training and is directly applicable to closed-source models.
4. **"Knowing what to look for" enhances perception**: In Online mode, models produce more accurate visual descriptions after becoming aware of the query and retrieval context.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Limited benchmark scale**: Only 238 queries and 22 monster subgraphs; scaling up may reveal additional patterns.
2. **BFS outperforming DFS suggests relatively simple queries**: Most questions require only a few hops to answer, leaving complex reasoning chains underexplored.
3. **Video modality underutilized**: Current MLLMs cannot directly process video input and rely on keyframes and captions as indirect proxies.
4. **Limited human baseline**: Even knowledgeable players achieve only 0.53 accuracy in the Vanilla setting, indicating that the visual domain gap is genuinely large.
5. **Single-game representativeness**: Generalizability remains to be verified beyond the single game used.

## Related Work & Insights

- **GraphRAG** introduced graph retrieval into the RAG paradigm; this paper extends that idea to the multimodal domain.
- **StructGPT** enables LLMs to reason directly over structured data; the multi-agent design in this paper applies that approach to MMKGs.
- Game cognition offers an interesting dimension for evaluating AI intelligence; similar approaches could be extended to other virtual worlds (e.g., cinematic universes, fictional worlds).

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The combination of game-domain MMKG and multi-agent retrieval is novel, though individual technical components are relatively mature.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — 14 models, 6 subtasks, and multiple settings yield comprehensive and in-depth analysis.
