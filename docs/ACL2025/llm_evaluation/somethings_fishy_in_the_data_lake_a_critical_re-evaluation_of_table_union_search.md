# Something's Fishy In The Data Lake: A Critical Re-evaluation of Table Union Search Benchmarks

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.21329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21329)  
**Code**: [Available](https://github.com/Allaa-boutaleb/fishy-tus)  
**Area**: NLP / Data Discovery / Benchmark Evaluation  
**Keywords**: Table Union Search, Benchmark, Data Lake, Representation Learning, Semantic Understanding

## TL;DR

This work presents a systematic analysis of three structural weaknesses—excessive overlap, semantic simplicity, and ground-truth noise—in prevailing Table Union Search (TUS) benchmarks, revealing that simple bag-of-words (BoW) and pre-trained embedding baselines can match or outperform complex SOTA methods on these benchmarks. The findings suggest that existing benchmarks fail to effectively evaluate semantic understanding capabilities.

## Background & Motivation

Table Union Search (TUS) aims to retrieve candidate tables from a data lake that can be vertically concatenated (unionized) with a query table. Recently, various complex deep learning methods (e.g., Starmie, HEARTS, TabSketchFM) have emerged, achieving high scores on existing benchmarks. However, whether these high scores truly reflect semantic understanding capability remains questionable.

Core Problem: If simple, non-semantic methods can achieve high scores, the benchmarks themselves may be flawed—the high performance might stem from statistical and structural properties specific to the datasets rather than the models' semantic understanding capabilities.

Three hypothetical questions:
1. Partition-based benchmarks may introduce excessive schema and value overlap.
2. Benchmarks constructed from open-source corpora might be semantically too simple.
3. Ground-truth labels annotated by LLMs or humans may suffer from consistency issues.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The benchmark quality is evaluated through a three-step strategy:
1. Analyze the benchmark construction process to identify potential structural weaknesses.
2. Employ simple baseline methods as diagnostic tools to test the sensitivity of the benchmarks to different information types.
3. Verify the reliability of ground-truth labels.

### Key Designs

1. **Benchmark Construction Analysis**: Identification of three categories of structural issues:

    - **Excessive Overlap**: Partition-based benchmarks (TUS_Small, TUS_Large, SANTOS) create samples by splitting seed tables, resulting in excessive schema and value overlap between query tables and ground-truth candidate tables. Measurements show that $>90\%$ of ground-truth pairs share $\geq 50\%$ identical column names.
    - **Semantic Simplicity**: Although corpus-derived benchmarks (PYLON, LakeBench) avoid partition-based overlap, they focus on generic topics (such as common topics in GitTables) which are highly likely to be already included in pre-trained corpora.
    - **Ground-truth Noise**: Benchmarks generated by LLMs (UGEN) may introduce noisy labels due to inconsistent interpretations of unionability by LLMs.

2. **Selection of Simple Baseline Methods**

    - **Bag-of-Words Vectorizers** (HashingVectorizer, TfidfVectorizer, CountVectorizer): Test whether high-overlap benchmarks can be solved relying solely on word frequency signals.
    - **Pre-trained Sentence Embeddings** (SBERT all-mpnet-base-v2): Test whether general-purpose embeddings are already sufficient to handle semantically simple benchmarks.
    - Three column serialization strategies: Values + Column Names (V+C), Column Names only (C), and Values only (V).

3. **Overlap Quantification**

   Quantify the column name and value overlap using the Szymkiewicz–Simpson coefficient:

    $Overlap_c(Q,C) = \frac{|Cols_Q \cap Cols_C|}{\min(|Cols_Q|, |Cols_C|)}$

### Loss & Training

As this is an analytical study, the simple baselines require no training (bag-of-words and pre-trained embeddings are used directly). The compared SOTA methods (Starmie, HEARTS) are retrained following their original setups.

### Key Findings

1. **TFIDF reaches SOTA on Partition-based Benchmarks**: On SANTOS and TUS benchmarks, the simple bag-of-words method is on par with, or even outperforms, deep learning methods like Starmie.
2. **SBERT performs exceptionally well across nearly all benchmarks**: Pre-trained embeddings without any fine-tuning achieve high performance, demonstrating that the benchmarks lack sufficient semantic challenge.
3. **SBERT(C) with only column names achieves great results**: On high-overlap benchmarks, looking at value content is not even necessary.
4. **UGEN suffers from ground-truth inconsistency**: LLMs exhibit inconsistent standards for determining unionability across different generations.
5. **LakeBench-derived benchmarks inherit the flaws of partition-based benchmarks**: A large number of synthetic queries lead to high overlap.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Counter-intuitive conclusion**: Elaborately designed deep learning methods do not perform significantly better than TFIDF on current benchmarks—this suggests a lack of discriminative power in the benchmarks rather than ineffectiveness of the methods.
2. **Diagnosis rather than dismissal**: The paper positions itself as "using simple baselines to diagnose benchmarks" rather than "simple methods are superior to complex ones."
3. **Criteria proposed for future benchmarks**:
    - Minimize schema and value overlap.
    - Include semantic challenges requiring deep reasoning.
    - Ensure high quality and consistency of ground-truth labels.
4. **Evidence of computational efficiency**: Simple methods vastly outperform SOTA in terms of efficiency, though efficiency is not the main focus here.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. Only existing benchmark flaws are analyzed, without proposing a concrete new benchmark.
2. The inability to distinguish between real and synthetic queries in some LakeBench instances affects the precision of the analysis.
3. TabSketchFM's original paper results had to be used due to its pre-trained weights being unavailable.
4. The study does not discuss how to construct more challenging benchmarks while maintaining usability.
5. Limitations of simple baselines—SBERT might perform poorly on highly complex, real-world enterprise semantic data.

## Related Work & Insights

- Starmie (Fan et al., 2023): A contrastive learning-based column embedding method, representative of the TUS field.
- HEARTS (Boutaleb et al., 2025): A hypergraph representation learning method.
- TabSketchFM (Khatiwada et al., 2025): A data sketching method with advantages in scalability.
- SANTOS (Khatiwada et al., 2023): Introduces relationship representation between columns for TUS, though the benchmark itself suffers from partition overlap.
