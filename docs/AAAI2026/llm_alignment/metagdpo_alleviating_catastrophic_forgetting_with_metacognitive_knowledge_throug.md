# MetaGDPO: Alleviating Catastrophic Forgetting with Metacognitive Knowledge through Group Direct Preference Optimization

**Conference**: AAAI 2026  
**arXiv**: [2511.12113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12113)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/Qlanxue/MetaGDPO](https://github.com/Qlanxue/MetaGDPO)  
**Area**: Alignment RLHF  
**Keywords**: Catastrophic Forgetting, Knowledge Distillation, DPO, Metacognitive Knowledge, Small Model Reasoning

## TL;DR
This paper proposes MetaGDPO, which addresses catastrophic forgetting in reasoning distillation for small models (<8B) from two complementary perspectives: (1) the data side, constructing a 5K dataset (MetaKL) based on metacognitive knowledge annotation; and (2) the training side, introducing GDPO—a DPO variant that replaces GRPO's online sampling with offline response groups generated by a large model.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Works such as DeepSeek-R1, LIMO, and s1k have demonstrated that reasoning capabilities of large models can be distilled into smaller ones. DeepSeek-R1 performs large-scale distillation with 800K samples, while LIMO/s1k distill into 32B models using small-scale, high-quality mathematical data.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Compressing reasoning capabilities into small models (<8B) leads to severe catastrophic forgetting—after fine-tuning with LIMO data, models exhibit significant degradation in MMLU, commonsense reasoning, and safety benchmarks. Even performance on simple math problems deteriorates following training on mathematical data.

**Key Challenge**:
   - **Data side**: Existing high-quality datasets (LIMO, s1k) select samples based on difficulty alone, neglecting the relationship between training data and the model's inherent knowledge, causing the model to forget simpler knowledge while learning harder problems.
   - **Training side**: SFT directly trains the model to imitate large-model responses without constraining parameter drift; GRPO incorporates reference-based constraints but requires online sampling, leading to high resource consumption.

**Goal**: How can reasoning capabilities of small models (<8B) be improved under limited resources while minimizing catastrophic forgetting?

**Key Insight**: The paper introduces the concept of *metacognitive knowledge*—annotating each problem with the knowledge skills it requires, then selecting training data according to the model's proficiency on each skill. GDPO is proposed to replace GRPO, using offline response groups from a large model for preference learning.

**Core Idea**: Skill-profile-based data selection combined with offline preference optimization over large-model response groups, jointly alleviating catastrophic forgetting in small model distillation.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
MetaGDPO consists of two stages:
1. **Data Construction (MetaKL)**: Collect diverse reasoning tasks → annotate each problem's required metacognitive knowledge via GPT-4o → cluster into 8,325 knowledge units → analyze the base model's proficiency per knowledge unit → select 5K training samples based on proficiency and complexity.
2. **Training (GDPO)**: Sample a group of responses per question from a large model → compute group-wise advantages → train the small model via preference optimization over adjacent pairs, with a reference model constraining parameter drift.

### Key Designs

1. **Metacognitive Knowledge-Based Data Construction (MetaKL)**:

    - **Function**: Construct a 5K training dataset that covers both knowledge the model has already mastered and knowledge it has not yet acquired.
    - **Mechanism**:
      1. Collect 38,838 samples from mathematical reasoning (NuminaMath-CoT), commonsense reasoning (CommonsenseQA), logical reasoning (LogiQA), safety tasks, and others.
      2. Use GPT-4o to annotate each problem's required metacognitive knowledge (e.g., "quadratic equation solving," "probabilistic reasoning"), clustering into 8,325 knowledge units.
      3. Evaluate proficiency (accuracy) on each knowledge unit across five base models.
      4. Retain all complex problems requiring combinations of more than five skills; for simpler skills, set retention ratios based on model proficiency—only a small number of reminder samples for well-mastered knowledge, more samples for weaker areas.
      5. Greedy selection: retain up to 20 correctly answered problems per knowledge unit (to preserve existing capabilities), then prioritize difficult problems ordered by average proficiency.
    - **Design Motivation**: LIMO/s1k select only by difficulty, resulting in datasets dominated by hard problems that cause the model to forget simpler knowledge. MetaKL balances old and new knowledge by retaining both "problems the model already handles" and "problems the model cannot yet solve." Human annotation consistency reaches 92.18%.

2. **Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO)**:

    - **Function**: Perform preference learning using offline response groups from a large model, replacing GRPO's online sampling.
    - **Mechanism**: For each question $q$, sample a group of responses $\{r_1, ..., r_G\}$ from a large model and compute the advantage $A_i$ for each response. The optimal policy is derived as $\pi_\theta = \frac{1}{Z(q)}\pi_{ref}(y_i|q)\exp(\frac{A_i}{\beta}r(q,y_i))$. Pairwise preferences are established via the Bradley-Terry model, but all $O(G^2)$ pairs are reduced to an adjacent-pair chain of $O(G)$ by sorting responses by advantage:
    $\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{approx}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{G-1}\sum_{i=1}^{G-1}\sigma\left(\frac{\beta}{A_i}\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|q)}{\pi_{ref}(y_i|q)} - \frac{\beta}{A_j}\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_j|q)}{\pi_{ref}(y_j|q)}\right)$
    The paper proves that the relative gradient estimation error is <10% when $G \geq 10$.
    - **Design Motivation**: GRPO requires online sampling from the current policy; the initially weak capabilities of small models lead to poor sample quality and uncontrollable exploration. GDPO substitutes high-quality responses from a large model for online sampling while preserving preference learning's parameter constraints (via implicit reference model regularization).

3. **Computational Reduction from $O(G^2)$ to $O(G)$**:

    - **Function**: Simplify all pairwise comparisons within a group to a chain comparison over adjacent pairs after sorting.
    - **Mechanism**: Responses are sorted by advantage, and only adjacent pairs are compared rather than all pairs. The paper proves that the partition function term $f(Z(q))$ is independent of $\pi_\theta$ and can be omitted.
    - **Design Motivation**: Full pairwise comparison is computationally redundant, while adjacent pairs already capture sufficient ranking information.

### Loss & Training
- GDPO loss: DPO-style loss over adjacent pairs, weighted by advantage.
- Group size $G \geq 10$ (gradient error <10%).
- Reference model: the original base model (constraining parameter drift).
- Evaluation: 12 benchmarks covering mathematics, commonsense reasoning, and safety.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Performance | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| MetaKL + SFT | Improved but below full method | Data side effective; training-side constraint insufficient |
| Random data + GDPO | Improved but below MetaKL | Training side effective; data coverage insufficient |
| MetaKL + GDPO (full MetaGDPO) | Best | Joint data + training optimization yields best results |
| GRPO | Strong performance but high cost | Requires online sampling; high resource consumption |

### Key Findings
- After fine-tuning Qwen3-8B with LIMO data, MMLU drops only 1% while AIME24 drops 16% and GPQA drops 25%—catastrophic forgetting is severe and uneven across tasks.
- MetaGDPO is the only method that improves reasoning capability while preserving performance on other tasks (Overall AVG: 78.98 → 80.86).
- Model performance degrades as the number of required knowledge unit combinations increases, validating the rationale behind metacognitive knowledge annotation.
- $G \geq 10$ represents a sweet spot for GDPO: gradient error <10% with manageable computational cost.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Metacognitive knowledge-based data construction** is highly novel: rather than selecting problems by difficulty alone, the method annotates each problem's required knowledge skills and personalizes data selection according to the model's skill profile. This ensures the training data both introduces new knowledge and reinforces existing knowledge.
- **GDPO's design is practically valuable**: replacing GRPO's online sampling with offline response groups from a large model is particularly advantageous under resource-constrained settings. The reduction from $O(G^2)$ to $O(G)$ is supported by rigorous theoretical error bounds.
- **Synergistic data-training co-design**: the two innovations are not independent—MetaKL ensures data covers both new and existing knowledge, while GDPO's reference constraint prevents excessive deviation during training. Their combined effect substantially outperforms either component in isolation.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Dependence on GPT-4o for knowledge annotation**: annotation quality is bounded by GPT-4o's capabilities; inaccurate annotations may compromise data selection quality.
- **Granularity of knowledge unit clustering**: the appropriateness of 8,325 knowledge units is unclear—too coarse a granularity may miss important distinctions, while too fine a granularity increases annotation cost.
- **Validation limited to <8B models**: whether the method retains its advantages for larger models (32B+) is unknown—larger models may be inherently less susceptible to catastrophic forgetting.
- **GDPO response quality is bounded by the large model**: if the large model also performs poorly on certain tasks, the quality ceiling of the response group will be correspondingly low.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. LIMO/s1k**: These methods select data by difficulty alone and apply SFT, leading to severe catastrophic forgetting. MetaGDPO addresses the problem from both the data and training perspectives.
- **vs. GRPO**: GRPO requires online sampling and a reward model, incurring high resource costs. GDPO uses offline response groups with DPO-style optimization, is resource-efficient, and constrains parameter drift via the reference model.
- **vs. STAR-1**: STAR-1 focuses on safety data, causing drastic drops on knowledge tasks (MMLU: 79 → 46). MetaGDPO achieves balanced improvement through multi-dimensional data coverage.
- **vs. LoRA/PEFT**: Parameter-freezing approaches protect existing knowledge but limit the acquisition of new knowledge. GDPO's soft constraint via the reference model offers greater flexibility.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The metacognitive knowledge perspective on data construction is creative; GDPO's adaptation of GRPO is well-motivated and practical.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — 12 benchmarks, 3 base models, multiple baselines, detailed ablation, and theoretical analysis.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Problem formulation is clear and the data construction pipeline is well-described, though the GDPO derivation is somewhat verbose.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Catastrophic forgetting in small model reasoning distillation is a practical pain point; MetaGDPO provides a viable solution.
