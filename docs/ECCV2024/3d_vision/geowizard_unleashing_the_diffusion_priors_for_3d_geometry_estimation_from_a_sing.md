# GeoWizard: Unleashing the Diffusion Priors for 3D Geometry Estimation from a Single Image

**Conference**: ECCV 2024  
**arXiv**: [2403.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12013)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/fuxiao0719/GeoWizard](https://github.com/fuxiao0719/GeoWizard)  
**Area**: 3D Computer Vision  
**Keywords**: Depth Estimation, Normal Estimation, Diffusion Priors, Joint Estimation, Scene Distribution Decoupling

## TL;DR

The authors present GeoWizard, a foundational model for geometry estimation based on Stable Diffusion priors. It jointly predicts depth and normals using a unified model with a Geometry Switcher, and eliminates layout ambiguity in mixed scenes via a Scene Distribution Decoupler, achieving state-of-the-art results on zero-shot depth and normal benchmarks.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Estimating 3D geometry (depth and normals) from a single image is a fundamental computer vision task, widely used in autonomous driving, 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, etc. Current approaches primarily rely on discriminative models (CNNs/Transformers) trained on scene-specific datasets.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Low diversity of public datasets: most are restricted to specific environments like autonomous driving and indoor scenes, leading to poor generalization; (2) Low quality of pseudo-labels: labels generated by MVS reconstruction or self-training are often incomplete or noisy, which fails to capture fine geometric details despite improving generalization; (3) Separate estimation of depth and normals: existing approaches usually model depth and normals independently, neglecting their inherent geometric consistency.

**Key Challenge**: Building a foundational model capable of high-fidelity geometry estimation on arbitrary scenes (including AI-generated virtual scenes) requires both strong generalizability and high-detail precision. However, purely data-driven discriminative methods struggle to satisfy both requirements under the dual constraints of data diversity and label quality. Furthermore, training on mixed datasets introduces layout ambiguity, as the depth distributions of outdoor, indoor, and object-centric scenes are vastly different.

**Goal**: (1) How to leverage priors from large-scale pre-trained diffusion models to enhance the generalizability and detail fidelity of geometric estimation? (2) How to jointly estimate depth and normals in a single model while maintaining geometric consistency? (3) How to address layout ambiguity caused by mixed-scene training?

**Key Insight**: Leverage the rich 3D world knowledge inherent in the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and fine-tune it for geometry estimation. A Geometry Switcher allows a U-Net to handle both depth and normal domains, cross-domain self-attention ensures mutual consistency, and a Scene Distribution Decoupler separates the complex mixed distributions into learnable sub-distributions.

**Core Idea**: Unleash the diffusion priors of Stable Diffusion for 3D geometry estimation, achieving high-fidelity, joint zero-shot depth-normal prediction via a geometry switcher and scene distribution decoupling.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

GeoWizard is fine-tuned based on Stable Diffusion V2. Given an input image $x$, it is first encoded into a latent representation $Z_x$ using the original SD VAE, while ground-truth depth $d$ and normal $n$ are similarly encoded into $Z_d$ and $Z_n$. The image latent and noisy geometric latents are concatenated as two groups, $Z_x \circ Z_t^d$ and $Z_x \circ Z_t^n$, and fed into the U-Net equipped with a Geometry Switcher. The input image provides global guidance via CLIP embedding (for classifier-free guidance) and precise local control through latent concatenation. During inference, depth and normal maps are jointly generated starting from pure noise.

### Key Designs

1. **Geometry Switcher**:
    - **Function**: Enables a single U-Net to estimate both depth and normals, avoiding the need to train two independent models.
    - **Mechanism**: Introduces one-dimensional vectors $s_d$ and $s_n$ as domain indicators. These are added to the U-Net's timestep embeddings after passing through low-dimensional positional encodings. During training, the indicators are alternated to switch the model between depth and normal domains. Compared to shared modeling or sequential modeling, this approach converges faster and delivers more stable results.
    - **Design Motivation**: Depth and normals describe different aspects of 3D geometry: normals represent surface variations, while depth describes spatial layout. Sharing a single model allows both modalities to mutually benefit each other.

2. **Cross-Domain Geometric Self-Attention**:
    - **Function**: Enables mutual information exchange between depth and normal latent representations to maintain geometric consistency.
    - **Mechanism**: Modifies the self-attention layers in the U-Net. For the depth latent $\hat{z}_d$, the query is derived from itself, while the keys and values are computed from the concatenation of depth and normal latents $\hat{z}_d \oplus \hat{z}_n$; the normal side is processed in a symmetric manner. Effectively: $q_d = Q \cdot \hat{z}_d, k_d = K \cdot (\hat{z}_d \oplus \hat{z}_n), v_d = V \cdot (\hat{z}_d \oplus \hat{z}_n)$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Quantitative experiments show that removing the cross-domain self-attention degrades geometric consistency (GC) from 16.2 to 18.1, and training two independent models leads to an overall drop across all metrics.

3. **Scene Distribution Decoupler**:
    - **Function**: Resolves layout ambiguities caused by mixed-scene training.
    - **Mechanism**: Statistical analysis reveals that the scale-invariant depth distributions of outdoor, indoor, and object-centric scenes are drastically different (varying in mean and variance). Since direct learning of the mixed distribution is challenging, three one-hot vectors representing the three scene categories are introduced. They are added to the timestep embedding after positional encoding to guide the diffusion model in learning the counterpart sub-distributions. During inference, scene categories can be specified by the user or automatically classified.
    - **Design Motivation**: Outdoor depth extends indefinitely, while indoor depth is enclosed and object-centric depth is tighter. Failing to decouple the scene types results in inaccurate spatial layouts in the predicted depth maps (e.g., treating a globe image as object-level depth).

### Loss & Training

The models utilize the v-prediction objective: $\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}[\|\hat{\epsilon}_\theta(Z_t^d; x, s_d, s_i) - v_t^d\|^2 + \|\hat{\epsilon}_\theta(Z_t^n; x, s_n, s_i) - v_t^n\|^2]$. Multi-resolution noise is adopted to preserve low-frequency details. Both geometric branches employ the same timestep scheduler to simplify multimodal learning. The entire U-Net is fine-tuned at $576 \times 768$ resolution with a batch size of 256, trained on $8 \times \text{A100}$ GPUs for 2 days at a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-5}$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**Zero-Shot Depth Estimation (AbsRel ↓ / $\delta_1$ ↑):**

| Method | NYUv2 | KITTI | ETH3D | ScanNet | DIODE |
|------|-------|-------|-------|---------|-------|
| Marigold | 5.5/96.4 | 9.9/91.6 | 6.5/96.0 | 6.4/95.1 | 30.8/77.3 |
| DepthAnything | **4.3**/98.1 | 7.6/94.7 | 12.7/88.2 | **4.2**/98.0 | 27.7/75.9 |
| GeoWizard | 5.2/96.6 | 9.7/92.1 | **6.4**/96.1 | 6.1/95.3 | **29.7**/79.2 |

**Zero-Shot Normal Estimation (Mean ↓ / 11.25° ↑):**

| Method | NYUv2 | ScanNet | iBims-1 | DIODE-out | OmniObj3D |
|------|-------|---------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| DSINE | 16.4/59.6 | 16.2/61.0 | 17.1/67.4 | 19.3/44.1 | 21.7/45.1 |
| GeoWizard | 17.0/56.5 | **15.4**/61.6 | **13.0**/65.3 | 20.6/38.9 | **20.8**/47.8 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Indoor AbsRel/Normal/GC | Outdoor AbsRel/Normal/GC | Object AbsRel/Normal/GC |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Two Separate Models | 7.4/15.1/18.2 | 12.5/26.2/27.9 | 5.2/18.2/20.1 |

### Key Findings

- The cross-domain geometry switcher is critical for consistency; removing it degrades GC from 16.2 to 18.1.
- Scene decoupling yields the greatest improvement in outdoor environments (10.5 $\rightarrow$ 9.6) and the least in object-centric scenarios.
- Trained on only 0.28M images, GeoWizard achieves competitive performance compared to Depth Anything, which is trained on 63.5M images.
- GeoWizard shows superior generalization on in-the-wild images (including generative AI images) compared to discriminative baselines.
- Using incorrect scene indicators decreases absolute performance, yet the geometric consistency remains surprisingly stable.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Elegant leveraging of diffusion priors**: Instead of training from scratch, the method utilizes pre-trained 3D world knowledge in SD, attaining remarkable results using very limited training data.
- **In-depth insight into scene distribution decoupling**: Unveiling the fundamental flaw of mixed training via statistical analysis and introducing a clean yet effective remedy.
- **Coherent joint depth-normal consistency**: Cross-domain attention allows depth to guide normal orientations while normals constrain local depth variations.
- **Outstanding generalization**: Demonstrates exceptional performance particularly on AI-generated images and in-the-wild wildcard scenes, outperforming prior discriminative methods.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The iterative denoising process is time-consuming, making it less suitable for real-time applications or large-scale video processing.
- Stochasticity in the diffusion process leads to temporal inconsistency when processing video sequences frame-by-frame.
- Predicted depth maps are affine-invariant (scale and shift are unknown), requiring post-processing steps to recover metric depth.
- Future work could utilize Latent Consistency Models (LCM) to accelerate inference to sub-second timescales.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Marigold**: Contemporary work that also fine-tunes SD for depth estimation, but does not perform joint normal prediction or address scene decoupling.
- **Wonder3D**: Introduced the domain-switching concept, which inspired the design of GeoWizard's Geometry Switcher.
- **Depth Anything**: A representative discriminative method, functioning as a foundation model trained on a massive 63.5M dataset.
- **Insight**: The combination of diffusion priors and a small volume of high-quality data can effectively compete with the "discriminative model + big data" paradigm.
