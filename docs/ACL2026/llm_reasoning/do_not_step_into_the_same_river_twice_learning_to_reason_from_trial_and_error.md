# Do Not Step Into the Same River Twice: Learning to Reason from Trial and Error

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2510.26109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26109)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/JamyDon/LTE)  
**Area**: LLM Reasoning / Reinforcement Learning  
**Keywords**: Exploration Stagnation, Trial-and-Error Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Hint-guided Exploration, Mathematical Reasoning

## TL;DR

Proposes LTE (Learning to reason from Trial and Error), which effectively alleviates exploration stagnation in RLVR by using the model's own generated incorrect answers as hints to guide extra rollouts, without relying on external experts.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards) is a core paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, with GRPO standing as the de facto standard algorithm. However, RLVR training suffers from severe **exploration stagnation**—when training samples are too difficult for the model, all rollouts fail to generate correct answers, resulting in zero gradient signals from these samples and preventing the model from breaking through its capability ceiling.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing methods attempt to break exploration stagnation by introducing external guidance: (1) utilizing human-annotated standard solutions, which is costly and non-scalable; (2) using reasoning chains generated by stronger LRMs (e.g., LUFFY), though stronger models are not always accessible (e.g., when training flagship models). These methods function more as "temporary fixes" rather than fundamental solutions.

**Key Challenge**: Under the 0/1 reward setting of standard GRPO, when all $G$ rollouts fail (none-pass samples), all group-relative advantages collapse to zero ($\hat{A}_{i,t}=0$), leading to zero gradients and making the strategy unable to learn from these samples. Simply increasing the number of rollouts (GRPOExtra) yields minimal effect because repeating sampling under the same policy distribution does not provide an essential breakthrough.

**Goal**: Design a method that alleviates exploration stagnation in RLVR without any reliance on external expert guidance.

**Core Idea**: Analogous to human learning—once students are informed of their prior mistakes, they avoid them, thus becoming more likely to find the correct answer. LTE collects incorrect answers generated by the model in initial rollouts and uses them as "do not repeat these mistakes" hints to guide the model toward exploring different solution paths in extra rollouts.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The core workflow of LTE: For each training prompt, $G$ standard rollouts are initially performed. If all fail (none-pass), an appropriate hint template is selected based on the failure patterns to execute $G$ additional hint-guided rollouts. If any correct answer appears in the extra rollouts, it randomly replaces an initial failed rollout, and the policy is updated via mixed-policy GRPO. The entire process depends solely on the model's own behavioral information.

### Key Designs

1. **Hinted Extra Rollouts**:

    - **Function**: Provide guided hints based on the model's own mistakes for none-pass samples to increase the probability of generating correct answers.
    - **Mechanism**: Handles three cases based on initial failure patterns: (a) **all-truncated**: all responses are truncated due to length, prompting the model to "think concisely"; (b) **some-truncated**: extract incorrect answers from non-truncated responses as hints while requesting conciseness; (c) **none-truncated**: purely extract the set of incorrect answers $\{a_1, a_2, ...\}$ as hints. The model is instructed not to explicitly mention or use these hints to maintain "clean" reasoning chains.
    - **Design Motivation**: Incorrect answers narrow the solution space, telling the model "these are wrong, do not take this path," similar to the human trial-and-error learning process.

2. **Mixed-Policy Optimization**:

    - **Function**: Incorporate correct answers generated via hint guidance into policy updates in an off-policy manner.
    - **Mechanism**: If extra rollouts yield $G'$ correct answers $\{o'_1, ..., o'_{G'}\}$, they randomly replace an equal number of initial failed rollouts. Since correct answers are generated under hint conditions, the hint policy is defined as $\hat{\pi}(\cdot|\cdot) = \pi_{\mathrm{old}}(\cdot|\mathcal{H}_q, \cdot)$, and the importance sampling ratio is adjusted to $\hat{r}'_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(o'_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o'_{i,t}|\mathcal{H}_q, q, o_{i,<t})}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Hint-guided trajectories originate from different input conditions (with vs. without hints) and require off-policy handling. Regularized importance sampling $f(r) = r/(r+\gamma)$ is used to ensure stability.

3. **Entropy-Controlled variant (LTE†)**:

    - **Function**: Further promote exploration by adding an entropy loss.
    - **Mechanism**: Adds an entropy regularization term on top of LTE to encourage policy diversity. This allows LTE† to be fairly compared with methods using entropy control like DAPO and LUFFY.
    - **Design Motivation**: While LTE implicitly promotes exploration via hint guidance, explicit entropy control can further release the model's exploration potential.

### Loss & Training

LTE utilizes a mixed-policy GRPO objective function, comprising an on-policy part (standard GRPO clip loss) and an off-policy part (regularized importance sampling for hint-guided trajectories). All rollouts jointly calculate the group-relative advantage. Training is conducted using the verl framework, with 8 rollouts sampled per prompt, a temperature of 1.0, a maximum response length of 8192, and 500 training steps.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Pass@1)

| Model | Method | MATH-500 | AIME24 | AIME25 | Avg. |
|------|------|----------|--------|--------|------|
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPO | 77.70 | 22.08 | 15.83 | 43.99 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPOExtra | 75.75 | 22.29 | 14.79 | 41.72 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE | 78.85 | 30.62 | 27.29 | 49.01 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LUFFY | 80.20 | 29.17 | 21.25 | 47.15 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE† | 79.80 | 30.83 | 25.83 | 49.56 |

### Pass@k (Exploration Upper Bound)

| Model | Method | AIME24 | AIME25 | Avg. |
|------|------|--------|--------|------|
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPO | 43.33 | 30.00 | 56.82 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE | 70.00 | 46.67 | 66.78 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LUFFY | 60.00 | 33.33 | 61.55 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE† | 66.67 | 50.00 | 67.58 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Key Metrics | 说明 |
|------|---------|------|
| GRPOExtra vs LTE | -7.29 Pass@1, -10.04 Pass@k | Extra rollouts without hints perform poorly |
| LTE vs LUFFY (no entropy control) | +1.86 Pass@1 | Outperforms LUFFY without relying on external experts |
| LTE† vs LUFFY (with entropy control) | +2.41 Pass@1, +2.15 Pass@k | Advantage becomes more pronounced with entropy control |

### Key Findings
- LTE continuously reduces the number of none-pass samples during training, effectively alleviating exploration stagnation.
- LTE maintains higher long-tail entropy, encouraging depth of thought at test time (longer response lengths).
- Simply increasing the number of rollouts (GRPOExtra) can actually perform worse than standard GRPO, verifying that "quantity does not lead to qualitative change."
- LTE† stands out particularly in Pass@k metrics, indicating that LTE effectively expands the exploration ceiling.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Elegant Analogy to "Trial-and-Error Learning"**: Transfers the human learning style of improvement through error feedback to RL training, which is intuitive, concise, and effective.
- **Zero External Dependency**: Fully leverages the model's own behavioral information (incorrect answers) as guidance, presenting no barrier to practical deployment.
- **Differentiated Treatment of Three Failure Modes**: Designing hint templates based on truncation status reflects a deep understanding of the problem.
- **Significant Gain in Pass@k**: Particularly on AIME24, increasing from 43.33 to 70.00 (Pass@k), proving that LTE significantly expands the model's exploration limit.
- **Strong Comparison with LUFFY**: LTE†, without using any external expert trajectories, surprisingly surpasses LUFFY, which relies on stronger models.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Extra rollouts introduce approximately 2x sampling overhead (for none-pass samples); training efficiency requires optimization.
- Validated only on mathematical reasoning tasks; the applicability to other reasoning domains (code, logic) remains unknown.
- Hint templates are manually designed; automated hint generation strategies might lead to further improvements.
- Some baselines (ReLIFT, EvoCoT) performed poorly on Qwen3, possibly due to model compatibility issues.
- Future work could explore more dynamic hint strategies (e.g., utilizing richer error pattern information instead of just incorrect answers).

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs LUFFY**: LUFFY uses reasoning trajectories from stronger LRMs as off-policy samples, whereas LTE uses only its own incorrect answers as hints; LTE surpasses LUFFY on both LMs.
- **vs GRPOExtra**: Simply increasing the number of rollouts on Qwen3-8B-Base is even worse than standard GRPO (-2.27 Avg), proving the necessity of hint guidance.
- **vs DAPO**: DAPO improves the optimization algorithm itself via techniques like clip-higher, while LTE enhances exploration by improving the sampling strategy; the two are orthogonal and complementary.
- **vs EvoCoT**: EvoCoT uses ground truth answers as hints, while LTE uses its own incorrect answers as "negative examples"; the approaches are completely different, and LTE's is more effective.
