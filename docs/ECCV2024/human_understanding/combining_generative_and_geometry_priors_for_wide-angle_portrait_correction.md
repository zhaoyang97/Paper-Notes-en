# Combining Generative and Geometry Priors for Wide-Angle Portrait Correction

**Conference**: ECCV2024  
**arXiv**: [2410.09911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09911)  
**Code**: [Dev-Mrha/DualPriorsCorrection](https://github.com/Dev-Mrha/DualPriorsCorrection)  
**Area**: Human Understanding  
**Keywords**: wide-angle portrait correction, generative prior, StyleGAN, symmetry prior, optical flow

## TL;DR
A dual-module framework combining a StyleGAN-based generative prior (for face correction) and a geometric symmetry prior (for background line correction) is proposed, significantly improving the visual quality and quantitative metrics of wide-angle portrait distortion correction.

## Background & Motivation
- Wide-angle lenses are increasingly popular in mobile photography, but they introduce significant barrel distortion, causing background lines to bend and faces to stretch and deform, severely degrading photo aesthetics.
- Since faces are often close to the lens and heavily affected by perspective distortion, correcting the background and the face cannot be simply handled by a single unified method; they must be processed separately.
- Although existing deep learning methods (e.g., Tan et al., Zhu et al.) eliminate the dependency on camera parameters, they still produce unnatural correction results when faced with complex real-world facial distortions, which is especially prominent when training data is limited.
- Key observations: (1) Pre-trained StyleGAN can map latent codes to normal-shaped faces, serving as a natural manifold of facial structure to guide the correction; (2) Barrel distortion in wide-angle lenses exhibits central symmetry, which has been underutilized in previous works.

## Core Problem
How can we effectively correct unnatural facial deformations and bent background lines in wide-angle portraits without relying on camera parameters? Existing methods lack explicit constraints on the naturalness of facial structures and fail to utilize the symmetrical geometric properties of the distortion.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
The framework consists of three modules:
1. **LineCNet**: Corrects geometric distortion (line bending) in background regions.
2. **FaceCNet**: Corrects the face region using StyleGAN generative priors.
3. **Face Fusion Block**: Fuses the corrected face back into the background image.

Both networks are based on the U-Net architecture, trained independently, and then used in combination.

### Key Designs

#### FaceCNet — Generative Prior-Guided Face Correction
- **GAN Inversion**: Uses a pre-trained e4e encoder to map the distorted face into the $\mathcal{W}+$ space of StyleGAN2 to obtain the facial latent code.
- **Multi-Scale Feature Fusion**: Instead of directly using the image generated by StyleGAN (which would lose identity information), multi-scale intermediate features of the StyleGAN decoder are extracted and injected into different levels of the U-Net to provide structural prior guidance.
- **e4e Fine-Tuning**: Since the original e4e was trained on high-quality faces, it lacks generalization capability on distorted faces. Therefore, the e4e encoder is fine-tuned using synthesized wide-angle distorted faces.
- **Output**: FaceCNet predicts the corrected optical flow field $\Phi_{\text{face}}$ for the face region, and the corrected face image is obtained through a warp operation.

#### LineCNet — Symmetry Prior-Guided Background Correction
- **Key Observation**: Barrel distortion is solely related to the lens optical characteristics, where distortion increases with distance from the center and satisfies a central symmetry relationship (expressed in the formula $r_u = r_d \times (1 - k_1 r_d^2 - k_2 r_d^4)$).
- **Symmetry Regularization Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Sym}}$**: Applies horizontal, vertical, and central flips to the predicted background optical flow field, constraining the flipped optical flows to be consistent with the original one.
$$\mathcal{L}_{Sym} = \|\Phi^v_{bg} - \Phi_{bg}\|_2^2 + \|\Phi^h_{bg} - \Phi_{bg}\|_2^2 + \|\Phi^c_{bg} - \Phi_{bg}\|_2^2$$
- The network structure is simple, consisting only of a U-Net and symmetry loss, without requiring additional modules.

#### Face Fusion and Post-Processing
- ParseNet is used for face parsing to extract only the precise facial component regions.
- Facial displacement before and after correction by LineCNet is calculated to ensure precise alignment between the face and the background.
- Image inpainting algorithms (LaMa or diffusion models) are used to fill in missing areas at the fusion boundaries.

### Loss & Training
**FaceCNet Loss:**
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{FaceCNet}} = \mathcal{L}^f_{\text{face}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}^p_{\text{face}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}^{tv}_{\text{face}}$$
This includes optical flow regression loss, pixel reconstruction loss, and TV smoothness regularization ($\lambda_1=2, \lambda_2=0.5$).

**LineCNet Loss:**
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{LineCNet}} = \|\Phi_{bg} - \Phi^{gt}_{bg}\|_2^2 + \lambda_3 \|I^w_{bg} - I^{gt}_{bg}\|_2^2 + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sym}}$$
This includes optical flow regression loss, pixel reconstruction loss, and symmetry regularization ($\lambda_3=1, \lambda_4=2$).

## Highlights & Insights
1. **Clever Use of Generative Priors**: Instead of directly replacing the face with StyleGAN outputs (which would lose identity), multi-scale intermediate features are utilized as structural guidance, balancing naturalness and fidelity.
2. **Simple and Effective Symmetry Prior**: A regularization loss is designed based on the physical symmetry of barrel distortion, significantly improving line correction without requiring camera parameters.
3. **Divide-and-Conquer Framework Design**: The face and background are processed separately and then fused, avoiding conflicting objectives during unified processing.
4. **Open-Source Code**, facilitating reproducibility.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Correction performance is limited for large-angle profile faces, as StyleGAN is primarily trained on frontal or near-frontal faces.
- Distortions in other body parts (e.g., feet) are left unaddressed, which may lead to unnatural body proportions in the results.
- Post-processing relies on an image inpainting model (LaMa), meaning fusion quality is bound by the performance of the inpainting model.
- The limited size of the training dataset (approximately 5,000 training images) may affect the generalization ability across diverse real-world scenarios.
- Future work could introduce generative body priors for further improvement.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs Shih et al. (2019)**: Traditional optimization method requiring FOV parameters; this work does not require camera parameters and yields better results.
- **vs Tan et al. (2021)**: The first parameter-free deep learning method, but it lacks explicit constraints on facial naturalness; its ShapeAcc is significantly inferior to this work.
- **vs Zhu et al. (2022)**: A semi-supervised method using semantic information, but without incorporating facial structure priors; its face correction performance is inferior to this work.
- **vs GFP-GAN and Other Face Restoration Methods**: Face restoration focuses on degradation recovery, whereas this work focuses on geometric distortion correction, though both utilize StyleGAN priors.

### Insights & Connections
- Utilizing intermediate features of pre-trained generative models (rather than final outputs) as a prior is a paradigm worth adopting, which can be extended to other low-level vision tasks requiring structural guidance.
- The concept of symmetry regularization can be extended to other image correction problems with known geometric constraints.
- The spatial partition-and-fusion framework design has reference value for handling highly heterogeneous image correction tasks.
