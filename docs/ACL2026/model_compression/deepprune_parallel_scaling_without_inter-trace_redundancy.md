# DeepPrune: Parallel Scaling without Inter-Trace Redundancy

**Conference**: ACL 2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2510.08483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08483)  
**Code**: [https://deepprune.github.io/](https://deepprune.github.io/)  
**Area**: Model Compression  
**Keywords**: Parallel Inference, CoT Pruning, Inference Redundancy, Answer Equivalence Prediction, Inference Efficiency

## TL;DR

This paper proposes DeepPrune, which predicts answer equivalence from partial reasoning traces using a specialized judge model combined with an online greedy clustering algorithm to dynamically prune redundant parallel CoT paths. It reduces token consumption by 65.73%-88.50% while maintaining competitive accuracy (within 3 percentage points).

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Parallel scaling (such as best-of-n sampling) enhances LLM reasoning capabilities by generating multiple reasoning traces simultaneously, but total token consumption can exceed 100M+. Existing efficient inference methods primarily focus on the overthinking problem in sequential expansion, with less research on the efficiency of parallel scaling.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Over 80% of parallel reasoning traces produce the same final answer, representing a massive waste of computation; (2) Confidence-based early stopping methods cannot reduce inter-trace redundancy and risk prematurely terminating correct reasoning; (3) Shallow semantic similarity (such as SentenceBERT) cannot predict final answer equivalence from early reasoning stages.

**Key Challenge**: The benefits of parallel scaling come from answer diversity (a minority of different answers may contain the correct one), but the vast majority (80%+) of parallel traces produce the same answer, resulting in very low diversity.

**Goal**: To proactively prune redundant parallel reasoning traces while preserving answer diversity.

**Key Insight**: Train a specialized judge model to understand the deep semantics of the reasoning process and predict whether two traces will eventually yield the same answer from partial reasoning traces.

**Core Idea**: Early discovery of answer equivalence $\rightarrow$ Retaining diverse traces + Pruning redundant traces $\rightarrow$ Efficient parallel scaling.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

DeepPrune consists of two components: (1) a judge model—predicts whether the answers of two traces are equivalent from partial reasoning traces (AUROC 0.7072); (2) online greedy clustering—dynamically clusters traces into answer-equivalence groups as inference progresses, pruning redundant traces within each group and keeping only one representative trace.

### Key Designs

1.  **Answer Equivalence Judge Model**:
    - **Function**: Predicts whether the final answers are the same from partial reasoning traces.
    - **Mechanism**: Based on a trained Qwen3-4B using OOD data (AIME 2022/2023 and MATH 500) + oversampling techniques to balance positive and negative samples. The input consists of the first $N$ tokens of two traces, and the output is the answer equivalence probability.
    - **Design Motivation**: Shallow similarity methods (AUROC=0.58) and general LLMs (AUROC=0.66) are not accurate enough; a specialized model is needed to understand the reasoning process.

2.  **Online Greedy Clustering and Dynamic Pruning**:
    - **Function**: Prunes redundant paths in real-time during the inference process.
    - **Mechanism**: Maintains a set of answer-equivalence groups. For each new trace segment generated, the judge model checks if it is equivalent to existing groups. If equivalent, it is pruned (generation stops); if not, a new group is created. One representative trace per group is retained to continue generation.
    - **Design Motivation**: Online processing saves more computation than post-hoc pruning, and the greedy strategy balances efficiency and diversity in practice.

3.  **OOD Generalization Strategy**:
    - **Function**: Ensures the judge model remains effective on unseen reasoning models.
    - **Mechanism**: Trained on AIME 2022/2023 and MATH 500 (non-overlapping with evaluation data AIME 2024/2025) to generalize to traces generated by different reasoning models.
    - **Design Motivation**: In practical deployment, it is impossible to retrain the judge model for every new reasoning model.

### Loss & Training

The judge model uses binary cross-entropy loss, with oversampling of the minority class (non-equivalent pairs) to balance the data. Training data comes from parallel trace pairs from multiple reasoning models.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**Comparison with Standard Consensus Sampling (LLaDA Reasoning Model)**

| Method | Token Reduction Rate | Accuracy Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Standard Consensus Sampling | 0% | Baseline |
| Confidence Early Stopping | ~30% | Potentially Harmful |
| **Ours (DeepPrune)** | **65.73%-88.50%** | **$\le$ 3%** |

### Ablation Study

| Component | Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| Judge Model AUROC | 0.7072 (OOD Generalization) |
| SentenceBERT Baseline | 0.58 (Near random) |
| General LLM Baseline | 0.66 (Sub-optimal) |

### Key Findings

- DeepPrune reduces tokens by 65-88% across three challenging benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, GPQA).
- Accuracy loss is controlled within 3 percentage points.
- The judge model successfully generalizes to unseen reasoning models.
- Pruning preserves answer diversity—high-diversity traces are not mistakenly pruned.

## Highlights & Insights

- Quantitatively reveals the core efficiency issue in parallel reasoning: 80%+ of traces produce the same answer.
- Training the judge model based on "reasoning understanding" rather than "text similarity" represents a significant improvement over shallow methods.
- The online pruning design allows acceleration to take effect immediately during the inference process.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The AUROC of the judge model (0.7072) still has room for improvement, which may lead to a small number of valuable traces being mistakenly pruned.
- The greedy strategy of online clustering may be sub-optimal.
- It relies on specific judgment thresholds; different scenarios may require adjustment.
- Validated only on mathematical reasoning tasks; effectiveness on other reasoning types remains to be confirmed.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. Confidence Early Stopping**: Confidence methods cannot reduce inter-trace redundancy, whereas DeepPrune directly addresses the redundancy issue.
- **vs. Sequential Pruning**: Sequential methods reduce the length of a single trace, while DeepPrune reduces the number of parallel traces.
