# Think in Sentences: Explicit Sentence Boundaries Enhance Language Model's Capabilities

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2604.10135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10135)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/CLCS-SUSTech/think-in-sentence)  
**Area**: LLM/NLP  
**Keywords**: Sentence Boundaries, Separators, In-Context Learning, Supervised Fine-Tuning, Free Lunch

## TL;DR

This paper proposes the insertion of separator tokens at sentence boundaries within LLM inputs to implement a "think-in-sentence" reasoning paradigm via both ICL and SFT. This approach achieves consistent improvements across models ranging from 7B to 600B (GSM8k +7.7%, DROP +12.5%) with negligible additional computational overhead.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Sentence-level structure was once core to early neural language models—Skip-thought trained to reconstruct adjacent sentences, and BERT's Next Sentence Prediction task encoded inter-sentence coherence. However, with the rise of LLMs, sentence boundaries have been demoted to ordinary tokens, causing models to completely ignore sentence structure within token-by-token processing pipelines.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Mainstream methods for enhancing LLM capabilities either require massive training costs (training-time scaling) or increase inference latency (test-time scaling like CoT). Goyal et al. (2024) proposed inserting "pause" tokens as a "free lunch" solution, but it suffers from severe limitations: (1) pause token placement lacks linguistic priors and requires manual adjustment of quantity per task; (2) it was not validated on 7B+ models; (3) it lacks robustness and generalizability.

**Key Challenge**: Human language generation relies on an incremental, sentence-by-sentence cognitive process, yet LLMs learn continuous text generated by this process, leading to an inherent misalignment between human cognitive mechanisms and model input processing.

**Goal**: To design a strategy that leverages sentence-level linguistic priors to enhance LLM performance in a robust and low-overhead manner.

**Key Insight**: The authors observe that sentences are the most natural "cognitive chunks" in natural language. Inserting structural separators at sentence boundaries can trigger a cycle of "context integration → next-step planning," simulating the human post-sentence reflection process.

**Core Idea**: Inserting task-agnostic separator tokens at sentence boundaries allows the LLM to perform implicit sentence-by-sentence reasoning. This is implemented via both ICL (demonstrating separator patterns in prompts) and SFT (fine-tuning on data with inserted separators).

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Given a text sequence $T = [t_1, t_2, ..., t_n]$, sentence boundaries are identified using a sentence segmentation tool (SaT-12L-sm). A separator $x_{seg}$ is inserted at the end of each sentence, resulting in a structured sequence $S = [s_1, x_{seg}, s_2, x_{seg}, ..., s_n, x_{seg}]$. The model's objective is not only to predict the next token but also to learn when to generate the separator, thereby performing implicit sentence segmentation.

### Key Designs

1.  **ICL Method (Sentence-Aware Prompting)**:
    - **Function**: Guides the model to adopt a sentence-by-sentence generation style during inference by demonstrating sentence separator patterns in few-shot examples.
    - **Mechanism**: In the few-shot examples of the prompt, each sentence is explicitly terminated with a `<seg>` tag. Through analogy learning, the model automatically continues this sentence-by-sentence structured generation pattern during inference and output. Standard autoregressive inference is used without modifying model weights.
    - **Design Motivation**: ICL is a lightweight inference-time method suitable for long-context scenarios. However, it is limited in zero-shot or context-constrained scenarios due to context length constraints.

2.  **SFT Method (Internalizing Sentence Structure)**:
    - **Function**: Directly internalizes sentence-level structural priors into model parameters through supervised fine-tuning.
    - **Mechanism**: Separators are systematically inserted at all sentence boundaries within the TULU3 dataset, followed by fine-tuning using standard causal language modeling loss. The separator is added to the tokenizer as a new special token, and its corresponding embedding and LM head weights are learned during training. After training, the model natively generates text with separators.
    - **Design Motivation**: To overcome the context dependency limits of ICL, enabling the model to work effectively in zero-shot scenarios and making it more suitable for practical deployment.

3.  **Separator Selection Strategy**:
    - **Function**: Determines the most effective form of the separator.
    - **Mechanism**: Various separators were tested—structured tokens (`<seg>`, `<and>`, `####`), semantic words ("seg", "and"), punctuation ("\n", "."), and arbitrary symbols. Results showed that structured separators consistently outperformed other types and were the only type to exceed the baseline across all tasks.
    - **Design Motivation**: An ideal separator should be a purely structural marker irrelevant to text semantics. Semantic separators cause ambiguity (the model must distinguish between the marker's use and its content), whereas structured tokens provide unambiguous sentence boundary signals.

### Loss & Training

SFT uses the standard causal language modeling loss: $$\mathcal{L}_{SFT}(\theta) = \sum_{s' \in S} \sum_{i=1}^{|s'|} \log P(t_i | t_{<i}; \theta)$$, where $s' = [s, x_{seg}]$ and the final token $t_{|s'|} = x_{seg}$. Full-parameter fine-tuning is conducted on 8×L40 GPUs.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (ICL)

| Model | GSM8k Δ | DROP Δ | MMLU Δ | MATH Δ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Qwen2-7B-Inst | +7.73% | +12.50% | +5.53% | +0.97% |
| Llama3-8B-Inst | +2.50% | +6.77% | +4.39% | -0.34% |
| Qwen2.5-72B-Inst | +1.82% | +1.64% | -0.24% | +2.74% |
| DeepSeek-V3 | +0.30% | +4.00% | +0.78% | +1.20% |

### SFT Results (Llama3-8B-Base)

| Method | MMLU | GSM8k | DROP | MMLU-Pro | HumanEval |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Std-FT | 59.02 | 72.48 | 48.50 | 34.25 | 56.71 |
| Pause-FT | 56.11 | 75.44 | 55.97 | 35.71 | - |
| **Seg-FT** | **60.13** | **74.91** | **54.26** | **40.71** | **62.80** |

### Key Findings
- Small models benefit the most (significant gains at the 7B level), while performance gains for large models are smaller but consistent.
- DROP (reading comprehension requiring cross-sentence reasoning) saw the most significant improvement, suggesting that sentence separation helps the model better process sentence-by-sentence facts and their relationships.
- Seg-FT outperformed Std-FT across all 7 benchmarks, while Pause-FT degraded on knowledge-intensive tasks (MMLU, GPQA).
- Sentence-aware capabilities generalize to code generation (HumanEval +6.09%), as the model learns to insert separators within code line structures.
- Evaluation via Prob-based vs CoT-based analysis discovered that separators do not improve knowledge retrieval but rather enhance the multi-step reasoning process.

## Highlights & Insights
- The insight that **sentences are "natural cognitive chunks"** is profound: performance for fixed n-token chunking follows an inverted U-shape, with the optimal range $n \in [32, 64]$ corresponding to typical sentence lengths. This correlates with human cognitive chunking.
- **Key improvement in the "free lunch" methodology**: Unlike the blind insertion of Pause tokens, leveraging linguistic priors (sentence boundaries) makes the method more robust and general, eliminating the need for per-task hyperparameter tuning.
- The **unexpected generalization of SFT to code** is inspiring: natural language sentence segmentation patterns transferred to code line structures, implying that both share a common structural prior.

## Limitations & Future Work
- ICL relies on sufficient context length to place few-shot examples, limiting its use in zero-shot or context-constrained scenarios.
- SFT was only validated on Llama3-8B-Base, lacking SFT experiments on larger models.
- Sentence segmentation depends on external tools (SaT-12L-sm), which may introduce segmentation errors.
- The authors did not explore adaptively selecting separator placement in training data (e.g., only inserting at critical sentence boundaries).
- For highly structured tasks like mathematical reasoning, the improvement is relatively limited (MATH even slightly decreased on some models).

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs Pause Token (Goyal et al. 2024)**: Pause tokens are blindly inserted and require per-task tuning. Ours leverages the linguistic prior of sentence boundaries, offering better robustness and generalizability. SFT experiments directly prove Seg-FT is overall superior to Pause-FT.
- **vs CoT Reasoning**: CoT enhances ability through explicit generation of reasoning steps but increases token consumption. Ours enhances reasoning ability via implicit sentence separation with almost zero additional overhead. Ablation studies show the two are synergetic.
