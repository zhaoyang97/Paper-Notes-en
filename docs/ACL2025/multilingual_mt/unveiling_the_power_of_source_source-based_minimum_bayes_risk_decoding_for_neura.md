# Unveiling the Power of Source: Source-based Minimum Bayes Risk Decoding for Neural Machine Translation

**Conference**: ACL2025  
**arXiv**: [2406.11632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11632)  
**Authors**: Boxuan Lyu, Hidetaka Kamigaito, Kotaro Funakoshi, Manabu Okumura
**Institutions**: Institute of Science Tokyo, Nara Institute of Science and Technology  
**Code**: [vlaks425/sMBR](https://github.com/vlaks425/sMBR)  
**Area**: Multilingual Translation  
**Keywords**: MBR decoding, quality estimation, neural machine translation, reranking, paraphrasing

## TL;DR

This paper proposes source-based MBR (sMBR) decoding, which utilizes "quasi-sources" generated via paraphrasing or back-translation as "support hypotheses." Combined with a reference-free Quality Estimation (QE) metric as the utility function, this approach is the first to completely rely on source-side information in MBR decoding. It outperforms both QE reranking and standard MBR decoding under both classical and LLM-based NMT settings.

## Background & Motivation

- **Fundamental flaws of MAP decoding**: NMT models typically use beam search for MAP decoding, but estimated probabilities do not positively correlate with translation quality. The estimated probability of human reference translations is often even lower than that of poor-quality translations. Excessive beam sizes paradoxically lead to performance degradation (the "beam search curse").
- **Limitations of standard MBR decoding**: MBR decoding selects translations by maximizing the expected utility over a set of hypotheses. While using neural metrics like COMET as utility functions is effective, the support hypotheses always originate from the translations generated by the model itself, i.e., the target-language side.
- **Potential of QE reranking**: Reference-free Quality Estimation (QE) models can evaluate translation quality using only the source text and candidate translations. However, relying on a single source sentence can make the QE model overly sensitive to the specific phrasing of the original source.
- **Key Insight**: By generating multiple semantically equivalent but surface-form diverse "quasi-sources" on the source side and aggregating multiple QE scores within the MBR framework, a more robust translation selection can be achieved.

## Method

### 1. sMBR Decoding Framework

The core idea is to replace the "support hypotheses" in MBR decoding from target-language hypotheses to quasi-source sentences in the source language. Given the source $x$, a candidate hypothesis set $C$, and a set of $K$ quasi-source sentences $\widetilde{X'}$:

$$score_h^{sMBR} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{x' \in \widetilde{X'}} u(x', h)$$

where $u(\cdot,\cdot)$ is a reference-free QE utility function (such as COMET-QE). When $K=1$, this degenerates to QE reranking, which theoretically unifies the two classes of methods.

### 2. Two Methods for Quasi-Source Generation

- **sMBR-PP (Paraphrase Method)**: Directly generates paraphrases in the source language using a fine-tuned T5-large paraphrase model, training an independent paraphrase generator for each source language (fine-tuned on back-translated data).
- **sMBR-BT (Back-Translation Method)**: First uses a forward translation model to generate the translation with the highest probability $h_0$, and then generates $K$ quasi-source sentences for $h_0$ via a back-translation model (using beam search, beam size = $K$), resulting in the final set $\{x, x'_1, ..., x'_k\}$.

### 3. Overall Decoding Pipeline

1. **Hypothesis Generation Phase**: Generate $N$ candidate translations using beam search or sampling methods.
2. **Quasi-Source Generation**: Obtain $K$ quasi-source sentences through paraphrasing or back-translation.
3. **Decision Phase**: Compute the average QE score for each candidate translation against all quasi-sources, and select the candidate with the highest score.

## Key Experimental Results

### Table 1: Classical Setting (Beam Search, High Resource)

| Method | En→De XCOMET↑ | En→De MetricX↓ | En→Ru XCOMET↑ | En→Ru MetricX↓ |
|------|---------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|
| MAP (beam=5) | 84.89 | 3.63 | 82.90 | 5.36 |
| QE reranking | 86.48 | 3.22 | 86.20 | 4.27 |
| MBR (|S|=400) | 85.74 | 3.50 | 84.95 | 5.17 |
| MBR (|S|=17) | 85.88 | 3.34 | 85.00 | 5.37 |
| **sMBR-PP** | **86.73††** | **3.09††** | **86.52††** | **4.14†** |
| sMBR-BT | 86.17 | 3.33 | 84.99 | 4.65 |

Under low-resource settings, sMBR-PP also significantly outperforms QE reranking (En→De XCOMET: 66.36 vs 65.63††, MetricX: 10.19 vs 10.34†).

### Table 2: LLM Setting (TowerInstruct-13B, Epsilon Sampling)

| Method | En→De XCOMET↑ | En→De MetricX↓ | Zh→En XCOMET↑ | Zh→En MetricX↓ |
|------|---------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|
| MAP (beam=5) | 86.06 | 3.32 | 88.15 | 2.41 |
| QE reranking | 88.76 | 2.56 | 90.64 | 1.89 |
| MBR (|S|=128) | 89.19† | 2.46†† | 90.39 | 1.90 |
| **sMBR-PP** | **89.47††** | **2.44††** | **90.70†** | **1.87** |

sMBR-PP remains significantly superior to QE reranking in the LLM setting, performing comparably to or even better than standard MBR.

### Ablation Study: Effect of Increasing the Number of Source Texts

| Number of quasi-sources |S| | 1 | 6 | 11 | 17 | 33 |
|------------|------|------|------|------|------|
| XCOMET↑ | 86.48 | 86.65 | 86.73 | 86.73 | 86.74 |
| MetricX↓ | 3.22 | 3.12 | 3.10 | 3.09 | 3.09 |

The number of source texts is positively correlated with performance, with gains saturating after 16 quasi-sources.

## Highlights

- **Clear theoretical contribution**: This work is the first to employ only source-side information in MBR decoding, breaking the long-standing paradigm of MBR decoding relying on mutual evaluation of target-side hypotheses, and theoretically proving that QE reranking is a special case of sMBR ($K=1$).
- **Comprehensive and solid experiments**: The evaluation covers both classical (high/low-resource) and LLM settings, three translation directions, and four hypothesis generation methods. Metrics like XCOMET and MetricX are employed, which are independent of COMET's training data, ensuring fairness.
- **In-depth mechanism analysis**: By analyzing the quality of quasi-sources through Self-BLEU and semantic similarity, the authors find that the higher surface diversity of quasi-sources generated by sMBR-PP is the key reason for its superiority over sMBR-BT.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Risk of metric overfitting**: Directly optimizing QE metrics for reranking may lead to selected translations that "pander" to the QE model without actual quality improvement; human evaluation verification is lacking.
- **Instability of sMBR-BT**: The back-translation approach underperforms QE reranking under classical settings due to the insufficient surface diversity of its generated quasi-sources.
- **Efficiency issues**: sMBR-PP is far slower than QE reranking and standard MBR, as it requires additional paraphrase generation and multiple rounds of QE scoring.
- **Upper bound of paraphrase generator quality**: Performance ceases to improve once the number of quasi-sources exceeds 16, restricted by the capability of the paraphrase generator.
- **Limited language pair coverage**: Only En→De, En→Ru, and Zh→En directions were tested, leaving its applicability to language pairs without high-quality QE models questionable.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs Standard MBR**: Standard MBR relies on the mutual evaluation of target-side hypotheses, whereas sMBR utilizes source-side quasi-sentences with QE scores. sMBR-PP significantly outperforms standard MBR under classical settings and performs comparably to it in LLM settings.
- **vs QE reranking (Fernandes et al., 2022)**: QE reranking can be viewed as a special case of sMBR ($K=1$). sMBR significantly enhances performance by introducing multiple source texts.
- **vs Efficient MBR Methods (Cheng & Vlachos, 2023; Deguchi et al., 2024)**: Differing in focus, sMBR changes the source of support hypotheses rather than accelerating computations.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — First to use only source-side information in MBR decoding, theoretically unifying QE reranking and MBR.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Covers multiple settings and directions with reasonably selected evaluation metrics, though lacking human evaluation.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Clear mathematical derivations, and the relationship between the proposed method and QE reranking is well-articulated.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Provides a novel and practical perspective for NMT decoding, although efficiency issues hinder actual deployment.
