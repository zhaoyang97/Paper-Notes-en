# Can MLLMs Reason Beyond Language? VisReason: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Vision-Centric Reasoning

**Conference**: ACL2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2605.25364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25364)  
**Code**: https://github.com/CASIA-IVA-Lab/VisReason  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM / Visual Reasoning / Benchmark  
**Keywords**: Vision-centric reasoning, multimodal evaluation, visual grounding, CoT, capability diagnosis

## TL;DR
VisReason constructs a multimodal benchmark comprising 1,505 daily visual reasoning questions, specifically designed to test whether models can reason directly based on visual evidence. Results demonstrate that even the strongest models achieve an average accuracy of only $47.5\%$, significantly lower than the human performance of $71.4\%$. Furthermore, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and larger reasoning budgets provide only limited improvements.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Existing MLLMs show increasingly strong performance on benchmarks such as MMMU, MathVista, science Q&A, and chart reasoning. Many of these results are interpreted as the models possessing complex visual reasoning capabilities.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Many visual reasoning problems can actually be solved after being converted from "image to language." Diagnostic tests using Qwen3-VL-32B revealed that on datasets like MMMU, MMMU-Pro, and MathVista, performance dropped by less than $6.35\%$ when images were replaced with captions generated by Qwen-VL-Max. This indicates that a large portion of reasoning in these tasks can be completed within the language space.

**Key Challenge**: Human visual reasoning often relies on direct observation of shapes, layouts, local differences, spatial relationships, and implicit rules. In contrast, MLLMs might simply compress images into text descriptions and then perform linguistic reasoning. Existing benchmarks struggle to distinguish between "true visual reasoning" and "reasoning after reading a caption."

**Goal**: To build a benchmark where visual evidence cannot be adequately replaced by language, to evaluate proprietary and open-source MLLMs using a unified protocol, and to analyze the impact of model capacity, CoT, reasoning budget, and error types on vision-centric reasoning.

**Key Insight**: VisReason focuses on daily visual scenarios rather than STEM knowledge. The questions cover localization, spot-the-difference, pattern counting, 3D spatial tasks, board games, Sudoku, geographic clues, cue insight, and inductive/deductive reasoning, requiring models to iteratively verify between perception and reasoning.

**Core Idea**: Use "irreplaceability of visual evidence" as the benchmark design principle to examine whether MLLMs truly reason beyond language.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
VisReason aims to expose the phenomenon where many tasks labeled as "visual reasoning" can be solved correctly even if the image is replaced by a caption, indicating that models are performing linguistic reasoning rather than true visual analysis. Centered on the principle that "visual evidence cannot be replaced by language," the paper first defines a taxonomy for vision-centric reasoning, then manually collects, rewrites, and validates a batch of multi-format questions. Finally, it uses large-scale model evaluation and capability decomposition to explain model failures. Specifically, inputs consist of images and questions. Outputs are categorized by question type: multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended, and bounding box. During evaluation, multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank use rule-based extraction for exact matching; open-ended responses are scored strictly by GPT-5-mini; and bounding boxes are matched using IoU and normalized per question. Accuracy and average accuracy across categories are reported.

### Key Designs

**1. Vision-Centric Data Filtering: Retaining only "Irreplaceable Visual Evidence"**

If a question can be completely replaced by a caption, answering it correctly proves nothing about a model's visual reasoning capability. To address this, the authors collected candidates from online visual puzzles and Chinese civil service exam databases. Graduate student annotators standardized these into VQA formats and systematically filtered out four types of "pseudo-visual" samples: those answerable by image alone, those answerable by text alone, those dependent on external knowledge, and those with non-unique answers. This ensures every remaining question requires returning to the image itself for evidence—a prerequisite for the finding that while old benchmarks drop by $< 6.35\%$, accuracy on VisReason plummets by $48.12\%$ when replaced with captions.

**2. Three-Layer Reasoning Taxonomy: Perception, Structure, and Concept**

Visual reasoning is a broad term; without decomposition, it is difficult to identify specific bottlenecks. VisReason divides it into three layers across 10 categories and 36 subcategories: the Perception layer (Localized Reasoning, Spot the Difference, Pattern Counting) evaluates visual grounding; the Structure layer (3D-Spatial, Board, Sudoku, Geolocation) assesses extracting structured states and executing rules; and the Conceptual layer (Cue Insight, Inductive Reasoning, Deductive Reasoning) focuses on abstract reasoning. This hierarchy allows failures in "localization/difference" to be attributed to grounding, while failures in "Sudoku/board games" are linked to structural state extraction.

**3. Model Diagnosis Protocol: Dissecting Scale, Budget, CoT, and Error Types**

Average scores only indicate difficulty without explaining why or whether scaling and CoT can compensate for visual grounding. The protocol evaluates a wide range of models—GPT-4o, o4-mini, GPT-5 series, Gemini series, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL (thinking/non-thinking), MiMo-VL, Keye-VL—under a unified zero-shot CoT prompt. It overlays four analyses: caption replacement diagnosis, category correlation, error taxonomy, and atomic capability analysis. This decomposition led to the conclusion that perception and reasoning errors account for nearly $90\%$ of failures, and CoT provides almost no help for tasks dominated by visual evidence.

### Loss & Training
This work does not train models but focuses on benchmark construction and zero-shot evaluation. During the construction phase, multiple rounds of human verification ensure consistency between images, questions, and answers. During evaluation, all models use prompts corresponding to the four question types, aiming for a consistent output format. For bounding boxes, predicted boxes are matched with ground truth using Hungarian matching; an IoU $> 0.5$ is marked as correct and normalized per question.

| Analysis Item | Key Metric | Description |
|--------|----------|------|

| Model Capacity scaling | Performance generally rises with Qwen2.5/Qwen3 scale | Capacity helps but remains far below human levels |
| Reasoning Budget scaling | GPT-5-mini gains from low to medium budget; saturates at high | Test-time compute shows diminishing marginal returns |

### Key Findings
- VisReason effectively distinguishes model capabilities: weak models perform near-random, and even the strongest proprietary models fall far short of humans, indicating a non-saturated benchmark.
- CoT may assist with high-level cue insight, induction, and deduction but offers little help (or even causes degradation) for visual evidence-led tasks like spot-the-difference, 3D spatial, and counting.
- Low scores on bounding-box questions are not just formatting issues; models struggle to maintain spatial hypotheses during reasoning, which is more difficult than simple detection.

## Highlights & Insights
- The most valuable contribution is the redefinition of "visual reasoning evaluation": a task is not visual reasoning simply because it includes an image; it must prove that visual evidence cannot be easily replaced by language.
- The taxonomy covers daily visual reasoning, exposing foundational visual cognitive gaps in MLLMs more effectively than STEM-centric or chart-based tasks.
- The results provide a strong counter-argument to the "long-CoT solves everything" narrative: when the bottleneck is visual parsing and evidence localization, linguistic thinking budgets cannot automatically compensate for perceptual deficiencies.

## Limitations & Future Work
- VisReason only covers static images, excluding video, interactive perception, embodied tasks, or continuous decision-making; future work could extend to dynamic visual reasoning.
- Data sources (online puzzles and exams) may introduce stylistic biases despite human filtering; model contamination and benchmark overfitting remain long-term risks.
- Open-ended questions are scored by GPT-5-mini, which, though strict, introduces judge-model subjectivity; future iterations could include more human review or multi-judge consensus.
- The work focuses on evaluation rather than training. VisReason's atomic capabilities could be used as a training curriculum to design specialized data for bottlenecks like correspondence and structured state extraction.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs MMMU / MathVista**: These benchmarks evaluate visual domain knowledge and mathematical reasoning, but many problems allow captions to retain most information; VisReason explicitly pursues caption irreplaceability.
- **vs Sudoku-Bench / REBUS / VGRP-Bench**: These focus on specific puzzles or rule reasoning, whereas VisReason covers broader daily scenarios and diverse answer formats.
- **vs CoT-based MLLM reasoning**: While CoT strengthens linguistic reasoning chains, VisReason's findings suggest that multimodal reasoning requires integrated training of visual parsing, grounding, and symbolic reasoning, rather than merely lengthening text output.
