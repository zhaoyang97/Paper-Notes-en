# HPSU: A Benchmark for Human-Level Perception in Real-World Spoken Speech Understanding

**Conference**: AAAI2026
**arXiv**: [2511.23178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23178)  
**Code**: [Ichen12/HPSU-Benchmark](https://github.com/Ichen12/HPSU-Benchmark)  
**Area**: Audio & Speech
**Keywords**: Speech LLM, benchmark, spoken language understanding, emotion reasoning, adversarial evaluation

## TL;DR
This paper proposes the HPSU benchmark, comprising 20,000+ expert-annotated Chinese and English samples across 16 tasks, to systematically evaluate Speech LLMs' deep perception and reasoning capabilities in real-world spoken language scenarios. The best-performing model (Gemini 2.5 Pro, 62.6%) still falls far short of human performance (87.3%).

## Background & Motivation
- Human auditory understanding encompasses multidimensional information beyond transcription, including speaker attributes, latent intent, and complex emotions. While Speech LLMs have made significant progress on basic tasks such as ASR and SER, whether they possess **human-level deep perception** remains underexplored.
- Existing benchmarks exhibit three major deficiencies: (1) most focus on coarse-grained tasks or text-level reasoning, neglecting deep perception; (2) many rely on non-interactive or synthetic speech data, deviating from real-world scenarios; (3) most are monolingual, precluding cross-lingual analysis.
- A unified benchmark that comprehensively evaluates Speech LLMs across the full spectrum from basic perception to complex reasoning is lacking.

## Core Problem
Can current Speech LLMs understand high-level semantics in real-world spoken language—such as implicit intent, emotional shifts, and conversational subtext—as humans do? How can a comprehensive, high-quality, bilingual benchmark be constructed to quantify this gap?

## Method

### Data Construction Pipeline (Three-Stage Semi-Automatic Annotation)
1. **Data Collection and Preprocessing**: High-quality clips are selected from public audiovisual datasets including CelebV-HQ, MAFW, MELD, and MER. Audio quality is scored using an assessment tool, and samples below a threshold undergo speech enhancement and denoising (ClearerVoice-Studio). Each sample retains three modalities: video, audio, and transcripts generated by Whisper/FireRedASR. VCTK and an internal dialect dataset serve as audio-only sources to support accent-related tasks.
2. **Information Extraction and Cross-Validation**: Transcripts are first fed into an LLM to infer probable speaker states (emotion, intent, etc.) as prior knowledge. This prior knowledge, combined with audio/video, is then fed into corresponding models to extract unimodal representations. A **cross-modal validation** strategy (visual descriptions fed into audio models and vice versa) is employed to verify cross-modal consistency and logical coherence.
3. **Information Fusion and Expert Verification**: A QWQ reasoning model performs hierarchical fusion, synthesizing the validated information into three descriptive layers—core layer (primary emotion/intent), detail layer (fine-grained cues such as micro-expressions), and context layer. 80% of the data forms the **HPSC dataset** (50,000 speech-description pairs); the remaining 20% is structured into evaluation triplets by Gemini 2.5 Pro and independently reviewed by three annotators. Only samples with unanimous agreement are retained (pass rate: 81.26%), yielding the final HPSU benchmark.

### Task Design (16 Tasks across 5 Domains)

| Domain | Tasks | Cognitive Level |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Social Attributes | Age, Gender, Accent | Basic Perception |
| Emotion Recognition | Primary Emotion, Multi-Emotion, Emotion Intensity | Basic Perception |
| Emotion Reasoning | Emotion Shift (Easy/Hard), Surface-Deep Inconsistency (Easy/Hard) | Complex Reasoning |
| Nonverbal Behavior | Speaking Style, Visual Behavior, Description Judgment | Complex Reasoning |
| Utterance Context | Intent Inference, Scene Judgment, Subtext Understanding | Complex Reasoning |

- Objective tasks use single-choice format; multi-emotion tasks use multi-choice; deep semantic tasks such as subtext use yes/no judgment.
- **Distractor Generation**: Gemini 2.5 Pro generates tiered distractors (similar / middle / opposite) to enhance discriminability.
- **Adversarial Induction Protocol**: Each task instance includes three prompt variants—standard unbiased, positive induction (with hints toward the correct answer), and negative induction (with misleading cues)—to evaluate robustness.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (13 Models + 3 Baselines)

| Model | English Avg. | Chinese Avg. | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Human | ~84% | ~90% | Overall avg. 87.3% |
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | Highest | Highest | Overall avg. 62.6% |
| Qwen2.5-Omni | 2nd highest | 2nd highest | Overall avg. 60.0%, trailing Pro by 2.6% |
| Whisper+GPT-4o | — | — | Competitive with Gemini Pro on Emos/Intent |

### Key Findings
- **Large Human–Machine Gap**: The best model trails human performance by 24.7 percentage points. Performance on basic tasks (gender recognition ~94%) approaches human levels, while high-level reasoning tasks (subtext, hard emotion shift) show a substantial gap.
- **Small Open- vs. Closed-Source Gap**: Qwen2.5-Omni outperforms Gemini 2.5 Pro on certain tasks (e.g., English Emotion Shift Easy, Chinese Visual Behavior), suggesting that training data diversity may matter more than whether a model is proprietary.
- **Limitations of Cascaded Approaches**: Whisper+GPT-4o performs poorly on tasks requiring acoustic detail (description judgment, hard emotion shift), demonstrating that text modality alone cannot replace acoustic information. However, it matches end-to-end models on semantically driven tasks (Emos, Intent), reflecting the generalization advantage of the text modality.
- **Adversarial Vulnerability**: All models are highly sensitive to misleading prompts, while humans are nearly immune. Models are especially vulnerable on high-ambiguity tasks (age, accent, hard surface-deep inconsistency) and open-ended tasks (intent, scene). Natively multimodal architectures (Gemini) are more robust than fine-tuned text LLM solutions.
- **Tiered Evaluation Insights**: Model errors concentrate on "Similar" distractors rather than "Opposite" ones, indicating that models grasp broad semantics but lack fine-grained discriminative ability—the bottleneck lies in resolving subtle ambiguity rather than in a fundamental lack of comprehension.
- **Cross-Lingual Inconsistency**: The gap between models' Chinese and English performance on the same task far exceeds that observed in humans. For instance, models perform notably worse in Chinese on subtext understanding tasks, possibly because implicit expressions in Chinese are harder to model.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Comprehensive Task Coverage**: 16 tasks spanning the full cognitive spectrum from basic attribute recognition to subtext understanding, making HPSU the most thorough speech understanding benchmark to date.
- **Innovative Annotation Pipeline**: The semi-automatic pipeline combining cross-modal validation and hierarchical fusion effectively addresses the high annotation cost of high-level tasks.
- **Rich Evaluation Dimensions**: Tiered distractors, adversarial induction protocols, and cross-lingual comparisons provide diagnostic power far beyond simple accuracy metrics.
- **Scale and Quality**: All 20,000+ samples are expert-verified; the accompanying 50,000-sample HPSC dataset is also released for community use.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Data sources are concentrated in film, television, and social media, lacking coverage of professional scenarios such as meetings, call centers, and medical consultations.
- Only Chinese and English are supported; low-resource languages (e.g., Arabic, Hindi) are not covered.
- Adversarial induction is limited to the text prompt level; audio-level adversarial attacks (e.g., noise injection, pitch shifting) are not explored.
- Evaluation relies on Gemini 2.5 Pro as an automated judge, potentially introducing systematic bias—especially when Gemini itself is among the evaluated models.
- SFT experiments on HPSC are only briefly presented in the appendix, without in-depth analysis of different training strategies.
- GPT-4o was excluded due to its safety policy refusing most queries, limiting the completeness of comparisons with top closed-source models.
- Model performance in multi-turn dialogue scenarios is not evaluated.

HPSU comprehensively surpasses existing benchmarks in scale, task depth, adversarial evaluation, and multilingual support.

The work reveals training data biases in current Speech LLMs: models are over-optimized for low-level tasks such as ASR/SER, while data supporting higher-order cognition is severely lacking—HPSU points the community toward the directions most in need of advancement. The cross-modal validation annotation pipeline is transferable to other tasks requiring fine-grained semantic annotation (e.g., multimodal affective computing, speech act analysis). Adversarial induction experiments demonstrate that natively multimodal architectures (e.g., Gemini) are more robust than fine-tuned text LLM solutions, providing architectural guidance—end-to-end multimodal training may be the superior path toward robust speech understanding. The tiered evaluation framework (True/Similar/Middle/Opposite) is worth adopting in other highly subjective NLP benchmarks, as it reveals model decision patterns more effectively than traditional binary scoring. The 50,000 speech-description pairs in HPSC have potential downstream value for controllable speech generation and speech emotion synthesis. The strong performance of cascaded methods on semantic tasks suggests that future end-to-end models must match the semantic reasoning capability of text LLMs while preserving acoustic detail.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The first benchmark to systematically evaluate human-level deep perception in Speech LLMs; task design and adversarial protocol are innovative.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Comprehensive comparison of 13 models with multi-dimensional analysis (adversarial, tiered, cross-lingual); experiments are highly rigorous.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Well-structured with rich figures and tables, though some analyses could be deepened.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Clear practical guidance for the speech understanding community; the HPSC dataset provides additional utility.

## Supplementary Notes
- The 13 evaluated models include 11 open-source models (Audio Flamingo 2/3/3.5, Kimi-Audio, Qwen-Audio, Qwen2-Audio, SALMONN, Soundwave, Baichuan-Omni-1.5, Qwen2.5-Omni, Audio-Reasoner) and 2 closed-source models (Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro).
- Human baselines were established by 10 native speakers per language on 300 stratified sampled instances.
- The dataset comprises 11,580 English samples and 8,786 Chinese samples, totaling over 20,000 instances.
- The paper also releases the HPSC dataset (50,000 samples); SFT experiments show that fine-tuning on HPSC improves model perception and understanding capabilities.
