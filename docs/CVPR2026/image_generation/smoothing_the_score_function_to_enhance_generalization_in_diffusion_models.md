# Smoothing the Score Function to Enhance Generalization in Diffusion Models

**Conference**: CVPR 2026  
**Paper**: [CVF Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Zhou_Smoothing_the_Score_Function_to_Enhance_Generalization_in_Diffusion_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.html)  
**Code**: https://github.com/XinyuZhou2001/smoothing_the_score  
**Area**: Diffusion Models  
**Keywords**: Memorization, Generalization, Score Function, Gaussian Mixture, Temperature Smoothing

## TL;DR
This paper theoretically demonstrates that memorization in diffusion models (where generated samples verbatim copy training samples) originates from the empirical score function being a sum of Gaussian components with "sharp softmax weighting," causing a single training point to dominate sampling and lead to collapse. Accordingly, two methods for smoothing weights—Noise Unconditioning and Temperature Smoothing—are proposed, significantly enhancing generalization and mitigating memorization with minimal loss in generation quality.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Diffusion models generate samples by gradually adding Gaussian noise to data and learning a reverse denoising score function $\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$. Under the manifold hypothesis, data resides on low-dimensional manifolds in high-dimensional space. Direct score matching yields inaccurate gradient estimates in low-density regions, hence the prevalent approach of estimating scores for a sequence of noisy marginal distributions to ensure learning accuracy along the entire sampling path.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Increasing research indicates that a portion of samples generated by diffusion models are **identical** to training samples—a phenomenon known as memorization, which poses privacy and copyright risks. Theoretically, this is almost "inevitable": the Fokker–Planck equation implies that if the reverse process is solved exactly using the **empirical score function** (the score of the empirical distribution), every sampled marginal distribution will equal its forward counterpart, eventually returning to the training distribution and failing to generate novel samples.

**Key Challenge**: While theory suggests that "perfectly learning the empirical score only leads to copying," neural networks in practice can generate novel samples. This contradiction between theory and practice is the fundamental question this paper addresses: **How do neural networks partially resolve memorization?**

**Goal**: The problem is decomposed into two sub-questions: (1) What is the specific structure of the empirical score function that leads to memorization? (2) What is the difference between the NN-learned score and the empirical score, and how does this difference facilitate generalization? After answering these, the paper further explores whether this difference can be **actively** induced to enhance generalization.

**Key Insight**: Recognizing that the NN-learned $s_\theta(x,\sigma)$ inevitably differs from the empirical score $s^*(x,\sigma)$, the authors explicitly formulate the empirical score as a "softmax-weighted sum of Gaussian components." This weighted structure is analyzed from both algebraic and geometric perspectives (observing that high-dimensional Gaussian mass concentrates on thin shells).

**Core Idea**: The root cause of memorization is that the softmax weights are too sharp, allowing a single training point to monopolize the score. Due to capacity and regularization constraints, neural networks learn **smoother** weights, allowing the sampling process to be determined by a "local manifold spanned by several nearby samples" rather than a "single point." Thus, actively **smoothing** the weights (via noise unconditioning and temperature adjustment) can yield generalization.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The core of this work is a theoretical "diagnosis → prescription" framework rather than a sequential engineering pipeline. The empirical score function is first dissected into a softmax-weighted structure to locate the mathematical root of memorization (sharp weights, single-point dominance). Based on this, two training-side modifications to smooth the weights are proposed. Finally, a "Sampling Expansion Ratio" $\gamma_{ex}$ is defined as a quantitative proxy for generalization to compare various scores.

Let $\{\mu_j\}_{j=1}^M$ be i.i.d. samples from the true distribution $p_{\text{data}}$, with the empirical distribution $p^*(x)=\frac1M\sum_j\delta(x-\mu_j)$. After adding noise, the marginal distribution at the $i$-th noise level $\sigma_i$ is a Gaussian mixture $p_i^*(x)=\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_j,\sigma_i^2 I)$. Its empirical score can be explicitly written as:

$$\nabla_x\log p_i^*(x) = -\sum_{j=1}^M w_{ij}(x)\,\frac{x-\mu_j}{\sigma_i^2},\qquad w_{ij}(x)=\frac{\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_j,\sigma_i^2 I)}{\sum_{l}\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_l,\sigma_i^2 I)}.$$

The score is a weighted average of "directions pointing toward training centers $\mu_j$." The weight $w_{ij}$ is a softmax representing the probability that $x$ was generated by the Gaussian component centered at $\mu_j$. The methodology revolves around the sharpness of these softmax weights through three key designs: **① Locating the root of memorization via the softmax structure** (Diagnosis), **② Noise Unconditioning**, and **③ Temperature Smoothing** (The Treatments).

### Key Designs

**1. Softmax Structure of Empirical Score as the Root of Memorization (Diagnosis)**

To treat memorization, its origin must be clarified. High-dimensional geometry provides intuition: in $\mathbb{R}^d$, the probability mass of a single Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu_j,\sigma_i^2 I)$ concentrates on a thin shell with radius $\sigma_i\sqrt d$ and thickness approximately $3\sqrt2\,\sigma_i$. A sampling point $x$ can be viewed as falling within a shell of a certain noise level. The weight is formulated as a softmax: $w_{ij}(x)=\mathrm{Softmax}_{j,i}\big(f(x,\mu_j,\sigma_i)\big)$, where $f(x,\mu_j,\sigma_i)=-(d-2)\ln\sigma_i-\frac{\|x-\mu_j\|^2}{2\sigma_i^2}$.

The authors prove two dominant properties: **$\sigma$-dominance**—for a fixed center $\mu_j$ and position $x$, there exists an "optimal noise level" $\sigma_j^*$ whose component monopolizes the weight, and $f(\sigma)$ is extremely sensitive to deviations from $\sigma_j^*$; **$\mu$-dominance**—weights decay exponentially with the distance between the center and $x$. As $\sigma$ decreases in the late sampling stages, the dominance of a **single nearest training point $\mu_j$** becomes absolute, causing the denoising steps to collapse toward $\mu_j$. Moreover, weights are extremely sharp in shell overlap regions, where minor perturbations in $x$ cause the dominance to jump between centers.

To quantify generalization vs. memorization, the **Sampling Expansion Ratio** $\gamma_{ex}:=\frac{\|y'-x'\|}{\|y-x\|}$ is defined. For two nearest training points $\mu_0, \mu_1$ near $x$, a perturbation $y$ is taken along $(\mu_0-\mu_1)$. Memorization leads to an unbounded ratio (strong local expansion), while a **bounded** ratio implies the sampling mapping is locally non-expansive, preserving local connectivity as a proxy for generalization. Under the empirical score, $\gamma_{ex}\to \infty$ as $d\to \infty$. NNs exhibit a much smaller $\gamma_{ex}$, indicating they **implicitly smooth the weights**.

**2. Noise Unconditioning: Allowing Training Points to Select Optimal Shells**

Standard diffusion fixes the noise level $\sigma$ at each sampling stage, but the sampling point $x$ may not fall on the "optimal shells" of most training points, suppressing their weights and favoring single-point dominance. The proposed approach **removes the noise conditioning from the score network**, $s_\theta(x,t)\to s_\theta(x)$. Under Variance Exploding (VE) SDE, marginal distributions across all noise levels are unified into a single $M\times N$ Gaussian mixture:

$$p_{MN}(x)=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{j=1}^M\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda(\sigma_i)\,\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_j,\sigma_i^2 I),\qquad \lambda(\sigma_i)=\sigma_i^2.$$

This allows each training point $\mu_j$ to adaptively find its optimal shell $\sigma_j^*$ to contribute to the score at a given $x$. Sampling is reformulated as gradient ascent on a **time-independent** objective $\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{\eta}{2}\nabla_x\log p_{MN}(x)$. The training loss simply removes the conditioning from the standard NCSN loss: $\mathcal{L}_u=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i,\mu,x}\big[\frac{\sigma_i^2}{2}\|s_\theta(x)+\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma_i^2}\|^2\big]$.

**3. Temperature Smoothing: Adjustable Temperature for Softmax Weights**

Since the weights are inherently softmax-based, the most natural smoothing method is introducing temperature. A temperature $T_i$ is assigned to each shell $i$, and the temperature-adjusted weight is defined as:

$$w_j^*(x;T)=\frac{\exp\!\big(f(x,\mu_j,\sigma_j^*)/T_j^*\big)}{\sum_{l=1}^M \exp\!\big(f(x,\mu_l,\sigma_l^*)/T_l^*\big)}.$$

Increasing $T_i$ reduces the dominance ratio $a$ and decreases $\gamma_{ex}$, fostering exploration of the local manifold. To prevent samples from deviating too far from the image manifold, temperature is only enabled when $\sigma_i \le \sigma_{\text{collapse}}$. The score is approximated using top-$K$ nearest training samples:

$$\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i\sim p_\sigma}\Big[\,\mathcal{L}_u\ \text{if}\ \sigma_i>\sigma_{\text{collapse}};\quad \mathcal{L}_T\ \text{if}\ \sigma_i\le\sigma_{\text{collapse}}\,\Big],$$

where $T_i=\max\!\big(\sigma_{\text{collapse}}/\sigma_i,\,1\big)$.

### Loss & Training
- **Noise Unconditioning Loss $\mathcal{L}_u$**: Standard NCSN loss without noise input, equivalent to explicit score matching on the unified Gaussian mixture $p_{MN}$.
- **Temperature Loss $\mathcal{L}_T$**: Approximated via top-$K$ nearest neighbors for the smoothed score, activated only when $\sigma_i \le \sigma_{\text{collapse}}$.
- **Strategy**: Uses VE-SDE noise scheduling. The unconditioned version uses the same architecture as the baseline but removes time embedding layers.

## Key Experimental Results

Experiments were conducted on CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet 64×64, and CelebA-HQ using VE-SDE as the baseline. A custom dataset of 1,000 house cats and 200 caracals was used to specifically evaluate generalization. Quality was measured via the FID between generated samples and both training ($\text{FID}_{tr}$) and test ($\text{FID}_{test}$) sets.

### Main Results: FID vs. Generalization Trade-off

| Dataset / Sampler | Method | FID(G,T_tr) | FID(G,T_test) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CIFAR-10, SDE 1K NFE | Conditioning (Baseline) | 6.49 | 6.56 |
| CIFAR-10, SDE 1K NFE | Unconditioning | 7.33 | 7.34 |
| CIFAR-10, SDE 1K NFE | $T_i{=}7/\sigma_i, K{=}100$ (Feature KNN) | **7.96** | 7.98 |
| CelebA 64×64, SDE 1K NFE | Conditioning (Baseline) | 7.25 | 7.81 |
| CelebA 64×64, SDE 1K NFE | Unconditioning | 7.07 | 7.34 |
| CelebA 64×64, SDE 1K NFE | $T_i{=}10/\sigma_i, K{=}100$ (Feature KNN) | **8.40** | 8.19 |

Key Insight: Unconditioning performs comparably to or better than the baseline on CelebA. Temperature smoothing slightly increases FID but significantly enhances visual generalization. Transitioning from pixel-space KNN to feature-space (ResNet-18) KNN stabilizes quality, as the manifold curvature is lower in feature space.

### Key Findings
- **Memorization ≈ Sharp Score Weights**: Smoothing the weights prevents sampling from collapsing into single training points.
- **Feature-Space KNN Supremacy**: Feature-space smoothing consistently outperforms pixel-space smoothing by allowing stronger smoothing without leaving the data manifold.
- **Unconditioning & Step Size**: Unconditioned scores are well-defined across the support of $p_{MN}$, avoiding error accumulation and potentially reducing NFE.
- **NN Behavior**: The $\gamma_{ex}$ of learned NNs mirrors that of temperature-smoothed empirical scores, confirming that NNs generalize by implicitly smoothing weights.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Memorization as a Softmax Temperature Problem**: Redefining memorization as "sharp weights leading to single-point dominance" provides a clean causal chain and intuitive solutions.
- **Unification of Priors**: Noise unconditioning merges marginal distributions into a single $p_{MN}$, unifying sampling with optimization.
- **Generalization Proxy**: The expansion ratio $\gamma_{ex}$ serves as a transferable diagnostic tool for analyzing memorization in generative models.
- **Feature-Space Smoothing**: Smoothing in low-curvature feature spaces is a robust strategy for local neighborhood-based score modifications.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The temperature-based version is a smoothed proxy rather than a strict score for $p_{MN}$, leaving a gap between theory and implementation.
- Noise level mismatch exists between unconditioned scores and ODE samplers, requiring correction or nearest-neighbor searches which are computationally expensive.
- Temperature and $K$ are sensitive hyperparameters.
- Future work may extend this to latent diffusion models to enhance generalization at reduced computational costs.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Empirical Score / Generalization Dichotomy**: Replaces the "failure to memorize" explanation with a mechanistic one: generalization arises from weight smoothing and local manifold determination.
- **vs. Standard NCSN / VE-SDE**: Maintains the core architecture but reformulates sampling as gradient ascent on a unified mixture.
- **vs. Data-Side Mitigation**: Unlike data-centric approaches (deduplication), this work addresses the underlying mechanism of score function sharpness.
