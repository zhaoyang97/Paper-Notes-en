# HPSU: A Benchmark for Human-Level Perception in Real-World Spoken Speech Understanding

**Conference**: AAAI2026  
**arXiv**: [2511.23178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23178)  
**Code**: [Ichen12/HPSU-Benchmark](https://github.com/Ichen12/HPSU-Benchmark)  
**Area**: Audio & Speech  
**Keywords**: Speech LLM, benchmark, spoken language understanding, emotion reasoning, adversarial evaluation

## TL;DR
This work proposes the HPSU benchmark, which includes over 20,000 expert-annotated Chinese and English samples across 16 tasks. It systematically evaluates the deep perception and reasoning capabilities of Speech LLMs in real-world spoken scenarios, revealing a significant gap between the strongest model (Gemini 2.5 Pro, 62.6%) and human performance (87.3%).

## Background & Motivation
- Human auditory understanding involves multi-dimensional information beyond transcription, including speaker attributes, latent intentions, and complex emotions. While existing Speech LLMs have made significant progress in fundamental tasks like ASR and SER, whether they possess **human-level deep perception** remains under-explored.
- Existing benchmarks suffer from three major limitations: (1) they mostly focus on coarse-grained tasks or text-based reasoning, ignoring deep perception; (2) they rely on non-interactive data or synthetic speech, separating evaluation from real-world scenarios; (3) most are limited to a single language, preventing cross-lingual analysis.
- There is a lack of a unified benchmark to comprehensively evaluate the full spectrum of Speech LLM capabilities, from basic perception to complex reasoning.

## Core Problem
Can current Speech LLMs understand high-order semantics such as implied intentions, emotional shifts, and conversational subtexts in real-world spoken language like humans do? How can a comprehensive, high-quality, and bilingual benchmark be constructed to quantify this gap?

## Method

### Data Construction Pipeline (Three-Stage Semi-Automatic Annotation)
1. **Data Collection and Preprocessing**: High-quality clips are filtered from public audio-visual datasets (such as CelebV-HQ, MAFW, MELD, and MER) and scored using audio quality evaluation tools. Samples falling below the threshold undergo speech enhancement and denoising (ClearerVoice-Studio). For each sample, three modalities are preserved: video, audio, and transcriptions generated by Whisper/FireRedASR. The VCTK and an internal dialect dataset serve as audio-only sources to support accent tasks.
2. **Information Extraction and Cross-Validation**: Transcriptions are first fed into an LLM to infer potential speaker states (emotions, intentions, etc.) as prior knowledge. Then, the priors along with audio/video are fed into corresponding models to extract single-modality representations. A **single-modality cross-validation** strategy (e.g., feeding visual descriptions into the audio model, and vice-versa) is applied to verify cross-modal consistency and logical coherence.
3. **Information Fusion and Expert Verification**: A QWQ reasoning model is utilized for hierarchical fusion, synthesizing the verified information into a three-layered description: the core layer (primary emotion/intention), the detail layer (fine-grained cues like micro-expressions), and the background layer (context). Eighty percent of the data forms the **HPSC dataset** (50,000 speech-description pairs), while the remaining 20% is structured into evaluation triplets by Gemini 2.5 Pro. These triplets are then independently audited by three annotators, retaining only the samples with unanimous consensus (an approval rate of 81.26%) to construct the final HPSU benchmark.

### Task Design (16 Tasks Across 5 Domains)

| Domain | Task | Cognitive Level |
|------|------|----------|
| Social Attributes | Age, Gender, Accent | Basic Perception |
| Emotion Recognition | Primary Emotion, Multi-Emotion, Emotion Intensity | Basic Perception |
| Emotion Reasoning | Emotion Shift (Easy/Hard), Duplicity (Easy/Hard) | Complex Reasoning |
| Non-verbal Behavior | Speaking Style, Visual Behavior, Description Judgment | Complex Reasoning |
| Conversational Context | Intention Inference, Scene Judgment, Subtext Understanding | Complex Reasoning |

- Objective tasks use single-choice questions, the multi-emotion task uses multi-choice, and deep semantics such as subtext use Yes/No judgments.
- **Distractor Generation**: Gemini 2.5 Pro is used to generate graded distractors (similar / middle / opposite) to enhance discriminative power.
- **Adversarial Induction Protocol**: Each task instance contains three prompt variants—standard unbiased, suggestive (containing hints to the correct answer), and misleading (containing misleading cues)—to evaluate robustness.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (13 Models + 3 Baselines)

| Model | English Average | Chinese Average | Note |
|------|----------|----------|------|
| Human | ~84% | ~90% | Overall average 87.3% |
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | Highest | Highest | Overall average 62.6% |
| Qwen2.5-Omni | Second highest | Second highest | Overall average 60.0%, lagging behind Pro by only 2.6% |
| Whisper+GPT-4o | — | — | Comparable to Gemini Pro on Emos/Intent |

### Key Findings
- **Large Human-Machine Gap**: A 24.7 percentage point gap exists between the strongest model and humans. Models approach human performance on basic tasks (gender identification ~94%) but fall far short on high-order reasoning tasks (subtext, tough emotion shift tasks).
- **Small Gap Between Open-source and Closed-source**: Qwen2.5-Omni dominates Gemini 2.5 Pro on certain tasks (e.g., English Emotion Shift Easy, Chinese Visual Behavior), suggesting that training data diversity might be more crucial than whether a model is open or closed-source.
- **Limitations of Cascade Methods**: Whisper+GPT-4o performs poorly on tasks that rely heavily on acoustic details (description judgment, tough emotion shift), demonstrating that the text modality alone cannot replace acoustic information. However, it matches end-to-end models on semantic reasoning tasks (Emos, Intent), reflecting the generalization advantages of the text modality.
- **Vulnerability to Adversarial Induction**: All models are highly sensitive to misleading prompts, whereas humans are almost immune. Models are particularly fragile on highly ambiguous tasks (age, accent, tough duplicity) and open-ended tasks (intention, scene). Native multimodal architectures (Gemini) are more robust than text-LLM fine-tuning solutions.
- **Graded Evaluation Insights**: Models tend to select "Similar" distractors instead of "Opposite" ones, indicating that they understand the general semantics but lack fine-grained differentiation. The barrier is not a complete lack of comprehension, but rather insufficient precision to resolve subtle ambiguities.
- **Cross-Lingual Inconsistency**: The English-Chinese performance gap of models in the same task is far greater than that of humans. For instance, models perform significantly worse in Chinese subtext understanding than in English, possibly because implicit Chinese expressions are more challenging to model.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Comprehensive Task Coverage**: 16 tasks cover the complete cognitive spectrum from basic attribute recognition to subtext understanding, making it the most comprehensive speech understanding benchmark to date.
- **Innovative Annotation Process**: The semi-automatic annotation pipeline combining multimodal cross-validation and hierarchical fusion effectively addresses the high annotation cost of high-level tasks.
- **Rich Evaluation Dimensions**: Graded distractors, adversarial induction protocols, and multilingual comparisons provide diagnostic capabilities far beyond simple accuracy.
- **Balanced Scale and Quality**: All 20,000+ samples are verified by experts, and the 50,000-entry HPSC dataset is simultaneously released for the community.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Data sources are concentrated on movies, TV shows, and social media, lacking coverage of professional scenarios such as meetings, customer service calls, and medical consultations.
- Only English and Chinese are supported, leaving low-resource languages (e.g., Arabic, Hindi) uncovered.
- Adversarial induction is limited to the text prompt level, without exploring adversarial attacks at the audio level (e.g., adding noise, altering pitch).
- The evaluation relies on Gemini 2.5 Pro as an automatic judge, which may introduce systematic bias, particularly representing a conflict of interest when evaluating Gemini itself.
- SFT experiments on the HPSC dataset are only briefly presented in the appendix, lacking an in-depth analysis of different training strategies.
- GPT-4o was excluded due to safety policy refusals on most queries, limiting the completeness of comparisons with top-tier closed-source models.
- The deep understanding capability of models in multi-turn conversation scenarios remains unevaluated.

## Related Work & Insights
- It reveals the training data bias of current Speech LLMs: models are over-optimized for low-level tasks like ASR/SER, while high-level cognitive data is severely lacking. HPSU highlights key directions for the community to address.
- The multimodal cross-validation annotation pipeline can be extended to other tasks requiring fine-grained semantic annotation (e.g., multimodal affective computing, discourse act analysis).
- Adversarial induction experiments show that native-multimodal architectures (such as Gemini) are more robust than text-LLM fine-tuning approaches. This provides architectural guidelines for model design, suggesting that end-to-end multimodal training might be a superior path toward robust speech understanding.
- The graded evaluation framework (True/Similar/Middle/Opposite) is worth promoting in other highly subjective NLP benchmarks, as it reveals model decision patterns better than traditional binary scoring.
- The 50,000 speech-description pairs in HPSC hold potential application value for downstream tasks like controllable speech generation and emotional speech synthesis.
- The strong performance of cascade methods on semantic tasks suggests that future end-to-end models must preserve acoustic details while matching the semantic reasoning of text LLMs.

## Supplementary Notes
- The 13 evaluated models include 11 open-source models (Audio Flamingo 2/3/3.5, Kimi-Audio, Qwen-Audio, Qwen2-Audio, SALMONN, Soundwave, Baichuan-Omni-1.5, Qwen2.5-Omni, Audio-Reasoner) and 2 closed-source models (Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro).
- The human baseline was completed by 10 native speakers per language on 300 hierarchically sampled instances.
- There are 11,580 English samples and 8,786 Chinese samples, totaling over 20,000.
- The paper also releases the HPSC dataset (50,000 samples). SFT experiments show that fine-tuning on HPSC improves model perception and understanding.
