# SURGE: Approximation and Training Free Particle Filter for Diffusion Surrogate

**Conference**: ICML 2026  
**arXiv**: [2605.18745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18745)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Scientific Computing / Data Assimilation / Diffusion Model Inference  
**Keywords**: Data Assimilation, Particle Filter, Diffusion Surrogate Model, Girsanov Transformation, Sequential Monte Carlo  

## TL;DR
SURGE treats the guided sampling of diffusion surrogate models as a biased distribution on path measures. It utilizes the Girsanov formula to compute importance weights for SMC resampling, resulting in an approximation-free data assimilation filter for diffusion surrogates without retraining or approximating the Doob $h$-transform. It consistently outperforms BPF, EnKF, SDA, and FlowDAS on Lorenz, Navier-Stokes, and SEVIR weather forecasting tasks.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Data Assimilation (DA) fuses "model forecasts" with "sparse, noisy observations" to estimate the posterior of system states, serving as a core task in fields like meteorology, oceanography, and seismology. Recently, using diffusion models as "digital twins" for conditional transition density $p(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)$ surrogates (e.g., FlowDAS, SDA) has become popular due to their ability to represent high-dimensional complex distributions and generate realistic stochastic evolutions.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: When new observations $y_{t+1}$ arrive, the mainstream approach involves adding the observation likelihood as a guidance term $\lambda(s)\nabla_x \log p(y_{t+1}\mid x_s)$ into the reverse SDE drift to "push" samples toward observation-consistent states. However, this guidance only yields the exact posterior when $G=\log h$ exactly matches the Doob $h$-transform. Solving for the exact $h$-transform requires solving a high-dimensional backward Kolmogorov equation, which is nearly impossible. Practical guidance terms are typically approximate or heuristic, introducing systematic bias.

**Key Challenge**: The flexibility of diffusion surrogates comes at the cost of an intractable posterior score $\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t\mid y_{1:t})$ (requiring marginalization over the diffusion forward kernel). It appears difficult to retain the modeling power of diffusion while achieving approximation-free posterior sampling during inference. Furthermore, directly applying classical particle filters suffers from the "curse of dimensionality," leading to severe weight degeneracy in high-dimensional spaces.

**Goal**: (1) Correct the bias generated by guided sampling back to the true posterior without modifying pre-trained diffusion surrogates; (2) Maintain numerical stability under high-dimensional, long-sequence scenarios to mitigate particle degeneracy; (3) Provide a plug-and-play inference-stage solution compatible with existing backbones like FlowDAS and SDA.

**Key Insight**: The authors observe that diffusion sampling itself generates a **trajectory** rather than a single point over an internal time interval $s\in[t,t+1]$. By viewing both "guided diffusion" and "unguided diffusion + observation tilting" as path measures $\mathbb{P}^G$ and $\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t+1})$, the Radon–Nikodym derivative between them can be expressed in closed form using the Girsanov theorem—providing the necessary bridge to construct unbiased importance weights.

**Core Idea**: The SMC resampling is lifted from "terminal particles" to "entire diffusion paths." Weights are derived directly from the Girsanov measure transformation + observation likelihood tilting, combined with "time-distributed likelihoods" to control weight variance, resulting in an approximation-free particle filter.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
SURGE is an inference-stage filtering loop. The outer loop handles assimilation steps across physical time $t=0\to T-1$, while the inner loop divides the diffusion sampling time $s\in[t,t+1]$ into $K$ sub-windows $[s_k, s_{k+1}]$. Within each inner window, $N$ particle trajectories are maintained:
1.  **Propagation**: Use the guided SDE (Eq. 4) to advance each particle by $\Delta s$, where the drift term = diffusion network $v_\theta$ + likelihood guidance $\Sigma(s)\nabla_x G(x_s, s\mid y_{t+1})$, and the diffusion term is $\Sigma^{1/2}(s)\mathrm{d}W_s$.
2.  **Weighting**: Calculate the importance weight increment $\beta_t^{(i)}$ for each trajectory segment using the Girsanov formula + progressive likelihood, and multiply it into $w_{t+s_k}^{(i)}$.
3.  **Resampling**: Normalize weights. If the effective sample size $\widehat{N}_{\text{eff}} = 1/\sum_i (\tilde w^{(i)})^2 < c$, perform Categorical resampling and reset weights to $1/N$.

At the end of each physical step, the weighted empirical measure $\sum_i \tilde w_{t+1}^{(i)} \delta_{x_{t+1}^{G,(i)}}$ serves as an approximation-free estimate of the posterior $p(x_{t+1}\mid y_{1:t+1})$.

Inputs: Pre-trained diffusion surrogate $v_\theta$, initial particle set, observation sequence $\{y_t\}$, guidance potential $G$, variance schedule $\Sigma(s)$, sub-steps $K$, resampling threshold $c$. Output: Posterior particle trajectories $\{x_t^{(i)}\}$. No new parameters are trained.

### Key Designs

1.  **Girsanov Importance Weights on Path Measures**:
    - **Function**: Maps the biased path distribution $\mathbb{P}^G$ generated by guided diffusion to the true posterior path distribution $\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t+1})$ via weights, yielding unbiased posterior particle representations.
    - **Mechanism**: Using the Radon–Nikodym chain rule $\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t+1})}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^G(\cdot\mid y_{1:t+1})} = \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t+1})}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t})} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid y_{1:t})}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^G(\cdot\mid y_{1:t})}$, the weight is decomposed: the first part $\propto p(y_{t+1}\mid x_{t+1})$ is the classical observation tilting; the second part, derived via Girsanov, is $\exp\bigl(-\int_0^1 \Sigma^{1/2}(s)\nabla_x G \cdot \mathrm{d}W_s - \tfrac12\int_0^1 \|\Sigma^{1/2}(s)\nabla_x G\|^2 \mathrm{d}s\bigr)$, correcting the drift bias introduced by the guidance term.
    - **Design Motivation**: Traditional particle filters only weight at the terminal state $x_{t+1}$ using $p(y_{t+1}\mid x_{t+1})$, failing to correct path biases introduced by approximate guidance during the sampling process. By lifting to path space, the guidance potential $G$ no longer needs to equal the Doob $h$-transform; any heuristic guidance can be automatically corrected.

2.  **Time-distributed Observation Likelihood**:
    - **Function**: Distributes the total observation log-likelihood $\log p(y_{t+1}\mid x_{t+1})$ across $K$ sub-windows along the internal time $s\in[0,1]$, preventing extreme weight values and particle degeneracy.
    - **Mechanism**: Replaces the terminal likelihood in the weight formula (Eq. 6) with a progressive term $s_{k+1}\log p(y_{s_{k+1}}\mid x_{s_{k+1}}^{(i),G}) - s_k\log p(y_{s_k}\mid x_{s_k}^{(i),G})$. When $\Delta s$ is small, the likelihood difference between adjacent steps is small, leading to smooth weight changes and controllable resampling variance. The cumulative effect across sub-windows is mathematically equivalent to $\log p(y_{t+1}\mid x_{t+1})$ (telescoping sum).
    - **Design Motivation**: In high-dimensional spaces, coefficients $\propto p(y\mid x)$ in classical particle filters often collapse to zero for almost all particles. Spreading the likelihood across sub-windows ensures the "cost" of importance sampling is paid in small increments, keeping the effective sample size above threshold $c$, which is an essential stabilization technique for high-dimensional SMC.

3.  **Inference-time Plugin Wrapper**:
    - **Function**: Serves as an inference-time wrapper for diffusion DA methods like FlowDAS and SDA, requiring zero additional training and zero model modifications.
    - **Mechanism**: Apart from $v_\theta$ (from any pre-trained diffusion surrogate) and guidance $G$ (standard choices from backbones, e.g., $\Sigma(s)\nabla_x G = \lambda(s)\nabla_x \log p(y\mid x_s)$), the algorithm only requires Euler–Maruyama discretization of the SDE, weight accumulation, and ESS-triggered resampling. It utilizes the backbone's own variance schedule $\Sigma$ and architecture.
    - **Design Motivation**: Training diffusion surrogates is extremely expensive (often requiring days of GPU time for weather/fluid fields). Existing backbones represent significant investment; allowing correction to occur as SMC during inference enables immediate benefits from backbone upgrades while avoiding compatibility issues.

### Loss & Training
SURGE is entirely training-free. Required hyperparameters: number of particles $N$ (experimentally $N=20$), internal time sub-steps $K$, resampling threshold $c$ (typically $N/2$), and guidance strength $\lambda(s)$ inherited from the backbone. The computational overhead primarily stems from running $N$ parallel diffusion sampling trajectories per physical step, roughly a factor of $\sim N$ increase over a single sample.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results
Evaluations cover three systems: low-dimensional chaotic ODE (Lorenz 63), high-dimensional SPDE (forced incompressible Navier-Stokes), and real atmospheric data (SEVIR VIL weather forecasting), all using FlowDAS as the SOTA baseline.

| Dataset | Metric | FlowDAS | + SURGE | Gain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lorenz 63 (15 steps, only $\arctan(x)$ obs) | RMSE ↓ | 0.0545 | **0.0502** | -7.9% |
| Lorenz 63 | $W_1$ ↓ | 0.0388 | **0.0363** | -6.4% |
| NS Super-resolution ($8^2\to128^2$) | KES-RE ↓ | 0.401 | **0.317** | -20.9% |
| NS Super-resolution | RMSE ↓ | 1.018 | **0.851** | -16.4% |
| NS Sparse Recovery (5%→100%) | KES-RE ↓ | 0.543 | **0.278** | -48.8% |
| SEVIR Weather (10% obs) | RMSE ↓ | 0.0657 | **0.0513** | -21.9% |
| SEVIR Weather | CSI($\tau_{40}$) ↑ | 0.4044 | **0.4541** | +12.3% |

In the NS sparse recovery task, the SDA backbone outperformed FlowDAS (KES-RE 0.231 vs 0.543), but SDA+SURGE further reduced it to **0.207**, indicating that SURGE's improvements are orthogonal to the choice of backbone.

### Ablation Study
The paper does not provide a separate "module-by-module" ablation but validates the value of components through a cross-comparison between SDA and FlowDAS backbones.

| Configuration | Lorenz RMSE | NS Super-res KES-RE | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BPF (Classical PF $N=20$) | 0.0625 | 0.490 | Terminal weights only; severe high-dim degeneracy |
| EnKF | 0.0624 | 0.551 | Gaussian approximation; distortion in non-linear scenes |
| SDA (Score-based DA) | 0.0589 | 0.473 | Guidance but no SMC correction |
| SDA + SURGE | 0.0555 | 0.417 | SURGE added to the same backbone |
| FlowDAS | 0.0545 | 0.401 | Flow matching guidance |
| FlowDAS + SURGE | **0.0502** | **0.317** | Full proposed method |

### Key Findings
- Consistent improvements across two different backbones (SDA, FlowDAS) strongly suggest the gains arise from "path-space SMC correction" rather than specific backbone tuning.
- Improvements are most significant in extremely sparse scenarios (5% NS obs, 10% SEVIR obs, Lorenz with only 1D $\arctan(x)$), as less accurate guidance provides more room for SURGE's correction.
- BPF performs significantly worse (RMSE 1.143) than EnKF (0.847) on high-dimensional NS, replicating the classical "particle filter failure in high dimensions." By embedding path-space SMC into the diffusion internal time, SURGE performs continuous small-step resampling, successfully bypassing this pitfall.
- Under long-term autoregressive rollout (100 steps for NS), SURGE's energy spectrum error curve stays close to the ground truth, whereas FlowDAS diverges, showing that Girsanov correction stabilizes long-range trajectories.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Path space is the correct abstraction level**: Previous works treated diffusion guidance bias as a "sampling error"; this paper views it as a "Radon–Nikodym derivative between two path measures," yielding closed-form weights— a elegant application of stochastic analysis to generative model inference.
- **Girsanov weights transform "approximate guidance" into an "unbiased estimator"**: Any heuristic guidance can be used as $G$; weights automatically counteract its bias. This converts the intractable problem of "implementing the exact Doob $h$-transform" into a tractable one of "estimating expectations with SMC," similar to the relationship between variational inference and importance sampling, but localized on paths.
- **Time-distributed likelihood is a key accelerator for high-dimensional SMC**: Breaking a large terminal log-likelihood jump into $K$ telescoping small steps is essentially a manifestation of Annealed Importance Sampling (AIS) within diffusion internal time. This can be generalized to any task requiring "interjecting observation signals into generative trajectories" (e.g., conditional video generation, constrained MD).

## Limitations & Future Work
- Computational cost scales linearly with particle count $N$. The experiments used $N=20$; whether ESS can be maintained without degeneracy for truly large-scale models (DOF $\sim 10^9$) remains unverified.
- Performance is "intrinsically linked to the base model's quality"—if the backbone's diffusion surrogate is physically flawed, SURGE can only approach the backbone's upper bound and cannot compensate for fundamental model deficiencies.
- The gradient of the guidance potential $\nabla_x G$ must still be computable and well-defined, which is challenging for black-box observation operators; model-gradient approximations or score networks could be considered.
- A full "time-distributed vs. terminal likelihood" ablation is missing, leaving the stability gains across different $K$ unquantified.
- Stress tests for very small $N$ (e.g., $N=4$) or very long-range steps (e.g., $T>1000$) are currently lacking.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs FlowDAS (Chen et al., 2025b)**: FlowDAS uses stochastic interpolant guidance but results in a biased terminal distribution. By placing FlowDAS's guidance term as $G$ inside an SMC wrapper, SURGE acts as its unbiased counterpart. Gains in NS and SEVIR confirm this relationship.
- **vs $h$-transform learning methods (e.g., DEFT)**: Those methods train networks to approximate the Doob $h$-function, which remains an approximation and incurs training costs. SURGE requires zero training and relies solely on guidance + resampling.
- **vs Classical BPF / EnKF**: BPF weights in terminal space and suffers in high dimensions; EnKF sacrifices non-linearity for Gaussian approximations. SURGE preserves the non-linear diffusion prior while avoiding the curse of dimensionality through path distribution.
- **Insights**: The combination of path measures + Girsanov + SMC can be extended to any task requiring "guided generation → unbiased posterior"—such as CFG correction in conditional text/image/video generation, policy correction sampling in RL, or molecular conformation generation under constraints.
