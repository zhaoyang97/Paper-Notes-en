# Generating Diverse Training Samples for Relation Extraction with Large Language Models

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.23108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23108)  
**Code**: [github.com/Lzx-ZBC](https://github.com/Lzx-ZBC)  
**Area**: NLP understanding  
**Keywords**: Relation Extraction, Data Augmentation, LLM-generated training data, DPO fine-tuning, Diversity  

## TL;DR

This paper investigates how to use LLMs to generate high-quality and diverse training samples for relation extraction (RE). It proposes an in-context learning (ICL) based one-by-one generation strategy and a DPO-based diversity fine-tuning method. The generated training data effectively improves the performance of few-shot RE models.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Relation extraction (RE) is a core task in information extraction, aiming to identify relationship types between entity pairs in text. PLM-based RE models (such as KnowPrompt and RetrievalRE) perform well when sufficient annotated data is available, but the scarcity of labeled data is a widespread issue in practical applications.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: When directly using LLMs to generate RE training samples, the generated results exhibit **high structural similarity**—LLMs tend to use a limited set of verb phrases and almost identical sentence structures to express relations between entities. For example, for the relation "died in country," LLMs repeatedly generate patterns like "died in [Country]".

**Key Challenge**: There is a contradiction between the powerful generation capabilities of LLMs and the low diversity of their generated results. If the generated training samples are highly similar, it not only wastes computational resources but also limits the generalization ability of the trained RE model.

**Goal**: To effectively improve the diversity of RE training samples generated by LLMs while ensuring correctness.

**Key Insight**: A two-pronged approach—first improving generation diversity through an ICL strategy (one-by-one generation + diversity instructions), and then using DPO fine-tuning to make the LLM internalize the preference for "diverse generation".

**Core Idea**: By constructing preference data where "similar/incorrect samples represent negative preferences, and ground-truth annotations represent positive preferences," the LLM is fine-tuned with DPO to simultaneously consider both diversity and correctness when generating RE training samples.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The proposed method consists of two parts:
1. **ICL Strategy**: Designing a prompt containing three modules: Task Description, Relation Explanation, and Sample Demonstration, supporting both All-At-Once (AAO) and One-By-One (OBO) generation modes.
2. **DPO Fine-tuning**: Constructing preference data pairs to optimize the LLM's generation strategy using DPO.

### Key Designs

**One-by-One Generation Strategy (OBO)**: Instead of generating all samples at once, the LLM is prompted to generate only one training sample at a time, which is then added to the Sample Demonstration Module in the prompt. During the next generation step, the LLM can see all previously generated samples and is instructed to generate a new sample that is "as different as possible" from existing ones. This incremental strategy ensures greater diversity compared to generating all samples at once (AAO).

**DPO Fine-tuning Objective**: Given a preference dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_{i,1}, y_{i,2})\}$, the objective optimizes:

$$\max_{\pi} \Sigma_i \log \sigma\left(\frac{1}{\beta} \log \frac{\pi(y_{i,1}|x_i)}{\pi(y_{i,2}|x_i)}\right)$$

where $y_{i,1}$ represents the preferred data (randomly sampled from the ground-truth annotation set) and $y_{i,2}$ represents the dispreferred data.

**Three Ways to Construct Dispreferred Data**:
1. **Incorrect Annotation**: Taking samples from other relation types and changing their labels to the target relation—constructing samples with incorrect annotations.
2. **High Similarity**: Taking demonstration samples from the prompt, replacing head/tail entities, and slightly modifying the context—constructing overly similar samples.
3. **Direct Copying**: Being identical to the demonstration samples—constructing completely duplicate samples.

**Simulating the OBO Process**: In the DPO training data, the number of samples in the Sample Demonstration Module increases step-by-step. The positive output of the previous training data is placed into the prompt of the next one to simulate the actual one-by-one generation process.

### Loss & Training

- Base LLM: LLaMA2-7b-Chat, fine-tuned using LoRA for DPO.
- Hyperparameters: temperature=0.4, top_p=0.9, top_k=20, repetition_penalty=1.15.
- DPO Fine-tuning: truncation length=1024, lr=5e-5, batch size=4, epoch=20.
- **Key Cheat-Prevention Design**: The relation categories are split into two halves: one half is used for DPO fine-tuning, and the other is used for generating samples. This is swapped to cover all categories, preventing leakage.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Hybrid-OBO, KnowPrompt, Micro F1%)

| Method | TACRED K=8 | TACRED K=16 | Re-TACRED K=8 | SemEval K=8 |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Pure Human Annotation (KnowPrompt) | 22.07 | 30.00 | 44.77 | 61.39 |
| Pure LLM-generated (Ours pure) | 22.48 | 27.99 | 34.77 | 42.02 |
| **Hybrid-OBO** | **33.48** | **34.93** | **57.41** | **67.92** |
| **Hybrid-AAO** | **34.30** | **35.56** | **56.01** | **70.32** |
| Direct LLM for RE | 21.17 | — | 33.30 | 25.55 |

### Ablation Study (KnowPrompt, Micro F1%)

| Variant | TACRED K=8 | TACRED K=32 | Re-TACRED K=32 | SemEval K=32 |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| OBO + DPO | 22.48 | 30.41 | 50.72 | 47.70 |
| OBO (w/o DPO) | 18.35 | 23.70 | 40.45 | 30.03 |
| OBO (w/o DPO, w/o diversity instructions) | 21.86 | 25.66 | 32.74 | 25.88 |
| AAO + DPO | 21.86 | 26.39 | 46.50 | 56.34 |
| AAO (w/o DPO) | 18.97 | 36.04 | 36.66 | 49.58 |

### Key Findings

1. **Hybrid Training is Optimal**: Mixing LLM-generated samples with human-annotated samples outperforms using either alone under most settings, elevating TACRED K=8 from 22.07 to 34.30.
2. **Training Non-LLM Models > Direct LLM for RE**: Ours (pure) outperforms Direct-RE across all four datasets, indicating that using LLMs to generate training data for training smaller models is more effective than direct LLM inference.
3. **DPO Fine-Tuning is Effective**: Under most OBO configurations, DPO fine-tuning significantly enhances the quality of the generated samples.
4. **Diversity Instructions are Effective**: The instruction "Please make samples as different as possible" reduces the average cosine similarity by 0.051.
5. **Fixed Number of Demonstrations is Harmful instead**: When demonstrations are replaced, the LLM has a high probability of generating content similar to the replaced samples.
6. **There is an Upper Limit on the Number of Generated Samples**: Performance saturates at around $K \approx 32$, and further increases yield no significant improvements.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Identified and Addressed a Practical Issue**: The low diversity in training data generated by LLMs is a widely overlooked yet highly impactful issue.
- **DPO for Quality Control in Data Generation**: Creatively transferring DPO from the alignment domain to data generation quality control, using automatically constructed preference pairs instead of human annotations.
- **"Training Small Models is Better than Using Large Models Directly"**: This finding has important practical implications for deployment, reducing the cost of continuous LLM APIs.
- **Leak-Prevention Design**: Splitting the relation categories for DPO training and generation avoids information leakage.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Experiments were conducted only on LLaMA2-7b, without evaluating larger or newer LLMs (such as LLaMA3, GPT-4, etc.).
- The computational overhead of DPO fine-tuning was not reported in detail.
- The marginal utility of generated samples diminishes as the number of human-annotated samples increases ($K=32$).
- More complex relation types (e.g., cross-sentence relations, nested relations) were not explored.
- The upper limit of generated sample diversity is constrained by the pre-training corpus of the LLM, saturating at $K \approx 32$.

## Related Work & Insights

- Self-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023) pioneered the direction of using LLMs to generate instruction data, but did not focus on diversity issues.
- The data generation method of Xu et al. (2023) directly compares against the base, with the proposed method demonstrating advantages in most settings.
- DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) was originally used for summarization and dialogue; this paper demonstrates its novel application in data augmentation.
- Insight: The "Imitation Behavior" of LLMs is a phenomenon that warrants in-depth investigation.
