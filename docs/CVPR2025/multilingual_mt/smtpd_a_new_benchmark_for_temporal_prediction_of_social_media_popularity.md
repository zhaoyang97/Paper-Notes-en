# SMTPD: A New Benchmark for Temporal Prediction of Social Media Popularity

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.04446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04446)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/zhuwei321/SMTPD](https://github.com/zhuwei321/SMTPD)  
**Area**: Multilingual Translation  
**Keywords**: Social Media Popularity Prediction, Temporal Prediction, Multi-Modal Features, Multilingual, Benchmark Dataset

## TL;DR

Constructs SMTPD, the first time-aligned temporal prediction benchmark for social media popularity (282K YouTube samples with 30-day continuous observations), proposes a baseline framework based on multi-modal feature extraction and LSTM temporal regression, and reveals that early popularity (EP) is key to accurately predicting subsequent popularity.

## Background & Motivation

Social media popularity prediction is of significant application value for content optimization, digital marketing, and online advertising. Existing datasets (e.g., SMPD, TPIC17) suffer from three major limitations:
1. **Lack of temporal alignment**: Post publication times vary, leading to inconsistent prediction points. Since popularity distribution changes over time (exhibiting decay patterns), unaligned times make accurate prediction difficult.
2. **Insufficient multi-modal data**: Certain datasets contain only a single modality.
3. **Limited language diversity**: Most datasets only cover English, failing to adapt to international social media platforms.

Furthermore, most existing methods perform single-point prediction (predicting popularity at a specific time point), ignoring the temporal dynamics of popularity. SMTPD achieves time-aligned temporal prediction by tracking daily popularity for 30 days starting from the post publication.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The framework consists of two layers: the upper layer is multi-modal feature extraction (features are independently extracted from four modalities: vision, text, numerical, and categorical, then aligned and concatenated via MLP); the lower layer is LSTM temporal regression (the fused features are encoded into the initial state and inputs for each step, and 30 LSTM cells sequentially output the popularity predictions for 30 days).

### Key Designs

1. **Multi-Modal Feature Extraction**:
    - Function: Extract rich features from the multi-modal content of posts.
    - Mechanism:
        - **Vision**: Use an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-101 to extract 2048-dimensional semantic features $f_v = \text{ResNet}(\mathcal{S}(I))$ from cover images; missing covers are replaced with all-zero vectors.
        - **Text**: Encode category, title, tags, description, and userID using BERT-Multilingual to obtain five 768-dimensional vectors. After concatenation, a $5\times5$ convolution is applied to fuse them into a 768-dimensional text feature $f_t$.
        - **Numerical**: Perform log transformation on follower counts to handle long-tail distributions. All numerical metrics (followers, posts, duration, title length, tag count, description length, and EP) are normalized via Z-score and concatenated.
        - **Categorical**: Categorical labels and languages are individually processed via embedding + MLP, followed by element-wise multiplication $f_c = \text{MLP}_1(\text{E}_1(T^{cat})) \odot \text{MLP}_2(\text{E}_2(\text{langid}(T^{tit})))$.
    - Design Motivation: Monolingual models fail in multilingual environments; BERT-Multilingual resolves cross-lingual issues. Categorical features utilize multiplicative fusion of label and language information to capture region-category interactions.

2. **Temporal Popularity Regression**:
    - Function: Perform sequence prediction by leveraging the high correlation between adjacent days' popularity.
    - Mechanism: Fused features $F$ are encoded via a shared MLP into the initial hidden state and cell state of the LSTM, i.e., $h_0 = c_0 = \text{MLP}^{hc}(F)$. Step inputs are generated by independent temporal encoding MLPs $x_s = \text{MLP}_s^x(F)$. For each step, $h_s$ and $c_s$ are concatenated and passed through an MLP + ReLU (to ensure non-negativity) to obtain the predicted value $pre_s = \max(0, \text{MLP}_s^{out}(\text{Concat}(h_s, c_s)))$.
    - Design Motivation: Since both the Pearson Correlation (PC) and Spearman Rank Correlation (SRC) between adjacent days' popularity are high, LSTM is naturally suited for modeling such temporal dependencies. Thirty independent output MLPs allow for distinct mappings at each step.

3. **Composite Gradient Loss (CGL)**:
    - Function: A multi-objective loss that coordinates predicted values, trends, and peak positions.
    - Mechanism: Composed of 5 terms: Smooth L1 Loss (prediction error), first-order derivative difference (trend slope), second-order derivative difference (trend curvature), L1 difference of the one-hot encoded peak position, and Laplacian residual regularization. The loss weights are in a ratio of 1:1:1:1e-6, and weights for the first/second-order derivatives are dynamically adjusted via cosine annealing.
    - Design Motivation: Optimizing only MSE/L1 loss solely minimizes daily absolute values, failing to guarantee correct trend shapes. Incorporating derivative terms constrains trend monotonicity and curvature, while the peak term ensures accurate timing of popularity peaks.

### Loss & Training

$\mathcal{L} = \text{SL}(\hat{P}, P) + \lambda_1 \text{SL}(\hat{P}^{(1)}, P^{(1)}) + \lambda_2 \text{SL}(\hat{P}^{(2)}, P^{(2)}) + \alpha \sum|\delta\text{argmax}(\hat{P}) - \delta\text{argmax}(P)| + \epsilon \cdot \text{LR}$

Adam optimizer, L2 regularization of 1e-3, batch size 64, learning rate of 1e-3 + ReduceLROnPlateau, and 5-fold cross-validation.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Comparison of Multiple Methods on SMTPD, with/without Early Popularity)

| Method | Day7 MAE/SRC | Day14 MAE/SRC | Day30 MAE/SRC |
|------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Ding et al. (w/o EP) | 1.715/0.849 | 1.669/0.846 | 1.592/0.843 |
| Ding et al. (+EP) | 0.715/0.964 | 0.742/0.959 | 0.749/0.931 |
| Lai et al. (w/o EP) | 1.573/0.875 | 1.524/0.872 | 1.495/0.864 |
| Lai et al. (+EP) | 0.725/0.957 | 0.753/0.962 | 0.760/0.957 |
| **Ours (w/o EP)** | 1.673/0.852 | 1.628/0.850 | 1.563/0.848 |
| **Ours (+EP)** | **0.713/0.964** | **0.735/0.959** | **0.732/0.959** |

### Key Findings

- **Early Popularity (EP) is a decisive factor**: Injecting the ground-truth first-day popularity reduces the MAE of all methods by approximately 55-60% and increases SRC by over 10 percentage points.
- **SRC on SMTPD is generally higher than on SMPD**: Temporal alignment eliminates the distribution shifts caused by varying publication times.
- **Necessity of multilingual modeling**: Replacing BERT-Base with BERT-Multilingual yields an 8.3% reduction in MAE.
- **MLP vs. LSTM**: MLP regression (similar to Ding's method) is inferior to LSTM, demonstrating that modeling temporal dependency is highly critical.
- **Popularity trends are highly predictable**: Both PC and SRC between adjacent days within the 30-day window are consistently high.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Novel dataset construction approach**: Continuous daily observations for 30 consecutive days starting from the post publication achieve temporal alignment, which is more practical than "retrospective" collection.
- **Core role of EP is systematically verified**: The strong correlation between EP and subsequent popularity is validated through multi-faceted analyses and experiments.
- **Multilingual coverage**: The 282K samples cover over 90 languages, revealing the popularity bias across different languages.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Video multi-frame information is unexploited (relying only on cover images), leading to a loss of dynamic visual content.
- Deep multi-modal interaction is not fully explored (relying on simple MLP concatenation).
- Using EP as an input requires real-world first-day data, which limits its application in pure content-based prediction scenarios.
- The pretrained feature extractors (ResNet-101 and BERT) are relatively legacy; more advanced models (e.g., ViT, LLMs) were not evaluated.
- Results are based only on the YouTube platform; generalization to other platforms (such as TikTok, Instagram) remains unverified.

## Related Work & Insights

- Szabo et al. previously discovered the significant impact of initial popularity on subsequent stages; this paper re-validates this finding using large-scale data.
- The unaligned distribution issues of existing datasets like SMPD and TPIC17 are explicitly pointed out and addressed for the first time.
- Khosla's popularity score transformation $p = \log_2(v/d + 1)$ effectively maps long-tailed view counts to a well-behaved distribution.
- This benchmark has the potential to transition the paradigm of popularity prediction from single-point prediction to temporal sequence prediction.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐ The primary contributions are the benchmark and analyses, with limited methodological innovation.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The multi-method comparison, ablation study, and EP analysis are comprehensive, though comparisons with additional SOTA baselines are missing.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐ The structure is clear, but some equations are redundant and the baseline method is described in excessive detail.
