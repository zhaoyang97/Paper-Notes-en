# Translate Policy to Language: Flow Matching Generated Rewards for LLM Explanations

**Conference**: ICLR 2026  
**arXiv**: [2502.12530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12530)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / LLM Alignment  
**Keywords**: Policy Explanation, Rectified Flow, Distributional Reward, RLAIF, LLM Interpretability

## TL;DR
A general framework is proposed that utilizes Rectified Flow to generate distributional rewards for training explanation-generating LLMs. By capturing the pluralistic and probabilistic nature of human judgment through Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNF), it is theoretically proven that CNF can effectively recover the true human reward distribution. This method significantly outperforms RLHF/RLAIF baselines on tasks such as SMAC, MMLU, and MathQA.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: As agents like RL and LLMs deeply integrate into daily life, explaining agent policies in natural language has become essential. RLHF/RLAIF has become the mainstream method for aligning LLM behavior, but it faces unique challenges in explanation tasks.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Human judgment of explanations is inherently pluralistic and probabilistic, making the collection of diverse human feedback costly. (2) Existing RLAIF methods use rewards generated by proxy LLMs which contain noisy bias, and there has been no rigorous study on generating distributional rewards that manage proxy errors. (3) Current distributional reward modeling methods (QRM, DPRM, URM) require discretization or assume specific distribution forms.

**Key Challenge**: There is an inevitable bias $W_2(\hat{p}, p) = \sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}|\sigma_r|$ between proxy LLM rewards and the true human reward distribution. Directly using proxy rewards for training leads to suboptimal explanations.

**Goal**: How to generate distributional rewards that accurately reflect pluralistic human judgment to train explanation LLMs without requiring extensive human feedback?

**Key Insight**: Embed Rectified Flow into the LLM architecture as a reward model, utilizing the denoising properties of CNF to restore noisy rewards from proxy LLMs back to a distribution nearing true human rewards.

**Core Idea**: Treat the noise in proxy LLM rewards as Gaussian noise injected during a forward process. Use Rectified Flow to learn the inverse process to recover the true human reward distribution and provide a theoretical error bound.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
This paper addresses how to provide a reward signal that reflects pluralistic human judgment to an "Explanation LLM" without large-scale human feedback collection. The system comprises three roles trained in an alternating fashion. The Explanation LLM $\pi_e(\theta_e)$ receives a context with hidden ground-truth decisions and generates a natural language explanation; $K=3$ independent Proxy LLMs each score the explanation, providing noisy reward samples; the Rectified Flow reward model $\varphi(\theta_\varphi)$ treats these proxy samples as "true human rewards corrupted by Gaussian noise" and learns an inverse denoising flow to restore them to a distribution close to human judgment. In each round, the Flow model is updated with proxy samples first, then the Explanation LLM is updated via PPO using rewards provided by the Flow model, converging over two alternating iterations.

### Key Designs

**1. Embedding Rectified Flow into LLMs as a Reward Model: Making Rewards "Language-Aware"**

Rewards from proxy LLMs are noisy, and explanation quality is highly dependent on linguistic content—different phrasings for the same decision context should receive different rewards. Traditional MLP or U-Net models for reward distributions cannot interpret linguistic cues. This work embeds the Flow model directly onto the LLM: flow variables $\mathbf{z}_t$ and timestep positional encodings $PE(t)$ are projected into a "flow token" via an MLP. This token then uses cross-attention to "query" the hidden states of the decision context and explanation from the LLM. The cross-attention reuses the weight matrices $(W_Q, W_K, W_V)$ from the final layer of the Explanation LLM, inheriting its language understanding capabilities for free. The Flow model is trained with the standard rectified flow objective to regress the straight-line velocity field from $\mathbf{z}_0$ to $\mathbf{z}_1$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Flow}}(\theta_\varphi) = \mathbb{E}\big[\|(\mathbf{z}_1 - \mathbf{z}_0) - \varphi(t, \mathbf{z}_t \mid c_j, y_j^e; \theta_\varphi)\|^2\big]$$

**2. Theorem 1: Proving CNF Can Suppress Proxy Noise via Error Bounds**

Beyond empirical "denoising," the authors provide a theoretical guarantee for how close the recovered rewards are to true human rewards. When using proxy LLM rewards directly, there is an irreducible bias $W_2(\hat{p}, p) = \sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}|\sigma_r|$ (where $|\mathcal{A}|$ is the number of actions/options and $\sigma_r$ is the proxy noise scale). Theorem 1 proves that when the Flow initial distribution and the proxy noise share the same functional form (e.g., Gaussian), the recovered distribution satisfies:

$$W_2(p_{\text{flow}}, p) \leq \varepsilon + L\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}\,|\sigma - \sigma_r|$$

Consequently, the "hard" $\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}|\sigma_r|$ term is replaced by a controllable $L\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}|\sigma - \sigma_r|$ term. By matching the Flow noise scale $\sigma$ to the proxy noise $\sigma_r$, this term is minimized, leaving only the fitting error $\varepsilon$. This transforms the question of "recovering distributions" into a bounded guarantee.

**3. Sentence-level Dense Rewards: Decomposing Sparse Signals**

Providing a single total reward at the end of an explanation results in sparse signals and slow PPO convergence. This is modified to provide rewards sentence-by-sentence: as each sentence is added, the change in the logit of the hidden ground-truth decision is observed. This increment serves as the reward for that sentence—if a sentence increases the model's confidence in the correct decision, it receives positive feedback. This accelerates PPO convergence and identifies which specific part of the explanation is effective.

### Loss & Training
- Flow model uses rejection sampling: retaining only samples where the proxy LLM assigns the highest probability to the ground-truth decision.
- Softmax activation is applied to logits to mitigate the impact of large values.
- Explanation LLM is fine-tuned using PPO + LoRA.
- The Flow model consists of a frozen LLM backbone plus two trainable MLPs ($\varphi_{\text{Emb}}$ and $\varphi_{\text{Proj}}$).
- $K=3$ independent Proxy LLMs are used: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-2B-It.

### Key Findings
- Removing Rectified Flow and using proxy rewards directly results in a 6.9%-12.4% drop in performance, validating Flow denoising.
- The cross-attention mechanism contributes a 3-5% performance Gain, highlighting the importance of linguistic conditioning for reward generation.
- Sentence-level dense rewards provide a 2-3% improvement over sparse rewards.
- In human evaluation, 89.2% of explanations allowed humans to correctly infer decisions, surpassing DPO by 25.7%.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Unity of Theory and Practice**: Theorem 1 provides a rigorous error bound for CNF managing proxy noise, which is fully validated in experiments. This contributes significantly to the theoretical foundation of RLAIF.
- **High Generality**: Works across RL (SMAC, AI2-THOR) and LLM (MMLU, MathQA) tasks without mission-specific engineering.
- **Architectural Innovation**: Embedding Rectified Flow into LLMs via flow tokens and cross-attention is an elegant solution for bridging generative models with language models.
- **Explaining vs. Answering**: Focusing on "explaining policy" rather than "making decisions" fills a critical gap in Explainable AI (XAI).

## Limitations & Future Work
- Relies on 3 proxy LLMs for reward samples, which entails high computational costs.
- The Gaussian noise assumption may not hold in all scenarios, although the appendix discusses different functional forms.
- Validation is limited to multiple-choice or discrete action scenarios; performance on open-ended generation is unknown.
- Future work could explore applying this framework to preference modeling within RLHF.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs QRM/DPRM/URM**: These methods require discretization or restricted distribution forms, while this work uses CNF to model continuous distributions.
- **vs Skywork RLAIF**: Skywork ranks high on RewardBench but performs poorly on explanation tasks (0.692 vs 0.764 on SMAC), indicating the unique challenges of explanation tasks.
- **vs o3-mini**: Even strong reasoning models fall short of this method in explanation tasks, emphasizing the value of task-specific training.
