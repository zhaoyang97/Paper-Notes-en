# Can MLLMs Reason Beyond Language? VisReason: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Vision-Centric Reasoning

**Conference**: ACL2026  
**arXiv**: [2605.25364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25364)  
**Code**: https://github.com/CASIA-IVA-Lab/VisReason  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM / Visual Reasoning / Benchmark  
**Keywords**: Vision-centric reasoning, Multimodal evaluation, Visual grounding, CoT, Capability diagnostics

## TL;DR
VisReason introduces a multimodal benchmark comprising 1,505 daily visual reasoning problems, specifically designed to test whether models can reason directly based on visual evidence. Results show that the strongest models achieve an average accuracy of only 47.5%, significantly lower than the human level of 71.4%, with CoT and larger inference budgets providing limited improvements.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Existing MLLMs are showing increasingly powerful performance on benchmarks such as MMMU, MathVista, science Q&A, and chart reasoning. Many of these results are interpreted as the models possessing complex visual reasoning capabilities.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Many visual reasoning problems can actually be solved after being converted from "image to language." Diagnostic tests using Qwen3-VL-32B revealed that on datasets like MMMU, MMMU-Pro, and MathVista, performance dropped by less than 6.35% when images were replaced with captions generated by Qwen-VL-Max, indicating that a large portion of reasoning in these tasks can be completed within the linguistic space.

**Key Challenge**: Human visual reasoning often relies on direct observation of shapes, layouts, local differences, spatial relationships, and implicit rules, whereas MLLMs might simply compress an image into a text description and then perform linguistic reasoning. Existing benchmarks struggle to distinguish between "capable of visual reasoning" and "capable of reasoning after reading a caption."

**Goal**: Construct a benchmark where visual evidence cannot be adequately replaced by language, evaluate proprietary and open-source MLLMs using a unified protocol, and analyze the impact of model capacity, CoT, inference budgets, and error types on vision-centric reasoning.

**Key Insight**: VisReason focuses on everyday visual scenes rather than STEM knowledge problems. The tasks cover localization, spot-the-difference, pattern counting, 3D space, chessboards, Sudoku, geolocation clues, cue insight, inductive reasoning, and deductive reasoning, requiring models to iteratively verify between perception and reasoning.

**Core Idea**: Use "irreplaceability of visual evidence" as the benchmark design principle to test whether MLLMs truly reason beyond language.

## Method
The core contribution of this paper is the benchmark construction and diagnostic evaluation. The authors first define a taxonomy for vision-centric reasoning, then collect, rewrite, and verify multi-format problems through human annotation, and finally use large-scale model evaluation and capability decomposition to explain the reasons for model failures.

### Overall Architecture
VisReason takes an image and a question as input. The output format varies by task type, including multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended answers, and bounding boxes. During evaluation, multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank answers are extracted using rules for exact matching; open-ended questions are strictly scored by GPT-5-mini; and bounding boxes are matched using IoU and normalized by the number of objects per problem. Final reports include accuracy per category and overall average accuracy.

### Key Designs
1. **Vision-Centric Data Filtering**:
	- **Function**: Ensure that problems must rely on visual evidence and cannot be solved solely through text or external knowledge.
	- **Mechanism**: The authors collected candidates from online visual reasoning problems and Chinese civil service exam question banks, which were then standardized into VQA format by graduate student annotators. Samples that could be answered by looking at the image/text alone, or those relying on external knowledge or having non-unique answers, were filtered out.
	- **Design Motivation**: If a problem can be fully replaced by a caption, it cannot diagnose the model's vision-centric reasoning capability.

2. **Three-Tier Reasoning Taxonomy**:
	- **Function**: Decompose visual reasoning into three levels—Perception, Structure, and Concept—comprising 10 categories and 36 subcategories.
	- **Mechanism**: The Perception layer includes Localized Reasoning, Spot the Difference, and Pattern Counting; the Structure layer includes 3D-Spatial, Board, Sudoku, and Geolocation; the Concept layer includes Cue Insight, Inductive Reasoning, and Deductive Reasoning.
	- **Design Motivation**: Different categories correspond to different bottlenecks. Localization and spot-the-difference primarily test visual grounding, while Sudoku and chessboards test structural state extraction and rule execution. Induction and deduction lean towards abstract reasoning.

3. **Model Diagnostic Protocol**:
	- **Function**: Simultaneously compare model scale, inference budget, CoT, and error types.
	- **Mechanism**: The authors evaluated models including GPT-4o, o4-mini, GPT-5 series, Gemini series, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL (thinking/non-thinking), MiMo-VL, and Keye-VL using a unified zero-shot CoT style prompt. Diagnostics were also conducted through caption replacement, category correlation, error taxonomy, and atomic capability analysis.
	- **Design Motivation**: Average scores only indicate "difficulty." Category and error analysis are required to explain why tasks are difficult and whether scaling or CoT truly compensates for visual grounding deficiencies.

### Loss & Training
This paper does not train models; it focuses on benchmark construction and zero-shot evaluation. During the construction phase, multiple rounds of human verification ensure consistency between images, questions, and answers. During evaluation, all models use prompts corresponding to the four task types and aim to output in the same answer-format. For bounding boxes, predicted and ground-truth boxes are matched using Hungarian matching; an IoU > 0.5 is considered correct and normalized per problem.

### Ablation Study
| Analysis Item | Key Metric | Description |
|--------|----------|------|
| Image to Caption | Existing benchmarks drop < 6.35%, VisReason drops 48.12% | VisReason is more dependent on direct visual evidence |
| Model Capacity Scaling | Performance generally increases with scale in Qwen2.5/Qwen3 series | Capacity helps, but remains far below human levels |
| Inference Budget Scaling | GPT-5-mini shows gains from low to medium budget, saturates at high budget | Test-time compute has diminishing marginal returns |
| Error Types | Perception errors + Reasoning errors accounts for ~90% | The issue is primarily visual parsing, grounding, and hallucination rather than formatting |

### Key Findings
- VisReason clearly distinguishes model capabilities: weak models perform near chance, while even the strongest proprietary models fall far short of humans, indicating it is not a saturated benchmark.
- CoT may help with high-level cue insight, induction, and deduction, but provides minimal help or even causes degradation in tasks dominated by visual evidence, such as spot-the-difference, 3D space, and counting.
- Low scores on bounding-box tasks are not just formatting issues; they require maintaining spatial hypotheses during reasoning, which is harder than simple detection.

## Highlights & Insights
- The most valuable aspect of the paper is the redefinition of "visual reasoning evaluation": a task is not visual reasoning just because it includes an image; it must prove that visual evidence cannot be easily replaced by linguistic descriptions.
- The taxonomy covers daily visual reasoning, which exposes fundamental visual cognitive weaknesses in MLLMs more effectively than math charts or academic knowledge.
- The results provide a strong counter-example to the idea that "more CoT leads to better reasoning": when the bottleneck is visual parsing and evidence localization, linguistic thinking budgets cannot automatically compensate for perceptual defects.

## Limitations & Future Work
- VisReason only covers static images and does not involve video, interactive perception, embodied tasks, or continuous action decision-making; it could be extended to dynamic visual reasoning in the future.
- The data is sourced from online problem sets and exams; although manually filtered, style biases from source materials may persist. Data contamination and benchmark overfitting remain long-term risks.
- Open-ended questions are scored by GPT-5-mini, which, though strict, introduces the subjectivity of a judge model. Future work could include more human review or multi-judge consistency analysis.
- Currently, the focus is on evaluation. In the future, the atomic capabilities of VisReason could be used as a training curriculum, designing specialized data for bottlenecks like correspondence/difference and structured state extraction.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs MMMU / MathVista**: These benchmarks evaluate visual academic knowledge and mathematical reasoning, but some problems retain most information via captions. VisReason explicitly pursues caption-irreplaceability.
- **vs Sudoku-Bench / REBUS / VGRP-Bench**: These tasks focus on specific puzzles or rule-based reasoning, whereas VisReason covers a broader range of daily visual scenarios and answer formats.
- **vs CoT-based MLLM reasoning**: While CoT strengthens linguistic reasoning chains, VisReason's results remind us that multimodal reasoning requires joint training of visual parsing, grounding, and symbolic reasoning, rather than just lengthening textual output.
