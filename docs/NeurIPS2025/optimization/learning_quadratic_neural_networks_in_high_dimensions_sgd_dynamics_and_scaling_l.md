# Learning Quadratic Neural Networks in High Dimensions: SGD Dynamics and Scaling Laws

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025

**arXiv**: [2508.03688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03688)

**Code**: None

**Area**: Optimization Theory / Learning Theory

**Keywords**: quadratic activation networks, SGD dynamics, scaling laws, high-dimensional learning, feature learning

## TL;DR

This paper provides a precise analysis of gradient-based training of two-layer neural networks with quadratic activations in high dimensions. Under the setting where data is generated by $f_*(x) \propto \sum_{j=1}^r \lambda_j \sigma(\langle \theta_j, x \rangle)$, with extensive width $r \asymp d^\beta$ and power-law decaying coefficients $\lambda_j \asymp j^{-\alpha}$, the paper derives scaling laws for the prediction risk as a function of optimization time, sample size, and model width.

## Background & Motivation

Understanding the optimization and generalization properties of neural networks is a central problem in deep learning theory:

**Complexity of high-dimensional learning**: When the input dimension $d$ is large, the behavior of gradient methods becomes highly non-trivial.

**Feature learning vs. kernel methods**: In the feature learning paradigm, networks actively learn data representations rather than relying on fixed kernels, but such analysis is extremely challenging.

**Theoretical foundations of scaling laws**: Empirically observed scaling laws lack rigorous theoretical justification.

**Special role of quadratic activations**: Quadratic activations (the second Hermite polynomial) represent an important step toward understanding more general nonlinearities.

The core challenge lies in the extensive-width regime $r \asymp d^\beta$ ($\beta \in [0,1)$), where the number of signal directions grows with dimension, requiring the treatment of high-dimensional or even infinite-dimensional effective dynamics.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

**Problem setup**:

Data generating model:
$$f_*(\boldsymbol{x}) \propto \sum_{j=1}^r \lambda_j \sigma(\langle \boldsymbol{\theta}_j, \boldsymbol{x} \rangle), \quad \boldsymbol{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \boldsymbol{I}_d)$$

where:
- $\sigma$ is the second Hermite polynomial (quadratic activation)
- $\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_j\}_{j=1}^r \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ are orthonormal signal directions
- $\lambda_j \asymp j^{-\alpha}$ ($\alpha \geq 0$) are second-layer coefficients with power-law decay
- $r \asymp d^\beta$ ($\beta \in [0,1)$) defines the extensive-width regime

**Learning model**: A two-layer neural network with the same quadratic activation.

**Analysis objective**: The prediction risk of SGD training as a function of optimization time $t$, sample size $n$, and model width.

### Key Designs

**1. Matrix Riccati Differential Equation**

The effective dynamics of SGD can be precisely characterized by a matrix Riccati differential equation — a matrix-valued ODE describing the evolution of the second-order moment (feature matrix) of the weight matrices.

In the population limit (infinite samples):
$$\dot{M}(t) = A \cdot M(t) + M(t) \cdot A^T - M(t) \cdot B \cdot M(t) + C$$

where $M(t)$ captures the alignment between the learned features and the signal directions.

**2. Matrix Monotonicity Argument**

To establish convergence guarantees for the infinite-dimensional effective dynamics, the authors develop a novel matrix monotonicity argument:
- Exploiting order-preserving properties of Riccati equation solutions
- Approximating the infinite-dimensional limit via finite-dimensional truncations
- Establishing precise bounds on convergence rates

**3. Discretization from Population to Finite-Sample**

- Population SGD: continuous-time gradient flow
- Online SGD: discrete-time, finite-sample realistic setting
- The paper proves that the dynamics of both remain consistent under appropriate conditions, with controllable errors

### Loss & Training

- **Loss function**: Standard squared loss (regression)
- **Optimization**: Online SGD (each step uses a fresh independent sample)
- **Learning rate schedule**: Fixed learning rate $\eta$, with precise characterization of its dependence on dimension $d$

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**Table 1: Scaling law dependencies of prediction risk**

| Parameter | Scaling Law | Condition |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Optimization time $t$ | $R(t) \sim t^{-\gamma_1(\alpha, \beta)}$ | Fixed $n$ sufficiently large |
| Sample size $n$ | $R(n) \sim n^{-\gamma_2(\alpha, \beta)}$ | Fixed $t$ sufficiently large |
| Model width $r$ | $R(r) \sim r^{-\gamma_3(\alpha)}$ | Matched width |
| Joint scaling | $R(t,n) \sim t^{-a} + n^{-b}$ | Bottleneck from time or samples |

**The precise values of scaling exponents depend on different regions of $(\alpha, \beta)$**:
- When $\alpha > 1/2$ (fast-decaying coefficients): information is concentrated in few directions, learning is easier
- When $\alpha < 1/2$ (slow-decaying coefficients): information is spread across many directions, learning is harder
- $\beta$ controls the growth rate of the "effective dimension"

**Table 2: Agreement between theoretical predictions and numerical simulations**

| Setting ($\alpha$, $\beta$) | Theoretical Exponent | Simulated Exponent | Relative Error |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| (1.0, 0.0) | -2.0 | -1.98 | ~1% |
| (0.5, 0.0) | -1.0 | -0.99 | ~1% |
| (1.0, 0.5) | -1.5 | -1.48 | ~1.3% |
| (0.5, 0.5) | -0.75 | -0.74 | ~1.3% |
| (0.0, 0.5) | -0.5 | -0.49 | ~2% |
| (2.0, 0.0) | -4.0 | -3.95 | ~1.2% |

### Ablation Study

**Table 3: Contributions of different analysis components**

| Analysis Level | Coverage | Key Result |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Population SGD (continuous) | Infinite-sample limit | Exact Riccati dynamics |
| Online SGD (discrete) | Finite samples | Discretization error is controllable |
| Finite width $r$ | $r \asymp d^\beta$ | Width scaling law |
| Infinite-dimensional limit | $d \to \infty$ | Effective dynamics hold |

**Table 4: Comparison with existing theoretical results**

| Prior Work | Scope | Extension in This Paper |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Single neuron ($r=1$) | Low-dimensional, fixed width | Extensive width $r \asymp d^\beta$ |
| Fixed-$r$ dynamics | Finite number of signal directions | Infinite-dimensional limit analysis |
| Kernel method lower bounds | No feature learning | Feature learning yields improved scaling |
| Information-theoretic lower bounds | Population setting | Matched with finite-sample results |

### Key Findings

1. **Phase diagram in parameter space**: Different scaling law regimes exist in $(\alpha, \beta)$ space, each with distinct bottleneck mechanisms.
2. **Advantage of feature learning**: Scaling laws in the feature learning regime are superior to those of kernel methods/lazy training, confirming the necessity of feature learning.
3. **Time–sample tradeoff**: A precise tradeoff exists between optimization time and sample size.
4. **Central role of the Riccati equation**: High-dimensional SGD dynamics can be exactly reduced to a matrix Riccati equation.
5. **Perfect agreement between theory and experiment**: Discrepancy between numerical simulations and exact theoretical predictions is within 1–2%.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **First complete dynamics analysis in the extensive-width regime**: Prior work was largely limited to fixed width $r$.
2. **Theoretical foundation for scaling laws**: Provides rigorous mathematical proofs for neural scaling laws without relying on heuristic arguments.
3. **Methodological contribution of matrix monotonicity**: The new mathematical tool may be valuable for other high-dimensional learning problems.
4. **Unified treatment from finite to infinite**: Population/online and finite/infinite-dimensional settings are handled in a unified framework, demonstrating theoretical coherence.
5. **Physics-inspired perspective**: Riccati equations appear widely in control theory and physics, revealing deep connections between neural network learning and these fields.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Specificity of quadratic activations**: The analysis relies on algebraic properties of Hermite polynomials; extension to activations such as ReLU is non-trivial.
2. **Orthogonal signal assumption**: Signal directions are required to be orthogonal; relaxing this to general covariance structures requires new techniques.
3. **Gaussian input restriction**: The analysis assumes Gaussian input distributions; effective dynamics may differ for non-Gaussian cases.
4. **Gap to practical networks**: The bridge from two-layer networks with quadratic activations to deep networks with ReLU activations remains to be constructed.
5. **Potential integration with random matrix theory**: Further use of random matrix theory tools may simplify the analysis.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Neural Scaling Laws** (Kaplan et al., 2020): Discovery of empirical scaling laws
- **Mean-field theory** for neural networks: Dynamics analysis under mean-field approximation
- **Information exponents** (Arous et al., 2021): Information-theoretic complexity of high-dimensional learning
- **Riccati equations** in control theory: Connection to linear quadratic regulators
- **Random matrix theory** (Pennington & Worah, 2017): Spectral analysis of high-dimensional learning
- Insight: Deep learning scaling laws can have rigorous theoretical foundations; the key lies in identifying the correct effective dynamical description.
