# Toward a Vision-Language Foundation Model for Medical Data: Multimodal Dataset and Benchmarks for Vietnamese PET/CT Report Generation

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2509.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24739)  
**Code**: Not available  
**Area**: Medical Imaging
**Keywords**: vision-language model, PET/CT, Vietnamese medical report, dataset, report generation

## TL;DR

This work introduces ViMed-PET, the first Vietnamese PET/CT image-report dataset comprising 2,757 whole-body PET/CT volumes paired with complete clinical reports. Through a data augmentation strategy and a three-stage fine-tuning pipeline, the approach substantially improves VLM performance on medical report generation and VQA tasks. Novel evaluation metrics based on clinically critical information are also proposed.

## Background & Motivation

Vision-language foundation models (VLMs) have achieved remarkable success in general domains, yet their application to medical imaging faces two systemic barriers:

**Narrow imaging modality coverage**: Existing medical VLMs primarily focus on X-ray, CT, and MRI, while **PET/CT scans—indispensable in oncology, cardiology, and neurology—are critically underrepresented** in existing datasets and models. PET/CT enables non-invasive assessment of tumor metabolism and spread, making it an irreplaceable tool for early diagnosis and staging.

**Single-language dominance**: Existing medical VLMs and datasets are almost exclusively English-centric. Vietnamese, spoken by over 100 million native speakers, has virtually no resources in medical AI, exacerbating healthcare data inequity.

Preliminary experiments by the authors clearly reveal the severity of the problem: LLaVA-Med, M3D, and RadFM achieve near-zero BLEU-4 scores (0.01–0.06) on Vietnamese PET/CT report generation, and even GPT-4o under a few-shot setting reaches only 31.12%. This reflects not merely inadequate model capacity, but a **fundamental absence of training data**.

Furthermore, existing public PET/CT datasets (RIDER Lung, FDG-PET-CT-Lesions, etc.), while providing imaging data, **none include paired clinical reports**, limiting progress in generative modeling and multimodal reasoning.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

ViMed-PET contributes at three levels: dataset construction → data augmentation strategy → model fine-tuning pipeline.

### Key Designs

1. **ViMed-PET Dataset**: Sourced from a national tertiary hospital in Vietnam, the dataset contains PET/CT volumes from 2,757 individual patients (approximately 1,567,062 CT-PET slice pairs) paired with complete Vietnamese clinical reports. The data span 2017–2023 and cover multiple diseases including lung cancer and thyroid cancer. Data are stored in DICOM format containing pixel data and acquisition parameter metadata. All data have received IRB ethical approval and undergone de-identification.

   **Anatomical region segmentation**: Each whole-body scan is divided into three regions—head-and-neck, thorax, and abdomen-pelvis—with adaptive segmentation ratios determined by patient height. A 20-slice overlap is introduced to prevent loss of boundary information. This expands the sample count from 2,757 to 8,271 image-report pairs.

2. **Data Augmentation Framework**: Four task-specific subsets are constructed:

    - **VQA dataset** (8,271 dialogues): Single-turn descriptive QA paired with multi-turn dialogues generated by GPT-4o
    - **Report generation dataset** (5,571 reports): Clinically accurate paraphrases of original reports produced by GPT-4o
    - **Comparative study dataset** (10,000 pairs): Two PET images concatenated with text describing their differences
    - **Medical test set** (398 lesions): Focused on lung cancer diagnosis, extracting structured key information including lesion type, location, and FDG metabolism

3. **Three-Stage Fine-Tuning Pipeline**:

    - **Stage 1**: 3D visual encoder fine-tuning. CT-ViT is trained with CLIP-style text-image contrastive learning; Cosmos Tokenizer is trained with reconstruction loss.
    - **Stage 2**: Concept feature alignment. The visual encoder and language model are frozen; only the linear projection layer is trained to map visual features into the text embedding space.
    - **Stage 3**: Instruction tuning. LoRA is applied for efficient fine-tuning of the language model and projection layer using the VQA dataset.

   Visual encoders: CT-ViT (pre-trained on 3D medical CT) and Cosmos Tokenizer (pre-trained on general video, with causal attention removed).
   Language models: Mistral-7B and LLaMA-2-7B.

### Clinical Evaluation Metrics

Conventional NLP metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) fail to capture clinical accuracy. Four clinical F1 metrics are proposed:
- **F1-T**: Evaluates lesion type only
- **F1-TP**: Lesion type + location
- **F1-TF**: Lesion type + FDG metabolism
- **F1-TPF**: Type + location + FDG uptake (most stringent)

## Key Experimental Results

| LLaVA-Med | baseline | 0.01 | 50.08 | 64.63 | - | - | - |
| M3D | baseline | 0.04 | 41.01 | 67.21 | - | - | - |

### Key Findings

- After fine-tuning, BLEU-4 improves from near zero to 58+ (vs. GPT-4o's 31.12), representing a substantial gain.
- Clinical F1 peaks at approximately 51% (F1-T) and F1-TPF at approximately 22%, indicating considerable room for improvement in precise diagnosis.
- CT-ViT consistently outperforms Cosmos Tokenizer on clinical metrics, underscoring the importance of 3D medical pre-training.
- Mistral-7B outperforms LLaMA-2-7B with expanded data, attributed to its more efficient architecture.
- Report paraphrasing augmentation (G) contributes the largest gain; comparative augmentation (C) yields marginal additional benefit.

## Highlights & Insights

- The work addresses a dual gap—PET/CT imaging and a low-resource language—making ViMed-PET the first dataset of its kind.
- The proposed clinical evaluation metrics offer practical reference value, as conventional NLP metrics genuinely fail to measure the accuracy of medically critical information.
- The data augmentation strategies (anatomical segmentation, report paraphrasing, comparative studies) are generalizable to other imaging-report datasets.
- Results honestly acknowledge the severe limitations of current models: F1-TPF of approximately 22% indicates that clinical deployment remains a distant goal.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The clinical test set focuses solely on lung cancer, limiting its coverage.
- Although the dataset includes CT volumes, the benchmark uses only PET information (as reports are primarily PET-based).
- Data augmentation relies heavily on GPT-4o, introducing biases from AI-generated content.
- The dataset size of 2,757 cases is relatively modest; collecting additional data should further improve performance.
- The dataset is not publicly released (described only; subject to hospital data ethics restrictions).

## Related Work & Insights

- The key distinction from existing PET/CT datasets (RIDER, FDG-PET-CT-Lesions, etc.) is that ViMed-PET is the first to provide paired clinical reports.
- The three-stage fine-tuning pipeline draws on alignment methods from the LLaVA family, adapted for 3D medical imaging.
- The clinical F1 metric framework is generalizable to other generative tasks requiring assessment of "medically critical information" accuracy.
