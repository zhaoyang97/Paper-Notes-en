# PAS: Prelim Attention Score for Detecting Object Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models

**Conference**: CVPR 2026  
**Paper**: [CVF Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Hoang_PAS_Prelim_Attention_Score_for_Detecting_Object_Hallucinations_in_Large_CVPR_2026_paper.html)  
**Code**: https://github.com/lanl/pas  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM  
**Keywords**: Object Hallucination Detection, LVLM, Attention, Mutual Information, Training-free

## TL;DR
This study discovers that when Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) generate object hallucinations, they tend to "ignore the image and instead rely on previously generated tokens (prelim)". Based on this, it proposes the Prelim Attention Score (PAS), a training-free method requiring no additional forward passes. By summing the attention weights of prelim tokens as the hallucination score, PAS achieves SOTA object hallucination detection performance across multiple models and datasets.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: LVLMs often exhibit "object hallucinations"—mentioning objects not present in the image—when describing visuals. To intercept such errors in real-time within realistic scenarios lacking annotations or external judge models, mainstream approaches utilize **training-free and reference-free** introspective detection. These methods compute a score using only the model's own inputs/outputs and internal states (logits, hidden states, attention) and then determine hallucinations via a threshold $\tau$.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing methods almost exclusively focus on "high-information" image tokens. Logits-based methods are least utilized (worst performance); representation-based methods (IC, GLSim) use Logit Lens to map image hidden states to the vocabulary for "image-object compatibility" checks; attention-based methods (e.g., SVAR) use image attention ratios. Collectively, they **ignore a low-information source: the previously generated output tokens (termed "prelim" by the authors)**. Intuitively, prelim tokens contain little information and are unreliable since they are self-generated by the model, making them seem unworthy of use.

**Key Challenge**: The authors reverse this logic—precisely because prelim tokens are unreliable, **highly relying on prelims rather than the image when generating an object is itself a signal of "untrustworthiness"**. The softmax constraint of self-attention ensures $s_{\text{BOS}}+s_{\text{img}}+s_{\text{ins}}+s_{\text{prel}}=1$. While "low image attention" is a symptom, "high prelim attention" is a more direct evidence of the model switching to an unreliable generation mode.

**Goal**: (1) Formalize the "prelim reliance ↔ hallucination" hypothesis using information theory and verify it; (2) Design an efficient detection score that can be calculated in real-time during inference.

**Key Insight**: It is observed that the prelim attention of hallucinated tokens is significantly higher than that of real tokens (Fig. 3a), and this correlation is exactly opposite to "image attention"—higher prelim attention indicates a higher likelihood of hallucination.

**Core Idea**: Use the "sum of attention weights for prelim tokens" directly as the hallucination score, replacing expensive mutual information estimations.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
This paper deconstructs object hallucination detection into three steps: investment, hypothesis/verification, and detection. The autoregressive generation of LVLMs is formalized: when generating an object token $y_k$ at position $k$, its probability depends on input $x=(v,t)$ (image tokens $v$, instruction tokens $t$) and context $y_{<k}$ (i.e., prelim). The authors propose Hypothesis H1: **If the generation of $y_k$ highly depends on the prelim rather than the image, it is likely a hallucination**. This hypothesis leads to two paths: a **theoretically rigorous but expensive** mutual information detector $D_{\text{MI}}$, which requires marginalizing the image and running $L$ additional forward passes per input; and a **highly efficient and practical** attention score PAS, which uses attention weights already computed during inference with zero extra forward passes. Both paths yield effective detectors, mutually validating H1, while PAS becomes the final method due to its speed and accuracy.

### Key Designs

**1. Prelim Over-reliance Hypothesis and Mutual Information Detector (H1): Translating "Ignoring the Image" into a Measurable Metric**

Addressing the pain point that "low image attention is only a symptom and fails to capture the root cause," the authors formulate H1 into a computable criterion using conditional mutual information. Let $Y_k$ be the random variable for the token to be predicted at position $k$. If it no longer depends on the image $v$ given the prelim and instruction, $y_k$ is likely a hallucination. The detector is defined as:

$$D_{\text{MI}}(y_k,y,x) = -I(v;Y_k\mid y_{<k},t),\qquad I(v;Y_k\mid y_{<k},t)=H(Y_k\mid y_{<k},t)-H(Y_k\mid y_{<k},x).$$

The difficulty lies in obtaining $\Pr(Y_k\mid y_{<k},t)$ ("prediction given prelim but no image"). The authors approximate marginalization by averaging over a set of reference images $\mathcal I$: $\Pr(y\mid y_{<k},t)=\mathbb E_{I\sim\mathcal I}[\Pr(y\mid y_{<k},(I,t))]$. $\mathcal I$ is sampled by taking one image per object category in the dataset, such that $|\mathcal I| = \text{number of categories}$. This path proves H1 theoretically but is impractical as it requires $|\mathcal I|+1$ times more forward passes per input—its value lies in **providing theoretical backing for PAS**.

**2. Prelim Attention Score (PAS): Using Attention Weights Directly as Hallucination Scores**

Since the marginalization estimation in $D_{\text{MI}}$ is slow and noisy, the authors use the attention mechanism as an equivalent but cheaper metric. In decoder self-attention, the only information exchange path between tokens is attention; thus, the "sum of attention weights to a certain type of token" precisely quantifies that type's influence on the current token's generation. Let $s_{\text{prel}}$ be the sum of attention weights from prelim tokens to $y_k$. PAS is defined as:

$$D_{\text{PAS}}(y_k,y,x)=s_{\text{prel}}(y_k,y,x)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{j=m+1}^{k-1}A^{(l,h)}(k,j),$$

where $A^{(l,h)}(k,j)$ is the attention of token $j$ towards object token $y_k$ in the $h$-th head of the $l$-th layer, $H$ is the number of heads, and $m$ is the input length. It requires no additional forward passes and can be calculated "on the fly" during inference. Compared to $D_{\text{MI}}$, PAS directly measures internal information dependence for "this specific input" rather than relying on noisy estimations over a limited reference set, thus making it **more accurate** (see experiments)—this is the key of "theory leading the way, cheap implementation proving stronger."

**3. Layer Selection and Multi-head Averaging: Using Layer 0 and Averaging All Heads**

Which layer's attention Pas uses is critical. Based on layer-wise AUROC ablation in Fig. 5, the authors found that **the first layer (layer 0) performs best**. This aligns with existing explanations that "early layers aggregate information, while later layers process it"—hallucinations manifest during the information aggregation stage. For multi-head processing, a simple average is taken across all heads in a layer (following the SVAR setup); while some work suggests selecting head subsets might be better, it introduces additional complexity and validation costs, which the authors leave for future work. This design brings PAS to a default configuration without the burden of hyperparameter tuning: **layer 0 + average of all heads**.

### Loss & Training
This method is entirely **training-free**: no model modifications, no fine-tuning, and no external judges. It only reads attention weights during inference to calculate $s_{\text{prel}}$, and uses a threshold $\tau$ to classify $D_{\text{PAS}}(y_k)\ge\tau$ as a hallucination, enabling real-time filtering and intervention.

## Key Experimental Results

> Evaluation Metrics: **AUROC** (Area Under the ROC Curve for "truth/hallucination" binary classification, higher is better, avoids threshold selection); Object tokens are identified as truth/hallucination using CHAIR-style string matching against the image's known object list. Models include LLaVA-1.5-7B, MiniGPT-4, and Shikra (all 7B). Datasets are MSCOCO (80 classes, 5,000 samples) and Pascal VOC (20 classes, 5,823 samples), using greedy decoding and max new tokens=512.

### Main Results
Detection AUROC (%) of PAS compared to various training-free, reference-free baselines across 6 "model × dataset" combinations:

| Method | Type | LLaVA MSCOCO | LLaVA VOC | MiniGPT MSCOCO | Shikra MSCOCO | Average |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| NLL | logits | 56.5 | 64.0 | 62.1 | 54.3 | 62.2 |
| Entropy | logits | 71.7 | 64.3 | 69.8 | 71.4 | 67.4 |
| IC | Representation | 75.1 | 64.6 | 76.4 | 76.0 | 71.9 |
| GLSim | Representation | 64.1 | 69.4 | 63.6 | 67.8 | 65.6 |
| SVAR | Attention (Img) | 81.5 | 82.9 | **88.0** | 71.9 | 80.3 |
| **PAS (Ours)** | Attention (Prelim) | **84.2** | **85.1** | 85.6 | **84.5** | **85.0** |

PAS averages 85.0%, outperforming all baselines. Its strongest competitor, SVAR, is also a pure attention method but focuses on image tokens and lags significantly on Shikra (71.9 vs 84.5). Meanwhile, PAS's memory overhead (18GB) is comparable to SVAR and lower than hidden-state methods like GLSim (19GB) or IC (30GB). It uses approximately 33% less extra memory per sample than GLSim.

### Key Findings
- **Prelim attention is a stronger signal than image attention**: At layer 0, $s_{\text{prel}}$ (84.8) > $s_{\text{img}}$ (82.1). The authors explain that "low image attention is a symptom, while high prelim attention is more direct evidence of the model entering an unreliable mode." ⚠️ Instruction attention (84.7) is nearly as strong; the authors attribute this to the softmax constraint making prelim and instruction attention highly correlated, rather than being an independent theoretical signal.
- **Simple PAS outperforms theoretically rigorous MI detectors**: PAS (85.0) > Logit diff (82.2, the best MI variant) despite zero extra forward passes. This is because PAS directly reads the internal dependencies of the current input, avoiding noisy estimates of marginal probabilities on reference sets.
- **Robust to decoding strategies**: On LLaVA-1.5-7B/MSCOCO, PAS achieves 84.2/84.0/83.5/84.0 under greedy/beam/top-k/nucleus decoding respectively, consistently leading over SVAR (81.5/81.3/79.9/80.4). This proves its utility is independent of the decoding method.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Reclaiming "Garbage Information"**: While others discard prelim information due to its low value, this paper treats "the model looking more at garbage information" as a reliable alarm for untrustworthiness—an elegant perspective shift.
- **Dual-track Theory and Engineering**: Hypothesis H1 is solidified via conditional mutual information before being simplified into a zero-cost attention sum. The theoretical track provides the necessary justification for "why PAS works."
- **Transferable Discriminative Principle**: The idea of using "model dependency on unreliable input sources" as a hallucination/error signal can be extended to factuality detection in pure-text LLMs or RAG scenarios (to check if the model actually looked at retrieved evidence) by finding the corresponding "prelim attention" proxy.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The authors admit that multi-head processing only involves simple averaging; selecting head subsets might be superior but was not explored. The mutual information track is impractical due to $L+1$ forward passes and serves only for theoretical validation.
- ⚠️ Self-identified limitation: Detection and mitigation are complementary. PAS focuses on **detection** and does not provide mitigation. Deployment requires pairing it with intervention strategies. Furthermore, the strong correlation between prelim and instruction attention suggests the signal might not be a "pure" prelim effect, leaving some ambiguity in theoretical interpretation.
- Experiments focused on 7B-scale LLaVA/MiniGPT/Shikra (larger models in appendix) and covered only two object-presence datasets (MSCOCO/Pascal VOC). Applicability to open-vocabulary, long descriptives, or fine-grained attribute hallucinations remains to be validated.
- Future directions: Linking PAS with mitigation methods into a "detection-intervention" closed loop; exploring learned weighted prelim attention across heads/layers to further differentiate it from image attention.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs SVAR (Attention · Image)**: Both use pure attention, but SVAR quantifies reliance on image tokens (low → hallucination), while PAS quantifies reliance on prelim tokens (high → hallucination). This study empirically shows that although related by the softmax constraint, they are not redundant, and the prelim signal is more direct and accurate (85.0 vs 80.3).
- **vs IC / GLSim (Representation-based)**: These map image hidden states to the vocabulary via Logit Lens to check image-object compatibility. They rely on hidden states, have higher VRAM requirements (IC: 30GB), and still focus on the image. PAS uses only attention weights, is more efficient, and achieves higher average AUROC.
- **vs NLL / Entropy (Logits-based)**: Relying solely on output logit uncertainty uses the least information and yields the worst performance (62–67). This paper demonstrates that incorporating often-overlooked prelim attention can significantly boost performance.
- **vs Mitigation routes (VCD/OPERA, etc.)**: Those methods modify inputs or decoding to reduce hallucinations, complementing the detection goal of this paper. Detection does not eliminate hallucinations directly but provides real-time localization signals for intervention.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "Prelim attention as a hallucination signal" is a fresh perspective systematically overlooked by predecessors, supported by both theory and engineering.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Solid coverage of three models, two datasets, and four decoding strategies, though results for larger models and diverse hallucination types are mostly in the appendix or not covered.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear logic from hypothesis to verification to implementation, well-supported by formulas and figures.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Being training-free, zero-overhead, and real-time gives it high engineering value, though it only detects and requires paired mitigation.
