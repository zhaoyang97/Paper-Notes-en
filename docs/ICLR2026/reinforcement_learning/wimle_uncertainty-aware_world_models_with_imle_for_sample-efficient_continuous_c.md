# WIMLE: Uncertainty-Aware World Models with IMLE for Sample-Efficient Continuous Control

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2602.14351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14351)
**Code**: None (Apex Lab, SFU)
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning
**Keywords**: Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, IMLE, Uncertainty Estimation, Multimodal World Models, Sample Efficiency, Continuous Control

## TL;DR
WIMLE extends Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) to model-based RL, learning stochastic world models capable of capturing multimodal transition dynamics. Predictive uncertainty is estimated via ensemble and latent sampling, and is used to weight the RL objective on synthetic data. Across 40 continuous control tasks, WIMLE achieves superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance compared to strong model-free and model-based baselines.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Model-based RL (MBRL) augments policy training with synthetic data generated by a learned world model, which should theoretically yield substantial gains in sample efficiency. In practice, however, MBRL has long struggled to consistently outperform strong model-free baselines.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Standard predictive models average over conflicting supervision signals arising from the same state-action pair (due to partial observability, contact-rich dynamics, or intrinsic stochasticity), leading to regression to the mean and physically implausible predictions; (2) The absence of uncertainty awareness causes world models to be overconfident in regions with limited data or complex dynamics, thereby misleading policy learning.

**Key Challenge**: Multimodal world models are necessary, yet must remain computationally efficient (diffusion models are ill-suited for online RL due to iterative sampling); uncertainty weighting is desirable, yet must not alter the Bellman fixed point.

**Goal**: (1) How can multimodal world models be learned efficiently? (2) How should predictive uncertainty be estimated and exploited? (3) Does uncertainty weighting compromise the convergence of the value function?

**Key Insight**: Replace Gaussian or diffusion-based world models with IMLE—a one-step generative approach that is mode-covering and data-efficient—estimate total predictive variance via ensemble and multi-latent sampling, and apply inverse-variance weighting to synthetic transitions.

**Core Idea**: IMLE world models provide multimodal, mode-covering predictions; ensemble-times-latent uncertainty estimation captures both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty; inverse-variance weighting guarantees optimal Bellman convergence.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
WIMLE comprises three components: (1) a stochastic world model ensemble trained with IMLE; (2) a predictive uncertainty estimator based on ensemble and latent sampling; and (3) an uncertainty-weighted TD learning objective. The underlying RL algorithm is SAC combined with distributional Q-learning.

### Key Designs

1. **IMLE World Model**:

    - Conditional stochastic generator: $(\tilde{s}_{t+1}, \tilde{r}_t) = g_\theta(s_t, a_t, z), \quad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
    - Training alternates between two steps:
        - Assignment (no gradient): $z_i^* = \arg\min_{1 \leq j \leq m} \|g_\theta(s_i, a_i, z_j) - y_i\|^2$ (finds the nearest latent for each data point)
        - Update (gradient descent): $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{i \in B}\|g_\theta(s_i, a_i, z_i^*) - y_i\|^2$
    - **Core Advantage**: IMLE guarantees mode coverage—every data point is covered by at least one generated sample—avoiding the mean regression of Gaussian models.
    - vs. Diffusion: IMLE is a one-step generator with high throughput, making it suitable for online RL.

2. **Uncertainty Estimation**:

    - An ensemble of $K=7$ IMLE models, each sampling $m$ latents.
    - Predictive uncertainty: $\sigma(s,a) = \text{std}_{k,j}[g_{\theta_k}(s,a,z_j)]$
    - Total variance decomposition: $\sigma^2 = \underbrace{\text{Var}_k[\mathbb{E}_z[g_{\theta_k}]]}_{\text{epistemic}} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_k[\text{Var}_z[g_{\theta_k}]]}_{\text{aleatoric}}$
    - Simultaneously captures epistemic uncertainty (disagreement across ensemble members) and aleatoric uncertainty (variability due to latent sampling).

3. **Uncertainty-Weighted Learning**:

    - Weight: $w(s,a) = \frac{1}{\sigma(s,a) + 1} \in (0, 1]$
    - TD loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{critic}} = \mathbb{E}[w_i \cdot \delta_i^2]$
    - Real data uses $w=1$; synthetic data uses the computed weight.
    - **Theoretical Guarantees**:
        - Lemma 1: Positive weights do not alter the Bellman fixed point.
        - Lemma 2: Under a linear critic, inverse-variance weighting is the minimum-covariance unbiased estimator (Gauss–Markov theorem).

### Architecture
Three residual blocks (Dense + ReLU + L2 normalization), taking $(s_t, a_t, z)$ as input and producing separate heads for $(r_t, s_{t+1})$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (40 tasks, 10 seeds)

**DMC Dog & Humanoid (7 tasks)**: WIMLE IQM substantially outperforms all baselines.
- Humanoid-run: WIMLE improves sample efficiency by **>50%** over the strongest competitor.

**DMC Full Suite (16 tasks)**: WIMLE achieves the highest IQM.

**MyoSuite (10 tasks)**: WIMLE matches strong baselines that already approach ceiling performance.

**HumanoidBench (14 tasks)**:

| Method | Tasks Solved |
|--------|-------------|
| BRO | 4/14 |
| SimbaV2 | 5/14 |
| **WIMLE** | **8/14** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Effect |
|---------------|--------|
| WIMLE (full) | Best overall |
| Without uncertainty weighting ($w=1$) | May underperform model-free baselines early in training, validating the importance of uncertainty weighting |
| Gaussian replacing IMLE world model | Significantly worse, validating the value of multimodal modeling |
| Rollout $H=1\to4\to6\to8$ | Performance improves consistently with $H$; $H=8$ remains stable (traditional MBRL degrades at longer horizons) |

### Weight Evolution Analysis
- IMLE: weights are low early in training (high uncertainty) and increase as data accumulates (growing confidence).
- Gaussian: weights remain flat throughout, reflecting insufficient calibration.

### Wall-Clock Comparison
WIMLE is comparable to MBPO and substantially faster than TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3.

## Highlights & Insights
- **First Application of IMLE in MBRL**: The mode-covering property of IMLE is naturally suited to multimodal dynamics; one-step generation ensures rollout throughput.
- **Theoretical Rigor**: Dual guarantees—preservation of the Bellman fixed point and optimality of inverse-variance weighting—render uncertainty weighting principled rather than heuristic.
- **Long-Horizon Stability**: Whereas traditional MBRL degrades as rollout length increases, WIMLE naturally attenuates the influence of distant predictions via uncertainty weighting, enabling stable long-horizon rollouts.
- **Total Variance Decomposition**: Simultaneously capturing epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty ensures that, even with a near-perfect world model (pure aleatoric regime), weighting prevents bias introduced by stochasticity.

## Limitations & Future Work
- An ensemble of 7 models increases computational and memory overhead (though parallel training mitigates this).
- The IMLE assignment step, while gradient-free, increases implementation complexity.
- The rollout horizon is a hyperparameter that requires per-task tuning.
- Gains are limited on MyoSuite tasks that already approach ceiling performance.
- The current framework handles only state-based inputs; extension to visual observations warrants future exploration.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. MBPO**: WIMLE can be viewed as an IMLE-upgraded variant of MBPO—replacing the Gaussian ensemble with an IMLE ensemble and incorporating uncertainty weighting.
- **vs. DreamerV3**: DreamerV3 employs a latent-space world model with a learned reward; WIMLE operates in state space, offering greater transparency.
- **vs. Infoprop**: Infoprop uses information-theoretic measures to truncate rollouts; WIMLE applies inverse-variance soft-weighting—a smoother approach that retains all data.
- **vs. BRO/SimbaV2**: These are model-free methods; WIMLE's MBRL paradigm solves substantially more tasks on HumanoidBench.
