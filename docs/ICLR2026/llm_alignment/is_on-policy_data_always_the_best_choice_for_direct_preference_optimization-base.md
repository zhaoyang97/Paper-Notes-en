# Is On-Policy Data always the Best Choice for Direct Preference Optimization-based LM Alignment?

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2508.10530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10530)  
**Code**: None (see reproducibility statement)  
**Area**: Alignment RLHF / DPO
**Keywords**: on-policy vs off-policy, alignment stage, preference injection, preference fine-tuning, data selection

## TL;DR
This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that on-policy data is always superior, revealing that LLM alignment comprises two distinct stages — preference injection (requiring high-diversity off-policy data) and preference fine-tuning (requiring high-quality on-policy data) — with the optimal data type varying across models and stages. A boundary detection algorithm incurring only 3.2% additional computational overhead is proposed and validated across 5 models × 55 configurations.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: DPO and its variants are the dominant methods for LLM preference alignment. Recent research trends favor on-policy data (preference candidates generated by the current policy during training) over off-policy data (from predefined datasets).

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
   - Empirical results contradict the "on-policy is always better" claim: on-policy data improves performance by 3× on Llama-3, yet underperforms by 0.4× on Zephyr, and off-policy data consistently outperforms on Phi-2.
   - No systematic understanding of when to use on-policy vs. off-policy data exists.
   - On-policy data collection is computationally expensive and wasteful when unnecessary.

**Key Challenge**: The performance gap between on-policy and off-policy data is model-dependent, yet lacks explanatory or predictive mechanisms.

**Core Idea**: Alignment = preference injection (exploration, requiring diversity) → preference fine-tuning (exploitation, requiring quality), with a quantifiable boundary for stage detection.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
The paper proposes the **Alignment Stage Hypothesis**: LLM alignment proceeds in two stages, each suited to a different data type. During training, Algorithm 1 — a boundary detection algorithm with minimal overhead — determines the current stage and selects the corresponding data accordingly.

### Key Designs

1. **Alignment Stage Hypothesis**:

    - **Preference Injection Stage**: The model lacks sufficient understanding of human preferences and the high-reward region is unknown. High-diversity off-policy data is needed for broad exploration.
    - **Preference Fine-Tuning Stage**: The model has approximately identified the preference direction and requires high-quality on-policy data for fine-grained optimization within the identified high-reward region.
    - Analogous to the exploration–exploitation trade-off in reinforcement learning.

2. **Boundary Detection Algorithm**:

    - **Function**: Determines whether the current model is in the injection or fine-tuning stage.
    - **Mechanism**: On-policy and off-policy candidate pairs are generated from 2,000 prompts and cross-evaluated using a preference model $\mathbb{P}$. A boundary score is computed as $BS = V_{\text{off}} / (V_{\text{off}} + V_{\text{on}})$.
    - **Decision rule**: $BS < 0.5$ → injection stage (use off-policy); $BS \geq 0.5$ → fine-tuning stage (use on-policy).
    - **Computational overhead**: Only 3.2% of one training epoch.

3. **Theoretical Support**:

    - Theorem 5.1: A bijection exists between the reward function and the policy; the DPO objective is equivalent to the alignment objective.
    - Theorem 5.2: Only infinite preference data can achieve optimal alignment.
    - Key definition: Preference consistency as a sufficient condition for $\mathbb{P}_\theta$ to match the distribution of $\mathbb{P}^*$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Two-Round Iterative Full Combination (AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate)

| Model | Stage | Best Data Choice | LC Win Rate | Notes |
|-------|-------|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| Llama-3 | SFT→Iter1 | off→**on** | **40.57** | On-policy is 3× better in fine-tuning stage |
| Zephyr | SFT→Iter1 | **off**→on | **20.77→33.28** | Off-policy is 2.27× better in injection stage |
| Phi-2 | SFT→Iter1 | **off** | **5.97** | On-policy degrades performance (−1.60) |

### Diversity vs. Quality Experiment

| Stage | Data Source | Key Characteristic | Effect |
|-------|------------|-------------------|--------|
| Injection (Zephyr) | off-policy | High diversity | **+12.65** |
| Injection (Zephyr) | on-policy | Low diversity | +5.58 |
| Fine-tuning (Zephyr) | on-policy | High quality | **+12.51** |
| Fine-tuning (Zephyr) | off-policy | High diversity | +3.00 |

### Boundary Detection Results

| Model | Boundary Score Trajectory | Stage Determination |
|-------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| Llama-3 | >0.5 (throughout) | Fine-tuning stage throughout |
| Phi-2 | <0.5 (throughout) | Injection stage throughout |
| Zephyr | 0.40→0.66→0.69 | Injection → fine-tuning transition |

### Key Findings
- **On-policy data is not always optimal**: This challenges the prevailing community assumption.
- **Stage transition is critical**: Zephyr exhibits a clear transition from injection to fine-tuning (BS: 0.40→0.66).
- **Diversity vs. quality as exploration–exploitation**: Diversity matters in the injection stage (off-policy is better); quality matters in the fine-tuning stage (on-policy is better).
- **Strong models enter fine-tuning directly**: Llama-3 (24.59% LC after SFT) enters the fine-tuning stage immediately due to strong base capabilities.
- **Weak models remain in injection longer**: Phi-2 (5.81% LC) requires more exploratory data over an extended period.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Data selection > algorithmic innovation**: Choosing the correct data type in preference alignment may be more impactful than improving the loss function — a finding with significant practical implications.
- **Alignment stage hypothesis is concise and powerful**: It reduces a complex problem to a two-stage decision with a single scalar boundary score, facilitating engineering practice.
- **Courage to challenge consensus**: The paper explicitly demonstrates that "on-policy is always better" is inaccurate, supporting counter-intuitive conclusions with extensive experiments.
- **Minimal overhead of the boundary algorithm**: The 3.2% additional computational cost for data selection guidance offers an exceptionally high cost-effectiveness ratio.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Hard switching**: The paper proposes a hard switch (injection vs. fine-tuning as binary choices); a smoother mixing strategy may be preferable.
- **Dependence on the preference model**: Boundary detection relies on the quality of the preference model $\mathbb{P}$.
- **Use of PairRM for preference annotation**: This may introduce bias relative to genuine human preferences.
- **Potential improvements**:
    - Integrating quality weighting from Uni-DPO to apply quality-weighted off-policy data during the injection stage.
    - Designing smooth mixing strategies that blend on-policy and off-policy data proportionally as the BS value changes continuously.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Online DPO**: Online DPO assumes on-policy data is always superior; this paper demonstrates that assumption fails in the injection stage.
- **vs. Iterative DPO**: Iterative DPO uses on-policy data at every round; this paper suggests that using off-policy data in the early rounds may be more beneficial.
- **vs. DAR**: DAR focuses on RL training stability, while this paper addresses data selection — the two are orthogonal and can be combined.
