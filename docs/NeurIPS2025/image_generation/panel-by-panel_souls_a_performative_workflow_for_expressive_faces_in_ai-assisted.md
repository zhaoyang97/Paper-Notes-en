# Panel-by-Panel Souls: A Performative Workflow for Expressive Faces in AI-Assisted Manga Creation

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025  
**arXiv**: [2511.16038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16038)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Image Generation / Human-AI Co-Creation  
**Keywords**: Manga Creation, Expression Mapping, Facial Reenactment, Human-AI Co-Creation, LivePortrait, Dual-Hybrid Pipeline

## TL;DR

This paper proposes a "dual-hybrid" interactive workflow system for manga creation. It integrates automatic keypoint detection with manual bounding box selection during the face preparation stage, and merges performative video capture with fine-grained numerical sliders during the expression mapping stage. This enables manga artists to intuitively inject emotional narrative intent into the facial expressions of characters in AI-generated manga panels, addressing the fundamental "language-visual gap" where text prompts fail to precisely describe subtle emotions.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Current text-to-image generation models (such as diffusion models like Stable Diffusion and DALL·E 3) are already capable of generating high-visual-quality manga-style character illustrations. These models display impressive expressiveness in overall layout, color style, and character posing. Meanwhile, as a visual storytelling medium using panels as basic narrative units, the core of manga lies not just in aesthetic appeal, but more crucially in the emotional information conveyed by character facial expressions—the "soul" of a character is often found in subtle details like the corner of the eyes or slight adjustments of the lip curvature. In traditional manga workflows, artists must meticulously draw the expressions of each character frame-by-frame and panel-by-panel, which is highly time-consuming and skill-intensive. Maintaining character consistency and narrative coherence across multi-character, multi-panel sequential storytelling scenes is a particularly daunting challenge.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Despite significant progress in generating overall images, text-to-image models suffer from fundamental limitations when rendering the subtle facial expressions required for storytelling, primarily manifested in the following aspects: 
First, there is an "uncodability" issue in the cognitive linguistic sense—complex emotional states such as "a smile mixed with regret", "doubting eyes", or "tsundere" require subtle mixtures of various emotions like anger, affection, and embarrassment. Such rich mental imagery inherently resists complete linguistic encoding and cannot be precisely described through simple text prompts. Researchers have confirmed this barrier from a cognitive linguistics perspective: an unbridgeable gap exists between our rich mental imagination of character expressions and their textual descriptions.
Second, expressions generated by text prompts are often generic and stereotypical, lacking the depth and nuance required for manga narratives. This means that even with well-composed panels, the characters' faces often appear "emotionally hollow" or, worse, inconsistent across different panels, disrupting narrative coherence.
Third, existing digital solutions also incur severe workflow friction: manual redrawing in layered painting software (like Clip Studio Paint) offers fine control but is extremely time-consuming, while built-in 3D pose models aid consistency but look stiff and lifeless. On the other hand, facial reenactment technologies (like LivePortrait) are designed primarily for video production, making their interaction paradigms unsuitable for the iterative refinement needs of static manga panels. Node-based editors like ComfyUI, though powerful, require artists to leave their familiar drawing environments to navigate complex graphical programming interfaces, severely disrupting the critical "flow state" in manga creation.

**Key Challenge**: The root cause lies in a combination of two disconnects: first, the "language-visual gap"—text, as the sole communication channel between humans and AI models, has inherent expressiveness constraints in conveying delicate emotions, causing irreconcilable information loss between rich human imagination of facial expressions and discrete text descriptions. Second, the "tool-workflow fracture"—although existing facial manipulation technologies are mature, they exist as standalone tools, lacking organic integration with the core creative workflow of manga artists. Together, these challenges force manga artists to either resort to laborious manual redrawing or frequently switch between incompatible tools, severely hindering both creative efficiency and expressive precision.

**Goal**: This paper focuses on addressing three sub-problems: (1) How to establish an emotional communication channel that bypasses the text description bottleneck, allowing artists to directly and intuitively express their target facial expressions to the AI system? (2) How to locate an appropriate balance between automated efficiency and fine-grained creative control, keeping the system fast without stripping away the artist's final decision-making power? (3) How to seamlessly integrate such solutions into actual manga creation workflows to avoid disrupting the creative flow?

**Key Insight**: The authors start with a key observation—the most natural and direct way for manga artists to express emotions is not via text descriptions, but through acting them out with their own facial performances. This is inspired by a common practice in manga drawing where artists look in a mirror to render specific expressions. By feeding this "performative" expression channel into a facial reenactment engine, body language can replace text prompts, fundamentally bypassing the "language-visual gap". Meanwhile, the authors realize that coarse-grained performative input alone is insufficient—manga expressions require precise detail adjustments (e.g., gaze direction independent of head pose), necessitating a layer of fine numerical control overlaid on top of the performative input.

**Core Idea**: Replacing text prompts with a dual-hybrid interaction paradigm of "performative video capture + fine-grained numerical sliders" allows manga artists to directly "inject soul" via facial performances, resolving the lack of expression in AI-generated manga character faces.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The system adopts a three-stage pipeline architecture that can be seamlessly repeated across multiple characters and panels to support coherent emotional narratives. The input is an AI-generated manga panel (typically containing characters with neutral expressions), and the output is an updated panel where the same characters possess the target expressions. The three stages are: (1) Hybrid Face Preparation—extracting and cropping character facial regions from the manga panel; (2) Interactive Expression Mapping—mapping target expressions to the cropped face via performative inputs and fine-grained controls; (3) Composition and Refinement—recompositing the modified face back into the original panel, while leaving final seam blending and aesthetic adjustments to the artist.

The core concept throughout the system design is "dual-hybrid": providing both automated and manual control paths at each critical decision juncture, enabling artists to choose based on context. This design philosophy positions the system not as an automation tool to replace artists, but as a collaborative partner to empower them. The entire pipeline is designed to let manga artists focus on character dynamics and emotional storytelling, rather than being distracted by technical details.

### Key Designs

1. **Stage 1: Hybrid Face Preparation**:
    - **Function**: Precisely locates and crops the target character's face from the original manga panel, recording original scales and coordinates for future recomposition. This stage forms the foundation of the pipeline, as crop quality directly impacts subsequent expression mapping.
    - **Mechanism**: The system offers two complementary modes. First, the core keypoint-based automatic detection mode uses state-of-the-art face analysis models from the `insightface` library to detect 106 facial keypoints (covering eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, and head profiles), and computes a tight bounding box with an appropriate padding margin. Unlike basic bounding boxes from general object detectors, this keypoint-derived cropping targets facial feature areas more accurately and filters out extraneous non-face elements (e.g., hands or torso) often miscaptured by standard detectors. Second is the manual bounding box selection mode, showing an interactive window where artists can directly draw a square to specify the face area. Manual and automatic cropped regions are treated identically downstream, allowing seamless switching. A critical empirical trick found in case studies shows that a slightly larger cropped box including the hairstyle performs better than an ultra-tight crop of facial features, as it provides more spatial context for the subsequent reenactment model, yielding more harmonious and consistent results.
    - **Design Motivation**: Fully automated detection faces three main challenges in manga: complex hairstyles or facial accessories interfering with detection, distant small-scale faces falling outside the detector's range, and non-human manga characters (e.g., anthropomorphic animals, monsters) being entirely unrecognizable to standard detectors. Thus, the manual mode acts as an indispensable fallback to ensure any character type can be processed. This dual-mode design follows the principle of "efficiency first, control as safeguard"—using automatic detection to accelerate standard cases and manual cropping to guarantee full coverage, keeping final control with the artist.

2. **Stage 2: Interactive Expression Mapping**:
    - **Function**: Maps the target expressions requested by artists onto the character faces prepared in Stage 1. This is the core stage of the entire system, essential for "injecting soul." The system offers a progressive control hierarchy from coarse to fine-grained, allowing artists to first capture the emotional tone via performative inputs, then polish details with fine-grained numerical adjustments.
    - **Mechanism**: This stage also utilizes a dual-mode interactive design based on the pretrained LivePortrait engine. The first layer is the performative input mode, where artists provide facial references by capturing webcam video or uploading prerecorded clips. This replicates the standard habit of looking in a mirror during drawing, yielding a low-friction interaction. The system visualizes the video on an interactive timeline where artists slide across frames to inspect reenacted results, picking the narratively optimal keyframe (e.g., the peak smile moment or a specific head tilt). A key finding from the case studies is the "temporal offset" phenomenon: the most narratively correct reenactment often does not align with the frame showing the artist's perceived best facial performance, but instead resides a few frames before or after. This subjective misalignment disproves the simple "act-once-and-generate" mechanism, proving the necessity of an interactive timeline slider for exploration. The second layer is the fine-grained numerical control mode. Once the base expression is selected via performance, specialized sliders allow artists to manipulate the key eye and lip retargeting coefficients within LivePortrait. This enables precise adjustments hard to acting naturally (e.g., correcting gaze direction independently from head rotation, or sub-pixel adjustments to lip curvature to align with signature character styles). This progressive paradigm blends performative intuition with parameterized control.
    - **Design Motivation**: This dual-layered design stems from a deep understanding of manga creation workflows. Performing is intuitive but lacks accuracy due to the aforementioned "temporal offset"; sliders are precise but unintuitive, as artists cannot easily translate numerical values into nuanced emotional perceptions. Blending the two by using performance to approximate the emotional target, followed by slider-based refinements, mimics the natural draft-to-detail iteration in manga drawing. Most importantly, expanding control channels into bodily performances and direct parameter manipulations bypasses the "language-visual gap."

3. **Stage 3: Composition and Refinement**:
    - **Function**: Recomposites the target-face output from Stage 2 back into the original manga panel, outputting a complete, updated panel. It relies on a direct composition strategy and explicitly leaves the final aesthetic grooming to the artist.
    - **Mechanism**: The composite process operates as follows: the $512 \times 512$ standard-size reenacted face from Stage 2 is scaled to the original facial dimensions logged in Stage 1, and then pasted onto the coordinates corresponding to the original panel. The authors candidly point out that direct pasting, while efficient, introduces predictable artifacts along boundary seams (e.g., geometric misalignment at hair/neck borders, and minor color/lighting drifts stemming from the reenactment model). The system deliberately avoids integrating automated blending algorithms (such as Deep Image Blending); instead, it hands off the draft containing predictable boundaries as a clean junction point to the artist, who can use standard paint brushes, smudge tools, and airbrushes within their preferred digital painting programs to achieve polished results.
    - **Design Motivation**: This choice is not a technical compromise, but an intentional conceptual framing, highlighting the role of the system as an "empowering assistant" rather than a "complete replacement". Fully automated image blending may theoretically save time but frequently risks introducing unpredictable artifacts or style shifts, shifting cognitive overload back onto the artist. By placing the final polish directly in the artist's hands, the system honors the artist's sovereignty as the ultimate judge of quality, preserving their overall creative control. This division of labor—delegating bulk initial scaling and alignment tasks to the algorithm while letting the human handle final artistic adjustments—exemplifies the co-creation paradigm.

### Loss & Training

The core contribution of this work lies in proposing a novel interactive workflow system rather than a new generative model or objective loss function. The system directly utilizes the pre-trained LivePortrait engine as its underlying expression mapping engine, requiring no additional model training or fine-tuning. LivePortrait is an efficient portrait animation model supporting real-time facial reenactment and providing API control interfaces for independent eye and lip retargeting modules. This technical choice is motivated by LivePortrait's balance between real-time efficiency and high-fidelity output, which is highly suited for interactive workflows. Facial detection is powered by direct queries to pre-trained models within the `insightface` library, where the 106-keypoint model offers detailed facial feature coordinates.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

This work evaluates the proposal through an end-to-end case study rather than typical quantitative benchmarks. The case study is conducted using a multi-character, multi-panel manga draft generated by DALL·E 3. It contains three characters—a middle-aged mentor (solemn features, short grey hair, wearing a traditional hakama and dougi), a young trainee (young adult, slender build, messy black hair, wearing a sleeveless dougi), and an observing peer (young female, short bob haircut, wearing an athletic jacket)—with all characters initially styled with neutral expressions. The objective is to use the system to inject novel expressions into the characters, altering the overall narrative tone. The case study was executed by the authors, who possess formal training in both fine arts (visual communication, illustration) and human-computer interaction, allowing a dual evaluation of the engineering pipeline and the artistic workflow.

| Stage | Successes | Issues Identified | Key Insight |
|------|----------|-----------|---------|
| Stage 1: Face Preparation | The keypoint-based automatic detector successfully identified and bound the face of the primary male character. | Detection failed on a small, distant face (marked by red box in original images); complex hairstyles required reverting to manual crops. | Slightly larger bounding boxes including hair context outperform tight face-only frames by preserving critical scene context. |
| Stage 2: Expression Mapping | Successfully mapped target expressions to prepared character faces, delivering narratively correct emotional delivery. | The optimal frame in the output did not coincide with the subjectively optimal performance frame in the driver clip, indicating a temporal offset. | The interactive timeline is a crucial exploratory instrument rather than a luxury addition. |
| Stage 3: Composition & Output | Modified face crops were successfully merged back into original panels, effectively updating critical expressions. | Static hair/ears during head rotations (geometric inconsistencies); photorealistic elements mixed with monochrome manga style (style mismatch). | Modern reenactment is not a plug-and-play solution and requires downstream refinement. |

### Ablation Study

The authors systematically analyze the limitations encountered during the case study, which can be interpreted as an ablation-style exploration of component boundaries. Most of these limitations are inherited directly from the underlying LivePortrait reenactment model rather than faults in the orchestrating workflow.

| Limitation Category | Specific Manifestation | Root Cause Analysis | Impact Level |
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Geometric Inconsistency (e1-e3) | Hair and ears remain static during head rotations, causing physical implausibility. | LivePortrait operates purely as a 2D facial reenactment model and lacks proper 3D spatial awareness to adapt secondary features like hair. | High — especially pronounced in frames requiring substantial head motion. |
| Style Mismatch (e4) | Redrawn teeth/lips sometimes show photographic realism against classic hand-drawn lines, breaking aesthetic consistency. | LivePortrait is pretrained on real human facial distributions and not fine-tuned on cartoon/anime stylistic domains. | Medium to High — severely impacts visual cohesion in highly stylized artistic works. |
| Pose Sensitivity | Accuracy and fidelity drop considerably when head rotation exceeds ~45 degrees. | Steep profile angles result in self-occlusions of facial features, limiting the info available to reenactment. | Medium — restricts extreme camera compositions, though most panels feature head angles close to frontal views. |
| Non-human Character Limitations | The `insightface` module fails to detect keypoints on animals, robots, monsters, etc. | Detection models are trained exclusively on human proportions. | Medium — though many mangas focus on humans, sci-fi and fantasy contexts heavily rely on anthropomorphic subjects. |
| Expression Homogenization Risk | Repeating identical driver videos across panels can lead to generalized expression profiles if unadjusted. | Model outputs conform to pre-existing training sets and expressions. | Low to Medium — mitigated by diversifying human performances and utilizing fine-grained control sliders. |

### Key Findings

- **The "temporal offset" phenomenon is the core finding**: In the Stage 2 expression mapping process, the most aesthetically and narratively correct expression does not always align with the frame considered "best-looking" in the driver video. Optimal results often appear slightly before or after the perceived peak performance frame, indicating an unpredictable temporal offset. This finding yields deep design implications: it empirically proves that a simple "perform-and-generate" mechanism is insufficient for creative workflows, justifying the decision to elevate interactive exploration tools (timeline sliders) from luxury additions to indispensable core elements.
- **Cropping margins significantly affect output quality**: Broader crops containing surrounding hair contexts perform considerably better than tight crops focused solely on central facial features. A wider bounding box provides the LivePortrait engine with richer context, facilitating reenacted results that align harmoniously with hair structures and overall head shapes, which improves visual coherence upon final composition.
- **Expert interviews confirm the validity of the challenge**: Following the case study, the authors conducted an expert interview with professional manga artist YUNA (Mingxi Zhang). The artist validated a profound concern: the widespread adoption of modern digital tools (such as software with line-smoothing algorithms) has driven a "stylistic homogenization," eroding individual brushstroke identities. This testimony anchors the proposed work within a broader cultural context, showcasing the value of a performative workflow as a countermeasure—it captures and communicates the unique performance style of individual artists instead of conforming to standardized digital templates.
- **The necessity of dual-hybrid design is empirically validated**: Automated detection failures on small landscapes and complex character designs confirm that manual overrides are essential safe-havens, rather than trivial details. Similarly, the combination of performative inputs and fine sliders is shown to be non-negotiable—performance capture alone lacks precision (temporal offset), while sliders alone lack intuition, as artists cannot easily plan subtle emotion purely through abstract statistics.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Philosophical framing of the human-AI co-creation paradigm**: The most profound value of this work lies not in technical novelty, but in its co-creation design framework—the system does not strive for total automation, but presents itself as an enabling companion. Dividing tasks so that AI handles tedious initial processing while humans retain final creative ownership offers a constructive blueprint for generative AI applications. Amid anxieties concerning job displacement, "augmenting humans instead of replacing them" serves as an exemplary philosophy. Leaving seam blending as an intentional raw handoff in Stage 3 is a direct realization of this spirit.
- **Implications of the temporal offset discovery**: Revealing the systematic offset between the human-perceived "best frame" and the model-perceived "optimal frame" is a vital takeaway for all performance-driven AI systems. It demonstrates that a translation loss exists between human intent and machine execution. Consequently, any interactive human-evaluating application must offer flexible exploratory options (such as timeline sliders, grid views, etc.) rather than assuming single-shot perfection.
- **Interaction modality shift via performative input over text**: Digitizing and systematizing the natural artistic habit of practicing expressions in front of a mirror to query the model bypasses the "language-visual gap" entirely. Instead of battling textual limits through complex prompt engineering, this solution changes the interface modality to body language. This approach has broad transferability: any domain hampered by the limitations of verbal descriptions (e.g., emotional nuances in generated music, stylized dance steps) could adopt a performative input channel.
- **Distinguished framing of problem formulation**: Linking manga expression control to cognitive linguistics—drawing on theories of intent, convention, and the friction between mental imagery and verbal translation—provides a rigorous theoretical backbone to a system that could easily have been presented as simple pipeline engineering. This interdisciplinary approach clarifies why text prompts encounter hard expressiveness limits in high-nuance tasks.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Lack of 3D spatial awareness**: The most pronounced technical limitation of the current system is the underlying LivePortrait's neglect of 3D structures, generating geometric inconsistencies like static hair/ears during head rotations. Future iterations can integrate 3D geometry—for example, employing ControlNet-like architectures to achieve posture controls, enabling artists to manipulate head orientation alongside fine muscular expressions. Feasible technical directions include incorporating a 3D Morphable Model (3DMM) representation or explicitly modelling hair and secondary assets.
- **Unaddressed domain gap (style mismatch)**: Deploying LivePortrait (trained on real human distributions) directly onto line drawings introduces photographic skin/lip render artifacts. The authors note that fine-tuning animation engines on stylized datasets or integrating style preservation blocks could mitigate this. A potential direction is applying a GAN- or diffusion-based style transfer module immediately following the reenactment to align visual stylization with target brushwork.
- **Severely limited empirical evaluation**: The evaluation relies strictly on a single system walk-through by the authors, lacking validation in three key dimensions: (a) no formal user study with professional manga artists to establish real-world usability; (b) no quantitative metrics, such as automated quality measurements (e.g., FID, LPIPS) or comparative time-and-motion studies of task completion; (c) no comparative experiments against existing baselines (e.g., direct ComfyUI node execution or 3D reference rigs in Clip Studio Paint). The authors acknowledge this limitation and set formal user studies as a high-priority future direction.
- **Inability to handle non-human characters**: Face detection and reenactment pipelines depend on human anatomy assumptions, leaving out animal companions, fantasy creatures, robotic features, and monsters. This significantly limits utility in fantasy or sci-fi genres, prompting the need to adapt models to support non-human topologies.
- **Complete reliance on manual seam blending**: Delegating boundary cleaning entirely to human edits, although conceptually aligned with co-creation ideals, imposes manual labor demands—especially in high-throughput production. Future work could introduce "one-click automated blending" as an optional preset, providing a cleaner draft composition while retaining downstream tweaking capability.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs DiffSensei (Wu et al., CVPR 2025)**: DiffSensei attempts to generate customizable manga by integrating multimodal LLMs with diffusion models, focusing on enhancing manga control via prompt-level manipulation. In contrast, this work takes a post-processing approach—rather than changing generation architectures, it introduces an editing pipeline on the generated output. The two methods are complementary: DiffSensei controls early-stage layout, whereas this system tackles high-fidelity detail refinements in later stages. In practice, DiffSensei can easily serve as an upstream "panel generator" for this pipeline.
- **vs LivePortrait (Guo et al., 2024)**: LivePortrait is the core reenactment engine used by this system. It was originally designed for video-based portrait animation, anticipating a continuous video stream output. This work repackages LivePortrait into an interactive frame-by-frame refinement framework adapted for static panels by adding an interactive timeline, detailed slider adjustments, and keypoint-based cropping and composition. This shifts a video-centric tool into a static panel design assistant. The discovered "temporal offset" phenomenon further informs UI design paradigms for other facial drive engines.
- **vs DreamActor-M1 (Luo et al., 2025)**: DreamActor-M1 presents a hybrid-guided holistic portrait animation system, targeting high-fidelity and diverse performance envelopes. However, it remains a standalone, video-production-oriented model lacking the direct workflow interfaces required for comic artists. This system's value lies in establishing a general framework that can house any facial control engine (including future models like DreamActor-M1) within a practical manga workflow.
- **vs ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023)**: ControlNet adds spatial condition interfaces (e.g., poses, depth, edges) to diffusion models, representing a dominant conditional control methodology. The authors refer to ControlNet-like mechanics as a viable path for adding 3D structure controls. Using multi-angle spatial guidance alongside expression pipelines could eliminate the core issue of static hair and ears during head rotations.
- **vs GenEAva (Yu et al., 2025)**: GenEAva focuses on generating stylized avatars with fine expressions from diffusion models. Unlike this post-processing approach, GenEAva implements expression control directly during early generation phases. However, GenEAva targets single avatars, lacking native multi-panel continuity workflows or frame-by-frame interactive adjustments. This work's panel-centric workflow and timeline interactions are better suited to sequential storytelling.
- **The Unique Position of This Work**: All related works focus either on upgrading single technical building blocks (better generators, stronger reenactments, precise controls) or targeting native video creation. This work makes the unique contribution of being the first expression control system designed specifically around the panel-by-panel workflow of manga artists—it does not build new technological blocks but organizes existing blocks into artist-centric configurations. Defining the core contribution around "workflow design" is rare yet highly practical in computer vision. The dual-hybrid "performative input + slider control" framework could extend to other creative environments—e.g., animation, where animators can rough in posture with motion capture, then refine sub-second movements via sliders. Additionally, delegating bulk generation to AI and final corrections to human experts serves as an inspiring paradigm for other fields, such as generating rough audio drafts from a user's vocal hums followed by precise DAW editing.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The core contribution lies in workflow integration and interactive paradigms rather than algorithmic breakthroughs. This "performance-over-text" paradigm is elegant and practical, and the discovery of the "temporal offset" contributes meaningfully to human-computer interaction (HCI) research.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐ — Heavily under-evaluated; features only a single walkthrough case study run by the authors. The lack of structured user studies, quantitative metrics, or systemic baselines is a major shortcoming for a NeurIPS paper.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Thoroughly motivated and theoretically grounded (incorporating cognitive linguistics). Design choices are justified clearly, and limitations are discussed in a candid and constructive manner.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Strong practical value targeting real creative dilemmas. The philosophical framing of human-AI collaboration offers constructive ideas for the wider generative AI landscape, although the technical implementation threshold remains relatively low.
