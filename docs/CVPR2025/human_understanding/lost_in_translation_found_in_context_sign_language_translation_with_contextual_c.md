# Lost in Translation, Found in Context: Sign Language Translation with Contextual Cues

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2501.09754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09754)  
**Code**: [https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/research/litfic/](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/research/litfic/)  
**Area**: LLM Pre-training  
**Keywords**: Sign Language Translation, In-Context Learning, LLM Fine-Tuning, Multimodal Fusion, Pseudo-Vocabulary

## TL;DR
By introducing three contextual cues—background video descriptions, historical translations, and a pseudo-vocabulary—and combining them with LoRA fine-tuning of Llama3-8B, precise continuous sign language-to-text translation is achieved, yielding an improvement of over 40% compared to the SOTA on the BOBSL dataset.

## Background & Motivation

### Key Challenge

**Key Challenge**: **Background**: Automatic Sign Language Translation (SLT) faces challenges such as data sparsity, strong spatial contextual dependence, and frequent homophonic gestures. Existing methods mainly translate based on isolated sentence-level video features.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Approximately 1/3 of sign language sentences require context for correct comprehension—e.g., spatial indexing (pointing to previously introduced concepts), topic-comment structures (establishing a topic frame before commenting), and homophones (e.g., the gestures for "battery" and "uncle" in British Sign Language share the same handshape).

**Key Insight**: The BOBSL dataset provides 1400 hours of BBC broadcast videos, including background footage and speaker subtitles. Three automatically extracted contextual cues can be systematically utilized: background video descriptions (automatically labeled by BLIP2), predictions of the previous translation, and a pseudo-vocabulary (automatically generated by an ISLR classifier).

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Video → Video-Swin extracts 768-dimensional visual features → MLP maps to the 4096-dimensional space of Llama3 → Concatenation of various contextual cues → Llama3-8B LoRA fine-tuning for translation generation.

### Key Designs

1. **Visual Feature Mapping**: A 2-layer MLP (GELU) maps 768 to 4096 dimensions, using a 16-frame sliding window to cover the video sequence.
2. **Pseudo-vocabulary**: Word sequences produced by an ISLR classifier's sliding window predictions (containing noise), allowing the LLM to learn to filter them automatically.
3. **Background Descriptions**: BLIP2 annotations on background frames at 1 fps → deduplication and stop-word removal → retention of key terms.
4. **Historical Context**: 50% ground-truth translations and 50% model predictions are used during training, and autoregressive historical predictions are used during inference.

### Loss & Training
- LoRA (rank=4, alpha=16) fine-tunes only the Q and V projections, while freezing the text embedding layer.
- Word-level/cue-level random dropout is applied to enhance robustness.
- 4×H100 GPUs, bfloat16, FlashAttention-2, lr=0.0001.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: BOBSL Test Set

| Method | BLEURT↑ | R-L↑ | CIDEr↑ | LLM Score↑ |
|------|---------|------|--------|-----------|
| Sign2GPT | 34.3 | 10.6 | 12.8 | 0.37 |
| Ours (Visual Only) | 37.8 | 15.6 | 37.5 | 0.95 |
| **Ours (Full Modality)** | **40.3** | **16.9** | **41.9** | **1.20** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | BLEURT↑ | IoU↑ | LLM Score↑ |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| Visual Only | 41.0 | 16.6 | 1.29 |
| + Pseudo-vocabulary | 41.8 | 17.5 | 1.40 |
| + Historical Sentences | 42.5 | 18.1 | 1.45 |
| + Background Descriptions | **43.5** | **18.8** | **1.56** |

### Key Findings
- Background descriptions contribute the most (+1.0 BLEURT), which helps in disambiguation and referential comprehension.
- LoRA fine-tuning improves by 2.9 BLEURT compared to a frozen LLM, demonstrating that LLMs need to adapt to the characteristics of sign language.
- Cross-lingual validation on How2Sign achieves an R-L of 32.5 (vs. SOTA 27.8), proving the generalization ability of the proposed method.

## Highlights & Insights
- The first systematic integration of multiple contextual cues into an LLM framework for SLT.
- Automated extraction eliminates the need for manual annotation (background descriptions and pseudo-vocabulary are fully automated).
- Word-level/cue-level random dropout mitigates the domain gap between training and testing.
- LLM evaluation metrics capture semantic synonymy better than BLEU.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Error propagation issue in historical predictions.
- Difficulty in distinguishing interrogative and declarative sentences, with frequent omissions of negative words.
- The inference latency of Llama3-8B may limit real-time applications.
