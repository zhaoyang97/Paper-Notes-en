# RAE: A Neural Network Dimensionality Reduction Method for Nearest Neighbors Preservation in Vector Search

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2509.25839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25839)
**Code**: To be confirmed
**Area**: Vector Search / Dimensionality Reduction / Information Retrieval
**Keywords**: dimensionality reduction, k-NN preservation, autoencoder, regularization, Rayleigh quotient, vector search

## TL;DR
This paper proposes RAE (Regularized Auto-Encoder), a dimensionality reduction method based on a linear autoencoder with Frobenius norm regularization. The authors theoretically prove that the regularization coefficient $\lambda$ constrains the condition number $\kappa(W)$ of the encoder matrix via the Rayleigh quotient property, thereby bounding the norm distortion rate and preserving k-NN structure. RAE consistently outperforms PCA, UMAP, MDS, and ISOMAP on four datasets, achieving at least 12% higher k-NN preservation accuracy than PCA under cosine distance, with training requiring only 8 seconds and inference at millisecond latency.

## Background & Motivation
**State of the Field**: High-dimensional embedding vectors generated by models such as BERT, CLIP, and LLMs (ranging from hundreds to thousands of dimensions) are widely used in RAG, recommender systems, and semantic search. The curse of dimensionality leads to efficiency and storage challenges, making dimensionality reduction a natural remedy.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
- PCA optimizes variance preservation; t-SNE/UMAP optimizes visualization and topological structure — neither explicitly optimizes k-NN preservation.
- Neighborhood structure is often disrupted after dimensionality reduction, making the compressed vectors unsuitable for direct retrieval.
- Methods such as Matryoshka RL require end-to-end retraining of the original model, incurring high costs; PCA is fast but offers no k-NN guarantee.

**Root Cause**: Dimensionality reduction is inherently lossy — a trade-off between compression ratio and neighborhood preservation must be made, yet theoretical guidance on controlling the degree of neighborhood distortion is lacking.

**Core Idea**: Frobenius norm regularization of a linear autoencoder → constrains the maximum singular value of the encoder matrix → bounds the norm distortion rate → preserves k-NN structure. The coefficient $\lambda$ carries direct mathematical meaning: it controls the upper bound of distortion.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Input high-dimensional embedding $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ → linear encoder $W_e \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ (reducing dimension to $m < n$) → linear decoder $W_d \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ (reconstruction) → training minimizes $\|W_d W_e x - x\|_2^2 + \lambda \|W\|_F^2$ → at inference, only $W_e$ is used for the dimensionality reduction mapping.

### Key Designs

1. **Linear RAE Architecture**

    - **Function**: A two-layer linear network serves as the autoencoder; the encoder after training constitutes the dimensionality reduction mapping.
    - **Mechanism**: The loss function is $\mathcal{L} = \|W_d W_e x - x\|_2^2 + \lambda \|W\|_F^2$. The solution space of the pure reconstruction loss ($\lambda = 0$) subsumes PCA (Baldi & Hornik 1989), but the orthogonality constraint of PCA is not necessarily optimal — non-orthogonal bases may better preserve k-NN structure.
    - **Design Motivation**: The linear structure ensures analytical tractability (singular value decomposition is controllable), while regularization expands PCA's solution space.

2. **Theoretical Foundation of Frobenius Norm Regularization**

    - **Function**: Proves that $\lambda$ guarantees k-NN preservation by controlling the spectral properties of the encoder matrix.
    - **Theoretical Chain**:
        - **Step 1**: k-NN preservation is equivalent to preserving the relative ordering of pairwise distances.
        - **Step 2**: Define the norm distortion rate as the ratio of vector norms after linear transformation: $\|Wv\|_2 / \|v\|_2$.
        - **Step 3**: By the Rayleigh quotient property: $\sigma_{\min}^2(W) \leq \frac{\|Wv\|_2^2}{\|v\|_2^2} \leq \sigma_{\max}^2(W)$, so the distortion rate is governed by the extreme singular values.
        - **Step 4**: The condition number $\kappa(W) = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ controls the distortion ratio $\varphi_2/\varphi_1$. As $\kappa \to 1$, the transformation approximates an isometry and k-NN is perfectly preserved.
        - **Step 5**: Since $\sigma_{\max}(W) \leq \|W\|_F$, Frobenius norm regularization constrains the maximum singular value.
    - **Conclusion**: $\lambda$ directly controls the condition number, thereby providing a provable upper bound guarantee for k-NN preservation.

3. **Balancing Role of $\lambda$**

    - $\lambda$ too large → matrix becomes sparse, insufficient expressive capacity.
    - $\lambda$ too small → large condition number, high distortion rate, k-NN structure disrupted.
    - The optimal $\lambda$ balances reconstruction quality against distortion control.
    - Experiments verify the precise correspondence between $\lambda$, condition number, and k-NN accuracy.

### Loss & Training
- Adam optimizer (weight decay = $\lambda$)
- 3,000 update steps, batch size 128
- Cosine annealing learning rate schedule: $1 \times 10^{-3}$ → $1 \times 10^{-5}$
- Training requires only ~8 seconds on an RTX 4060 GPU.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Top-5 k-NN Preservation Accuracy)

| Dataset | Distance Metric | PCA | UMAP | MDS | ISOMAP | **RAE** |
|---------|----------------|-----|------|-----|--------|---------|
| ImageNet | Euclidean | 2nd best | Poor | Poor | Poor | **Best** |
| ImageNet | Cosine | 2nd best | Poor | Poor | Poor | **Best** |
| Other 3 datasets | Cosine | Baseline | Poor | Poor | Poor | **+12% over PCA** |
| Other 3 datasets | Euclidean | Comparable | Poor | Poor | Poor | **Comparable / slightly better** |

RAE demonstrates the largest advantage under cosine distance (≥12%); under Euclidean distance it is comparable to PCA.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Effect | Explanation |
|--------------|--------|-------------|
| Increasing $\lambda$ | $\sigma_{\max}$ decreases, $\kappa$ decreases | Precisely validates theoretical prediction |
| Optimal $\lambda$ | Highest k-NN accuracy | Balance between reconstruction and distortion control |
| $\lambda$ too large | Accuracy degrades | Over-regularization |

### Key Findings
- UMAP, MDS, and ISOMAP consistently fail on the k-NN preservation task due to their different design objectives.
- PCA is a strong baseline — variance preservation in linear reduction implicitly retains partial k-NN structure — but RAE further improves upon it by relaxing orthogonality constraints and controlling the condition number.
- The empirical relationship between $\lambda$, condition number, and k-NN accuracy perfectly matches theoretical predictions.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Interpretability of $\lambda$**: Beyond serving as a regularization hyperparameter, $\lambda$ carries precise mathematical meaning — it controls the upper bound of the transformation's condition number and thereby the quality of k-NN preservation, providing theoretical guidance for hyperparameter tuning.
- **Strong Practicality**: 8-second training, millisecond inference, and model-agnostic applicability (compatible with any embedding source) make RAE a drop-in dimensionality reduction solution.
- **Complete Theoretical Chain**: Regularization → Frobenius norm → spectral norm → maximum singular value → condition number → norm distortion rate → k-NN preservation, with rigorous mathematical derivation at each step.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Only a linear encoder is used — nonlinear dimensionality reduction may be superior on complex manifolds (acknowledged by the authors as a limitation).
- The theoretical guarantee for cosine distance preservation relies on the approximation assumption that embedding vectors have similar norms, rather than a strict proof.
- The experimental datasets are of limited scale (at most tens of thousands of samples); billion-scale retrieval scenarios have not been validated.
- No comparison is made against other acceleration methods such as Matryoshka RL or Product Quantization.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. PCA**: PCA is a special case of RAE with $\lambda = 0$ and an orthogonality constraint; RAE relaxes the orthogonality constraint and controls the condition number via $\lambda$, yielding significantly better performance than PCA under cosine distance (12%+).
- **vs. UMAP**: UMAP optimizes topological preservation rather than k-NN preservation and is transductive (unable to efficiently handle new data points); RAE is inductive.
- **vs. Johnson-Lindenstrauss Random Projection**: JL provides probabilistic distortion guarantees but has no trainable parameters; RAE is data-driven and achieves better performance on real-world data.
