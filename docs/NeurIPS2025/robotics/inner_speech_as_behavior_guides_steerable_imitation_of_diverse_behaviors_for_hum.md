# Inner Speech as Behavior Guides: Steerable Imitation of Diverse Behaviors for Human-AI Coordination

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight  
**arXiv**: [2602.20517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20517)  
**Code**: [Project Page](https://mimic-research.github.io)  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning / Imitation Learning
**Keywords**: Imitation Learning, Inner Speech, Behavioral Diversity, Human-AI Coordination, Diffusion Policy

## TL;DR

Inspired by Vygotsky's theory of inner speech, this paper proposes MIMIC, a framework that uses language as an intermediate representation between perception and action. A VLM provides language scaffolding to train a CVAE that generates inner speech, which then conditions a diffusion policy to produce diverse and steerable behaviors.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Imitation learning (IL) is a promising paradigm for building human-like AI agents. Behavior cloning (BC) learns policies from demonstrations via supervised learning, and recent advances in diffusion policies and Transformer architectures have substantially improved performance.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing BC methods ① fail to capture the multimodal distribution of human behavior (diversity arising from varying motivations and skill levels); ② lack inference-time behavioral controllability—either offering none at all or only supporting goal-conditioned generation; ③ ignore the non-Markovian nature of behavior, as human decisions are influenced by history and intrinsic motivation.

**Key Challenge**: Conventional BC directly learns the mapping $s_t \mapsto a_t$, yet cognitive science indicates that human decision-making is mediated by inner speech, i.e., $s_t \to m_t \to a_t$—the same environmental stimulus can elicit different behavioral responses depending on different internal monologues.

**Goal**: To construct an imitation learning framework capable of generating diverse behaviors and supporting fine-grained behavioral guidance without requiring additional demonstrations.

**Key Insight**: Formalizing Vygotsky's theory of inner speech as a computational framework—language serves not merely as a communication tool but as an internal cognitive mechanism that organizes behavior.

**Core Idea**: $P_\mathcal{H}(a|s) = \int p_\mathcal{H}(a|s,m) p_\mathcal{H}(m|s) dm$—actions are treated as jointly conditioned on state and inner speech. Inner speech is autonomously generated by a CVAE and can be overridden by designer-provided text descriptions to enable controllable generation.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

MIMIC consists of three components: ① a VLM language scaffold (generates behavioral descriptions during training) → ② a CVAE inner speech generator (autonomously generates inner speech embeddings from behavioral history) → ③ a diffusion policy behavior cloner (generates actions conditioned on observations and inner speech). At inference time, the agent periodically generates inner speech autonomously, while designers can inject an initial inner speech via text to steer behavior.

### Key Designs

1. **Inner Speech-conditioned Behavior Cloner**:

    - **Function**: Generates actions via a diffusion process conditioned on observation $s$ and inner speech $m$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Conditioning on inner speech allows the same state to produce different behavioral patterns, thereby enabling diversity.
    - **Mechanism**: Adopts DDPM-T (diffusion policy + Transformer architecture), training a denoising network $\hat{\epsilon}_\theta(\hat{\mathbf{a}}_\tau, s, m, \tau)$ with the loss:
    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{diff}}(\mathcal{D}_M) = \mathbb{E}_{(s,\mathbf{a}_0,m)\sim\mathcal{D}_M, \tau\sim[1,T_D]} \|\hat{\epsilon}(\mathbf{a}_\tau, s, m, \tau) - \epsilon\|_2^2$
    - Classifier-free guidance is applied: during training, $m$ is randomly zeroed out with probability $p$.
    - Input encoding: domain-specific encoders (CNN/features) for state; a 2-layer MLP for $m$; linear encoding for actions; cosine encoding for timesteps; all concatenated and fed into a Transformer.
    - **Novelty**: Unlike direct $s \to a$ BC, a language-mediated latent variable is introduced.

2. **Behavior-Conditioned Inner Speech Generator**:

    - **Function**: Autonomously generates inner speech embeddings from behavioral history.
    - **Design Motivation**: VLMs are unavailable at inference time, so the agent must generate its own behavioral guidance.
    - **Mechanism**: A CVAE is used, with both encoder and decoder conditioned on a past image sequence (via pooled convolutional representations). The CVAE generates CLIP-encoded inner speech with the training objective:
    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{is}}(\mathcal{D}_M) = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=W}^{T} \|m^{(i)} - \Psi_{\text{dec}}(\Psi_{\text{enc}}(m^{(i)}, \mathbf{I}_{t-W:t}^{(i)}), \mathbf{I}_{t-W:t}^{(i)})\|_2^2 + \beta \Delta_{KL}(\Psi_{\text{enc}}(m^{(i)}), \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}))$
    - $\beta$ is annealed during training to simulate the transition from detailed external language to compressed autonomous inner speech.
    - Periodic update: new inner speech is generated every $W$ steps: $m_t = \Psi_{\text{dec}}(\Psi_{\text{enc}}(\mathcal{H}_t), \mathcal{H}_t)$ if $t \bmod W = 0$, otherwise $m_t = m_{t-1}$.

3. **Vision-Language Model Scaffolding**:

    - **Function**: Generates behavioral descriptions for each demonstration trajectory as training targets for inner speech.
    - **Design Motivation**: Demonstration data contain no annotations of human thoughts; an external model is needed to supply language labels.
    - **Mechanism**: Demonstration image sequences are converted to GIFs and sent to a VLM (GPT-4o) with carefully designed prompts that elicit descriptions distinguishing different behaviors; these descriptions are then CLIP-encoded into embeddings $m^{(i)}$.
    - **Novelty**: Unlike Thought Cloning, which requires step-level human thought annotations, only trajectory-level descriptions are needed, making the approach more scalable.

4. **Steerable Behavior Guidance**:

    - At inference time, behavior is controlled via two mechanisms: ① designers supply a text description as the initial inner speech $m \leftarrow \mathcal{B}$; ② the polling window $W$ is set to control the update frequency of inner speech.
    - Behavioral style switching is achievable without training on additional demonstrations.

### Theoretical Foundations

Three structural properties of Vygotsky's inner speech theory are formalized:
- **Predicativity**: Inner speech emphasizes relations and actions over entities.
- **Semantic Condensation**: Complex strategies are compressed into compact representations (corresponding to the CVAE information bottleneck).
- **Temporal Regulatory Dynamics**: Inner speech operates over extended temporal scales, conditioned on behavioral history.

### Loss & Training

- The behavior cloner and inner speech generator are trained in a decoupled manner.
- Adam optimizer; learning rate adjusted per dataset.
- Initial update step $t_0 \in \{0, 1, W/2, W-1\}$; window $W \in \{1, 10, 20, 50, 100\}$ (low-timestep tasks) or $W \in \{100, 200, 300\}$ (high-timestep tasks).
- Random dropout probability $p \in \{0, 0.1\}$; CVAE $\beta = 0.1$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: D3IL Benchmark

| Environment | Method | Success Rate↑ | Distance↓ | Entropy↑ | State-wass↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aligning | BC | 0.665 | 0.111 | 0.474 | 0.696 |
| Aligning | MIMIC-S | **0.802** | **0.066** | 0.418 | **0.046** |
| Aligning | MIMIC-E | 0.723 | 0.085 | **0.615** | 0.049 |
| Sorting-Vision | BC | 0.797 | - | 0.360 | - |
| Sorting-Vision | MIMIC-S | **0.842** | - | 0.372 | - |
| Sorting-Vision | MIMIC-E | 0.808 | - | **0.449** | - |
| Stacking (1/2box) | BC | 0.803/0.488 | - | 0.206/0.150 | - |
| Stacking (1/2box) | MIMIC-E | **0.821/0.533** | - | **0.212/0.156** | - |

MIMIC comprehensively outperforms state-of-the-art BC baselines in both success rate and behavioral diversity (entropy).

### Ablation Study

| Inner Speech Form | Success Rate | Entropy |
|---|---|---|
| MIMIC (Language) | **Best** | **Best** |
| Random Vector | Better than BC | Moderate |
| K-means Clustering | Worst | Lowest |
| No Inner Speech (BC) | Baseline | Baseline |

- Language-based inner speech significantly outperforms random and clustering alternatives.
- CLIP outperforms plain-text MPNET encoding (a shared visual-language space is beneficial).
- GPT-4o-generated descriptions yield the best results, with open-source Qwen performing competitively.
- Steerable generation: GPT-4o-judged scores of 3.83–4.23/5.0, validating behavioral controllability.

### Key Findings

- Inner speech remains effective in visual observation environments, indicating that language provides complementary information beyond state observations.
- Wasserstein distance is substantially reduced, demonstrating that MIMIC's behavioral distribution more closely matches real human demonstrations.
- Periodic inner speech updates help correct behavioral drift, a known fragility of behavior cloning.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Innovative integration of cognitive science and ML**: Vygotsky's inner speech theory is rigorously formalized as a computational framework, providing a solid theoretical foundation.
- **Three capabilities achieved simultaneously**: High-fidelity imitation + behavioral diversity + inference-time controllability, without requiring additional demonstrations.
- **Elegant VLM scaffolding**: Avoids the costly requirement of step-level human thought annotations; only trajectory-level VLM descriptions are needed.
- **Information bottleneck ↔ Semantic condensation**: The variational compression of the CVAE naturally corresponds to the transition from detailed external descriptions to compressed autonomous inner speech.
- **Practical behavior correction mechanism**: Periodic inner speech updates serve as a mitigation strategy for the inherent fragility of behavior cloning.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The quality and diversity of VLM-generated descriptions impose an upper bound (descriptions from o4-mini are overly fine-grained, making it difficult for CLIP to distinguish between them).
- Validation is currently limited to relatively simple manipulation tasks and Overcooked; complex real-world tasks remain unexplored.
- The CLIP embedding space may be a bottleneck—stronger vision-language alignment models could yield further improvements.
- Claims of "biological plausibility" should be interpreted with caution—the work is functionally inspired rather than a simulation of neural mechanisms.
- Future directions: inner speech interactions in multi-agent settings, integration with RLHF, and tasks requiring longer temporal horizons.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Thought Cloning**: Requires step-level human thought annotations; performance drops ~40% without task conditioning.
- **Diffusion Policy (DDPM-T)**: The behavior cloning backbone adopted in this work.
- **D3IL Benchmark**: An imitation learning benchmark specifically designed for behavioral diversity evaluation.
- **Autotelic AI**: Also leverages language internalization but focuses on autonomous learning rather than behavioral diversity.
- **Insight**: Language can serve not only as an agent's output interface but also as an internal cognitive mediator that organizes behavior—offering a new perspective for LLM-based agent design.
