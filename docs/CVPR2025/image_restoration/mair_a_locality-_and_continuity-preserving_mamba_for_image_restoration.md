# MaIR: A Locality- and Continuity-Preserving Mamba for Image Restoration

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2412.20066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20066)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/XLearning-SCU/2025-CVPR-MaIR)  
**Area**: Image Restoration  
**Keywords**: Mamba, state space model, image restoration, scanning strategy, NSS, SSA, super-resolution, denoising

## TL;DR

This work proposes MaIR. Its key innovations include a Nested S-shaped Scanning (NSS) strategy that preserves locality via stripe partitioning and continuity via S-shaped paths, and a Sequence Shuffle Attention (SSA) module that intelligently aggregates sequences from different scanning directions using channel-level attention. MaIR achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across 14 datasets in four major tasks: super-resolution, denoising, deblurring, and dehazing.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: CNNs and Transformers have performed exceptionally well in image restoration, but they are limited by local receptive fields (small convolutional kernels in CNNs, window attention in Transformers), making it difficult to efficiently capture long-range dependencies. Recently, Mamba has attracted attention for modeling long sequences with linear complexity.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
1. Mamba requires flattening 2D images into 1D sequences, but existing scanning strategies violate the locality and continuity of the images.
2. The Z-shaped scanning in VMamba/ViM disrupts both locality and continuity.
3. The S-shaped scanning in Zigma maintains continuity but overlooks locality.
4. The window scanning in LocalMamba preserves locality, but the Z-shaped paths inside and outside the windows disrupt continuity.
5. Existing methods aggregate sequences of different directions via simple pixel-wise addition, ignoring the differences between sequences.

**Key Challenge**: How to simultaneously preserve spatial locality and continuity when flattening 2D images to 1D sequences? How to effectively aggregate heterogeneous sequences generated by different unfolding directions?

**Key Insight**: To design a zero-allocation scanning strategy that simultaneously satisfies both locality and continuity, paired with a channel-level attention sequence aggregation module.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

A standard three-stage architecture: shallow feature extraction $\rightarrow$ deep feature extraction (multiple Residual Mamba Groups) $\rightarrow$ reconstruction. The core module, MaIRM, consists of three steps: NSS, SSO, and SSA. First, NSS is used to flatten 2D features into four 1D sequences, then SSO processes the long-range dependencies of each sequence, and finally SSA intelligently aggregates the four sequences.

### Key Designs

**1. Nested S-shaped Scanning (NSS)**
- **Function**: Partition the feature map into multiple non-overlapping stripes, and adopt an S-shaped path both within and between these stripes.
- **Mechanism**:
    - Stripe partitioning preserves **locality**: pixels within each stripe are spatially adjacent.
    - S-shaped paths preserve **continuity**: adjacent tokens in the sequence remain spatially adjacent in the original image (unlike Z-shaped scanning, which jumps across rows).
    - Shift-stripe mechanism: adjacent modules alternatively shift the stripe origin (similar to the shifted windows in Swin Transformer) to ensure the locality and continuity of stripe boundaries.
    - 4 scanning directions: top-left $\rightarrow$ bottom-right, bottom-right $\rightarrow$ top-left, top-right $\rightarrow$ bottom-left, bottom-left $\rightarrow$ top-right.
- **Design Motivation**: To combine the strengths of both LocalMamba (locality) and Zigma (continuity) with zero additional computational overhead.

**2. Sequence Shuffle Attention (SSA)**
- **Function**: Replaces simple pixel-wise summation with channel-level attention weights to aggregate sequences from four directions.
- **Mechanism**:
    - Apply spatial global average pooling to the sequences of 4 directions $\rightarrow$ concatenate to obtain $\tilde{X} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ ($L = K \times D$).
    - Sequence shuffle: rearrange to $[x^1_1, x^2_1, x^3_1, x^4_1, x^1_2, ...]$ so that features of different directions belonging to the same channel are adjacent.
    - Group convolution (group=4) to generate attention weights $\rightarrow$ sequence unshuffle to restore the original order.
    - Weighted summation: $Y = \sum_{i=1}^{4} W^i * X^i$.
- **Design Motivation**: Different scanning directions carry complementary information; channel-level attention can adaptively select the most valuable directional information for each channel.

**3. Shift-Stripe Mechanism**
- **Function**: Alternates the use of original stripes and shifted stripes in adjacent MaIRM modules.
- **Mechanism**: The stripe width of the first module is $w_s$. In the second module, the first and last stripe widths are set to $w_s/2$, and the middle stripe widths are $w_s$. This ensures that the stripe boundaries of the previous module are covered by complete stripes in the current module.
- **Design Motivation**: To resolve the discontinuity and locality fragmentation issues near stripe boundaries.

### Loss & Training

- Super-resolution: $L_1$ loss $\mathcal{L} = \|y - y'\|_1$
- Denoising/deblurring/dehazing: Charbonnier loss $\mathcal{L} = \sqrt{\|y - y'\|^2 + \epsilon^2}$, where $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$
- Reconstruction: Pixel-shuffle upsampling + 3×3 conv for super-resolution; 3×3 conv + residual connections for denoising and other tasks.

### Ablation Study

The supplementary materials of the paper provide comparisons between NSS and other scanning strategies, as well as SSA against simple summation:
- NSS outperforms Z-shaped, S-shaped, and window-based scanning across all datasets.
- SSA improves performance by approximately 0.1-0.2 dB on Urban100 (x2) compared to pixel-wise summation.

### Key Findings

1. **Simultaneously preserving locality and continuity** is crucial for Mamba-based image restoration; both are indispensable.
2. **SSA channel-level attention aggregation** outperforms simple summation, proving that different channels indeed rely on different directional information.
3. The **Shift-stripe** mechanism acts similarly to the shifted window mechanism, effectively resolving boundary issues.
4. MaIR outperforms over 40 baselines (including CNNs, Transformers, and Mamba-based methods) across 14 datasets in 4 different tasks.
5. Both NSS and SSA in MaIR are designed with zero or negligible additional computational overhead.

## Highlights & Insights

- NSS is an extremely elegant design: its stripe partitioning (rather than windows) preserves locality while avoiding the continuity disruption caused by Z-shaped paths inside windows.
- The explicit decomposition of "locality" and "continuity" provides valuable guidance for designing 2D Mamba scanning strategies.
- The shuffle-groupconv-unshuffle pipeline of SSA is simple yet highly efficient, inspired by the channel shuffle in ShuffleNet.
- Comprehensive evaluation covering 14 datasets across 4 tasks enhances the reliability of the conclusions.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The stripe width $w_s$ is a fixed hyperparameter, which might not be optimal for different resolutions or tasks.
- Scanning is still performed in 4 directions (even though SSA provides a better aggregation method), resulting in 4x the computation of a single direction.
- An efficiency analysis on high-resolution images lacks detailed data.
- A comparison with the latest hybrid attention-SSM approaches (e.g., MambaIRv2) is not included.

## Related Work & Insights

- LocalMamba proposes window scanning to preserve locality at the expense of continuity; Zigma proposes S-shaped scanning to maintain continuity but neglects locality. This work unifies the two.
- The concept of shifted windows (Swin Transformer) is successfully transferred to stripes (shift-stripe).
- Channel shuffle from ShuffleNet is creatively adapted for sequence aggregation.
- Insight: The flattening process from image to sequence itself remains a core challenge in visual Mamba design.
