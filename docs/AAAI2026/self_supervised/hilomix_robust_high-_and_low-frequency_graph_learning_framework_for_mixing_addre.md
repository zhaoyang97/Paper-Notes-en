# HiLoMix: Robust High- and Low-Frequency Graph Learning Framework for Mixing Address Association

**Conference**: AAAI 2026  
**arXiv**: [2511.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07759)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Self-Supervised  
**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Blockchain Analysis, Frequency-Aware Contrastive Learning, Label Noise, Tornado Cash

## TL;DR

This paper proposes HiLoMix, a robust graph learning framework for the mixing address association task. It addresses three core challenges—graph sparsity, label scarcity, and label noise—through a Heterogeneous Attribute Mixing Interaction Graph (HAMIG), frequency-aware graph contrastive learning, and confidence-based label weighting supervision, respectively. HiLoMix surpasses the second-best baseline by 5.69%, 7.34%, and 15.61% on F1, AUC, and MRR.

## Background & Motivation

As mixing services such as Tornado Cash are increasingly exploited by malicious actors for illicit fund transfers, **Mixing Address Association** has become a critical security research task. Tornado Cash allows users to deposit assets into a pool and withdraw them anonymously, severing transaction chains. According to Elliptic, it facilitated the transfer of over $1.5 billion in illicit funds before being sanctioned.

Existing methods face three major challenges:

**Label Noise**: Available labels are typically generated by heuristic rules and inevitably contain noise, introducing ambiguity during training and undermining generalization.

**Label Scarcity**: Ground-truth associations are extremely limited, preventing models from establishing comprehensive decision boundaries.

**Graph Sparsity**: Sparse address associations yield highly sparse graph structures with many isolated nodes, limiting the efficiency of message passing and feature aggregation.

Specific limitations of prior approaches:
- **Heuristic methods** (based on timing, gas price, and other transaction features): poor scalability; only detectable for careless users; unable to identify adversaries actively evading detection.
- **BERT4ETH**: ignores structural relationships between accounts and only models transaction sequences.
- **MixBroker**: models address interactions as a MIG graph but is constrained by graph sparsity and label noise.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

HiLoMix comprises four core components:
1. **HAMIG Construction**: Heterogeneous Attribute Mixing Interaction Graph to enrich topological structure.
2. **Frequency-Aware Graph Contrastive Learning**: Learns robust node representations by contrasting high- and low-frequency views.
3. **Confidence-Based Supervision**: Assigns adaptive weights to noisy labels.
4. **Stacking Ensemble**: Integrates predictions from multiple heterogeneous models.

### Key Designs

#### 1. **HAMIG Construction (Addressing Graph Sparsity)**

The MIG proposed by MixBroker (containing only address association edges) is extended into a heterogeneous graph:

$$\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}_a, \mathcal{V}_t, \mathcal{E}_{at}, \mathcal{E}_{aa}, \mathcal{X})$$

- $\mathcal{V}_a$: account address nodes interacting with Tornado Cash
- $\mathcal{V}_t$: Tornado Cash smart contract nodes
- $\mathcal{E}_{at}$: mixing transaction edges (account ↔ contract) — **newly added**
- $\mathcal{E}_{aa}$: address association edges
- $\mathcal{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$: node feature matrix (197-dimensional), encoding interaction frequency, timestamps, and gas price statistics

**Effect**: Average node degree increases by approximately 45×, with clustering coefficient and graph density improving by several orders of magnitude.

#### 2. **Frequency-Aware Graph Contrastive Learning (Addressing Label Scarcity)**

Core Idea: Decompose the graph signal into high- and low-frequency components, process each with a corresponding high-pass or low-pass GNN, and apply contrastive learning.

**Step 1: Frequency Decomposition**  
An MLP estimates the edge smoothness probability $s_{ij}$ to partition the graph into a low-frequency view $\mathcal{G}^{(LF)}$ and a high-frequency view $\mathcal{G}^{(HF)}$:

$$A_{ij}^{(LF)} = s_{ij}, \quad A_{ij}^{(HF)} = 1 - s_{ij} \quad \text{where } A_{ij} = 1$$

**Step 2: Dual-Branch GNN**
- Low-pass GNN (captures smooth global semantics): $\mathcal{H}_l^{(LF)} = (I + \tilde{A}^{(LF)})\mathcal{H}_{l-1}^{(LF)}$
- High-pass GNN (preserves discriminative local signals): $\mathcal{H}_l^{(HF)} = (I - \alpha \cdot \tilde{A}^{(HF)})\mathcal{H}_{l-1}^{(HF)}$

**Step 3: InfoNCE Contrastive Loss**  
Contrasts the representations of each node across the two frequency views:

$$\mathcal{L}_{con} = \frac{1}{2|\mathcal{E}_{aa}|}\sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{aa}}[\mathcal{L}^{(LF)} + \mathcal{L}^{(HF)}]$$

#### 3. **Confidence-Based Supervision (Addressing Label Noise)**

Leveraging the memorization effect of deep networks—which first learn clean samples before fitting noisy ones—labels are dynamically partitioned into three categories:

**Step 1: Clean Label Set Extraction**  
A mutual loss (joint prediction loss from two networks) and a dynamic threshold are used:

$$\mathcal{L}_{thre}^t = \text{Percentile}(\mathcal{L}_{mul}^{ij}, 1 - \frac{t}{2 \cdot T_{\max}})$$

**Step 2: Confident-Flip Label Set Extraction**  
Triggered when both networks consistently and confidently predict a label different from the given one:

$$\mu(e_{ij}) = \sqrt{p_{ij,c}^{(LF)} \cdot p_{ij,c}^{(HF)}}$$

**Step 3: Differentiated Weights for Three Label Categories**
- $\mathcal{E}_{cl}$ (clean): weight $\xi=1$, original label used
- $\mathcal{E}_{cf}$ (confident-flip): weight $\xi=\mu(i)$, flipped label used
- $\mathcal{E}_{re}$ (remaining): weight $\xi=0.5$, original label used

**Step 4: Total Training Loss**

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{con} + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{sup}$$

#### 4. **Stacking Ensemble Prediction**

Since the high-pass and low-pass GNNs are architecturally homogeneous, direct combination yields limited gains. Three additional heterogeneous models (Random Forest, Logistic Regression, MLP) are introduced for stacking ensemble to enrich decision boundaries and representational capacity.

### Loss & Training

- The high-pass and low-pass GNNs are jointly trained using a contrastive loss combined with a confidence-based supervision loss.
- Adam optimizer; learning rate 0.003; batch size 128; 50 training epochs.
- Label partition threshold is dynamically adjusted: lenient in early epochs to retain more samples, progressively tightened thereafter.
- 5-fold cross-validation is used for the meta-learner in the stacking ensemble.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Dataset: 554,589 Tornado Cash mixing transactions (December 2019–January 2025), 106,982 account nodes, 375,255 edges.

| Method | F1 | AUC | MRR | Hits@5 | Hits@10 |
|------|-----|-----|-----|--------|---------|
| DeepWalk | 0.6844 | 0.7078 | 0.0866 | 0.0932 | 0.1940 |
| GCN | 0.7639 | 0.7795 | 0.1011 | 0.1233 | 0.3151 |
| GIN | 0.7869 | 0.8167 | 0.1415 | 0.1900 | 0.3802 |
| Co-teaching+ | 0.7690 | 0.8230 | 0.2712 | 0.4281 | 0.6169 |
| NRGL | 0.7866 | 0.8513 | 0.3989 | 0.5896 | 0.7263 |
| BERT4ETH | 0.6990 | 0.7897 | 0.3366 | 0.4694 | 0.5714 |
| MixBroker | 0.7930 | 0.8182 | 0.1062 | 0.1231 | 0.3323 |
| **HiLoMix** | **0.8382** | **0.9137** | **0.4612** | **0.6556** | **0.8037** |
| **Gain** | **5.69%** | **7.34%** | **15.61%** | **11.18%** | **10.65%** |

HiLoMix achieves consistent superiority across all metrics. The most substantial gain is observed in MRR (+15.61%), indicating that the model effectively ranks true associations at the top of the candidate list.

### Key Findings

1. **Heterogeneous models are critical in stacking**: Removing the heterogeneous base models causes the largest performance drop, demonstrating that homogeneous high- and low-pass GNNs alone cannot provide sufficient complementary information.
2. **Frequency decomposition outperforms standard GCN**: Replacing the dual-branch design with standard GCNs (w/o HiLo learning) leads to substantial degradation.
3. **HAMIG effectively alleviates sparsity**: Average node degree increases 45×, with graph density improving by several orders of magnitude.
4. **Confidence-based label partitioning effectively handles noise**: The dynamic threshold strategy enables the model to progressively filter erroneous labels during later training epochs.
5. **BERT4ETH underperforms standard GNNs**: Sequence modeling neglects structural information, resulting in inferior performance on the association task.
6. **Training efficiency is favorable**: 6.91 seconds per epoch, substantially faster than NRGL (15.27 seconds), with inference time under 0.01 seconds.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Systematic solution design**: Each module directly targets one of the three core challenges—label noise, label scarcity, and graph sparsity—resulting in a well-motivated and modular architecture.
- **The frequency-domain perspective** is particularly insightful: in the mixing address setting, low-frequency components capture global community structure, while high-frequency components capture anomalous local patterns.
- **The three-way confidence-based label partition** (clean / confident-flip / remaining) is more nuanced than simple noise filtering, effectively exploiting samples that are likely mislabeled yet correctable.
- **High practical value**: With the potential lifting of Tornado Cash sanctions, demand for mixing address association techniques may increase.
- A up-to-date ground-truth dataset (2019–2025) is constructed, covering the complete operational history of Tornado Cash, which constitutes a meaningful community contribution.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Validated only on Tornado Cash**: Although Tornado Cash accounts for approximately 95% of Ethereum mixing transaction volume, the generalizability of the proposed method to other mixing protocols remains unverified.
- **Heuristic labels as foundation**: Despite the noise-handling mechanism, the quality of the initial labels still constitutes a performance ceiling.
- **Feature engineering dependency**: The 197-dimensional feature set requires domain knowledge of Tornado Cash contract details; adaptation to new protocols would necessitate redesign.
- **Static graph modeling**: The temporal dynamics and evolving nature of the transaction graph are not modeled.
- **Privacy and ethical considerations**: Mixing address analysis may infringe on the privacy of legitimate users, necessitating a careful balance between security and privacy.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Relationship to MixBroker**: HiLoMix builds upon the MIG formulation by extending it to HAMIG and adding unsupervised and robust learning mechanisms.
- **Frequency-aware GCL** is inspired by spectral methods such as S3GCL, with an added signal decoupling step prior to decomposition.
- **Confidence-based label learning** draws from Co-teaching and NRGL, with the addition of a dedicated "confident-flip" category.
- **Implications for other blockchain analysis tasks** (exchange monitoring, DeFi anomaly detection): label noise and scarcity are pervasive challenges to which this framework may generalize.
