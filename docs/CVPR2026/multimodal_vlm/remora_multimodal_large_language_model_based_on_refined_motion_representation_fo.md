# ReMoRa: Multimodal Large Language Model based on Refined Motion Representation for Long-Video Understanding

**Conference**: CVPR 2026
**arXiv**: [2602.16412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16412)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: Long-video understanding, compressed video representation, motion vectors, state space model, optical flow refinement

## TL;DR
This paper proposes ReMoRa, which operates directly on compressed video representations (I-frames + motion vectors). A Refined Motion Representation (RMR) module refines coarse block-level motion vectors into fine-grained motion representations approximating optical flow, while a Hierarchical Motion State Space (HMSS) module performs linear-time long-range temporal modeling. ReMoRa surpasses baselines on LongVideoBench, NExT-QA, MLVU, and other benchmarks.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Video MLLMs have achieved notable progress on short videos, but long-video understanding (minute- to hour-scale) remains a significant challenge.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
   - Uniform frame sampling involves an inherent trade-off: sparse sampling misses critical events, while dense sampling is computationally infeasible due to quadratic attention complexity.
   - Frame-based methods repeatedly encode redundant content (e.g., static backgrounds), resulting in extremely low efficiency.
   - Token compression (pooling/reduction) applied after dense sampling blurs fine-grained details and motion cues.

**Key Challenge**: Long videos require dense temporal coverage to capture transient but important events, yet dense frame processing incurs prohibitive computational cost.

**Goal**: To exploit the inherent appearance–motion decomposition present in compressed video formats (H.264), achieving dense temporal coverage at minimal cost.

**Key Insight**: Modern video codecs already perform keyframe selection and motion compensation. Motion vectors serve as a cheap approximation of optical flow and can be utilized directly without decoding all frames.

**Core Idea**: Retain a small number of I-frames for appearance, replace intermediate frames with motion vectors for temporal dynamics, and use the RMR module to compensate for the noise and coarseness of motion vectors.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Video → H.264 compression encoding → Extract (I-frame, motion vector sequence) per GOP → Image Encoder encodes I-frames + RMR refines motion → HMSS fuses appearance and motion and performs cross-GOP temporal modeling → LLM generates answer.

### Key Designs

1. **Refined Motion Representation (RMR) Module**:

    - Input: Block-level motion vectors of P/B frames (codec-native, no decoding required).
    - Problem: These motion vectors are block-level, sparse, noisy, and temporally inconsistent.
    - Approach: A module is pre-trained to map coarse motion vectors to dense optical flow fields, supervised by dense optical flow generated by Co-Tracker3 using an $L_2$ loss.
    - During fine-tuning, it serves as a feature encoder outputting motion embeddings $E_M^{(k,t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_m \times d_s}$.
    - Design Motivation: Motion vectors are extremely cheap to compute but low in quality; after refinement they approach the information richness of optical flow while preserving efficiency.

2. **Hierarchical Motion State Space (HMSS) Module**:

    - Two-level structure mirroring the hierarchical organization of GOPs:
    - **Codec-aware Selective Scan (intra-GOP)**: A bidirectional Mamba block fuses I-frame appearance embeddings with motion embeddings, then extracts the top $N_p$ tokens as motion-enhanced I-frame representations.
    - **Bidirectional Token Mixer (inter-GOP)**: Summary vectors from all GOPs are concatenated and processed by bidirectional Mamba for global long-range modeling.
    - Sequence length is reduced by a factor of $L_g/N_p$ compared to naive flattening, enabling linear-time complexity for long-range dependency modeling.
    - Design Motivation: Avoids quadratic attention over >100K tokens while preserving both local (intra-GOP) and global (inter-GOP) temporal context.

3. **Scene-Adaptive GOP Construction**:

    - Videos are re-encoded using ffmpeg's scene-adaptive detection, which dynamically inserts I-frames at visual discontinuities.
    - This approach better reflects content structure than fixed-interval sampling and is equivalent to implicit keyframe extraction.

### Loss & Training
The RMR module is first pre-trained with an $L_2$ optical flow reconstruction loss. The full model is then fine-tuned with standard cross-entropy instruction tuning. The LLM backbone is fine-tuned with LoRA; the visual encoder is frozen.

### Key Findings
- ReMoRa achieves the best performance on LongVideoBench (+1.3), NExT-QA (+1.0), and MLVU (+1.3).
- On ActivityNet-QA, accuracy surpasses the second-best method by 8.4 percentage points, demonstrating the critical role of motion representation for temporal reasoning.
- Compared to EMA, which uses the same codec information, the RMR motion refinement module yields substantial improvements.
- Qualitative analysis shows that ReMoRa clearly outperforms LLaVA-Video on questions requiring fine-grained action understanding.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Exploiting the natural structure of compressed video**: By operating directly in the encoded domain without decoding all frames, this work breaks the paradigm of mandatory uniform RGB frame sampling. Although motion vectors are low in quality, they can be extracted densely; combined with the refinement module, they become high-quality temporal cues.
- **Elegant design of the RMR module**: Pre-training learns the mapping from coarse motion to dense optical flow; during fine-tuning it serves as a feature encoder, benefiting from the informativeness of optical flow while avoiding its computational overhead.
- **Hierarchical design of HMSS**: Intra-GOP fusion (analogous to segment-level attention) followed by inter-GOP modeling (analogous to video-level attention) naturally matches the codec structure and achieves linear complexity.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The method depends on H.264 encoding; its adaptability to other formats (HEVC, AV1) has not been verified.
- The maximum GOP length is fixed at 32 frames, which may result in sparse motion information for very long static scenes.
- RMR pre-training requires optical flow supervision generated by Co-Tracker3, increasing data preparation costs.
- ReMoRa is not the top performer on VideoMME (64.4 vs. 65.1), suggesting that motion information may not be critical in all scenarios.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Video-LaVIT**: Also uses codec information but performs only simple appearance–motion tokenization, lacking motion refinement and hierarchical modeling.
- **vs. EMA**: EMA introduces a GOP encoder but does not refine motion vectors; ReMoRa's RMR module addresses this quality gap.
- **vs. LongVU (token pruning)**: LongVU still processes RGB frames and merely reduces token count, whereas ReMoRa avoids redundant frame processing at the source.

## Supplementary Analysis
- ReMoRa uses 200K instruction fine-tuning samples from the LLaVA-Video-178K dataset, covering three task types: open-ended QA, multiple-choice QA, and captioning.
- Scene-adaptive GOP construction: ffmpeg's scene-adaptive detection automatically inserts I-frames at scene transitions, making GOP boundaries more semantically meaningful than fixed-interval GOPs.
- Maximum GOP length is 32 frames with a macroblock size of 4×4, ensuring fine-grained motion vector resolution.
- The visual encoder uses a frozen SigLIP ViT-SO; the LLM backbone is fine-tuned with LoRA, achieving high parameter efficiency.
- The 8.4-point accuracy lead over EMA on ActivityNet-QA is primarily attributed to the dataset's emphasis on long-span action understanding, which is more dependent on fine-grained motion information.
