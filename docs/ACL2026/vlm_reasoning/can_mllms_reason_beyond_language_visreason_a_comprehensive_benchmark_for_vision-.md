# Can MLLMs Reason Beyond Language? VisReason: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Vision-Centric Reasoning

**Conference**: ACL2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2605.25364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25364)  
**Code**: https://github.com/CASIA-IVA-Lab/VisReason  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM / Visual Reasoning / Benchmark  
**Keywords**: Vision-centric reasoning, multimodal evaluation, visual grounding, CoT, capability diagnosis

## TL;DR
VisReason constructs a multimodal benchmark containing 1,505 daily visual reasoning problems to specifically test whether models can reason directly based on visual evidence. Results show that even the strongest model achieves an average accuracy of only 47.5%, significantly lower than the human performance of 71.4%, and that CoT and larger reasoning budgets provide limited improvements.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Existing MLLMs are showing increasing strength on benchmarks like MMMU, MathVista, ScienceQA, and chart reasoning. Many of these results are interpreted as models possessing complex visual reasoning capabilities.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Many visual reasoning problems can actually be solved after "image-to-language" conversion. Diagnostics using Qwen3-VL-32B revealed that on datasets like MMMU, MMMU-Pro, and MathVista, performance drops by less than 6.35% when images are replaced with captions generated by Qwen-VL-Max, indicating that a large portion of reasoning in these tasks can be completed in the language space.

**Key Challenge**: Human visual reasoning often relies on direct observation of shapes, layouts, local differences, spatial relationships, and implicit rules. In contrast, MLLMs might simply compress images into text descriptions followed by linguistic reasoning. Existing benchmarks struggle to distinguish between "capability for visual reasoning" and "capability for reasoning after reading captions."

**Goal**: Construct a benchmark where visual evidence cannot be adequately replaced by language, evaluate proprietary and open-source MLLMs using a unified protocol, and analyze the impact of model capacity, CoT, reasoning budgets, and error types on vision-centric reasoning.

**Key Insight**: VisReason focuses on everyday visual scenes rather than STEM knowledge problems. The problems cover localization, spot-the-difference, pattern counting, 3D space, board games, Sudoku, geolocation clues, cue insight, and inductive/deductive reasoning, requiring models to verify back and forth between perception and reasoning.

**Core Idea**: Use "irreplaceability of visual evidence" as the benchmark design principle to examine whether MLLMs truly reason beyond language.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
VisReason aims to expose the phenomenon where many tasks deemed "visual reasoning" can be solved even after converting images to captions, suggesting models perform language reasoning rather than true visual analysis. Centered on the design principle that "visual evidence cannot be replaced by language," the paper first defines a taxonomic system for vision-centric reasoning, then manually collects, rewrites, and verifies a set of multi-format problems, and finally utilizes large-scale model evaluation and capability deconstruction to explain model failures. Specifically, inputs consist of images and questions, while outputs are categorized by task type into multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended answers, and bounding boxes. During evaluation, multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank answers are extracted via rules for exact matching; open-ended questions are scored strictly by GPT-5-mini; bounding boxes are matched using IoU and normalized per problem. Finally, accuracy for each category and overall average accuracy are reported.

### Key Designs

**1. Visual-Centric Data Filtering: Retaining only "irreplaceable visual evidence"**

If a problem can be fully replaced by a caption, answering it correctly does not demonstrate a model's visual reasoning capability. To address this, the authors collected candidates from online visual puzzles and Chinese civil service exam databases. Graduate student annotators standardized these into VQA formats and systematically filtered out four types of "pseudo-visual" samples: those answerable by image only, text only, those relying on external knowledge, and those with non-unique answers. Consequently, every remaining problem requires returning to the image itself to find evidence—providing the basis for the diagnostic finding that VisReason performance drops by 48.12% when images are replaced by captions, compared to less than 6.35% for older benchmarks.

**2. Three-layer Reasoning Taxonomy: Deconstructing visual reasoning into perception, structure, and concept**

Visual reasoning is a broad term; without deconstruction, it is difficult to identify specific bottlenecks. VisReason divides it into three layers with 10 categories and 36 sub-categories: the Perception layer includes Localized Reasoning, Spot the Difference, and Pattern Counting, primarily testing visual grounding; the Structure layer includes 3D-Spatial, Board, Sudoku, and Geolocation, testing the extraction of structured states and rule execution; the Concept layer includes Cue Insight, Inductive Reasoning, and Deductive Reasoning, focusing on abstract reasoning. This hierarchy allows failures in "localization/difference" to be attributed to grounding, and "Sudoku/board" failures to structural state extraction.

**3. Model Diagnosis Protocol: Dissecting scale, reasoning budget, CoT, and error types**

Average scores only indicate difficulty without explaining "why." The protocol evaluates a wide range of models—GPT-4o, o4-mini, GPT-5 series, Gemini series, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, thinking/non-thinking versions of Qwen3-VL, MiMo-VL, and Keye-VL—under unified zero-shot CoT prompting. It overlays four types of analysis: caption replacement diagnostics, category correlation, error taxonomy, and atomic capability analysis. This decomposition leads to conclusions such as "perceptual and reasoning errors account for approximately 90%" and "CoT provides almost no help for tasks dominated by visual evidence."

### Loss & Training
This paper does not train models but focuses on benchmark construction and zero-shot evaluation. During the problem construction phase, multiple rounds of manual verification ensure consistency between images, questions, and answers. During evaluation, all models use prompts corresponding to the four task types and output according to a uniform answer-format requirement. For bounding boxes, predicted boxes and ground truth boxes are matched using Hungarian matching; an IoU greater than 0.5 is considered correct and normalized by task.

### Ablation Study

| Analysis Item | Key Metric | Description |
|---------------|------------|-------------|
| Caption replacement | Prev. benchmarks drop < 6.35%, VisReason drops 48.12% | VisReason relies more on direct visual evidence |
| Model capacity scaling | Qwen2.5/Qwen3 series generally improve with scale | Capacity helps, but remains far below human levels |
| Reasoning budget scaling | GPT-5-mini shows gains from low to mid budget, saturates at high | Test-time compute shows diminishing marginal returns |
| Error types | Perception + Reasoning errors account for ~90% | Issues are primarily visual parsing, grounding, counting, and hallucination |

### Key Findings
- VisReason clearly distinguishes model capabilities: weak models perform near random, while the strongest proprietary models remain far below human performance, indicating it is not a simple saturated benchmark.
- CoT may help with high-level cue insight, induction, and deduction, but provides very little help—and may even cause degradation—for tasks dominated by visual evidence like spot-the-difference, 3D space, and counting.
- Low scores on bounding-box tasks are not merely due to output format issues; they require maintaining spatial hypotheses while reasoning, which is harder than simple detection.

## Highlights & Insights
- The most valuable contribution is the redefinition of "visual reasoning evaluation" standards: it is not visual reasoning just because there is an image; one must prove the visual evidence cannot be easily replaced by language.
- The taxonomic design covers daily visual reasoning, exposing basic visual cognitive shortfalls of MLLMs more effectively than mathematical charts or academic knowledge.
- The results provide a strong counter-example to "CoT solves reasoning": when the bottleneck is visual parsing and evidence localization, verbal thinking budgets cannot automatically compensate for perceptual defects.

## Limitations & Future Work
- VisReason only covers static images and does not involve video, interactive perception, embodied tasks, or continuous action decision-making; future work could extend to dynamic visual reasoning.
- Data is sourced from online puzzle sets and exam databases; despite manual filtering, style bias may persist. Model training set contamination and benchmark overfitting remain long-term risks.
- Open-ended questions are scored by GPT-5-mini, which introduces the subjectivity of the judge model. Future work could include more manual review or multi-judge consistency analysis.
- Currently focused on evaluation rather than training. In the future, VisReason's atomic capabilities could be used as a training curriculum, designing specialized data for bottlenecks like correspondence/difference and structured state extraction.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs MMMU / MathVista**: These benchmarks evaluate visual academic knowledge and mathematical reasoning, but some problems allow captions to retain most information; VisReason explicitly pursues caption-irreplaceability.
- **vs Sudoku-Bench / REBUS / VGRP-Bench**: These tasks focus on specific puzzles or rule-based reasoning, while VisReason covers broader daily visual scenes and multiple answer formats.
- **vs CoT-based MLLM reasoning**: While CoT strengthens language-side reasoning, VisReason’s results remind us that multimodal reasoning requires joint training of visual parsing, grounding, and symbolic reasoning, rather than just extending text output.
