# One Image is Worth a Thousand Words: A Usability Preservable Text-Image Collaborative Erasing Framework

**Conference**: ICML2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.11131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11131)  
**Code**: [Ferry-Li/Co-Erasing](https://github.com/Ferry-Li/Co-Erasing)  
**Area**: Diffusion Model Safety / Concept Erasing  
**Keywords**: Concept Erasing, Diffusion Model Safety, Text-Image Collaboration, Negative Guidance, Cross-Attention

## TL;DR

This paper proposes Co-Erasing, which introduces image supervision into the concept erasing pipeline for the first time. By leveraging text-image collaborative negative guidance and a text-guided image concept refinement module, Co-Erasing significantly improves the erasing efficacy of undesirable concepts while preserving the generation quality (usability) of benign concepts.

## Background & Motivation

Text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) can generate harmful content such as NSFW. **Concept Erasing** aims to modify model weights to prevent the generation of specific undesirable concepts. Existing methods (such as ESD, AdvUnlearn, SalUn, etc.) rely entirely on text prompts as erasing guidance, facing two core limitations:

**Limitation 1: Text-Image Modality Gap**
- Semantically completely unrelated words (e.g., "rhodesian", "birth") can still induce the model to generate nudity.
- Erasing in the text space cannot cover all triggering paths within the image space.

**Limitation 2: Concept Entanglement and Text Description Limitations**
- Testing with the ESD framework shows that extending the description of "nudity" from 1 word to 10 words results in rapid saturation of the erasing effect.
- More text descriptions tend to inadvertently damage unrelated concepts, leading to FID degradation.

The authors demonstrate through CLIP similarity experiments that the semantic similarity between NSFW images self-generated by clean models and residual undesirable generations is much higher than that of the text "nudity". This indicates that **images represent the model's intrinsic knowledge of undesirable concepts much better than text**.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Co-Erasing introduces an image branch into the negative guidance framework of ESD. The core modifications consist of three parts:

1. **Decoupled Cross-Attention**: Text and images interact with U-Net separately through independent K/V projections.
2. **Text-Guided Image Concept Refinement**: An attention mechanism is used to extract visual features from images that are relevant to the target concept.
3. **Self-Generated Images as Visual Templates**: Undesirable images generated by the clean model using the prompt "a photo of c" are used as erasing guidance.

### Decoupled Cross-Attention

Text embeddings $c_{\text{text}} \in \mathbb{R}^{b \times 77 \times 768}$ and image embeddings $c_{\text{image}} \in \mathbb{R}^{b \times 4 \times 768}$ perform cross-attention with the shared Query separately:

$$\mathbf{Z}_t^{\text{text}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\mathbf{V}, \quad \mathbf{Z}_t^{\text{image}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{K}')^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\mathbf{V}'$$

where the text and image branches have independent $\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{W}_v$ and $\mathbf{W}'_k, \mathbf{W}'_v$ respectively. The final output is $\mathbf{Z}_t^{\text{att}} = \mathbf{Z}_t^{\text{text}} + \mathbf{Z}_t^{\text{image}}$.

**Key Designs**: The image branch is only utilized during training. During inference, the model architecture remains unchanged, and the concept knowledge is removed from the U-Net via full-parameter fine-tuning.

### Text-Guided Image Concept Refinement

Images often contain multiple concepts (e.g., an image of a church may also contain trees), and directly using them can inadvertently erase unrelated concepts. The refinement module extracts visual features most relevant to the target text via cross-attention:

$$c_{\text{image}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}^r (\mathbf{K}^r)^\top}{\sqrt{d^r}}\right)\mathbf{V}^r$$

where $\mathbf{Q}^r = \mathcal{E}_{\text{image}}(\mathbf{X})$ is the image encoding, and $\mathbf{K}^r = \mathbf{V}^r = \mathcal{E}_{\text{text}}(\mathbf{Y})$ is the text encoding of the target concept. The effect is similar to using text as an attention mask to focus on concept-related regions in the image (e.g., focusing on the cross and Gothic windows in a church image while ignoring the trees).

### Negative Guidance Erasing Objective

Based on the negative guidance formulation of ESD combined with Tweedie's formula:

$$\epsilon_{\theta^*}(\mathbf{Z}_t, c, t) \leftarrow \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t, t) - \eta \left[\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t, c, t) - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t, t)\right]$$

where $c = [c_{\text{text}}, c_{\text{image}}]$ and $\eta$ is the guidance scale. During training, full-parameter fine-tuning is performed on the U-Net to minimize the probability of the model generating the target concept given the text + image condition.

### Self-Generated Image Strategy

Using images generated by the model itself rather than real datasets is motivated by:
- t-SNE visualization shows that the distribution of self-generated images is more consistent with the model's internal concept knowledge.
- There are statistical discrepancies between real NSFW datasets and the distribution learned by the model.
- One image is randomly sampled from $n$ pre-generated images during each iteration.

## Training & Experimental Settings

- **Base Model**: Stable Diffusion v1.4
- **Image Encoder**: Pre-trained CLIP image encoder (frozen), used only during training
- **Erasing Tasks**: nudity, style (Van Gogh style), object (parachute/church/tench)
- **Image Sources**: $n$ images generated by clean SD using the template "a photo of c" (refer to Appendix for specific values of $n$)
- **Baselines**: ESD, FMN, AC, UCE, SPM, SH, ED, SalUn, AdvUnlearn (9 in total)
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
    - Erasing Efficacy: pre-ASR, ASR, P4D, CCE (lower is better)
    - Generation Quality: FID (lower is better), CLIP Score (higher is better)
    - Robustness: Ring-A-Bell (RAB) red-team attack

## Main Results

### Erasing Efficacy vs. Generation Quality

Co-Erasing outperforms existing methods comprehensively in terms of the efficacy-usability trade-off:

| Dimension | Co-Erasing Advantage |
|------|--------------|
| Nudity Erasing | ASR decreases from 76.05% (ESD) to 16.96%, while FID only slightly increases to 18.77 (17.29 for ESD). |
| Style Erasing | Van Gogh erasing performance is comparable to SalUn, but with significantly better FID/CLIP. |
| Object Erasing | High erasing success rates on parachute/church/tench without significant quality degradation. |

### Ablation Study (Nudity Erasing)

| Configuration | pre-ASR↓ | ASR↓ | FID↓ | CLIP↑ |
|------|---------|------|------|-------|
| text only | 20.42 | 76.05 | 17.29 | 0.302 |
| text + image (no refine) | 4.30 | 32.60 | 22.98 | 0.301 |
| image only | 5.08 | 41.52 | 25.82 | 0.298 |
| text + image + refine | **0.85** | **16.96** | **18.77** | **0.302** |

The refinement module drastically reduces FID from 22.98 to 18.77 (close to text-only) while further reducing ASR to 16.96%.

### Cross-Framework Transfer (RAB Attack)

Co-Erasing can be integrated into other erasing frameworks as a plug-and-play module:
- ESD + Co-Erasing: RAB_K16 decreases from 0.47 to 0.18.
- MACE + Co-Erasing: All three RAB metrics drop to 0.00 with virtually unchanged FID.
- SLD + Co-Erasing: Nudity detection on the I2P dataset decreases from 125 to 22.

### Multi-Concept Erasing

When combined with MACE to erase 5 object categories (dog/cat/bird/fish/horse), the $H_c$ comprehensive metric remains comparable or improves across most categories, demonstrating the scalability of the method.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Dependence on Self-Generated Image Quality**: Erasing efficacy depends on whether the clean model can accurately generate representative images of the target concept; the performance gain from image guidance might be limited for concepts poorly represented by the model.
2. **Increased Training Overhead**: Introducing the image encoder branch and the refinement module increases training computation, though inference is not affected.
3. **Validated Only on SD v1.4 (UNet Architecture)**: Not extended to DiT architectures (e.g., FLUX, SD3) or larger-scale models.
4. **Scalability of Simultaneous Multi-Concept Erasing**: Although compatible with MACE, each concept requires independent generation of image templates.
5. **Erasing is Still Not Perfect**: ASR remains at 16.96% (nudity), leaving room for potential bypasses.

## Reproducibility Points

- Code is open-sourced: [github.com/Ferry-Li/Co-Erasing](https://github.com/Ferry-Li/Co-Erasing)
- Based on Stable Diffusion v1.4, utilizing a pre-trained CLIP image encoder for the image branch.
- Self-generated images are produced using the template "a photo of c".
- Evaluation relies on standard tools such as NudeNet (nudity detection), UDA (adversarial prompt generation), CLIP Score, and FID.
- Training details (learning rate, number of iterations, $\eta$ values, etc.) are provided in Appendix A.4 of the paper.

## Highlights & Insights

**Pros**:
- Analyzes the limitations of text-only erasing methods from the perspective of the modality gap with a clear and convincing motivation, verified by clever experimental designs.
- The design of the text-guided refinement module is elegant, and ablation studies prove it is key to maintaining usability (reducing FID from 22.98 to 18.77).
- Excellent framework generalizability, allowing plug-and-play integration into multiple baselines such as ESD, SLD, and MACE.

**Cons**:
- The definition and analysis of the "modality gap" are somewhat intuitive and lack solid theoretical support.
- Insufficient sensitivity analysis of hyperparameters such as the number of self-generated images ($n$) and the sampling strategy.
- Lack of verification on popular newer models and DiT architectures (such as SDXL, FLUX), which limits its current practical application value.

**Insights**:
- The approach of "teaching the model to forget using bad samples generated by itself" is simple and effective, mirroring the self-play concept.
- The text-query-image-as-key design of the refinement module can be transferred to other scenarios that require concept-level image feature extraction.
