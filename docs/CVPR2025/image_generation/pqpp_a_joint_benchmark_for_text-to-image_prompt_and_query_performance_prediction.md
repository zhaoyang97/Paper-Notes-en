# PQPP: A Joint Benchmark for Text-to-Image Prompt and Query Performance Prediction

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2406.04746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04746)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Eduard6421/PQPP)  
**Area**: Image Generation / Information Retrieval  
**Keywords**: Prompt Performance Prediction, Query Performance Prediction, Text-to-Image, Human-Annotated Benchmark, Diffusion Models

## TL;DR

PQPP is proposed, the first joint benchmark for text-to-image prompt and query performance prediction, containing over 10K queries and 1.6 million human annotations. It is found that query difficulty in generation and retrieval is almost uncorrelated (Pearson correlation is only 0.135).

## Background & Motivation

Text-to-image generation has emerged as a powerful alternative to traditional image retrieval, with generative diffusion models producing high-quality, realistic images. In the information retrieval domain, query performance prediction (QPP) is an active research area—predicting the difficulty of a query for a retrieval system in advance to trigger optimization strategies such as automatic query reformulation or resource allocation.

However, in the field of text-to-image generation, **prompt performance prediction** remains an almost unexplored problem: given a text prompt, can one predict the quality of the image generated by a diffusion model? If a prompt can be predicted as difficult, the system can proactively guide the user to optimize the prompt, allocate more computational resources, or warn them in advance of poor generation outcomes.

The closest existing work (Bizzozzero et al.) uses automatically generated ground truth to assess prompt difficulty, but automatic annotations may introduce significant bias. More importantly, while generation and retrieval tasks utilize the same textual queries, do they share the same "difficulty" characteristics? This question has never been systematically investigated.

The core contribution of this paper is the establishment of the first **human-annotated joint benchmark PQPP**, containing 10,200 text queries annotated with both generation and retrieval performance, allowing for cross-task comparison of query difficulty and evaluation of various predictors.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The construction workflow of PQPP: (1) Out of approximately 590K captions in MS COCO, 10,000 queries are selected via k-means clustering, supplemented by 200 queries from DrawBench, totaling 10,200 queries. (2) Two generative models (SDXL, GLIDE) are used to generate 2 images each $\rightarrow$ 247K human annotations are collected. (3) Two retrieval models (CLIP, BLIP-2) are used to retrieve images $\rightarrow$ 1.39 million human annotations are collected. (4) Multiple predictors are designed and evaluated.

### Key Designs

1. **Prompt Assessment (Generation Assessment)**:
    - Function: Establish a human-judgment-based ground truth of generation quality for each prompt.
    - Mechanism: For each prompt, SDXL and GLIDE generate 2 images each, plus the original MS COCO image as a calibration control, totaling 5 images. 147 annotators rate each image on a custom web interface as highly relevant (score=2), somewhat relevant (1), irrelevant (0), or unrealistic (-1). Fleiss' $\kappa$ is used for quality control ($\kappa > 0.4$), and outlier annotations are removed via majority voting. The final prompt performance (HBPP) is defined as the average relevance score of all its generated images.
    - Design Motivation: Automatic evaluation (e.g., CLIP score) may align poorly with human perception, while human annotations provide a more reliable ground truth.

2. **Query Assessment (Retrieval Assessment)**:
    - Function: Establish a human-judgment-based ground truth of retrieval quality for each query.
    - Mechanism: CLIP ViT-B/32 and BLIP-2 ViT-Large are used for retrieval. Potential relevant images are pre-screened based on Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (threshold 0.7, restricting each query to at most 2,000 images). Then, 100 annotators perform a binary assessment (relevant/irrelevant) for each image (each image annotated by 2 people). Finally, 530K relevant images are retained (filtering out about 2/3 of the candidate images). P@10 and RR are used to measure query performance.
    - Design Motivation: Large-scale human verification is required to guarantee the reliability of the retrieval ground truth.

3. **Performance Predictors**:
    - Function: Predict prompt/query performance either before (pre-execution) or after (post-execution) generation/retrieval.
    - Mechanism: Pre-predictors include text features (word count, number of WordNet synsets) and a fine-tuned BERT model; post-predictors include a fine-tuned Long-CLIP (using a regression head to predict generation relevance / classification head for retrieval relevance), NQC, and WIG (based on retrieval score distribution statistics). The training, validation, and test splits are 6,080 / 2,040 / 2,080, respectively.
    - Design Motivation: Compare the performance of pre- and post-predictors to establish baselines for future research.

### Loss & Training

- **BERT fine-tuning**: A regression head is added to a pre-trained BERT-base-cased model, which is then fine-tuned on HBPP/P@10/RR.
- **CLIP fine-tuning**: Regression/classification heads are added to Long-CLIP ViT-B/32, which takes (query, image) pairs as input.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Pearson correlation coefficient and Kendall's $\tau$ are used to measure the correlation between predicted values and ground truth.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Correlation of difficulty between generation and retrieval (Full Dataset, Table 2):

| Comparison | Pearson | Kendall $\tau$ |
|------|---------|----------------|
| HBPP vs P@10 | 0.135 | 0.093 |
| HBPP vs RR | 0.072 | 0.048 |
| P@10 vs RR | 0.560 | 0.512 |

Generation performance prediction (SDXL $\rightarrow$ HBPP, partial results):

| Predictor | Category | Pearson | Kendall $\tau$ |
|--------|------|---------|----------------|
| #synsets | Pre | 0.162 | 0.113 |
| BERT fine-tuned | Pre | 0.329 | 0.234 |
| CLIP fine-tuned | Post | 0.369 | 0.262 |

### Ablation Study

| Cross-Task Experiments | Description | Pearson |
|-----------|------|---------|
| SDXL Predictor $\rightarrow$ GLIDE | Cross-model transfer | Slightly decreased but still significant |
| Generation Predictor $\rightarrow$ Retrieval | Cross-task transfer | Almost ineffective |
| CLIP Predictor $\rightarrow$ BLIP-2 | Cross-model retrieval | Remains effective |

### Key Findings

- Query difficulties for generation and retrieval are nearly orthogonal (Pearson correlation is only 0.135), demonstrating that prompt performance prediction warrants dedicated research.
- The supervised pre-predictor (fine-tuned BERT) approaches the performance of the post-predictor on the generation task.
- Prompts that are difficult to generate but easy to retrieve typically describe specific athletic poses (where MS COCO is rich in sports images); prompts that are difficult to retrieve but easy to generate often contain concrete attribute descriptions (generative models excel at details but retrieval struggles to match them).

## Highlights & Insights

- **The discovery of orthogonality between generation and retrieval is the most valuable contribution**: Despite being seemingly similar tasks (both text $\rightarrow$ image), their query difficulty profiles are entirely different. This implies that one cannot simply reuse QPP methods from information retrieval for generative scenarios.
- **The scale of over 1.6 million human annotations is impressive**: With 247 annotators participating, the annotation pipeline is rigorously designed (control sets, calibration images, outlier removal via majority voting), providing a high-quality benchmark for future studies.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Only two generative models (SDXL and GLIDE) were used, lacking coverage of the latest DiT-based models.
- The data sources primarily rely on MS COCO captions, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world user prompts.
- The retrieval and generation tasks share the same dataset (MS COCO), which might introduce data bias.
- Future work can extend this to other text-guided generation tasks, such as text-to-video and text-to-3D.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs Bizzozzero et al.**: The only similar work, but it relies on automatically annotated ground truth, which may introduce bias. PQPP is the first prompt performance prediction benchmark based on human judgment.
- **vs Traditional Text QPP**: Traditional QPP methods focus on text retrieval, whereas multimodal scenarios require separate, independent investigations.
- **vs Human Feedback Works (ImageReward, HPS)**: These works target improving generation quality, whereas PQPP focuses on predicting query difficulty, making them complementary fields.
