# RebuttalAgent: Strategic Persuasion in Academic Rebuttal via Theory of Mind

**Conference**: ICLR2026  
**arXiv**: [2601.15715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15715)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Zhitao-He/RebuttalAgent)  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning  
**Keywords**: academic rebuttal, Theory of Mind, strategic persuasion, GRPO, self-reward, reward model  

## TL;DR
This paper is the first to introduce Theory of Mind (ToM) into academic rebuttal, proposing a three-stage ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) framework: first modeling the reviewer's mental state, then formulating a persuasion strategy, and finally generating evidence-grounded responses. Combined with self-reward RL training and a dedicated Rebuttal-RM evaluator, the approach achieves an average improvement of 18.3% over the base model.

## Background & Motivation
- Academic rebuttal is not a simple technical debate but rather **strategic communication** under severe information asymmetry (analogous to a dynamic game of incomplete information).
- Authors lack knowledge of reviewers' backgrounds, inherent biases, or the cascading effects of their responses.
- **Root Cause of existing methods**: Prior approaches primarily rely on SFT over review datasets, learning only superficial linguistic patterns (surface-level politeness but template-driven), without strategic depth.
- Successful rebuttals fundamentally require **perspective-taking**: knowing when to concede, when to stand firm, and when to reframe the narrative.
- This cognitive capability is referred to in cognitive science as Theory of Mind (ToM)—understanding others' beliefs, intentions, and viewpoints to predict their behavior.

## Method

### Overall Architecture: ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) Three-Stage Reasoning
The complex rebuttal task is decomposed into three coherent steps:

**Input**: original manuscript $M$, review $R_i$, target comment $c_{target}$  
**Output**: strategic response $r_{target} = \mathcal{G}(M, R_i, c_{target})$

### Key Design 1: Hierarchical Reviewer Profiling
- **Macro-level Analysis**: Infers the reviewer's overall intent across four dimensions: Overall Stance, Overall Attitude, Dominant Concern, and Reviewer Expertise. Produces a structured macro profile to guide global strategy and tone.
- **Micro-level Analysis**: Decomposes specific comments across four dimensions: Significance, Methodology, Experimental Rigor, and Presentation. Produces a micro profile to guide targeted responses.

### Key Design 2: ToM-Driven Strategy Generation + Evidence Fusion
- **Strategy Generation**: Based on the complete reviewer profile and target comment, the LLM synthesizes a concise high-level strategic plan—deciding *how* to respond before deciding *what* to respond—ensuring replies strategically address the reviewer's underlying concerns rather than passively reacting to surface-level issues.
- **Evidence-Grounded Response**: A three-stage contextual retrieval module segments the manuscript, encodes it into embeddings, ranks chunks by cosine similarity, and selects top-$k$ chunks. Response generation is conditioned on: reviewer profile $\mathcal{P}$, strategy $S$, retrieved chunks $C_E$, and original response $r_{orig}$.

### Key Design 3: Self-Reward Reinforcement Learning
**Data Construction (RebuttalBench, 70K samples)**:
- Source: Re2-rebuttal dataset; GPT-4.1 parses 200K+ comment-response pairs.
- TSR chains are generated by a mixture of teacher models (GPT-4.1, Claude 3.5).
- Comments requiring new experiments are excluded; the focus is on linguistic persuasion and strategic argumentation.

**Two-Stage Training**:
1. **SFT Cold Start**: Qwen3-8B backbone learns structured TSR reasoning.
2. **GRPO RL + Self-Reward**: A four-dimensional reward function is defined as:
$$R(o) = w_1 R_{format} + w_2 R_{think} + w_3 R_{resp} + w_4 R_{div}$$
    - $R_{format}$: format correctness (binary)
    - $R_{think}$: reasoning quality (model self-evaluation of Analysis and Strategy)
    - $R_{resp}$: response quality (model self-evaluation of persuasiveness, clarity, and evidence use)
    - $R_{div}$: response diversity (semantic dissimilarity from pre-defined negative samples, guarding against reward hacking)

### Evaluator: Rebuttal-RM
- Fine-tuned on Qwen3-8B with 102K training samples from three sources: original author responses, GPT-4.1-refined responses, and multi-model-generated responses.
- Outputs multi-dimensional scores with explanations.
- Human agreement (average 0.812) significantly exceeds GPT-4.1 (0.745).

### Key Findings
1. **ToM analysis is the core contribution**: Removing the ToM stage leads to significant performance degradation, confirming the necessity of reviewer modeling.
2. **Self-reward RL is effective**: Compared to pure SFT, the RL stage yields significant gains in persuasiveness and constructiveness.
3. **An 8B model can rival large models**: RebuttalAgent matches or surpasses GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-R1 on multiple metrics.
4. **Rebuttal-RM outperforms general-purpose judges**: The specialized evaluator is more reliable than general LLM judges.
5. **Diversity reward mitigates reward hacking**: $R_{div}$ effectively prevents model collapse into template-driven responses.

## Highlights & Insights
- First work to introduce Theory of Mind into academic rebuttal, reframing the problem from a game-theoretic perspective.
- The TSR three-stage framework elegantly decomposes a complex task into trainable sub-tasks.
- The self-reward mechanism eliminates the additional training cost of a separate reward model.
- The Rebuttal-RM evaluator has independent application value beyond this work.
- Code and models are open-sourced.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Comments requiring new experiments or data are excluded, yet such comments constitute a non-trivial fraction of real-world rebuttals.
- Contextual retrieval is based on the full manuscript; long papers may result in key information being missed.
- The quality ceiling of self-reward is bounded by the capability of the SFT model.
- Evaluation relies primarily on automated metrics, with limited-scale human evaluation.
- In practice, the system should be used with caution and must not replace the author's own critical thinking.

## Related Work & Insights
- **LLM-assisted research**: Spans literature summarization, hypothesis generation, and full paper writing.
- **Academic rebuttal research**: The Re2 dataset (Zhang et al., 2025) provides the original review-rebuttal data.
- **Theory of Mind in AI**: Machine ToM (Rabinowitz et al., 2018); GPT-4 has demonstrated preliminary ToM capabilities.
- **Reasoning LLMs**: DeepSeek-R1's GRPO algorithm provides the foundation for RL post-training.

## Rating
⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

The problem formulation is novel; modeling rebuttal as a game of information asymmetry and incorporating ToM is a creative contribution. The TSR framework is logically well-structured. The primary concern is that excluding comments requiring experiments reduces task difficulty, and real-world effectiveness requires further validation.
