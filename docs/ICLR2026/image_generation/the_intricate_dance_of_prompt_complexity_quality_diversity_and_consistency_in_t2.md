# The Intricate Dance of Prompt Complexity, Quality, Diversity, and Consistency in T2I Models

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2510.19557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19557)
**Code**: None
**Area**: Image Generation / Text-to-Image
**Keywords**: T2I models, prompt complexity, synthetic data, diversity, consistency, diffusion models

## TL;DR

This paper systematically investigates how prompt complexity affects three key dimensions of synthetic data generated by T2I models—quality, diversity, and consistency—proposes a new evaluation framework, and finds that prompt expansion, as an inference-time intervention, optimally balances diversity and aesthetic quality.

## Background & Motivation

Text-to-image (T2I) models can generate virtually unlimited synthetic data, offering substantial advantages over finite real-world datasets. Prior work evaluates the utility of synthetic data along three key dimensions: **quality** (visual fidelity of generated images), **diversity** (coverage of different visual patterns), and **consistency** (alignment between images and text prompts). However, prompt engineering—the primary means of interacting with T2I models—lacks a systematic understanding of how prompt complexity affects these three dimensions.

Specifically, the following critical questions remain open:

**The effect of prompt complexity is understudied**: How do images generated from simple prompts (e.g., "a cat") differ from those generated by complex prompts (e.g., "an orange Maine Coon cat sitting on an antique sofa with sunlight streaming through the window") in terms of quality, diversity, and consistency?

**Distribution gap between synthetic and real data**: How can this gap be systematically quantified?

**Comparison of inference-time intervention methods**: What are the respective trade-offs of methods such as guidance, negative prompts, and prompt expansion?

The paper's core insight is that a complex trade-off exists between prompt complexity and generation quality, and understanding this "intricate dance" is essential for effectively leveraging synthetic data.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The paper adopts a methodology combining theory and experiment:

1. **Synthetic experiments**: Controlled experiments in simplified settings are first conducted to reveal the essential relationship between prompt complexity and generalization difficulty.
2. **Theoretical analysis**: Mathematical derivations explain the observed phenomena.
3. **Large-scale empirical studies**: Theoretical findings are validated across multiple real-world datasets.

### Key Designs

#### 1. **Synthetic Experiments and Theoretical Analysis**

Core finding: generalizing from more specific conditions to more general ones (simplified prompts) is harder than the reverse, because the former requires an estimated likelihood that the diffusion model does not directly learn.

From the perspective of conditional generation, diffusion models learn the conditional distribution $p(x|c)$, where $c$ is the text condition. When prompts are more detailed, the condition $c$ imposes stronger constraints, yielding a narrower but more precise generation distribution. When prompts are simple, the model effectively needs to marginalize $p(x|c_{simple}) = \int p(x|c_{complex}) p(c_{complex}|c_{simple}) dc_{complex}$, which requires estimating the likelihood distribution.

**Design Motivation**: This theoretical explanation accounts for why complex prompts, despite reducing diversity, decrease the distribution shift between synthetic and real data—complex prompts more precisely locate the target distribution.

#### 2. **Evaluation Framework**

The paper introduces a new evaluation framework that compares the utility of real and synthetic data under unified criteria across three core dimensions:

- **Quality**: Measured via FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and human aesthetic scores.
- **Diversity**: Conditional diversity (degree of variation given the same prompt) and global diversity (coverage across different prompts).
- **Consistency**: Semantic alignment between generated images and input prompts (measured via CLIP score and related metrics).

#### 3. **Systematic Evaluation of Inference-Time Interventions**

The paper evaluates multiple inference-time intervention strategies:

- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**: Modulates generation by weighting conditional and unconditional scores.
- **Negative prompts**: Specifies content to be excluded from generation.
- **Prompt Expansion**: Uses a pretrained language model to expand simple prompts into more detailed descriptions.

### Loss & Training

The paper does not involve training new models; instead, it systematically evaluates existing T2I models. Experiments use commonly adopted T2I models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and are conducted on CC12M, ImageNet-1k, and DCI datasets.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

The effect of prompt complexity is evaluated across three datasets:

| Dataset | Prompt Type | Conditional Diversity | Prompt Consistency | Distribution Shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC12M | Simple prompts | High | High | Large |
| CC12M | Complex prompts | Low | Low | Small |
| ImageNet-1k | Class names | High | High | Large |
| ImageNet-1k | Detailed descriptions | Low | Low | Small |
| DCI | Short captions | High | High | Large |
| DCI | Long captions | Low | Low | Small |

### Ablation Study

Comparison of inference-time intervention methods:

| Intervention | Image Diversity | Aesthetic Quality | Deviation from Real Data Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | Baseline | Baseline | Baseline |
| CFG (enhanced) | Decreased | Increased | Deviates |
| Negative prompts | Slightly increased | Slightly increased | Slight deviation |
| Prompt expansion | **Highest** | **Highest** | Moderate |

### Key Findings

1. **Increasing prompt complexity** → lower conditional diversity, lower prompt consistency, but smaller synthetic-to-real distribution shift. This is consistent with the theoretical predictions from the synthetic experiments.
2. **Inference-time interventions** can enhance generation diversity at the potential cost of deviating from the support of the real data distribution.
3. **Prompt expansion, by employing a pretrained language model as a likelihood estimator**, consistently achieves the best performance on image diversity and aesthetic quality, even surpassing real data.
4. **Consistent trends across different T2I models** indicate that these findings are broadly generalizable.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **First systematic characterization**: Fills the research gap regarding how prompt complexity affects the utility of T2I synthetic data.
2. **Elegant theoretical explanation**: The observed empirical phenomena are explained through the simple mechanism of missing likelihood estimation, providing deep insight from the perspective of conditional distribution marginalization.
3. **New interpretation of prompt expansion**: Prompt expansion is reframed as "using a language model to estimate the missing conditional likelihood," providing a theoretical grounding for this previously empirical technique.
4. **High practical value**: The conclusions directly inform real-world AIGC applications—when to use simple versus complex prompts, and how to select an appropriate intervention method.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Only inference-time interventions are evaluated**: The possibility of improving the diversity–consistency–quality trade-off via fine-tuning the T2I model itself is not explored.
2. **Limited dataset scale**: Although large datasets such as CC12M are used, certain fine-grained datasets (e.g., DCI) are relatively small in scale.
3. **Downstream task performance not considered**: Evaluation metrics are primarily image-level; the actual utility differences of synthetic data on downstream visual tasks (e.g., classification, detection) are not validated.
4. **Coarse definition of prompt complexity**: Complexity is quantified mainly by prompt length and level of detail, without distinguishing finer-grained factors such as semantic complexity (e.g., spatial relations, attribute binding).
5. **No evaluation of state-of-the-art models**: The behavior of frontier models such as DALL-E 3 and Midjourney V6 may differ.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Synthetic data utility**: Complements prior work on synthetic data for training (e.g., Azizi et al., He et al.) by providing a deeper mechanistic understanding.
- **Theory of conditional generation in diffusion models**: Connects to theoretical work on likelihood estimation in diffusion models, revealing the implicit probabilistic structure in conditional generation.
- **Practical implications**: For applications requiring large-scale synthetic data (e.g., data augmentation, privacy-preserving learning), the appropriate prompt complexity and intervention strategy should be selected based on the requirements of the target task.
