# Continual Learning for fMRI-Based Brain Disorder Diagnosis via Functional Connectivity Matrices Generative Replay

**Conference**: CVPR 2026
**arXiv**: [2604.14259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14259)  
**Code**: [github.com/4me808/FORGE](https://github.com/4me808/FORGE)  
**Area**: Medical Imaging
**Keywords**: continual learning, fMRI, functional connectivity, generative replay, knowledge distillation

## TL;DR

This paper proposes FORGE, the first continual learning framework specifically designed for cross-site fMRI-based brain disorder diagnosis. FORGE generates realistic functional connectivity matrices via a structure-aware VAE for privacy-preserving generative replay, and combines dual-level knowledge distillation with a hierarchical contextual bandit sampling strategy to effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Background & Motivation

fMRI functional connectivity (FC) matrices serve as powerful representations for brain disorder diagnosis; however, clinical data typically arrive sequentially from different institutions. Existing diagnostic models are either trained on a single site or require access to the full multi-site dataset, making them susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. Conventional continual learning methods are primarily designed for image data, leaving graph-structured medical data—particularly fMRI—underexplored. Privacy regulations further restrict the sharing of raw data across institutions.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

FORGE integrates generative replay into a dual-level knowledge distillation scheme. At each new site, a student classifier learns jointly from current-site data and replayed synthetic FC matrices, aligning with the previous-site teacher via both logit-level and graph-representation-level distillation. The FCM-VAE is updated at each site to generate site-specific synthetic samples.

### Key Designs

1. **FCM-VAE Structure-Aware Encoder**: A graph Transformer architecture is adopted, where each ROI node feature is formed by concatenating FC connectivity spectra, spectral embeddings, and node degree. Attention layers fuse local adjacency bias (preserving local topological constraints) and spectral positional bias (capturing global geometric information), enabling the encoder to perceive the intrinsic topology and spectral properties of FC networks.

2. **Low-Rank Decoder with Accessibility Gating**: The decoder exploits the known low-rank structure of FC matrices, modeling Fisher-z-transformed and normalized connectivity strengths under a Poisson likelihood. The low-rank assumption aligns with established properties of large-scale functional connectivity, improving the fidelity of generated samples.

3. **Hierarchical Contextual Bandit Adaptive Replay Sampling**: Rather than uniformly sampling from the replay buffer, a hierarchical contextual bandit strategy adaptively selects the most informative synthetic samples for replay, improving replay efficiency. Dual-level distillation encompasses both classification logit alignment and graph readout representation alignment.

### Loss & Training

The unified objective comprises: (1) classification loss on current-site data; (2) classification loss on replayed synthetic data; (3) logit-level distillation ($L_2$ alignment between student and teacher logits); and (4) graph readout-level distillation ($L_2$ alignment of graph-level representations). Teacher outputs are computed once upon entering the buffer and remain fixed thereafter.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Evaluation is conducted on large-scale neuroimaging datasets ABIDE (autism), REST-meta-MDD (depression), and BSNIP (schizophrenia):

| Dataset | Task | Method | Forgetting Mitigation | Prediction Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ABIDE | ASD Diagnosis | FORGE | **Best** | **Best** |
| REST-meta-MDD | MDD Diagnosis | FORGE | **Best** | **Best** |
| BSNIP | SZ Diagnosis | FORGE | **Best** | **Best** |

### Ablation Study

- FC matrices generated by FCM-VAE substantially outperform those from existing graph generative models in quality.
- Graph readout distillation further improves performance over logit-only distillation.
- The hierarchical sampling strategy outperforms uniform random sampling.

### Key Findings

- Structure-aware encoding effectively captures the topological properties of FC matrices.
- Generative replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting while preserving privacy.
- The low-rank decoder produces FC matrices with higher biological fidelity.

## Highlights & Insights

- FORGE is the first complete framework integrating continual learning with fMRI functional connectivity analysis.
- Privacy-preserving generative replay addresses regulatory constraints on medical data sharing.
- The structure-aware design fully exploits domain-specific properties of FC matrices.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The fixed brain atlas of 116 ROIs (AAL-116) limits finer-grained brain region analysis.
- Generative model fidelity may degrade at sites with extremely small sample sizes.
- Domain adaptation for inter-site scanner variability is not addressed.

## Related Work & Insights

- The graph-level continual learning design is generalizable to other graph classification scenarios.
- Spectral positional encoding in graph Transformers merits further attention.
- The low-rank decoder concept is applicable to data generation in other domains with known low-rank structure.

## Rating

7/10 — The problem is clearly defined and the method is comprehensively designed, representing a valuable interdisciplinary contribution; however, downstream task impact is constrained by the scale of the fMRI research community.
