# BYORn: Bootstrap Your Own Responses to Defend Large Vision-Language Models Against Backdoor Attacks

**Conference**: ICML 2026  
**arXiv**: [2606.02947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02947)  
**Code**: https://github.com/ivansabolic/BYORn  
**Area**: LLM Security  
**Keywords**: Backdoor defense, Vision-Language Models, Robust Fine-tuning, Response bootstrapping, Data poisoning

## TL;DR

BYORn identifies poisoned samples by detecting high-perplexity target responses that are semantically inconsistent with the input, and dynamically replaces them with clean responses generated by the model itself. This breaks the association between backdoor triggers and malicious outputs, reducing the attack success rate by an average of 40 percentage points while maintaining clean task performance.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is the dominant method for adapting pre-trained Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to downstream tasks. By training on annotated image-instruction-response triplets, models achieve strong performance in tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Recent studies reveal that SFT is highly vulnerable to backdoor attacks. Attackers can inject a small number of poisoned samples into the fine-tuning dataset (e.g., embedding visual triggers in images, inserting specific words in instructions, or replacing responses with malicious content), causing the model to produce specified malicious outputs when encountering triggers during inference. Existing defense methods are either designed specifically for image classification (unable to handle open-ended generation), focused only on single-modal triggers (e.g., ONION only detects text triggers), or assume specific trigger patterns (unable to handle diverse attacks).

**Key Challenge**: In open-ended multi-modal generation scenarios, defense methods must simultaneously handle triggers in both visual and textual modalities without relying on prior assumptions about trigger types. This renders traditional defense strategies from classification scenarios ineffective.

**Key Insight**: The authors observe that the target responses of poisoned samples are typically semantically inconsistent with the corresponding image-text inputs. Consequently, they exhibit low conditional likelihood (high perplexity) under the pre-trained model. This distributional characteristic provides a basis for general detection without assuming trigger types.

**Core Idea**: Use perplexity scores from the pre-trained model to detect suspicious poisoned samples, then replace the targets of flagged samples with clean responses generated by an EMA version of the model to "bootstrap" clean training signals.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The training pipeline of BYORn consists of two stages: (1) **Detection Stage**—calculating the response perplexity for each sample in the poisoned dataset and categorizing samples into "clean" and "suspicious" groups using a percentile threshold; (2) **Robust Training Stage**—training normally on clean samples using original target responses, while training on suspicious samples using alternative responses generated by the EMA version of the model ($\theta_{\text{EMA}}$). Samples are grouped into specialized minibatches to improve efficiency. The final optimization objective is equivalent to minimizing an upper bound of the population risk over the clean data distribution.

### Key Designs

1. **Perplexity Detection Score**:

    - **Function**: Identify poisoned samples in the dataset.
    - **Mechanism**: For each sample $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{y})$, calculate the average negative log-likelihood of the target response under the pre-trained model: $s(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\theta) = -\frac{1}{K}\sum_{l=1}^{K}\ln p_\theta(y_l|\mathbf{y}_{<l},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})$, where $K$ is the number of response tokens. Normalization by length eliminates the influence of response length. A 60th percentile threshold $\delta_{0.6}$ is set, marking the 40% of samples with the highest perplexity as suspicious to ensure high recall of poisoned samples.
    - **Design Motivation**: Malicious responses in backdoor attacks (e.g., demanding the output "banana" regardless of the image) do not semantically match the input, leading the pre-trained model to assign low likelihood. This method is entirely trigger-agnostic and applicable to visual, textual, or hybrid triggers.

2. **Response Bootstrapping Training Objective**:

    - **Function**: Break the trigger-malicious output association without discarding data.
    - **Mechanism**: Introduce a latent clean response $\mathbf{t}$ and a poisoning indicator variable $u$. Use the original response $\mathbf{y}$ for samples detected as clean, and use an alternative response $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ sampled from the EMA model $\theta_{\text{EMA}}^t = \lambda \cdot \theta_{\text{EMA}}^{t-1} + (1-\lambda) \cdot \theta^{t-1}$ for suspicious samples. The hybrid objective is $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{BY}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_p|}\sum[(1-P_{\hat{u}})\cdot\mathcal{L}(\theta|\mathbf{y},\cdot) + P_{\hat{u}}\cdot\mathcal{L}(\theta|\hat{\mathbf{t}},\cdot)]$. Based on the Donsker-Varadhan bound and Hoeffding's Lemma, the gradient of this objective equals the gradient of the upper bound of the population risk on the clean data distribution.
    - **Design Motivation**: Compared to BYORn-F (which directly filters suspicious samples, leading to data reduction), bootstrapping alternative responses maintains full utilization of training data, significantly improving clean task performance. EMA parameters provide stable generation quality.

3. **Poison-Aware Minibatch Construction**:

    - **Function**: Reduce the computational overhead introduced by generating bootstrapped responses.
    - **Mechanism**: Complete detection before training and group the dataset into clean and suspicious sets. Construct minibatches that are either entirely clean or entirely suspicious. Clean batches skip the expensive auto-regressive generation step, while suspicious batches process generation in a centralized manner. Poisoned batches are distributed uniformly during training.
    - **Design Motivation**: Naive random mixing increases training time by more than 6x (as every batch requires generation), whereas the grouping strategy reduces overhead by 2.2x without affecting model accuracy.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Image Captioning (Average of Four Attacks)

| Model | Defense Method | Flickr CIDEr↑ | Flickr ASR↓ | COCO CIDEr↑ | COCO ASR↓ |
|------|---------|---------------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| LLaVA | SFT (No Defense) | 51.4 | 57.3% | 74.9 | 48.0% |
| LLaVA | ONION | 51.3 | 23.0% | 74.8 | 20.7% |
| LLaVA | BYE | 54.7 | 62.4% | 80.6 | 54.8% |
| LLaVA | **BYORn-F** | 42.4 | **0.3%** | 60.6 | 1.6% |
| LLaVA | **BYORn** | **62.0** | **0.0%** | **90.9** | **0.4%** |
| Qwen-VL | SFT | 60.7 | 100.0% | 66.9 | 89.8% |
| Qwen-VL | **BYORn** | 52.2 | **2.1%** | 59.6 | **2.7%** |
| InternVL | SFT | 57.3 | 99.8% | 64.9 | 71.6% |
| InternVL | **BYORn** | 56.0 | **0.8%** | 65.1 | **2.8%** |

### Ablation Study

| Experimental Configuration | CIDEr↑ | ASR↓ | Description |
|---------|--------|------|------|
| BYORn-F (Filtering only) | 42.4 | 0.3% | Discarding 40% of data leads to CIDEr drop |
| BYORn (Full Method) | 62.0 | 0.0% | Bootstrapping restores performance, even exceeding SFT |
| Threshold p=0.3 | 55.6 | 0.0% | Flagging too many clean samples slightly lowers CIDEr |
| Threshold p=0.6 (Default) | 62.2 | 0.0% | Optimal balance point |
| Threshold p=0.95 | 60.4 | 1.1% | Low recall but BYORn remains robust; BYORn-F fails (ASR 57.6%) |
| Poisoning Rate 1% | 62.0 | 0.0% | Stable across all poisoning rates |
| Poisoning Rate 50% | 61.5 | 0.2% | Effective even under extreme poisoning |
| Adaptive Attack (Patch) | 62.0 | 1.6% | Robust under semantically aligned triggers |
| Adaptive Attack (Adversarial) | 60.1 | 0.2% | Robust under PGD-optimized triggers |

### Key Findings

- BYORn achieves Pareto optimality (the best trade-off between robustness and generalization) across all models and attack settings, even exceeding the CIDEr of undefended SFT on LLaVA (62.0 vs 51.4).
- Even when the detector recall is only 50% (p=0.95), BYORn suppresses ASR to 1.1%, while BYORn-F’s ASR surges to 57.6% under the same conditions—demonstrating that the bootstrapping objective possesses intrinsic robustness.
- Faced with three types of adaptive attacks (semantically aligned triggers, PGD adversarial perturbations, and approximate clean-label attacks), BYORn remains effective, with ASR not exceeding 8.4%.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Perplexity as a Safety Signal**: Utilizing perplexity scores from the pre-trained model as a poison detector requires no clean reference set or assumptions about trigger types, making it concise and general. This approach can be transferred to any scenario requiring detection of training data anomalies (e.g., label noise filtering).
- **Bootstrapping Over Simple Filtering**: The core advantage of BYORn over BYORn-F is that it does not discard data. Replacing the targets of suspicious samples with model-generated responses breaks the backdoor association while retaining visual-textual information at the input for normal learning. The jump in CIDEr from 42.4 to 62.0 confirms the value of this design.
- **Theoretical Guarantees**: By deriving the BYORn objective using the Donsker-Varadhan bound, it is shown to be equivalent to minimizing an upper bound of the risk on the clean data distribution. This provides theoretical guidance for hyperparameter selection in practice (e.g., threshold and EMA decay rate).

## Limitations & Future Work

- Current defense relies on the assumption that poisoned responses are semantically inconsistent with inputs. It may fail against true clean-label attacks (where malicious responses are semantically plausible), which the authors list as an open challenge.
- The quality of bootstrapped responses is limited by the generation capability of the EMA model, which may introduce noise during early training or when model capacity is small.
- Although the poison-aware minibatch strategy effectively reduces cost, the total training time for full BYORn is still approximately 3x that of standard SFT (including 20 minutes for detection + ~135 minutes per epoch vs. 45 minutes).
- The experiments cover four representative attacks and three adaptive attacks but do not test more complex multi-step attacks or backdoor scenarios in multi-turn dialogues.

## Related Work & Insights

- **ONION**: Detects only textual trigger words (deleting high-perplexity words); ineffective against visual triggers.
- **BYE**: Filters low-attention image-text pairs via attention scores; fails against full-image triggers (Blend, VL-Trojan).
- **VL-Trojan / DualKey / BadNets / Blend**: The four representative attacks evaluated in this paper, covering image patches, full-image overlays, and bi-modal trigger types.
- **Insights**: The "Detection-Replacement" framework of BYORn can be extended to the safety of LLM instruction fine-tuning—using perplexity to detect malicious annotations in RLHF data and replacing them with model-generated responses.
