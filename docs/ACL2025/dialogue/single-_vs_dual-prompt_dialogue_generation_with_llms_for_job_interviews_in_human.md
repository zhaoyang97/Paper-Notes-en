# Single- vs. Dual-Prompt Dialogue Generation with LLMs for Job Interviews in Human Resources

**Conference**: ACL 2025 (GEM² Workshop)  
**arXiv**: [2502.18650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18650)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: NLP / Dialogue Generation  
**Keywords**: Dialogue Generation, LLM Evaluation, Single- vs. Dual-Prompt, Synthetic Data, Human Resources

## TL;DR

This paper systematically compares two strategies for generating job interview dialogues using LLMs—single-prompt (generating the entire dialogue at once) and dual-prompt (two agents role-playing as interviewer and candidate to talk turn-by-turn). It finds that dialogues generated by the dual-prompt method achieve a win rate 2 to 10 times higher than that of the single-prompt method in terms of naturalness, albeit at approximately 6 times the token cost.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Training dialogue systems requires large amounts of high-quality dialogue data. However, in professional domains such as Human Resources (HR), acquiring real human dialogue data is both expensive and difficult due to privacy and compliance issues. Consequently, utilizing LLMs to synthesize dialogues has become a mainstream alternative. Currently, two primary synthesis strategies exist: the single-prompt method, which has the LLM generate the entire dialogue at once, and the dual-prompt method, where two LLM agents role-play the two parties to interact over multiple turns.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Although both strategies are widely used, there has been a lack of systematic comparative research to address fundamental questions: Which strategy generates dialogues that are more similar to real human conversations? What is the cost-benefit trade-off? How do different LLMs affect the outcome?

**Key Challenge**: The dual-prompt method is intuitively more natural as it simulates the turn-taking process of real dialogue. However, it requires multiple API calls, and the full history must be passed in each time, resulting in significantly higher costs. Researchers need to quantify whether this quality difference justifies the extra cost investment.

**Goal**: (1) To rigorously compare the dialogue quality of single-prompt and dual-prompt strategies; (2) To test whether the conclusions hold consistently across different generator models (GPT-4o vs. Llama 3.3 70B); (3) To evaluate the consistency across different evaluator models.

**Key Insight**: The authors adopt the PairEval framework, employing an LLM evaluator to perform AI detection on paired interview dialogues—judging which dialogue is more likely to be AI-generated. The dialogue judged as "less likely to be AI" is deemed to be of higher quality.

**Core Idea**: Using "dialogue authenticity" as a proxy metric for quality, the paper conducts pairwise comparisons via LLM-as-a-judge to systematically quantify the quality difference between the two generation strategies.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The experimental pipeline consists of three phases: seed construction $\rightarrow$ dialogue generation $\rightarrow$ quality evaluation. First, 100 anonymized seed summaries are constructed from the professional experiences of real job seekers. Then, for each seed, interview dialogues are generated using 4 configurations (2 strategies $\times$ 2 LLMs). Finally, pairwise comparisons are performed using LLM evaluators.

### Key Designs

1. **Seed Construction and Dialogue Generation**:

    - **Function**: To provide a realistic and diverse content foundation for dialogue generation.
    - **Mechanism**: 100 real resumes are randomly selected from a large-scale career history dataset, and GPT-4T (temperature=1) is used to generate anonymized summaries as seeds. For the **dual-prompt strategy**, distinct system prompts are configured for the interviewer agent and the candidate agent. The interviewer is instructed to explore the candidate's professional capabilities, the candidate answers questions based on the seed information, and both are instructed to "pass the Turing test" to simulate human dialogue style. For the **single-prompt strategy**, the LLM is directly asked to generate a complete interview dialogue of approximately 16 turns based on the seed.
    - **Design Motivation**: Using the same seeds ensures that content variables remain consistent, with the only variables being the generation strategy and the choice of LLM. The Turing test requirement encourages the models to generate more natural dialogue.

2. **LLM-as-Judge Pairwise Evaluation**:

    - **Function**: To quantify the realism and quality of the dialogues.
    - **Mechanism**: GPT-4o and Llama 3.3 are used as evaluators to conduct pairwise comparisons between two dialogues originating from the same seed. Evaluators are asked to judge which dialogue is more likely to be AI-generated, with ties allowed. To eliminate order bias, each pairwise comparison is executed twice (swapping the order). The win rate is calculated using the formula: $\text{Win Rate}(M_i) = \frac{\#\text{Wins}_{M_i}}{\#\text{Wins}_{M_i} + \#\text{Losses}_{M_i} + \#\text{Ties}_{M_i}}$
    - **Design Motivation**: Compared to absolute scoring, pairwise comparison is more stable and reliable. Incorporating a "reason-before-judgment" ordering requirement improves evaluator consistency. Explicitly instructing the evaluator to "ignore dialogue length" helps mitigate length bias.

3. **Length Bias Control and Evaluator Consistency Validation**:

    - **Function**: To ensure the validity of the evaluation results.
    - **Mechanism**: Ordinal logistic regression is used to test the impact of dialogue length on evaluation outcomes. The results show that the overall length of the dialogue has a minimal (and negative, i.e., longer dialogues are less preferred) impact, whereas the length of individual utterance turns has a positive impact. The consistency between the two evaluators achieves a high agreement rate of 85%+ under a "relaxed" criterion (treating ties as agreement).
    - **Design Motivation**: Since LLM evaluators are known to exhibit issues such as length bias and self-preference, empirical validation is necessary to confirm whether these biases affect the core conclusions.

### Loss & Training

This work does not involve model training and focuses solely on evaluating the dialogue quality of different generation strategies.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Win rate when GPT-4o is the evaluator:

| Generation Config | Dual Strategy | Single Strategy | Total |
|----------|----------|------------|------|
| GPT-4o Generated | **0.49** | 0.18 | 0.36 |
| Llama 3.3 Generated | **0.62** | 0.09 | 0.33 |
| Total | **0.71** | 0.02 | — |

Win rate when Llama 3.3 is the evaluator:

| Generation Config | Dual Strategy | Single Strategy | Total |
|----------|----------|------------|------|
| GPT-4o Generated | **0.54** | 0.24 | 0.39 |
| Llama 3.3 Generated | **0.81** | 0.08 | 0.43 |
| Total | **0.86** | 0.03 | — |

| Comparison Config | Strict Agreement Rate | Relaxed Agreement Rate |
|----------|----------|----------|
| D,GPT vs D,Llama | 30.5% | **86.5%** |
| D,GPT vs S,GPT | 40% | **91%** |

| D,Llama vs S,Llama | 76.5% | **99%** |

### Key Findings

- **Dual-prompt strategy significantly outperforms single-prompt**: Regardless of which LLM is used for generation or evaluation, the win rate of the dual-prompt strategy is substantially higher than that of the single-prompt strategy, with differences ranging from 2 to 10 fold.
- **The choice of generation model has little impact**: The overall win rates of GPT-4o and Llama 3.3 as generators are nearly identical (the "Total" column on the right). This finding is surprising given the common self-preference bias of LLM evaluators.
- **Drastic difference in token costs**: The dual-prompt method consumes an average of approximately 6,092–7,583 tokens, whereas the single-prompt method uses only 946–1,143 tokens, representing a roughly 6x difference. This is because the dual-prompt strategy requires passing the entire dialog history in each turn.
- **Optimal solution**: Given that Llama 3.3 is more cost-effective and generates quality comparable to GPT-4o, the combination of the dual-prompt strategy + Llama 3.3 offers the best performance-to-cost ratio.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Concise Experimental Design**: The entire study employs only one core experiment (pairwise comparison), yet controls for multiple confounding variables through an elegant cross-design (2 strategies $\times$ 2 generators $\times$ 2 evaluators $\times$ forward/reverse order). This minimalist yet rigorous experimental design serves as a valuable reference.
- **Counter-intuitive Finding**: GPT-4o and Llama 3.3 show almost no difference in generation quality, yet differ drastically in cost. This has direct practical implications—using a cheaper open-source model combined with a dual-prompt strategy may be the optimal choice.
- **Length Bias Control Method**: Biases are managed by explicitly instructing evaluators in the prompt to "ignore length factors," and the effectiveness of this approach is validated through statistical testing. This simple prompt engineering technique can be widely reused.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The experiments only cover a single dialogue type—job interviews; whether the conclusions generalize to other domains (such as customer service or medical consultations) remains to be verified.
- The agreement between LLM-as-a-judge and human judgment is approximately 65.74% (based on GPT-4 data), indicating it is not a perfect substitute.
- Dialogue quality is measured solely along the dimension of "difficulty of AI detection," without evaluating the accuracy of the dialogue content or the professionalism of the interview questions.
- The token cost analysis is based on API call counts, without considering real-world engineering factors such as latency and concurrency.
- Future work can expand to more LLMs (such as Claude and Gemini) and dialogue generation in more diverse domains.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. SODA (Kim et al., 2023)**: SODA uses a single-prompt strategy to generate millions of dialogues. This paper demonstrates that the dual-prompt strategy provides higher quality but at a higher cost, and the specific choice depends on the quality-cost trade-off.
- **vs. BotChat (Duan et al., 2024)**: BotChat first proposed using PairEval to evaluate the multi-turn dialogue capability of two LLMs. This work borrows its evaluation framework but focuses on comparing generation strategies.
- **vs. DiaSynth (Suresh et al., 2025)**: DiaSynth is also a dialogue synthesis framework but utilizes knowledge seeds and single-prompt generation; this work provides a complementary, systematic comparison of the dual-prompt strategy.
