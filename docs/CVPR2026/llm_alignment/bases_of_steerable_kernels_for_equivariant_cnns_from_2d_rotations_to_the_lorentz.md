# Bases of Steerable Kernels for Equivariant CNNs: From 2D Rotations to the Lorentz Group

**Conference**: CVPR 2026
**arXiv**: [2603.12459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12459)
**Code**: N/A
**Area**: Equivariant CNN / Group Representation Theory
**Keywords**: Equivariant CNN, Steerable Kernels, Clebsch-Gordan Coefficients, Lorentz Group, Group Representation Theory

## TL;DR

This paper proposes a method that bypasses Clebsch-Gordan coefficients to solve the steerable kernel constraint in equivariant CNNs by solving simple invariance conditions on stabilizer subgroups and then "steering" to arbitrary points, providing explicit kernel bases for symmetry groups ranging from SO(2) to the Lorentz group.

## Background & Motivation

**Current landscape**: Embedding symmetry priors into CNN design has become a key technique for improving model performance. Steerable equivariant CNNs represent a general framework in which feature maps transform under specific representations of a symmetry group $G$, and convolution kernels must satisfy the steerability constraint.

**Limitations**: The standard method for solving steerable kernel bases (Lang & Weiler 2021) relies on three mathematical tools: generalized reduced matrix elements, Clebsch-Gordan (CG) coefficients, and harmonic basis functions on homogeneous spaces. Computing CG coefficients is expensive for certain groups and requires repeated switching between coupled and uncoupled bases.

**Core tension**: The steerability constraint is essentially a linear equation $K(g \cdot x) = \rho_{\text{out}}(g) K(x) \rho_{\text{in}}(g)^{-1}$ that theoretically admits a more direct solution path, yet existing methods introduce unnecessarily complex intermediate steps.

**Objective**: Can one find a method that directly uses input/output representation matrix elements—without computing CG coefficients—to solve steerable kernel bases for arbitrary symmetry groups?

**Approach**: Leveraging the orbit-stabilizer theorem: select a fixed point $x_0$ on the orbit where the kernel only needs to satisfy invariance under the stabilizer subgroup $H$ (a simpler equation), then extend $K(x_0)$ to the entire orbit via steering.

**Core idea**: The stabilizer constraint is equivalent to finding the homomorphism space between restricted representations of $H$, which can be solved analytically via Schur's lemma without any CG coefficients.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Given a symmetry group $G$, input representation $\rho_l$, and output representation $\rho_j$, the steerability constraint is:

$$K^{(j,l)}(g \cdot x) = \rho_j(g) K^{(j,l)}(x) \rho_l(g)^{-1}, \quad \forall g \in G$$

The method proceeds in three steps:
1. **Select a fixed point**: Choose $x_0$ on the $G$-orbit and determine its stabilizer $H = \text{Stab}_{x_0}$
2. **Solve the stabilizer constraint**: At $x_0$, the constraint simplifies to $K(x_0) = \rho_j^H(h) K(x_0) \rho_l^H(h)^{-1}$, meaning $K(x_0)$ is a homomorphism from $\rho_l^H$ to $\rho_j^H$. After decomposing restricted representations into irreducibles, Schur's lemma provides block-by-block solutions
3. **Steering**: For each $g \in G/H$, set $K(g \cdot x_0) = \rho_j(g) K(x_0) \rho_l(g)^{-1}$ to obtain the kernel over the entire orbit

### Key Designs

**Block-wise application of Schur's lemma**: After decomposing $\rho_l^H$ and $\rho_j^H$ into direct sums of $H$-irreducible representations, $K(x_0)$ becomes a block matrix. Blocks between non-isomorphic irreducibles are zero; blocks between isomorphic irreducibles are "scalar" times identity (for real representations, possibly $a\mathbb{I} + bJ$).

**Solutions for specific groups**:

- **SO(2)**: The stabilizer is the identity element; the constraint is trivial. The kernel basis consists directly of representation matrix elements (trigonometric functions). Complex representations yield a 1D basis $\{e^{i(j-l)\phi}\}$; real representations yield a 4D basis.

- **O(2)**: The stabilizer includes the reflection $r_y$; the additional constraint halves the solution space from SO(2). For instance, the $(j,l)$ case reduces from 4D to 2D.

- **SO(3)**: The stabilizer is SO(2) rotations about the $z$-axis. The restricted representation decomposes into multiplicity-free SO(2) irreducibles, yielding $2\min(j,l)+1$ independent intertwiners via Schur's lemma. Kernel bases are expressed using Wigner D-matrices.

- **O(3)**: The stabilizer extends to O(2), requiring additional consideration of parity quantum numbers $\epsilon = \pm$. Same-parity representation pairs use $\mathbb{I}$, opposite-parity pairs use $\sigma_3$, giving $\min(j,l)+1$ complex intertwiners.

- **Lorentz group SO$^+(1,3)$**: This is the most novel part. Two cases are distinguished:
    - **Massive particles**: The orbit is a timelike hyperboloid with stabilizer SO(3). The Lorentz representation is restricted to SO(3) and decomposed using SU(2) CG coefficients (which are known and easy to compute). Intertwiners manifest as projection operators in spacetime tensors, e.g., spin-0 intertwiner $u^\mu u_\nu$ and spin-1 intertwiner $\Delta^\mu{}_\nu = \delta^\mu{}_\nu - u^\mu u_\nu$.
    - **Massless particles**: The orbit is the light cone with stabilizer ISO(2), considering only the SO(2) subgroup. The projection operator is $\Delta^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - n^\mu \bar{n}^\nu - n^\nu \bar{n}^\mu$, with gauge freedom.

**Half-integer spin handling**: The charge conjugation operator $\mathcal{C}$ and $\gamma$ matrices are introduced to construct quaternionic structures $\{\mathbb{I}, i\mathbb{I}, \mathcal{C}, i\mathcal{C}\}$. Spin-1/2 kernel bases comprise combinations of positive/negative energy projections $P_\pm(u) = \frac{1}{2}(1 \pm \not{u})$ with quaternionic coefficients.

### Loss Function / Training Strategy

This is a purely theoretical work involving no specific training strategy or loss function. Kernel basis coefficients serve as learnable parameters that can directly replace kernel parameterizations in existing equivariant CNN frameworks. The authors note that, unlike truncation on the coupled basis, their method allows independent truncation on input and output representation labels $l$ and $j$, which is theoretically more flexible and may yield stronger network expressiveness.

No experimental ablation. However, the paper systematically compares solution spaces for complex vs. real representations:

### Key Findings

- For SO(2) and O(2), this method directly yields exactly the same solution spaces as Weiler & Cesa 2019 (E2CNN), but with significantly simplified derivation
- For SO(3), kernel basis matrix elements are simply products of Wigner D-matrices, requiring no additional harmonic basis functions
- Lorentz group intertwiners in spacetime tensor representations naturally manifest as projection operators (e.g., the projection orthogonal to four-velocity $\Delta^\mu{}_\nu$)
- Half-integer spin cases exhibit quaternionic structure; the charge conjugation operator $\mathcal{C}$ is invariant under Lorentz transformations, so intertwiner forms remain unchanged after steering

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Extremely simple methodology**: The core idea can be summarized in one sentence—"solve the stabilizer constraint at the fixed point, then steer." The mathematical prerequisites are minimal, requiring only Schur's lemma and basic group representation theory
2. **Unified framework across multiple groups**: The same method systematically solves from the simplest 2D rotations to the physically most important Lorentz group, demonstrating broad universality
3. **Physical intuition of projection operators**: In the Lorentz group case, intertwiners are projection operators onto different spin subspaces, with clear physical interpretation (e.g., spatial projection orthogonal to four-velocity $u$)
4. **Practical value**: The provided bases are explicit, ready-to-use analytic formulae that do not require numerical CG coefficient computation

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **No experimental validation**: No numerical experiments demonstrate efficiency gains or performance improvements in actual equivariant networks. The authors acknowledge that this "needs to be shown in different experiments"
2. **Discretization/aliasing not addressed**: Practical CNNs sample kernels on discrete grids; the paper only treats the continuous case, with aliasing issues mentioned only in passing
3. **Completeness for Lorentz half-integer spins**: The authors note at spin-3/2 that further study is needed, as the Rarita-Schwinger field requires additional differential constraints
4. **Broader non-compact groups not covered**: E.g., complete treatment of the Poincaré group (including translations), affine groups, etc.
5. **Interface with existing implementations**: Integration with mainstream equivariant network libraries such as e3nn and escnn is not discussed
