# Do Not Step Into the Same River Twice: Learning to Reason from Trial and Error

**Conference**: ACL 2026
**arXiv**: [2510.26109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26109)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/JamyDon/LTE)  
**Area**: LLM Reasoning / Reinforcement Learning
**Keywords**: exploration stagnation, trial-and-error learning, reinforcement learning, hint-guided exploration, mathematical reasoning

## TL;DR

This paper proposes LTE (Learning to reason from Trial and Error), which mitigates exploration stagnation in RLVR by using the model's own erroneous answers as hints to guide additional rollouts, without relying on any external expert supervision.

## Background & Motivation

**State of the Field**: RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards) is the dominant paradigm for enhancing LLM reasoning ability, with GRPO as its de facto standard algorithm. However, RLVR training suffers from severe **exploration stagnation**—when training samples are too difficult for the model, all rollouts fail to produce correct answers, yielding zero gradient signal from these samples and preventing the model from ever surpassing its current capability ceiling.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing approaches attempt to break exploration stagnation through external guidance: (1) using human-annotated reference solutions, which is costly and unscalable; (2) leveraging reasoning chains from stronger LRMs (e.g., LUFFY), which are not always accessible (e.g., when training frontier models). These methods address the symptom rather than the root cause.

**Root Cause**: Under the standard GRPO 0/1 reward setting, when all $G$ rollouts fail (none-pass samples), all group-relative advantages degenerate to zero ($\hat{A}_{i,t}=0$), producing zero gradients, and the policy learns nothing from these samples. Simply increasing the number of rollouts (GRPOExtra) is ineffective, as repeated sampling from the same policy distribution brings no fundamental improvement.

**Paper Goals**: To design a method that alleviates exploration stagnation in RLVR without relying on any external expert guidance.

**Core Idea**: Analogous to human learning—when students are informed of their prior mistakes, they avoid those errors and are more likely to find the correct answer. LTE collects erroneous answers generated by the model in its initial rollouts and uses them as "do not repeat this mistake" hints to guide additional rollouts toward different solution paths.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The core pipeline of LTE: for each training prompt, $G$ standard rollouts are first performed. If all fail (none-pass), an appropriate prompt template is selected based on the failure pattern, and $G$ additional hint-guided rollouts are executed. If any additional rollout produces a correct answer, it randomly replaces an equal number of initial failed rollouts, and the policy is updated via mixed-policy GRPO. The entire process relies solely on the model's own behavioral information.

### Key Designs

1. **Hinted Extra Rollouts**:

    - **Function**: Provide none-pass samples with hint-based guidance derived from the model's own errors, increasing the probability of generating correct answers.
    - **Mechanism**: Three cases are handled based on the failure pattern of the initial rollouts: (a) **All-truncated**: all responses are truncated due to excessive length; the model is prompted to "think concisely"; (b) **Some-truncated**: erroneous answers from non-truncated responses are extracted as hints, with a conciseness requirement; (c) **None-truncated**: the set of erroneous answers $\{a_1, a_2, ...\}$ is extracted purely as hints. The model is instructed not to explicitly mention or use these hints, preserving the cleanliness of the reasoning chain.
    - **Design Motivation**: Erroneous answers narrow the solution space by informing the model "these are all wrong—do not go down these paths again," analogous to human learning through trial and error.

2. **Mixed-Policy Optimization**:

    - **Function**: Incorporate hint-guided correct answers into policy updates in an off-policy manner.
    - **Mechanism**: If $G'$ correct answers $\{o'_1, ..., o'_{G'}\}$ are obtained from additional rollouts, they randomly replace an equal number of initially failed rollouts. Since the correct answers are generated under hint-conditioned inputs, a hint policy $\hat{\pi}(\cdot|\cdot) = \pi_{\mathrm{old}}(\cdot|\mathcal{H}_q, \cdot)$ is defined, and the importance sampling ratio is adjusted to $\hat{r}'_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(o'_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o'_{i,t}|\mathcal{H}_q, q, o_{i,<t})}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Hint-guided trajectories arise from different input conditions (with vs. without hints) and must be handled off-policy. Normalized importance sampling $f(r) = r/(r+\gamma)$ is applied to ensure training stability.

3. **Entropy-Augmented Variant (LTE†)**:

    - **Function**: Further promote exploration by adding an entropy loss.
    - **Mechanism**: An entropy regularization term is added on top of LTE to encourage the policy to maintain output diversity. This enables fair comparison with entropy-controlled methods such as DAPO and LUFFY.
    - **Design Motivation**: LTE implicitly promotes exploration through hint guidance; explicit entropy control can further unlock the model's exploration potential.

### Loss & Training

LTE employs a mixed-policy GRPO objective comprising an on-policy component (the standard GRPO clipping loss) and an off-policy component (normalized importance sampling applied to hint-guided trajectories). Group-relative advantages are computed across all rollouts jointly. Training uses the verl framework, sampling 8 rollouts per prompt at temperature 1.0, with a maximum response length of 8192 tokens, for 500 training steps.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Pass@1)

| Model | Method | MATH-500 | AIME24 | AIME25 | Avg. |
|-------|--------|----------|--------|--------|------|
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPO | 77.70 | 22.08 | 15.83 | 43.99 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPOExtra | 75.75 | 22.29 | 14.79 | 41.72 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE | 78.85 | 30.62 | 27.29 | 49.01 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LUFFY | 80.20 | 29.17 | 21.25 | 47.15 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE† | 79.80 | 30.83 | 25.83 | 49.56 |

### Pass@k (Exploration Ceiling)

| Model | Method | AIME24 | AIME25 | Avg. |
|-------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Qwen3-8B-Base | GRPO | 43.33 | 30.00 | 56.82 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE | 70.00 | 46.67 | 66.78 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LUFFY | 60.00 | 33.33 | 61.55 |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | LTE† | 66.67 | 50.00 | 67.58 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Key Metric | Note |
|---------------|-----------|------|
| GRPOExtra vs. LTE | −7.29 Pass@1, −10.04 Pass@k | Unhinted extra rollouts perform poorly |
| LTE vs. LUFFY (without entropy) | +1.86 Pass@1 | Outperforms LUFFY without external expert |
| LTE† vs. LUFFY (with entropy) | +2.41 Pass@1, +2.15 Pass@k | Advantage widens with entropy control |

### Key Findings
- LTE continuously reduces the number of none-pass samples throughout training, effectively alleviating exploration stagnation.
- LTE maintains higher long-tail entropy, encouraging deeper thinking at test time (longer response lengths).
- Simply increasing rollout count (GRPOExtra) can even underperform standard GRPO, validating that quantitative scaling alone cannot produce qualitative breakthroughs.
- LTE† is especially prominent on Pass@k metrics, demonstrating that LTE effectively expands the exploration ceiling.

## Highlights & Insights
- **An elegant trial-and-error analogy**: The human learning mechanism of improving through error feedback is transferred to RL training in a concise and effective manner.
- **Zero external dependency**: The method relies entirely on the model's own behavioral information (erroneous answers) as guidance, imposing no barrier to practical deployment.
- **Differentiated handling of three failure modes**: Separate prompt templates are designed according to truncation status, reflecting a deep understanding of the problem structure.
- **Substantial Pass@k gains**: On AIME24 in particular, Pass@k improves from 43.33 to 70.00, demonstrating that LTE significantly expands the model's exploration ceiling.
- **Strong comparison against LUFFY**: LTE†, without using any external expert trajectories, surpasses LUFFY, which depends on a stronger model.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Additional rollouts introduce approximately twice the sampling cost for none-pass samples; training efficiency requires further optimization.
- Validation is limited to mathematical reasoning tasks; applicability to other reasoning domains (e.g., code, logic) remains unexplored.
- Prompt templates are manually designed; automated prompt generation strategies may yield further improvements.
- Some baselines (ReLIFT, EvoCoT) perform poorly on Qwen3, potentially indicating model compatibility issues.
- Future work may explore more dynamic hint strategies, such as leveraging richer error pattern information beyond just erroneous answers.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. LUFFY**: LUFFY uses reasoning trajectories from a stronger LRM as off-policy samples; LTE uses only its own erroneous answers as hints, and outperforms LUFFY on both evaluated language models.
- **vs. GRPOExtra**: Simply increasing rollout count on Qwen3-8B-Base performs even worse than standard GRPO (−2.27 Avg.), demonstrating the necessity of hint-guided exploration.
- **vs. DAPO**: DAPO improves the optimization algorithm itself (e.g., via clip-higher), while LTE enhances exploration through improved sampling strategy; the two approaches are orthogonal and complementary.
- **vs. EvoCoT**: EvoCoT uses ground-truth answers as hints, whereas LTE uses the model's own erroneous answers as "negative examples"—a fundamentally different approach that achieves superior results.
