# VTimeCoT: Thinking by Drawing for Video Temporal Grounding and Reasoning

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2510.14672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14672)  
**Code**: [Project Page](https://vtimecot.github.io)  
**Area**: Video Understanding
**Keywords**: video temporal grounding, chain-of-thought, progress bar, visual prompt, training-free

## TL;DR

> This paper proposes VTimeCoT, a training-free visual-temporal chain-of-thought framework that overlays a synchronized progress bar and highlights key segments at the bottom of video frames, enabling multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to accurately perceive timestamps. The approach substantially outperforms GPT-4o and Qwen2VL-7B baselines on temporal grounding and reasoning QA tasks.

## Background & Motivation

Temporal grounding is a critical challenge in video understanding. Existing approaches suffer from the following limitations:

**End-to-end video MLLMs** (e.g., VideoChat, VideoLLaMA, Qwen2VL) can generate plausible responses but fail significantly at temporal grounding — they cannot accurately perceive per-frame timestamps or localize the boundaries of events.

**Tool-based agent methods** (e.g., VisProg, VideoAgent) aggregate textual cues by invoking external tools (e.g., object detectors, captioning models), but **rely entirely on the language modality** and cannot directly capture visual-temporal dynamics in video.

**VTimeLLM** and similar approaches introduce temporal grounding capability through fine-tuning, but incur costs in terms of task-specific data and training overhead.

The core insight is that humans intuitively understand temporal progress and localize event segments via **progress bars** when watching videos. A progress bar, as a visual prompt presented alongside video content, directly conveys the notion of temporal progression. This paper leverages this insight to enable MLLMs to reason about time by "drawing" progress bars.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

VTimeCoT is an iterative reasoning framework:
1. **Initialization**: Provide the MLLM with a formatted prompt defining the THOUGHT/ACTION/TERMINATE structure along with tool declarations.
2. **Iterative Reasoning**: At each step, generate a Thought (analytical reasoning) → Action (invoke a tool to manipulate the video) → update the video memory.
3. **Termination**: When the model determines it can answer directly, it generates TERMINATE.

### Key Design 1: Frame-Synchronized Progress Bar Integration Tool

OpenCV is used to render a progress bar at the bottom of each video frame, annotated with the exact timestamp (in seconds) corresponding to that frame:

$$V'_t = V_t \oplus T_t$$

where $\oplus$ denotes vertical concatenation and $T_t$ is the progress bar image generated by OpenCV. The progress bar includes:
- A rectangular track, a circular current-position indicator, and second-level annotations.
- **Frame synchronization**: frame indices are converted to real-world seconds, accommodating arbitrary FPS sampling rates.

The MLLM's OCR capability is exploited to directly read temporal information from the visual progress bar, requiring no additional training.

### Key Design 2: Video-Text Similarity-Based Highlight Tool

Zero-shot moment retrieval is performed using the VideoCLIP-XL backbone:

1. Sample video frames at $r=1$ FPS; group every 8 frames into a clip, yielding $N$ clips in total.
2. Compute the cosine similarity between each clip and the query text:

$$\text{sim}(i) = \frac{e_i^v \cdot e^q}{|e_i^v| |e^q|}$$

3. Select the top-$k$ ($k=8$) most similar clips and extract the start and end times of contiguous segments.
4. Render colored highlight masks over the corresponding intervals on the progress bar using OpenCV.

Since moment retrieval runs as an **external module**, it is not constrained by the MLLM's token limit, making it naturally compatible with long videos.

### Visual-Temporal Chain-of-Thought Process

```
Algorithm: Visuotemporal CoT
Input: Video V, Question Q, Initialization prompt I
1. s←0; V_0←V; H_0←{I, Q}
2. while true:
3.   y_s ← MLLM(V_s, H_s)
4.   Parse THOUGHT_s, ACTION_s, TERMINATE_s
5.   if not TERMINATE_s:
6.     C_s ← construct_code(ACTION_s, Toolset)
7.     V_{s+1} ← C_s(V_s)      // update video (overlay progress bar / highlights)
8.     H_{s+1} ← {H_s, y_s}    // update history
9.   else: return y_s
```

The maximum number of reasoning steps is $T=3$; 32 frames are used by default, with the long side rescaled to 480 pixels.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Charades-STA Temporal Grounding

| Method | R1@0.3 | R1@0.5 | R1@0.7 | mIoU |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| VTimeLLM-13B | 55.30 | 34.30 | 14.70 | 34.60 |
| Qwen2VL-7B | 37.31 | 12.85 | 4.11 | 24.34 |
| **VTimeCoT_Qwen2VL** | **66.96** | **38.79** | **20.83** | **43.41** |
| GPT4o | 63.76 | 37.12 | 14.65 | 40.20 |
| **VTimeCoT_GPT4o** | **74.06** | **51.02** | **22.45** | **46.78** |

### Main Results: QVHighlights Non-Contiguous Segment Localization

| Method | R1@0.3 | R1@0.5 | R1@0.7 | mIoU |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| VTimeLLM-7B | 44.58 | 25.03 | 9.29 | 28.99 |
| GPT4o | 55.61 | 35.68 | 19.29 | 37.66 |
| **VTimeCoT_GPT4o** | **79.35** | **59.74** | **33.81** | **54.49** |

VTimeCoT achieves a 16.83% mIoU improvement on QVHighlights over the GPT-4o baseline.

### Key Findings

- VTimeCoT surpasses VTimeLLM-13B — which was fine-tuned on temporal grounding data — **without any training**.
- The progress bar contributes the largest gain (mIoU +7.55); highlights provide an additional improvement (+5.09), yielding a total gain of 16.83 points.
- On the Vript-RR long-video reasoning benchmark, VTimeCoT_GPT4o achieves a multiple-choice accuracy of 83.55% (vs. 70.39% for GPT-4o).
- With only 32 frames, VTimeCoT approaches the performance of vanilla GPT-4o using 384 frames on the VideoMME with-subtitles setting (73.6% vs. 77.2%).

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Progress bar as a medium for temporal reasoning**: This work breaks the paradigm of text-only CoT reasoning and, for the first time, extends the spatial visual prompt concept to the temporal dimension.
2. **Minimalist implementation**: Only OpenCV for rendering progress bars and VideoCLIP-XL for similarity computation are required — zero training, zero fine-tuning.
3. **Long-video friendly**: The moment retrieval module operates as an external component unconstrained by MLLM token limits, and the progress bar automatically adapts to arbitrary FPS.
4. **High interpretability**: Each reasoning step is visualized (progress bar + highlights + thought), making the model's temporal reasoning process transparent.
5. **The MLLM's OCR capability** is cleverly repurposed for temporal perception — the model "reads" time directly from the progress bar.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The framework depends on the MLLM's strong code generation and OCR capabilities; performance may degrade for weaker models.
- The maximum reasoning step count of $T=3$ limits the depth of complex inference, while more steps would increase latency and cost.
- The retrieval quality of VideoCLIP-XL determines highlight accuracy; robustness may degrade on out-of-distribution videos.
- The progress bar occupies space at the bottom of the frame, potentially occluding part of the visual content.
- Inference costs are non-trivial when using the GPT-4o API.

## Related Work & Insights

- Compared to spatial visual prompt methods such as Visual Sketchpad, VTimeCoT is the first to introduce **temporal-dimension visual prompting**, complementing the limitations of spatial prompts like SoM.
- Relative to VTimeLLM: while VTimeLLM acquires temporal grounding capability through fine-tuning, VTimeCoT achieves superior zero-shot performance via tool use, demonstrating the strong potential of the tool-use paradigm.
- The progress bar and highlight strategy is generalizable to other temporally sensitive tasks, including audio understanding (spectrogram progress bars) and time-series analysis.
