# The Invisibility Hypothesis: Promises of AGI and the Future of the Global South

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2603.01616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01616)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: AI & Society / AI Ethics
**Keywords**: AGI, Global South, Invisibility Hypothesis, economic inequality, informal economy, AI governance

## TL;DR

This paper introduces the **Invisibility Hypothesis**, arguing that as AI systems increasingly serve as the coordination layer for economic and political allocation, they will systematically favor "machine-readable" individuals. Informal workers in the Global South, lacking digital verifiability, face **managed exclusion**. The central risk shifts from job displacement to **relevance loss**, and this exclusion is self-reinforcing.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: AGI discourse centers on technical feasibility, timelines, and existential risk (Amodei 2024, OpenAI 2023, DeepMind 2025), with social impact analyses often assuming homogeneous effects across populations. Research on the differentiated impacts on developing countries remains severely underdeveloped (Wall et al. 2021).

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
- AI social impact research is developed-world-centric, neglecting the structural disadvantages of the Global South.
- AGI discourse is polarized between "universal tool" and "existential threat" framings, both ignoring differential impacts across populations.
- Existing automation anxiety literature focuses on job displacement, missing deeper mechanisms of exclusion.

**Key Challenge**: A fundamental tension exists between AGI's democratization promise (expert-level knowledge for all) and the actual trend of concentrated compute, proprietary models, and AI systems biased toward data-rich, formal-economy participants.

**Goal**: What does it mean for those underrepresented in data, weakly integrated into formal economies, and distant from centers of technological power, when scientific discovery, economic coordination, and governance decisions become increasingly automated?

**Key Insight**: Rather than debating whether or when AGI will arrive, this paper treats AGI as a "stress test" of existing global inequalities—examining how highly autonomous AI systems interact with existing infrastructure, institutions, and geopolitical power structures.

**Core Idea**: AI-driven economic systems optimize for measurable and verifiable entities, shifting the Global South's informal populations from being *exploited* to being *ignored*. Invisibility is harder to reverse than oppression.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

This is a conceptual analysis paper. The argumentative chain proceeds as follows: (1) operationally define the Invisibility Hypothesis → (2) distinguish "economically invisible" from "machine-readable" individuals → (3) reveal the self-reinforcing cycle of exclusion → (4) derive three future trajectories (utopia / collapse / middle ground) → (5) provide empirical signals via macroeconomic data.

### Key Designs

1. **Formal Definition of the Invisibility Hypothesis**:
    - *Function*: Operationally define "economic invisibility" under AI systems.
    - *Mechanism*: As AI systems become the coordination layer for economic and political allocation, AI-mediated allocation optimizes for measurable and verifiable entities—$\text{Visibility} \propto f(\text{digital identity}, \text{standardized transactions}, \text{auditable outputs})$—individuals lacking these attributes face managed exclusion.
    - *Design Motivation*: Unlike the traditional "digital divide" (which concerns access), the Invisibility Hypothesis addresses relevance and participation in the AI era.

2. **Self-Reinforcing Exclusion Cycle**:
    - *Function*: Reveal the positive feedback mechanism of exclusion—why invisibility, once initiated, is difficult to reverse.
    - *Mechanism*: Denied credit/contracts/insurance → fewer verifiable records and standardized signals generated → further reduced visibility in AI systems → harder to re-enter → $\text{Exclusion}_t \to \text{Less Data}_{t+1} \to \text{Lower Visibility}_{t+2} \to \text{More Exclusion}_{t+3}$
    - *Design Motivation*: This cycle explains why being *ignored* is harder to reverse than being *oppressed*—oppression still implies the system depends on you; being ignored means the system has already bypassed you.

3. **Three-Trajectory Analytical Framework**:
    - *Function*: Systematically derive three differentiated impact trajectories of AGI on the Global South.
    - *Mechanism*: Structural factors (compute, infrastructure, institutions, geopolitics) serve as branching variables, yielding three trajectories: utopia (geography becomes irrelevant), collapse (from exploitation to irrelevance), and middle ground (existing inequalities amplified).
    - *Design Motivation*: Avoid the single narrative of technological determinism; acknowledge uncertainty and provide a multi-path framework for risk analysis.

| Utopia | Cognitive leapfrogging bypasses development bottlenecks | Complete reversal | Low |

### Key Findings

- **"Capability ≠ Omnipotence"**: Even if AGI technology itself becomes universally accessible, physical and institutional constraints—energy, compute, data quality, institutional trust—still limit its practical utility in the Global South. Marginal returns to additional intelligence diminish when coordination and execution become the binding bottlenecks.
- **Exclusion Precedes Automation**: The near-term risk is not displacement by machines, but loss of access in AI-controlled credit, supply chain, insurance, and policy systems. Routine office work, already standardized, faces earlier displacement; physical labor is temporarily insulated by immature robotics, but exclusion can precede both.
- **Intra-Group Differentiation**: Exclusion does not occur uniformly at the national level—privileged elites in the Global South (with capital, infrastructure, and transnational networks) can still integrate into global AI systems, while those at the bottom face substantially higher exclusion risk.
- **Qualitative Shift from Historical Patterns**: Colonialism depended on people (requiring labor); the AGI regime may not. This represents a qualitative departure from historical inequality: populations once integrated through extraction become populations the system no longer requires.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Conceptual Innovation**: The analytical reframing from *displacement* to *irrelevance* captures a deeper mechanism of AI-driven inequality.
- **The Self-Reinforcing Cycle Is the Most Powerful Insight**: The feedback loop of invisibility introduces path dependency into exclusion, making it extremely difficult to reverse once initiated.
- **An Important Corrective to AGI Discourse**: Shifting focus from technical feasibility to differentiated structural impacts fills a critical gap in AI social impact research.
- **Depth of Historical Analogy**: The parallel—colonialism needed people; AGI may not—is both novel and analytically powerful.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The paper is purely conceptual, lacking quantitative modeling or empirical validation; the operationalization of "invisibility" lacks specificity.
- Substantial heterogeneity within the "Global South" (e.g., China/India vs. sub-Saharan Africa) is insufficiently addressed.
- Policy recommendations are weak—the paper identifies problems but offers limited solutions.
- The definition of AGI is vague, with unclear demarcation from existing AI capabilities.
- The active agency of the Global South (e.g., India's IT outsourcing, African mobile payment innovation) is not discussed.
- Case analyses of AI exclusion mechanisms in specific industries (agriculture, manufacturing) are absent.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. Korinek & Suh (2024)**: They discuss economic scenarios for AGI transition; this paper focuses on differentiated impact trajectories for the Global South.
- **vs. Restrepo (2025)**: That work examines work and growth in an AGI world; this paper emphasizes the disappearance of *relevance* rather than *work*—a deeper analytical perspective.
- **vs. Mainstream AI Ethics**: Most AI ethics research focuses on bias and fairness; this paper addresses the macro-level dimension of structural exclusion.
- **vs. Digital Divide Literature**: Traditional work concerns access to technology; the Invisibility Hypothesis addresses the deeper dimension of participation and relevance.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The Invisibility Hypothesis is an important conceptual contribution to AI inequality discourse; the self-reinforcing cycle is a profound insight.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐ — Purely conceptual analysis using macroeconomic statistics only for illustrative argumentation; lacks quantitative validation.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Argumentation is clear, historical analogies are effective, structure is compact; the five-page format delivers an efficient argument.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Provides an important and underexplored perspective on AGI's social impacts, though actionable policy guidance is lacking.
