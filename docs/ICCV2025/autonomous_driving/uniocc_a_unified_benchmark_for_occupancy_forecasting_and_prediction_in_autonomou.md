# UniOcc: A Unified Benchmark for Occupancy Forecasting and Prediction in Autonomous Driving

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2503.24381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24381)
**Code**: [uniocc.github.io](https://uniocc.github.io/)
**Area**: Autonomous Driving
**Keywords**: Occupancy Grid Prediction, Occupancy Prediction Benchmark, Voxel Flow, Cross-Dataset Training, Cooperative Driving

## TL;DR

This paper proposes UniOcc, the first unified benchmark for 2D/3D occupancy prediction and forecasting, integrating four data sources — nuScenes, Waymo, CARLA, and OpenCOOD — while introducing per-voxel flow annotations and ground-truth-free evaluation metrics. Large-scale experiments reveal the significant value of voxel-level flow information and cross-domain training for occupancy tasks.

## Background & Motivation

### Problem Definition

Occupancy grid maps (OGM) discretize the environment into 2D/3D grids, where each cell is annotated with occupancy status and semantic category, serving as a core representation for safety-critical perception in autonomous driving. Two representative tasks exist in this field:
- **Occupancy Prediction**: Reconstructing the current scene's occupancy representation from current-frame sensor data.
- **Occupancy Forecasting**: Predicting future multi-frame occupancy states based on historical observations.

### Limitations of Prior Work

**Poor pseudo-label quality**: nuScenes and Waymo lack official occupancy annotations; existing methods rely on pseudo-labels generated by tools such as Occ3D and SurroundOcc, which only capture LiDAR-reflected surfaces (e.g., vehicle sides) and fail to accurately represent the complete 3D occupancy shape of objects.

**Restricted evaluation metrics**: Standard IoU metrics can only compare against defective pseudo-labels, failing to identify cases where predictions are actually more complete than the "ground truth" (e.g., a model predicts a full vehicle shape, while the pseudo-label only covers the side).

**Dataset fragmentation**: Different datasets employ distinct sensor configurations, data formats, sampling rates, and annotation types, making it impossible to directly transfer models trained on nuScenes to Waymo.

**Absence of per-voxel flow**: Existing 3D occupancy labels lack motion flow information, limiting the exploitation of dynamic scene cues.

**No support for cooperative driving**: No dataset supports occupancy forecasting in multi-vehicle cooperative driving scenarios.

### Core Problem

To construct a **unified, comprehensive, and extensible** occupancy benchmark that addresses data fragmentation, poor label quality, and inadequate evaluation, while filling the gap in dynamic modeling through voxel-level flow information.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

UniOcc is not a prediction model but a complete benchmark framework operating at three levels:
1. **Unified data format**: Defines task-agnostic standardized data representations.
2. **Unified data sources**: Integrates four datasets into a unified format.
3. **Unified evaluation system**: Provides both ground-truth-based and ground-truth-free evaluation metrics.

### Key Designs

#### 1. **Unified Data Format and Per-Voxel Flow Annotation**

- **Function**: Defines standardized data formats including semantic occupancy labels $G \in \{0,...,C\}^{L \times W \times H}$, camera images, FOV masks, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and ego-vehicle poses.
- **Mechanism**:

  **Forward voxel flow**: Computes an independent 3D displacement vector $F \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times W \times H \times 3}$ for each voxel:
  - For dynamic foreground objects (vehicles, pedestrians): per-voxel displacement is computed using 3D bounding boxes and inter-frame pose transformations, capturing full translation and rotation.
  - For static background (roads, vegetation): displacement is derived from the inverse of ego-vehicle motion.

  **Backward voxel flow**: Similarly defines $B \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times W \times H \times 3}$, computing displacement from time $t$ to $t-1$.

- **Design Motivation**:
    - Unlike the **object-level flow** in CarlaSC (where all voxels of the same object share one velocity), UniOcc's voxel-level flow preserves **rotational motion** information.
    - Bidirectional (forward + backward) flow supports both forward forecasting and reverse-time training strategies.
    - Voxel-level flow can be directly used for flow-based object tracking.

#### 2. **Ground-Truth-Free Evaluation Metrics**

- **Function**: Proposes metrics for assessing occupancy quality without requiring perfect ground-truth labels.
- **Mechanism**:

  The fundamental issue with standard IoU is that pseudo-labels are incomplete (as shown in Figure 4, vehicles in Occ3D are only partially represented), which penalizes models that predict complete vehicle shapes. UniOcc introduces:

  - **IoU$_\text{bg}$**: IoU over background regions, evaluating the accuracy of scene layout.
  - **$P_\text{car}$**: A vehicle shape score based on Chamfer distance, robust to incomplete pseudo-labels.
  - Combined with traditional IoU$_\text{geo}$ and mIoU$_\text{geo}$ for comprehensive evaluation.

- **Design Motivation**: On real-world datasets such as nuScenes, pseudo-label quality is itself a problem. Ground-truth-free metrics reveal a model's true capability, resolving the paradox where "a model outperforms its labels yet scores poorly on metrics."

#### 3. **Unified Cross-Dataset Training and Evaluation Framework**

- **Function**: Unifies data from nuScenes, Waymo, CARLA, and OpenCOOD into a consistent format, enabling cross-dataset training with a single command.
- **Mechanism**:
    - Unified coordinate system: ego-vehicle-centric, with +x aligned to the driving direction, +y to the left, and +z upward.
    - Unified label mapping: semantic categories from different datasets are mapped to a shared category table.
    - Unified sampling rate handling: supports mixed training at 2 Hz (nuScenes) and 10 Hz (Waymo).
    - CARLA provides unlimited perfect ground-truth data for pre-training or augmentation.

- **Design Motivation**: Data diversity is key to improving model generalization. By eliminating engineering barriers through a unified format, cross-dataset experiments can be completed within a single command.

#### 4. **Occupancy Processing Toolkit**

- **Function**: Provides a complete toolchain for extracting, tracking, and shape-aligning objects from occupancy grids.
- **Mechanism**:
    - **Object segmentation**: Extracts individual objects via connected component labeling (6-connected CCL).
    - **Bounding box fitting**: Fits minimum bounding rectangles in the horizontal plane using the Rotating Calipers algorithm.
    - **Flow-based tracking**: Uses forward flow to predict each object's voxel positions in the next frame, with cross-frame association via the Hungarian algorithm.
    - **Shape alignment**: PCA determines canonical orientation, supporting cross-frame shape analysis.

- **Design Motivation**: Extends the potential of occupancy representations to downstream tasks such as detection and tracking, forming a complete pipeline.

### Loss & Training

As a benchmark, UniOcc supports training of various models. Key training findings include:
- A unified pipeline enables arbitrary dataset combinations to be configured for training with a single command.
- Voxel-level flow supervision is supported as an additional training signal.
- Cross-domain training supports source-domain pre-training followed by target-domain fine-tuning.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Effect of voxel-level flow on OccWorld occupancy forecasting (nuScenes):

| Flow Type | mIoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s)↑ | mIoU$_\text{geo}$ (3s)↑ | IoU$_\text{bg}$↑ | $P_\text{car}$↑ |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| No flow | 66.79 | 18.13 | 53.27 | 77.83 |
| Object-level flow | 65.41 | 18.40 | 54.15 | 76.25 |
| **Voxel-level flow** | **70.64** | **19.06** | **59.56** | **82.57** |

Effect of cross-dataset training on OccWorld (tested on nuScenes):

| Training Source | mIoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s)↑ | IoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s)↑ | IoU$_\text{bg}$↑ | $P_\text{car}$↑ |
|----------------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| nuScenes | 70.64 | 62.62 | 59.56 | 82.57 |
| Waymo | 63.22 | 60.42 | 49.90 | 72.54 |
| CARLA | 29.93 | 49.32 | 78.99 | — |
| nuScenes + Waymo | **71.80** | **63.12** | 60.23 | **83.14** |

### Ablation Study

Cross-domain generalization analysis (training/testing OccWorld on different data sources):

| Train→Test | mIoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s)↑ | IoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s)↑ | Note |
|------------|------------------------|------------------------|------|
| nuScenes→nuScenes | 70.64 | 62.62 | In-domain baseline |
| Waymo→nuScenes | 63.22 | 60.42 | Cross-domain performance drop |
| CARLA→nuScenes | 29.93 | 49.32 | Large sim-to-real gap |
| nuScenes+Waymo→nuScenes | 71.80 | 63.12 | Mixed training improves results |
| nuScenes+CARLA→nuScenes | 71.47 | 62.94 | Synthetic data provides modest benefit |

### Key Findings

1. **Voxel-level flow significantly outperforms object-level flow and no-flow baselines**: mIoU$_\text{geo}$ (0s) improves by 3.85 (70.64 vs. 66.79), confirming the value of voxel-level rotational information.
2. **Cross-domain generalization remains a core challenge**: Waymo→nuScenes performance drops by approximately 10%; CARLA→Real drops even more substantially.
3. **Mixed real-dataset training is effective**: Joint training on nuScenes+Waymo yields improvements on both domains.
4. **The imperfection of pseudo-labels is validated**: The $P_\text{car}$ metric reveals quality differences that IoU fails to capture.
5. **CARLA synthetic data has limited auxiliary value**: The sim-to-real gap remains the primary bottleneck.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Benchmark-level contribution**: UniOcc is not a model innovation but an infrastructure-level contribution that unifies four data sources, multiple tasks, and evaluation metrics.
2. **First systematic validation of voxel-level flow value**: Compared to object-level flow, voxel-level flow captures rotational motion and yields significant gains in forecasting tasks.
3. **Ground-truth-free evaluation metrics**: Fundamentally addresses the problem of "evaluating correct predictions against incorrect standards."
4. **First support for cooperative driving occupancy forecasting**: Extends to multi-vehicle cooperative scenarios via OpenCOOD.
5. **Complete toolchain**: An end-to-end toolkit from data generation to object tracking lowers the barrier to entry for research.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Pseudo-label quality remains a bottleneck**: Although ground-truth-free metrics are introduced, training still relies on pseudo-labels.
2. **Large sim-to-real gap**: CARLA data has limited direct applicability to real-world scenarios.
3. **Information loss in unified category mapping**: Different datasets have varying category granularities; unified mapping inevitably incurs information loss.
4. **Validation limited to OccWorld**: The generality of the benchmark requires validation across more models.
5. **No evaluation of end-to-end planning tasks**: The impact of improved occupancy prediction/forecasting on downstream planning has not been assessed.

## Related Work & Insights

- Inspired by **UniTraj** (a unified trajectory prediction benchmark), the paper introduces the concept of a unified framework to the occupancy prediction domain.
- **Distinction from CarlaSC**: CarlaSC provides object-level flow and is limited to simulated data, whereas UniOcc provides voxel-level flow and integrates both real and simulated data.
- **Distinction from LetOccFlow**: The latter only considers horizontal flow, while UniOcc provides complete 3D displacement.
- UniOcc plays a standardizing role for occupancy prediction/forecasting analogous to what nuScenes provided for 3D object detection.
