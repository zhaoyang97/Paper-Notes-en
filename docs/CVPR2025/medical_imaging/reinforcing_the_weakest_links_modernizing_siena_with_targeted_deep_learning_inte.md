# Reinforcing the Weakest Links: Modernizing SIENA with Targeted Deep Learning Integration

**Conference**: CVPR2025  
**arXiv**: [2603.12951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12951)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Raciti/Enhanced-SIENA)  
**Area**: Medical Imaging  
**Keywords**: brain atrophy, SIENA, PBVC, SynthStrip, SynthSeg, longitudinal MRI, neurodegeneration

## TL;DR

Modular replacement of classic skull stripping and tissue segmentation steps in the SIENA pipeline with deep learning modules (SynthStrip/SynthSeg) significantly improves the clinical sensitivity and robustness of longitudinal brain volume change (PBVC) estimation while preserving pipeline interpretability. Validated on two longitudinal cohorts, ADNI and PPMI.

## Background & Motivation

- SIENA is the most widely used pipeline for estimating longitudinal brain atrophy (Percentage Brain Volume Change, PBVC) and is extensively adopted in AD and PD clinical trials.
- SIENA relies on classical algorithms within the FSL toolbox: BET2 for skull stripping and FAST for tissue segmentation. These intensity-heuristic-based methods degrade in performance under severe atrophy or artifacts.
- Brain extraction errors from BET2 propagate downstream to registration and segmentation steps, ultimately biasing PBVC estimates.
- End-to-end deep learning methods (e.g., DeepBVC, EAM), while effective, lack transparency and depend on noisy targets generated by SIENA as supervision signals.
- This paper proposes a modular strategy: strengthening SIENA's weakest image processing links with DL instead of replacing the entire pipeline.

## Method

### Pipeline Variant Design
Three SIENA variants are constructed, keeping the core registration and edge displacement estimation framework unchanged:
1. **SIENA-SS**: Replaces BET2 (skull stripping) with SynthStrip + retains FAST (segmentation).
2. **SIENA-SEG**: Retains BET2 + replaces FAST (segmentation) with SynthSeg.
3. **SIENA-SS-SEG**: SynthStrip + SynthSeg (fully integrated).

### SynthStrip Integration
- SynthStrip provides brain masks but does not generate skull masks, requiring manual derivation of approximate skull masks to maintain compatibility with SIENA's skull-constrained registration.
- Specifically, the brain mask is Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma=1.0$), surface normals are estimated from gradients, and rays are projected along the normal directions (up to 30mm) to detect the inner skull boundary using BET2's intensity gradient heuristic.
- This approximation method cleverly leverages BET2's local capabilities while avoiding its global brain extraction weaknesses.

### SynthSeg Integration
- SynthSeg outputs anatomical structure labels instead of three tissue classes, which requires mapping and merging these labels into CSF, GM, and WM.
- CSF includes structures such as the lateral ventricles, inferior lateral ventricles, and third and fourth ventricles.
- GM includes the cerebral cortex, thalamus, caudate, putamen, pallidum, hippocampus, amygdala, cerebellar cortex, accumbens, and ventral diencephalon.
- WM includes cerebral white matter, cerebellar white matter, and brainstem.

### Evaluation Framework
1. **Clinical Correlation Assessment**: Pearson correlation between PBVC and disease progression indicators (MMSE, CDR-SB, ADAS-13, MoCA, FAQ, BPF, etc.). Pipeline differences are compared using the Steiger's $Z$-test with Bonferroni correction.
2. **Scan-Order Consistency**: Swapping the baseline/follow-up scan order should only change the sign of the PBVC. Deviations are quantified using the Mean Forward-Reverse Residual (MFRR).
3. **End-to-End Runtime**: Average wall-clock time under both CPU and GPU modes.

## Key Experimental Results

### Datasets
- **ADNI**: 1006 patients with AD (44% female, mean age $73.18 \pm 7.07$), longitudinal T1 MRI, equipped with MMSE/CDR-SB/ADAS-13/MoCA/FAQ/BPF.
- **PPMI**: 310 patients with PD (43% female, mean age $63.0 \pm 8.90$), longitudinal T1 MRI, equipped with MSEADLG/MoCA/GM volume.
- The first and last available scans for each subject are selected.

### Clinical Correlation (ADNI)

| Metric | SIENA Vanilla | SIENA-SS | SIENA-SS-SEG |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $\Delta$MMSE | $r=-0.226$ | **$r=-0.497$** ($p<0.001$) | $r=-0.384$ |
| $\Delta$CDR-SB | $r=-0.258$ | **$r=-0.608$** ($p<0.001$) | $r=-0.453$ |
| $\Delta$ADAS-13 | $r=-0.254$ | **$r=-0.524$** ($p<0.001$) | $r=-0.405$ |
| $\Delta$FAQ | $r=-0.260$ | **$r=-0.540$** ($p<0.001$) | $r=-0.394$ |

- Replacing the skull stripping module (SIENA-SS) yields the most consistent and significant improvements, reaching $p < 0.001$ across all cognitive and functional metrics.
- SIENA-SEG only shows statistically significant improvement on $\Delta$MoCA (with $r$ increasing from -0.161 to -0.278).
- SIENA-SS-SEG performs intermediate to SIENA-SS and Vanilla in terms of clinical correlation, but achieves the best performance in scan-order consistency.
- The effect is weaker on the PPMI dataset, where differences across pipelines do not reach statistical significance after Bonferroni correction, likely due to the smaller sample size.

### Scan-Order Consistency

| Pipeline | ADNI MFRR | Relative Improvement | PPMI MFRR | Relative Improvement |
|------|:---------:|:--------:|:---------:|:--------:|
| Vanilla | 0.379% | — | 0.246% | — |
| SIENA-SS | 0.067% | 82.4% | 0.002% | 99.0% |
| SIENA-SS-SEG | **0.046%** | **87.8%** | **0.002%** | **99.1%** |

### Runtime
- In CPU mode, all variants are comparable to the original SIENA (~1855s).
- With GPU acceleration, SIENA-SEG decreases to ~1002s (a ~46% reduction), and SIENA-SS-SEG decreases to ~1166s.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Modular Modernization Strategy**: Instead of replacing the entire pipeline, the weakest link is precisely substituted, preserving clinical trust and interpretability. This approach can be generalized to other legacy neuroimaging pipelines.
2. **Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Framework**: Comprehensive evaluation across three complementary perspectives: clinical correlation (6 metrics), scan consistency, and runtime efficiency, which far exceeds the depth of evaluation in most methodological papers.
3. **Skull Stripping as the Key Bottleneck**: Experiments clearly reveal that BET2 is the biggest weakness in the SIENA pipeline; replacing it yields the greatest gain, offering a clear direction of improvement for the community.
4. **Stunning Scan-Order Consistency Improvement**: MFRR error is reduced by up to 99.1%, which is highly significant for the reliability of longitudinal research and the reproducibility of clinical trials.
5. **High Practicality**: CPU runtime does not increase, and GPU acceleration can provide an additional speedup of approximately 46%, making it ready for direct deployment into existing clinical workflows.
6. **Open Source Code**: The complete implementation is publicly available on GitHub, facilitating community validation and extension.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. Relies solely on the SIENA framework, lacking cross-framework comparisons with other brain atrophy measurement methods (e.g., BSI, FreeSurfer longitudinal).
2. Lacks in-vivo ground truth. The evaluation relies on surrogate metrics (clinical correlation) rather than direct atrophy precision, making it impossible to determine the absolute accuracy of PBVC estimates.
3. Limited effect on the PPMI (PD) dataset, where differences among pipelines did not achieve statistical significance after Bonferroni correction, likely due to the smaller sample size ($N=310$ vs. 1006 in ADNI).
4. Merging anatomical structural labels in SynthSeg into tissue categories may be less accurate than direct tissue segmentation; clinically, SIENA-SS actually outperformed SIENA-SS-SEG in terms of clinical correlation.
5. The manually derived skull mask still relies on BET2's intensity gradient heuristic, and is not completely detached from classical methods.
6. Alternative possibilities with other DL tools (e.g., other brain extraction methods like HD-BET) were not explored.

## Rating
- Novelty: 3/5 — At the methodological level, this is a modular replacement; innovation lies more in system integration and evaluation design.
- Experimental Thoroughness: 5/5 — Dual datasets (1006 cases from ADNI + 310 cases from PPMI), 6 clinical metrics, Steiger's test, Bonferroni correction, scan-order consistency (MFRR), and runtime analysis.
- Writing Quality: 5/5 — Clear logic, excellent chart design, and comprehensive discussion of limitations and future directions.
- Value: 4/5 — Direct practical value for longitudinal studies in neuroimaging, and the modular modernization mindset can be generalized to other legacy clinical pipelines.
