# GenDoP: Auto-regressive Camera Trajectory Generation as a Director of Photography

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2504.07083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07083)  
**Code**: [Project Page](https://kszpxxzmc.github.io/GenDoP/)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: camera trajectory generation, auto-regressive model, cinematography, multimodal conditional generation, dataset construction

## TL;DR

This paper introduces the DataDoP dataset (29K free-moving camera trajectories with descriptions extracted from real films) and the GenDoP auto-regressive Transformer model, which generates artistic, high-quality camera motion trajectories conditioned on text and/or RGBD input, outperforming existing methods in controllability, motion smoothness, and complexity.

## Background & Motivation

Camera motion design is a core element of video production, where professional directors of photography carefully craft camera movements to convey directorial intent. However, existing approaches have notable limitations:

**Traditional methods**: Rely on geometric optimization or handcrafted procedural systems, requiring separate geometric modeling or cost-function engineering for each motion type, which constrains creative synthesis.

**Diffusion-based methods**: CCD and E.T. are trained on human-tracking datasets and inherit subject-centric motion biases; Director3D introduces object/scene-centric trajectories but lacks trajectory-level textual descriptions, resulting in paths driven by geometric plausibility rather than directorial intent.

**Dataset gap**: Existing datasets (RealEstate10K, MVImgNet, etc.) focus either on simple object/scene-centric paths or tracking motions, and lack free-moving artistic trajectories paired with directorial intent descriptions.

Core insight: Camera trajectories are inherently **sequentially dependent** — each frame's pose depends on the previous one — making auto-regressive models more suitable than diffusion models for capturing this temporal continuity.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

GenDoP adopts a joint dataset-and-model strategy:
- **DataDoP dataset**: 29K free-moving camera trajectories extracted from art films, paired with RGBD images and two types of textual descriptions (Motion Caption + Directorial Caption).
- **GenDoP model**: A decoder-only Transformer-based auto-regressive generation model that takes text descriptions (with optional first-frame RGBD) as input and outputs a discretized camera trajectory token sequence.

### Key Designs

1. **DataDoP Dataset Construction Pipeline**:

    - **Preprocessing**: Shots are segmented from film videos using PySceneDetect, subtitles are removed, 10–20 second clips are retained, and GPT-4o filters out static and tracking shots, keeping only free-moving clips.
    - **Trajectory extraction**: MonST3R is used to estimate dynamic scene geometry, extracting camera trajectories and depth maps, which are then cleaned, smoothed, and interpolated into fixed-length sequences.
    - **Motion annotation**: Trajectories are segmented and annotated with motion labels covering 27 translation combinations × 7 rotation combinations.
    - **Description generation**: Motion Captions (pure motion descriptions) are generated from motion labels using GPT-4o; Directorial Captions (motion + scene interaction + directorial intent) are further generated by combining video frames arranged in a 4×4 grid.

2. **Camera Trajectory Tokenization**: Continuous camera parameters are discretized into token sequences.

    - **Canonical normalization**: The first-frame camera coordinate system serves as the world reference, with $\mathbf{R}_0^{\mathrm{norm}} = \mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbf{t}_0^{\mathrm{norm}} = \mathbf{0}$; subsequent poses undergo relative transformation and scale normalization.
    - **Parameter discretization**: The rotation matrix is converted to a quaternion $(r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4)$, combined with translation $(t_1, t_2, t_3)$, focal lengths $(f_1, f_2)$, and scale $s$, yielding 10 dimensions. Values are normalized to $[0,1]$, multiplied by bin count $B=256$, and rounded to integers.
    - Each trajectory is encoded as an integer sequence of length $10N$ ($N=60$ frames), with auxiliary BOS/EOS/PAD tokens.

3. **Multimodal Condition Encoders**:

    - **Text encoder** $\mathcal{E}_T$: A trainable text encoder from Stable Diffusion 2.1 + MLP, outputting $\mathbf{Z}_T \in \mathbb{R}^{77 \times 1024}$.
    - **RGB encoder** $\mathcal{E}_I$ and **depth encoder** $\mathcal{E}_D$: Both use a trainable CLIP Vision Model + MLP, outputting $\mathbf{Z}_I, \mathbf{Z}_D \in \mathbb{R}^{257 \times 1024}$ respectively.
    - Final concatenation: $\mathbf{Z} = [\mathbf{Z}_T; \mathbf{Z}_I; \mathbf{Z}_D]$.

4. **Auto-regressive Decoder**: Built on the OPT architecture (12 layers, 12 heads). The conditional latent code is prepended before the BOS token, and trajectories are predicted token-by-token via causal self-attention.

### Loss & Training

$$L = \mathrm{CrossEntropy}(S[1:], \hat{S}[:,-1]) + \lambda \|\mathbf{Z}\|_2^2$$

- Cross-entropy loss: Classification loss between ground-truth token sequences and predicted logits.
- Regularization term: L2 regularization on the conditional latent code ($\lambda = 10^{-8}$).
- Optimizer: AdamW, lr=1e-5, weight decay=0.01, bfloat16 mixed precision.
- Training converges in ~8 hours on a single A100; inference takes ~3 seconds per trajectory.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Text-conditioned trajectory generation (Motion Caption):

| Method | Dataset | F1-Score↑ | CLaTr-CLIP↑ | Coverage↑ | CLaTr-FID↓ |
|--------|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| CCD | Pre-trained | 0.297 | 5.288 | 0.332 | 357.822 |
| E.T. | Pre-trained | 0.330 | 2.450 | 0.020 | 609.906 |
| Director3D | DataDoP | 0.391 | 31.689 | 0.839 | 31.979 |
| **GenDoP** | **DataDoP** | **0.400** | **36.179** | **0.872** | **22.714** |

User study (AUR, 5-point scale):

| Method | Alignment↑ | Quality↑ | Complexity↑ |
|--------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| CCD | 3.013 | 3.022 | 3.273 |
| Director3D (DataDoP) | 3.753 | 3.260 | 3.493 |
| **GenDoP** | **4.693** | **4.573** | **4.713** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | F1-Score | CLaTr-CLIP | Coverage | CLaTr-FID | Note |
|---------------|---------|------------|----------|-----------|------|
| Full model | 0.400 | 36.179 | 0.872 | 22.714 | Trainable encoders + canonical normalization |
| w/o canonical normalization | 0.322 | 14.917 | 0.766 | 68.590 | CLaTr-CLIP drops 59%; severe quality degradation |
| Frozen encoders | 0.389 | 31.420 | 0.866 | 22.841 | Reduced alignment capability |

Hyperparameter ablations indicate that 256 discrete bins, trajectory length 30, and the base model size are optimal.

### Key Findings

- On the same dataset, the auto-regressive GenDoP outperforms the diffusion-based Director3D by 4.5–9.1 points on CLaTr-CLIP, validating the advantage of the AR framework for trajectory generation.
- RGBD + Text conditioning effectively resolves spatial ambiguities in text descriptions (e.g., the precise direction of "move forward and to the left").
- Canonical normalization (converting scene-centric trajectories to an egocentric path representation) is critical to performance.
- Trainable encoders outperform frozen encoders, as cross-modal mapping from motion semantics to trajectories must be learned.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Auto-regressive vs. diffusion**: The design choice reflects a deep understanding of task characteristics — the sequential dependence of trajectories makes AR models naturally superior to diffusion models in continuity and stability.
- **Three-level description design** (motion labels → Motion Caption → Directorial Caption): A progression from low-level motion to high-level intent, representing an elegant multi-granularity annotation scheme.
- **Dataset contribution**: Even the same Director3D model, when trained on DataDoP, sees CLaTr-CLIP jump from near 0 to 30+, demonstrating the substantial standalone contribution of the dataset.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Currently only the first-frame RGBD is used as visual conditioning; the 4D point cloud information extracted from the dataset is not exploited.
- Trajectory generation and video generation remain a two-stage pipeline; future work could unify them into an end-to-end system.
- Canonical normalization discards absolute scale information, making the approach unsuitable for scenarios requiring precise physical scale.

## Related Work & Insights

- The DataDoP construction paradigm (video → trajectory extraction → motion annotation → LLM description) is transferable to dataset construction for other structured sequence domains.
- The camera parameter tokenization scheme (canonical normalization → quaternion → discretization) is worth generalizing to 3D generation tasks.
