# Length-Adaptive Interest Network for Balancing Long and Short Sequence Modeling in CTR Prediction

**Conference**: AAAI 2026
**arXiv**: [2601.19142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19142)  
**Code**: [FuxiCTR](https://github.com/reczoo/FuxiCTR)  
**Area**: Recommender Systems
**Keywords**: CTR prediction, sequence length adaptation, attention polarization, long/short sequence modeling, recommender systems

## TL;DR

This paper proposes LAIN, a framework that injects sequence length as an explicit conditional signal into CTR models to mitigate performance imbalance between long- and short-sequence users. LAIN comprises three lightweight, plug-and-play modules: a Spectral Length Encoder, Length-Conditioned Prompting, and Length-Modulated Attention.

## Background & Motivation

### Root Cause: Performance Imbalance Between Long- and Short-Sequence Users

User behavior sequences in modern recommender systems exhibit significant length heterogeneity, ranging from sparse short-term interactions to rich long-term histories. Mainstream CTR models (e.g., SIM, SDIM, TWIN) adopt two-stage architectures (GSU + ESU) to model long sequences with considerable success. However, the authors identify a **counterintuitive phenomenon**:

**Performance imbalance**: Increasing the maximum input sequence length improves AUC for long-sequence users (>200), while substantially degrading AUC for short-sequence users (<100).

**Data imbalance**: Short-sequence users constitute 57% of the user base but contribute only 14.3% of training samples, whereas the 25% of long-sequence users dominate 68% of training data.

This leads models to implicitly optimize for high-frequency long-sequence users while neglecting the majority of short-sequence users (typically new or low-activity users), undermining platform growth.

### Two Root Causes

**Attention Polarization**: Softmax attention over long sequences concentrates excessively on a small number of salient behaviors (Gini coefficient increases sharply with sequence length). While this benefits long-sequence users by filtering noise, it is detrimental to short-sequence users—when only a few behaviors are available, attention collapses onto one or two items, leading to insufficient utilization of limited signals.

**Length Signal Deficiency**: Existing models treat behavior sequences as homogeneous event sets, entirely ignoring sequence length as an important prior. In practice, short-sequence users exhibit lower click-through rates (8.28% vs. 9.41%), higher behavioral instability (coefficient of variation: 0.515 vs. 0.268), and lower interest diversity (2.5 vs. 4.6 categories on average). Applying a single set of parameters to users with 3 interactions and those with 300 is fundamentally unreasonable.

### Theoretical Analysis of Gradient Conflict

From an optimization perspective, short-sequence users require smooth, dispersed attention to maximize utilization of limited signals, whereas long-sequence users require sparse, focused attention to identify key interests. This induces conflicting gradient signals: $\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{short}},\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{long}}\rangle<0$, degrading generalization performance.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

LAIN is a lightweight, plug-and-play length-aware augmentation framework that injects sequence length $L$ as a conditional signal into CTR models. Parameters are decomposed into shared and adaptive components: $\theta=\{\theta_{\text{shared}},\theta_{\text{prompt}}(L)\}$, where the latter is dynamically generated by a learnable function of $L$, thereby decoupling gradient flows across users of different sequence lengths.

### Key Designs

#### 1. Spectral Length Encoder (SLE)

**Function**: Encodes discrete sequence length $L$ into a continuous dense vector representation.

**Mechanism**: Learnable Fourier basis functions map the length to a continuous space, avoiding information loss from discretization:

$$\mathbf{f}_{\text{fourier}}=[\sin(L\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega});\cos(L\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega})]\in\mathbb{R}^{2d_f}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_f}$ are learnable frequency parameters. A shared embedding is then obtained via an MLP:

$$\mathbf{h}_{\text{len}}=\text{MLP}(\text{LayerNorm}(\text{Linear}(\mathbf{f}_{\text{fourier}})))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$$

**Design Motivation**: Inspired by positional encoding in NeRF (Tancik et al. 2020), Fourier features naturally capture the continuous semantics of length while remaining sensitive to length variations across different scales.

#### 2. Length-Conditioned Prompting (LCP)

**Function**: Generates $k$ learnable prompt tokens from the length embedding and injects them into the behavior sequence.

**Mechanism**: A prompt matrix is generated from $\mathbf{h}_{\text{len}}$:

$$\mathbf{P}(L)=\text{reshape}(\text{MLP}_{\text{prompt}}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{len}}))\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}$$

Prompt tokens are prepended to both the short-term and long-term behavior sequences:

$$\mathbf{S}'_{\text{short}}=[\mathbf{P};\mathbf{S}_{\text{short}}], \quad \mathbf{S}'_{\text{long}}=[\mathbf{P};\mathbf{S}_{\text{long}}]$$

**Design Motivation**: LCP effectively injects global user-state information, expands the parameter space, and provides different conditional signals to users of different lengths, mitigating interference caused by parameter sharing.

#### 3. Length-Modulated Attention (LMA)

**Function**: Adaptively adjusts the behavior of the attention mechanism according to sequence length. Consists of two sub-modules.

**Query-Key Conditioning**: The length embedding is concatenated to each query and key vector:

$$\mathbf{Q}'=W_q([\mathbf{Q};\mathbf{e}_{\text{len}}]), \quad \mathbf{K}'=W_k([\mathbf{K};\mathbf{e}_{\text{len}}])$$

**Softmax Temperature Scaling**: A length-aware temperature coefficient is defined as:

$$\tau=1+\text{sigmoid}(-\beta(L-L_0))\cdot\gamma$$

The attention weights become: $\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\exp(\frac{\mathbf{Q}'_i\cdot\mathbf{K}'_j}{\sqrt{d}\cdot\tau})}{\sum_j\exp(\frac{\mathbf{Q}'_i\cdot\mathbf{K}'_j}{\sqrt{d}\cdot\tau})}$

**Design Motivation**: When $L$ is small, $\tau$ increases to smooth the attention distribution and reduce over-polarization; when $L$ is large, $\tau$ decreases to promote focus on salient items. $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are learnable parameters, allowing the model to automatically find the optimal balance.

### Loss & Training

LAIN is trained end-to-end with the standard binary cross-entropy loss:

$$\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[y_i\log\hat{y}_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-\hat{y}_i)]$$

Additional parameter overhead is less than 1.5%, and inference latency increases by approximately 2.3%. With $k$=2–4, the sequence length grows from $L$ to $L+k$, and the overall complexity remains $\mathcal{O}(L^2d)$.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Dataset | Metric | TWIN | TWIN+LAIN | Gain |
|--------|------|------|-----------|------|
| EBNeRD-small | GAUC | 0.6930 | 0.7012 | +1.19% |
| EBNeRD-small | AUC | 0.6993 | 0.7074 | +1.15% |
| KuaiVideo | AUC | 0.6918 | 0.6976 | +0.84% |
| MicroVideo1.7M | AUC | 0.7158 | 0.7233 | +1.05% |
| MicroVideo1.7M | Logloss | 0.4164 | 0.4097 | -1.63% |

LAIN achieves **consistent improvements** across 5 backbone models (DIN, DIEN, SIM, SDIM, TWIN) and 3 datasets, with AUC improvements of up to 1.15% and Logloss reductions of up to 2.25%.

### Length-Stratified Evaluation (TWIN, MicroVideo1.7M)

| Sequence Length | AUC Gain | Logloss Reduction |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| 0–100 (short) | +1.08% | -2.17% |
| 100–200 (medium) | +0.50% | -1.26% |
| 200+ (long) | +1.58% | -1.74% |

**Key Findings**: Short-sequence users achieve substantial gains (+1.08% AUC), while long-sequence users are not sacrificed but also improve (+1.58%), demonstrating a win-win outcome.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | AUC | Logloss | Note |
|------|-----|---------|------|
| LAIN (Full) | 0.7233 | 0.4097 | Complete model |
| w/o LCP | 0.7228 | 0.4107 | Remove length-conditioned prompting |
| w/o Query-Key Conditioning | 0.7195 | 0.4148 | Remove QK conditioning |
| w/o Temperature Scaling | 0.7212 | 0.4111 | Remove temperature scaling |
| w/o LMA | 0.7189 | 0.4157 | Remove entire length-modulated attention |
| w/o Short-term Branch | 0.7226 | 0.4137 | Do not apply LAIN to short-term branch |

### Key Findings

1. LMA (Length-Modulated Attention) is the most critical component; its removal leads to the largest AUC drop (−0.44%).
2. LAIN reduces the Gini coefficient of attention from 0.346–0.353 to 0.318–0.322, with variance reduced by 50.6%, significantly alleviating attention polarization.
3. All 12 hyperparameter configurations outperform the baseline, demonstrating the robustness of the method.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Valuable problem identification**: This is the first systematic identification and analysis of the length imbalance problem in CTR modeling, going beyond phenomenological observation to diagnose two root causes—attention polarization and length signal deficiency.
2. **Elegant solution design**: The three modules each serve a distinct purpose and cooperate synergistically, injecting length awareness at the encoding, injection, and modulation levels, while remaining plug-and-play with minimal overhead.
3. **Intuitive temperature scaling**: The sigmoid-based mechanism automatically transitions between "smoothing" (short sequences) and "focusing" (long sequences), representing a concise and effective design choice.
4. **Strong practicality**: With fewer than 1.5% additional parameters and ~2.3% inference overhead, the approach is friendly to industrial deployment.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. Validation is limited to public datasets; online A/B test results at industrial scale are not reported.
2. Length thresholds (cutoffs for short/medium/long) are manually defined; adaptive grouping strategies could be explored.
3. The temperature scaling formulation is relatively simple (sigmoid form); more flexible parameterizations are worth investigating.
4. The effectiveness of LAIN on non-CTR tasks (e.g., sequential recommendation, session-based recommendation) is not discussed.

## Related Work & Insights

- **SIM/SDIM/TWIN series**: Foundational two-stage architectures for long-sequence modeling, upon which LAIN operates as an enhancement plugin.
- **PPR (Wu et al. 2024)**: Generates soft prompts from user profiles for cold-start scenarios; LAIN generates prompts from the sequence length dimension.
- **DARE (Feng et al. 2025)**: Decouples attention and representation embeddings, but does not condition on sequence length.
- Insight: Injecting conditional signals (e.g., user activity level, temporal span) may represent a general paradigm for augmenting recommender models.
