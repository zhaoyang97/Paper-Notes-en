# Self-Correction is More than Refinement: A Learning Framework for Visual and Language Reasoning Tasks

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2410.04055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04055)  
**Code**: [Available](https://github.com/ivy3h/SCL)  
**Area**: Multimodal / VLM  
**Keywords**: Self-Correction, VLM, DPO, Preference Optimization, Multimodal Reasoning

## TL;DR

Proposes Self-Correction Learning (SCL), which categorizes self-correction data generated by the VLM itself (both successful and failed correction samples) into preference/dispreference pairs to perform preference fine-tuning via DPO. This fundamentally enhances the model's ability to directly generate correct answers, rather than relying solely on iterative refinement during inference.

## Background & Motivation

Although vision-language models (VLMs) perform exceptionally in multimodal tasks, they inevitably generate flawed responses. Self-correction is a method for models to identify and correct errors in their own outputs, which is considered an SQL-effective way to improve model quality.

However, existing research on self-correction has three core limitations:

**Limitations of Research Subjects**: Prior self-correction work has focused primarily on LLMs. The self-correction capabilities of VLMs—especially multimodal self-correction involving visual and language information—remain largely unexplored.

**Unreliable Inference-Time Self-Correction**: The performance of self-correction during the inference phase highly depends on prompt design. Moreover, the model's intrinsic reasoning capability remains unchanged, leading to reoccurring errors.

**Misaligned Objectives**: Existing methods focus on "better correction" (i.e., correcting initial responses through iterative modifications) rather than "better initial generation" (i.e., directly generating the correct answer).

The core argument of this paper: The ultimate goal of self-correction should not be patching initial errors, but fundamentally improving the model's ability to directly generate correct answers. Inference-time self-correction is merely a temporary fix; without model parameter updates, the underlying reasoning capability remains unchanged.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The SCL framework consists of three stages: Inference Stage → Dataset Construction → Preference Fine-tuning.

1. Inference Stage: Conduct intrinsic self-correction on the VLM using visual self-correction prompts to investigate whether the VLM can self-correct without external feedback.
2. Dataset Construction: Construct the SelfCorSet preference dataset based on the self-correction process.
3. Preference Fine-tuning: Fine-tune the VLM on SelfCorSet using DPO.

### Key Designs

1. **Visual Self-Correction Prompt Design**: Three self-correction prompts targeted at visual information are designed, guiding the VLM to inspect image information from different perspectives.

    - VP-1 (Comprehensive Details Prompt): Guides the model to check for missed image details.
    - VP-2 (Contextual Understanding Prompt): Guides the model to verify its understanding of the overall scene context.
    - VP-3 (Scene Analysis Prompt): Guides the model to confirm its holistic understanding of the image.

   **Design Motivation**: Since VLMs integrate visual and language information, self-correction needs to examine multimodal content simultaneously, where general-purpose prompts may lack sufficient granularity.

2. **SelfCorSet Preference Dataset Construction**: Based on the combination of correctness of Initial Response (IR) and Revised Response (RR), self-correction samples are classified into four types:

   | Type | IR → RR | Meaning |
   |------|---------|------|
   | Type 1 | Correct → Correct | Keep Correct |
   | Type 2 | Incorrect → Correct | Successfully Corrected |
   | Type 3 | Correct → Incorrect | Harmful Correction |
   | Type 4 | Incorrect → Incorrect | Failed to Correct |

   **Mechanism**: The RR of Type 2 and IR of Type 3 are treated as preferred samples (correct responses), while the IR of Type 2 and RR of Type 3 are treated as dispreferred samples (incorrect responses). This constructs the preference-dispreference pairs required for DPO.

3. **Model-Specific Datasets**: Each VLM has its own independent SelfCorSet, as different VLMs possess different self-correction behavioral patterns. This embodies the core philosophy of "self"—leveraging the model's own correction patterns to improve itself.

### Loss & Training

Standard DPO loss function is used for preference fine-tuning:

$$\mathcal{L}(\pi_\theta; \pi_{\text{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}[\log\sigma(\beta(\log\frac{\pi_\theta(R_c|Q,I)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(R_c|Q,I)} - \log\frac{\pi_\theta(R_r|Q,I)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(R_r|Q,I)}))]$$

Training details:
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (rank=8).
- 7/8B models trained in approximately 1.5 GPU hours on a single RTX 4090 GPU (24GB).
- SelfCorSet dataset size: LLaVA-1.5-7B (4,797), MiniCPM (1,853), InternLM (2,361).

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Inference-time Self-Correction Performance)

| Model | Baseline (SP) | +CP | +VP-1 | +VP-2 | +VP-3 |
|------|-----------|-----|-------|-------|-------|
| MiniCPM (RealWorldQA) | **61.70** | 38.56 | 48.50 | 47.32 | 43.00 |
| MiniCPM (MMBench) | **79.00** | 68.60 | 76.40 | 61.00 | 54.00 |
| LLaVA-1.5-7B (MMBench) | **68.40** | 54.00 | 57.20 | 45.40 | 54.00 |

**Conclusion**: Intrinsic self-correction of VLMs during the inference stage mostly leads to performance degradation, demonstrating that inference-time self-correction is unreliable.

### Key Findings

1. Inference-time self-correction in VLMs mostly leads to performance degradation; the proportion of Type 3 (harmful correction) is higher than that of Type 2 (successful correction).
2. Performance continuously degrades with multi-turn self-correction: MiniCPM on RealWorldQA drops from 61.70% → 48.50% → 39.22% → 42.61%.
3. SCL achieves the best Rank across all four VLMs, proving that self-correction data can be effectively used for preference fine-tuning.
4. Even with a small data size (p=0.4), the fine-tuning effect remains significant.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Unique Perspective**: Redefining self-correction as "enhancing intrinsic model capabilities" rather than "iterative patching". This shift in perspective is the primary contribution of this work.
2. **Ingenious Use of Self-Generated Data**: Even if self-correction fails during inference, the successful/failed sample pairs generated still serve as valuable training signals.
3. **Human-like Learning Behavior**: Similar to humans, VLMs can learn simultaneously from both "the right things done" and "the wrong things done".
4. **High Practicality**: Extremely low training cost (~1.5 hours on a single GPU) and fully self-generated dataset without requiring external annotations.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. Evaluated only on MCQs (multiple-choice questions), without covering more complex tasks such as open-ended visual question answering or video understanding.
2. Some "successful corrections" in SelfCorSet might be accidental correct guesses by the model, containing flaws in the reasoning process (reliability issues).
3. The dataset is model-specific, which cannot be directly transferred across different VLMs.
4. The potential of larger-scale data augmentation has not been explored.
5. Multi-turn self-correction produces richer error patterns, which may provide more training signals.

## Related Work & Insights

- Comparison with SIMA: SIMA constructs preference data using single-turn temperature sampling + self-critique, whereas SCL constructs it using the initial-revised response pairs from the self-correction process.
- Comparison with SCoRe: SCoRe is a multi-turn online RL method, while SCL is an offline DPO method, which is simpler and more efficient.
- Comparison with CSR: CSR introduces iterative learning and reward paradigms, whereas SCL requires no extra reward model.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The perspective of transforming self-correction from an inference-time method into training signals is novel, and utilizing failed corrections as negative samples is an ingenious design.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Evaluated across multiple VLMs, 8 benchmarks, comparison with multiple baselines, and complete ablation studies.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear logic, and the research design driven by Research Questions (RQs) makes the paper structure compact.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Simple, effective, and low-cost, providing a practical paradigm for VLM self-improvement.
