# ADIEE: Automatic Dataset Creation and Scorer for Instruction-Guided Image Editing Evaluation

**Conference**: ICCV 2025  
**arXiv**: [2507.07317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07317)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/SherryXTChen/ADIEE.git](https://github.com/SherryXTChen/ADIEE.git)  
**Area**: Image Generation/Editing  
**Keywords**: Image editing evaluation, automatic dataset construction, VLM scorer, reward model, instruction-guided editing

## TL;DR
This paper proposes ADIEE, an automated pipeline for constructing training datasets for instruction-guided image editing evaluation. A LLaVA-NeXT-8B model is fine-tuned on over 100K samples as a scorer, surpassing all open-source VLMs and Gemini-Pro 1.5 on multiple benchmarks. The trained scorer can further serve as a reward model to improve image editing models.

## Background & Motivation
Instruction-guided image editing has advanced rapidly, enabling users to modify images via natural language. However, automated and accurate evaluation of editing quality remains a critical bottleneck. Traditional metrics such as MSE, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS fail to capture semantic alignment; CLIP-based metrics offer some improvement but are limited by the CLIP text encoder's poor understanding of long texts and compositional relationships.

Using vision-language models (VLMs) as judges is a promising direction, yet significant challenges remain: open-source VLMs are poorly aligned with human judgments, while closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o) perform better but lack transparency and incur high costs. A key obstacle is the absence of publicly available training datasets for fine-tuning open-source VLMs—existing resources consist only of small-scale benchmarks with inconsistent evaluation protocols.

The root cause lies in the fact that constructing large-scale, high-quality training data for image editing evaluation typically requires either costly manual annotation or labels generated by closed-source models. This paper cleverly leverages existing resources within the image editing space—editing training datasets and diverse text-guided editing models—to automatically generate evaluation training data.

**Core Idea**: Image editing training datasets implicitly contain evaluation training data—ground-truth edited images represent successful edits (high scores), original input images represent failed edits (low scores), and outputs from multiple models provide intermediate-quality samples.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
ADIEE consists of four components: (1) generating outputs of varying quality using multiple image editing models to construct training data; (2) constructing training samples with continuous scores from multi-turn editing sequences; (3) fine-tuning LLaVA-NeXT-8B as a scorer; and (4) applying the trained scorer as a reward model to fine-tune editing models.

### Key Designs

1. **Data Generation via Editing Models**:

    - **Function**: Nine text-guided editing methods (CycleDiffusion, DiffEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, Pix2Pix-Zero, SDEdit, Text2LIVE, InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, AURORA) are applied to the MagicBrush dataset to generate edited results.
    - **Mechanism**: CLIP directional similarity (CLIP-D) and CLIP/DINO image similarity are used to identify negative samples. If $\text{CLIP-D}(I^o, \hat{I^e}, p^o, p^e) < 0$, the editing result deviates further from the GT than the original image and is assigned a score of 0. For models requiring input/target captions, a fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B is used to generate caption pairs aligned with editing instructions. Among positive samples, a threshold $\tau_{\text{CLIP-D}} = 0.2$ distinguishes partially successful edits (0.5) from fully successful ones (1.0) for MagicBrush and AURORA outputs.
    - **Design Motivation**: Rather than directly using CLIP/DINO scores as evaluation scores (given their insensitivity to fine-grained details), these metrics are leveraged only for their global trends to identify easy negative samples, thereby avoiding the limitations of these models.

2. **Data Generation from Multi-Turn Editing Sequences**:

    - **Function**: Multi-turn editing datasets (MagicBrush, SEED-Data-Edit) are exploited by randomly sampling input-output pairs from editing sequences to construct training samples with continuous scores.
    - **Mechanism**: For an editing sequence $\mathcal{S} = [I_0, p_1, I_1, \ldots, p_l, I_l]$, $I_{j_1}$ and $I_{j_2}$ ($j_1 < j_2$) are randomly selected as input and GT output. The score of any intermediate image $I_k$ is computed proportionally: $f(I_{j_1}, P_{j_1 \to j_2}, I_k) = \frac{k - j_1}{j_2 - j_1}$ for $k \in [j_1, j_2]$; over-edited images receive 0.5 and unedited images receive 0.
    - **Design Motivation**: Multi-turn sequences naturally provide a continuous score distribution spanning "completely unedited," "partially completed," "fully completed," and "over-edited," enriching the diversity of training data.

3. **Scorer Architecture (VLM Fine-Tuning)**:

    - **Function**: LLaVA-NeXT-8B is fine-tuned with a special [SCORE] token whose embedding is decoded via an MLP into a final editing quality score.
    - **Mechanism**: The input and output images are concatenated and fed into the model together with the text query. The vocabulary is extended with the [SCORE] token, and the hidden state of this token is mapped to a scalar score via an MLP decoder. LoRA (rank=8) is used for fine-tuning, training for 30 epochs on 6×H100 GPUs with batch size=192.
    - **Design Motivation**: Compared to having the VLM directly output a numeric string, the dedicated token combined with an MLP decoder better isolates task-relevant information, reduces hallucination, and yields more precise scores.

### Loss & Training
Standard VLM training loss is used for scorer training. When applied as a reward model, two usage modes are supported: (1) **Reward-conditioned fine-tuning**: the scorer predicts an editing quality score for each training sample and appends it to the editing instruction; the highest quality score prompt is used at inference; (2) **Reward feedback learning**: gradients are back-propagated from the scorer, with loss $\mathcal{L}_{reward} = 10 - s$, jointly optimized with the diffusion model MSE loss.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Scorer Correlation with Human Judgments

| Method | ImagenHub (Spearman) | AURORA-Bench (Spearman) | GenAI-Bench (Acc%) | AURORA-Bench (Acc%) |
|------|---------------------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Human-to-Human | 0.4184 | - | - | - |
| GPT-4o | 0.3821 | 0.4038 | 53.54 | 50.81 |
| Gemini-Pro 1.5 | 0.2728 | 0.1052 | 55.93 | 28.13 |
| Qwen2.5-VL | 0.1859 | 0.2351 | 32.10 | 30.69 |
| LLaVA-OneVision | 0.0829 | 0.0555 | 22.31 | 33.25 |
| **ADIEE (Ours)** | **0.3450** | **0.4734** | **59.96** | **55.56** |

### Key Findings
- ADIEE improves score correlation on AURORA-Bench by 17.24% over the previous SOTA (absolute gain of 0.0696), and improves pairwise comparison accuracy by 7.21% (GenAI) and 9.35% (AURORA).
- ADIEE surpasses all open-source VLMs and Gemini-Pro 1.5, falling only slightly below GPT-4o on ImagenHub.
- The [SCORE] token + MLP decoder design is critical; removing it causes pairwise comparison accuracy to drop sharply from 52.38% to 20.50%.
- A human preference study (56 participants) confirms that the fine-tuned editing model is preferred at a rate of 41.67% vs. 32.00% for the baseline.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Clever data engineering**: Rather than relying on manual annotation or closed-source API labels, the method exploits the inherent structure of editing datasets (GT=high score, input=low score) and the quality variance across multi-model outputs to automatically construct diverse training data.
- The use of **multi-turn editing sequences** is particularly elegant, naturally generating continuous scores via edit-progress ratios.
- The **scorer → reward model** closed-loop design demonstrates the practical downstream value of evaluation metrics.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Score assignment relies on heuristic rules (e.g., CLIP-D threshold of 0.2), which may introduce bias.
- The scorer lacks interpretability and cannot identify the specific cause of editing failures.
- Reward model effectiveness has only been validated on MagicBrush; generalization to additional editing models remains to be verified.

## Supplementary Details

Training data composition: 93,915 samples generated by 9 editing methods + 21,382 multi-turn editing sequence samples, totaling over 100K samples. Negative samples are drawn from 4 models with poor consistency (DiffEdit, Pix2Pix-Zero, SDEdit, Text2LIVE); positive samples are taken from MagicBrush and AURORA. In the human preference study, 56 participants each compared 50 randomly sampled output pairs.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. VIEScore**: VIEScore applies off-the-shelf VLMs in a zero-shot scoring manner; performance is bounded by the model's inherent alignment capability. ADIEE substantially outperforms it through task-specific fine-tuning.
- **vs. OmniEdit**: OmniEdit uses GPT-4o-generated labels to train InternVL2; its performance ceiling is constrained by GPT-4o and incurs high costs. ADIEE requires no closed-source model labels.
- **vs. RewardEdit20K**: RewardEdit20K uses GPT-4o for multi-dimensional scoring, similarly limited by the cost and performance ceiling of closed-source models.
