# Symbolic Regression Is All You Need: From Simulations to Scaling Laws in Binary Neutron Star Mergers

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025 (AI for Science Workshop)
**arXiv**: [2511.08784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08784)
**Code**: Implemented using open-source frameworks PySR and PyOperon
**Area**: Scientific Computing
**Keywords**: Symbolic Regression, Binary Neutron Stars, Gravitational Waves, Kilonova, Equation of State

## TL;DR

This work applies Symbolic Regression (SR) to automatically discover analytic calibration relations for post-merger accretion disk mass in binary neutron star mergers from numerical relativity simulation data. The resulting compact expressions comprehensively outperform existing empirical fitting formulae in the literature in terms of predictive accuracy, generalization, and interpretability.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Binary neutron star (BNS) mergers are the primary sources of both gravitational waves and electromagnetic counterparts (kilonovae). Kilonova luminosity is jointly determined by the dynamical ejecta mass $M_{\text{dyn}}$ and the post-merger accretion disk mass $M_{\text{disk}}$; accurate modeling of these quantities is essential for inferring the neutron star equation of state (EoS).

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Establishing the relationship between $M_{\text{disk}}$ and source parameters (masses, compactnesses, tidal deformabilities) relies on numerical relativity (NR) simulations, which are extremely computationally expensive and highly sensitive to the EoS. Parametric fitting formulae have been proposed by Radice18, Coughlin19, Dietrich20, Krüger20, and Lund25, among others, yet inter-model discrepancies frequently exceed reported uncertainties, and extrapolation beyond the calibration range often yields unphysical results.

**Key Challenge**: Existing formulae are highly sensitive to microphysics and EoS choices. Commonly used dynamical ejecta models fail for systems such as GW190425, and conventional black-box machine learning models, while potentially more accurate, lack interpretability and generalize poorly outside the training domain.

**Goal**: Symbolic regression can autonomously explore both model structure and parameter space to discover compact, interpretable analytic expressions that simultaneously achieve high accuracy and physical interpretability.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

NR simulation data serve as the training set. Two SR frameworks—PySR and PyOperon—are employed to automatically search for closed-form expressions for $M_{\text{disk}}$. Three search modes are considered: free-form discovery, physics-informed template, and LLM-assisted template (generated by ChatGPT-4.0). All models are selected on the Pareto frontier according to the accuracy–complexity trade-off.

### Key Designs

#### Module 1: Dataset and Evaluation Strategy

- **Training set (Krüger20)**: 56 NR simulations, disk mass $10^{-4}$–$0.234\,M_\odot$, mass ratio $q = 0.77$–$1.0$
- **Test set (Nedora21)**: Training set + 63 additional simulations, disk mass $3 \times 10^{-5}$–$0.30\,M_\odot$, mass ratio $0.55$–$1.0$
- Key point: The test set covers a broader physical parameter space and is used to evaluate out-of-distribution generalization
- Input variables: $M_1, M_2, C_1, C_2, q, \tilde{\Lambda}$

#### Module 2: Symbolic Regression Frameworks

- **PyOperon**: Genetic-programming-based SR with operator set {+, -, *, /, sin, sqrt, log, tanh} and default hyperparameters
- **PySR**: Evolutionary SR framework optimizing a multi-objective loss to balance accuracy and complexity, with GPU acceleration support; three operator sets are tested:
    - Core: {+, -, *, /; sqrt, log, exp, abs}
    - Extended: adds max, min, square, cube
    - All: adds sin, cos, tan, sinh, cosh, tanh, pow
- **Training configuration**: 3,000 iterations, maximum expression size 20 (10 for template mode), maximum tree depth 5
- **Model selection**: Expressions with complexity < 10 and stable MSE on the Pareto frontier

#### Module 3: Physics-Informed Templates

- **Hand-crafted template**: $M_{\text{disk,pred}} = a \cdot f(C_1, M_1) + b \cdot g(C_2, M_2) + c \cdot h(q, \tilde{\Lambda}) + d$, allowing isolation of individual physical parameter contributions
- **LLM template**: A literature summary is provided to ChatGPT-4.0, which generates a physically motivated Ansatz: $M_{\text{disk}} = a_1 f(q_0, C_1) + a_2 g(C_1, \tilde{\Lambda}) + a_3 h(M_1, M_2) + a_4 t(q_0) + a_5$
- SR searches for sub-expressions $f, g, h, t$ under the template constraints, combining domain knowledge with data-driven optimization

### Loss & Training

- Standard multi-objective optimization: minimize MSE while penalizing expression complexity (each operator, variable, or constant contributes 1 unit of complexity)
- Evaluation metrics: MSE, MAE, $R^2$, BIC (Bayesian Information Criterion)

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: SR Models vs. Literature Fitting Formulae (Table 1)

| Model | MSE | MAE | $R^2$ | BIC | Complexity | Input Variables |
|-------|-----|-----|-------|-----|------------|-----------------|
| **PyOperon** | **0.002697** | **0.035435** | **0.612** | **-660.9** | 9 | $C_1$ |
| PySR_Free_Ext | 0.002751 | 0.035714 | 0.604 | -658.6 | 9 | $C_1, M_1$ |
| PySR_Free_M12 | 0.002789 | 0.037436 | 0.599 | -637.8 | 13 | $M_1, M_2, \tilde{\Lambda}$ |
| PySR_Template_Ext | 0.002827 | 0.036365 | 0.593 | -602.8 | 20 | $C_1, C_2, \tilde{\Lambda}$ |
| Lund25 (literature) | 0.002910 | 0.036369 | 0.581 | -637.6 | 12 | $C_1$ |
| PySR_Free_All | 0.003029 | 0.036696 | 0.564 | -651.9 | 8 | $C_1$ |
| Krüger20 (literature) | 0.003665 | 0.039238 | 0.473 | -595.8 | 15 | $C_1, M_1$ |
| Radice18 (literature) | 0.006179 | 0.057595 | 0.111 | -524.1 | 12 | $\tilde{\Lambda}$ |

With a complexity-9 expression $M_{\text{disk}} = 0.1188 - 0.1430 \times \sin(\sin(40.90 \times C_1))$, PyOperon surpasses the best literature model Lund25 on all metrics, with a BIC advantage exceeding 23 points.

### Performance Comparison in the Intermediate Disk Mass Range

| Model | MSE ($M_{\text{disk}} \sim 0.05$–$0.20\,M_\odot$) |
|-------|---------------------------------------------------|
| PyOperon | $2.12 \times 10^{-3}$ |
| PySR_Free_Ext | $2.09 \times 10^{-3}$ |
| Lund25 | $1.95 \times 10^{-3}$ |
| Krüger20 | $3.31 \times 10^{-3}$ |

### Key Findings

1. **4 out of 9 SR expressions outperform the state-of-the-art Lund25 on all metrics**, with PyOperon's BIC advantage of 23 points being statistically highly significant.
2. **SR autonomously rediscovers the dominant role of $C_1$ (lighter neutron star compactness)**: despite being provided all 6 input variables during training, most optimal models select $C_1$ as the core predictor.
3. **Alternative predictor variable sets**: PySR_Free_M12 relies solely on $(M_1, M_2, \tilde{\Lambda})$—without $C_1$—while achieving performance comparable to Lund25, potentially opening new pathways for constraining EoS parameters previously inaccessible from electromagnetic observations.
4. **Generalization to high disk masses**: Lund25 fails to predict $M_{\text{disk}} > 0.20\,M_\odot$, whereas PySR_Free_M12 and PySR_Template_LLM generalize effectively to this regime.
5. **LLM-assisted template design is viable**: physically motivated Ansätze generated by ChatGPT-4.0 lead to competitive expressions under template-constrained SR search.

## Highlights & Insights

- **From black-box to white-box**: SR replaces conventional ML models, achieving both predictive accuracy and scientific interpretability; the discovered expressions can be directly incorporated into physics publications.
- **Generalization under sparse data**: calibrated on only 56 training samples, the models generalize to a broader parameter space (119 samples).
- **Hybrid LLM + SR paradigm**: leveraging ChatGPT's domain knowledge to generate physical templates and then using SR to search for specific functional forms represents an intriguing collaborative AI for Science workflow.
- **Multi-objective Pareto selection**: the complexity–accuracy trade-off automatically mitigates overfitting in a more systematic manner than manual model tuning.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Focus limited to $M_{\text{disk}}$**: dynamical ejecta mass $M_{\text{dyn}}$ and mean ejecta velocity, which are equally important quantities, are not addressed.
2. **Extremely small training set (56 simulations)**: the scarcity of NR simulation samples means the statistical reliability of the SR expressions still requires validation with more data.
3. **Partial overlap between test set Nedora21 and training set**: the test set contains all training samples plus 63 new simulations, and thus does not constitute a fully independent evaluation.
4. **Extreme-regime physical behavior not analyzed**: the paper acknowledges that the qualitative physical validity of the expressions at large neutron star radii and high compactness extremes has not been investigated.
5. **Physical interpretability of SR expressions remains limited**: the $\sin(\sin(\cdot))$ form in PyOperon, for instance, achieves high accuracy but lacks intuitive physical meaning.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Empirical fitting formula lineage**: Radice18 → Coughlin19 → Dietrich20 → Krüger20 → Lund25, documenting a decade of progress in accretion disk mass modeling.
- **SR methods**: PySR (Cranmer, 2023) and PyOperon (Burlacu, 2020) are currently the leading open-source SR tools.
- **AI-Feynman (Udrescu, 2020)** and **SciMED (Keren, 2023)**: alternative SR algorithms the authors plan to explore in future work.
- **Insights**: SR as a tool for AI-driven scientific discovery holds unique advantages in physics problems characterized by sparse data and interpretability requirements, and is applicable to calibration relation discovery in cosmology, particle physics, and related fields.
