# BrainOmni: A Brain Foundation Model for Unified EEG and MEG Signals

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2505.18185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18185)
**Code**: [OpenTSLab/BrainOmni](https://github.com/OpenTSLab/BrainOmni)
**Area**: Self-Supervised Learning / Brain Signal Foundation Models
**Keywords**: EEG, MEG, foundation model, BrainTokenizer, Sensor Encoder, cross-device generalization, RVQ

## TL;DR

This paper proposes BrainOmni—the first brain signal foundation model that unifies EEG and MEG—by discretizing heterogeneous brain signals into a unified token space via BrainTokenizer (incorporating a physical Sensor Encoder), followed by self-supervised masked prediction pretraining with a Criss-Cross Transformer. The model achieves an 11.7 percentage-point improvement on Alzheimer's disease detection and demonstrates zero-shot reconstruction generalization to completely unseen devices.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: EEG (electroencephalography) and MEG (magnetoencephalography) share a common biophysical basis—both measure the electromagnetic fields generated by dendritic currents in the cortex—yet exhibit substantially different signal characteristics. Existing methods almost exclusively employ modality- and dataset-specific models, with severe heterogeneity across devices (channel counts ranging from 19 to 306, sensor types including EEG electrodes, gradiometers (GRAD), and magnetometers (MAG), each with distinct orientations and layouts).

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (a) EEG and MEG are modeled separately, precluding cross-modal knowledge sharing despite their shared biophysical origin; (b) models are tied to specific electrode topologies, requiring retraining or adaptation when the device changes; (c) while EEG foundation models (e.g., LaBraM, CBraMod) have shown promise, MEG foundation models remain largely absent, primarily due to limited data and device complexity.

**Key Challenge**: How to build a unified model that handles both EEG and MEG without relying on fixed electrode naming conventions or topologies, while still benefiting from cross-modal data?

**Goal**: To develop a device-agnostic brain signal foundation model that supports joint multimodal pretraining and cross-device zero-shot generalization.

**Key Insight**: Drawing inspiration from classical EEG/MEG source estimation—encoding sensors via their physical properties (3D Cartesian coordinates, orientation, and type) rather than electrode labels to represent spatial information, thereby recovering latent source variables from observed signals and decoupling the model from specific device configurations.

**Core Idea**: Encoding physical sensor attributes for device-agnosticism → RVQ discretization for unified token representation → Criss-Cross Transformer for separate spatial and temporal dependency modeling.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The framework adopts a two-stage training pipeline:

- **Stage 1**: Training BrainTokenizer—encoding heterogeneous EEG/MEG signals into a unified discrete token space (autoencoder framework with 25% random channel dropout and full-channel reconstruction).
- **Stage 2**: Self-supervised masked prediction pretraining of BrainOmni on the token sequences produced by BrainTokenizer, learning semantic representations of brain activity.

### Key Designs

#### 1. BrainTokenizer + Sensor Encoder

- **Function**: Encodes EEG/MEG signals from devices with varying channel counts into fixed-dimensional discrete tokens.
- **Mechanism**:
  - A SEANet encoder (1D convolution + residual blocks + strided convolution) extracts temporal representations $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{time}} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times W \times D}$.
  - The Sensor Encoder encodes the physical properties of each sensor: a position embedding layer encodes 6-dimensional Cartesian coordinates (3D position + 3D orientation), and a type embedding layer encodes sensor type (EEG=0, GRAD=1, MAG=2), yielding sensor embeddings $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times D}$.
  - Cross-attention channel compression: learnable queries compress variable channel counts into a fixed $C'=16$ latent source variables, with Keys = temporal representations + sensor embeddings and Values = temporal representations.
  - A 4-level RVQ (Residual Vector Quantization) discretizes continuous features into $\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{R}^{C' \times W \times 4}$.
- **Design Motivation**: The Sensor Encoder makes the model independent of electrode naming conventions, relying solely on physical coordinates—this is the key to cross-device generalization. The cross-attention compression corresponds to the classical "inverse problem" in source activity estimation.

#### 2. Criss-Cross Transformer

- **Function**: Models spatial and temporal dependencies over discrete token sequences in Stage 2.
- **Mechanism**: Features are split along the channel dimension into two halves—one half undergoes spatial attention (across $C'$ source variables) and the other temporal attention (across time steps $T$); the outputs are concatenated and passed through a feed-forward network. Temporal attention employs RoPE positional encoding.
- **Design Motivation**: Spatial dependencies (inter-region co-activation) and temporal dynamics in brain signals exhibit fundamentally different structural properties; modeling them separately is more efficient and targeted than full joint attention.

#### 3. Joint EEG-MEG Pretraining Strategy

- **Function**: Self-supervised pretraining on a unified token space using both EEG (1,997 hours) and MEG (656 hours) data simultaneously.
- **Mechanism**: Non-autoregressive masked token prediction with a 50% masking ratio, where all 4 RVQ levels are predicted simultaneously at each masked position. 80% of masked positions are replaced with a dedicated mask token and 20% with randomly sampled tokens to prevent overfitting to the mask token.
- **Design Motivation**: The relatively abundant EEG data can compensate for the data scarcity of MEG, enabling cross-modal knowledge transfer.

### Loss & Training

**Stage 1 BrainTokenizer Loss** (four terms jointly optimized):

- Time-domain L1 loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{time}} = \|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|$
- Frequency-domain loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{freq}} = \|\mathbf{A} - \hat{\mathbf{A}}\| + \|\mathbf{\Phi} - \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}\|$ (amplitude spectrum + phase spectrum)
- PCC alignment loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pcc}} = e^{-\text{PCC}(\mathbf{X}, \hat{\mathbf{X}})}$ (regularizes waveform trend consistency)
- RVQ commitment loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rvq}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_q}\|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_{q_i}\|^2$

**Stage 2 BrainOmni Loss**: Cross-entropy prediction over masked tokens $\mathcal{L}_{\text{model}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ce}}(q_{ij}, y_{ij})$

**Training Configuration**: BrainTokenizer pretraining ~11 hours; BrainOmni-base pretraining ~18 hours (16×A100); downstream fine-tuning for 30 epochs with 5-fold cross-validation and cross-subject splits.

|---------------|------|------|-------|-------------|

## Highlights & Insights

- **Device-agnostic physical encoding**: Replacing electrode labels with 3D coordinates, orientation, and sensor type overcomes the fixed-topology dependency of conventional methods—a principle that generalizes to other multi-sensor signals (e.g., fNIRS).
- **EEG data "teaching" MEG**: Joint pretraining yields a 12% improvement on the MEG ASD74 task, demonstrating the value of cross-modal transfer—data-rich modalities can effectively support data-scarce ones.
- **Complementarity of multi-level reconstruction losses**: Using only the time-domain loss causes the decoder output to collapse toward a near-constant signal; the frequency-domain loss improves spectral fidelity but introduces high-frequency impulses; the PCC loss suppresses impulses but weakens spectral detail—all four loss terms are indispensable.
- **16 latent source variables is the optimal trade-off**: Beyond 16, reconstruction loss improvements plateau while computational cost grows linearly.
- **The first RVQ codebook captures the richest semantics**: Masked prediction accuracy decreases with codebook depth, indicating a hierarchical distribution of information.

## Limitations & Future Work

- MEG pretraining data amounts to only 656 hours (one-third of EEG), potentially limiting MEG-side performance.
- Downstream MEG evaluation covers only 2 datasets, limiting the breadth of assessment.
- BrainOmni slightly underperforms CBraMod on PhysioNet-MI (59.0% vs. 59.5%), indicating that general-purpose foundation models may still trail task-specific models in certain settings.
- Generative applications (e.g., brain signal synthesis, cross-modal translation) remain unexplored.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. LaBraM / CBraMod**: These models handle EEG only and rely on fixed channel topologies; BrainOmni unifies EEG and MEG while achieving device-agnosticism.
- **vs. FAMED**: A MEG-specific model without pretraining; BrainOmni substantially outperforms it on MEG tasks.
- **Insights**: The physical attribute encoding strategy of the Sensor Encoder can be extended to other heterogeneous sensor settings (wearable devices, industrial sensor arrays, etc.); the two-stage paradigm of RVQ discretization + masked prediction is transferable to other time-series foundation models.
