# OCR Hinders RAG: Evaluating the Cascading Impact of OCR on Retrieval-Augmented Generation

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2412.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02592)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OHR-Bench)  
**Area**: Information Retrieval
**Keywords**: OCR noise, RAG systems, document parsing, knowledge base quality, benchmark

## TL;DR

This paper presents OHRBench—the first benchmark for evaluating the cascading impact of OCR on RAG systems. It comprises 8,561 document images across 7 domains and 8,498 QA pairs, and systematically reveals the distinct impact patterns of OCR-induced Semantic Noise and Formatting Noise on both the retrieval and generation stages.

## Background & Motivation

A critical yet overlooked component of RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) systems is the **quality of the external knowledge base**. In practice, large amounts of knowledge exist as unstructured PDF documents that must be parsed into structured data via OCR before being usable by RAG. However:

**OCR is imperfect**: Even state-of-the-art OCR solutions still produce errors on complex documents (scanned files, handwriting, multilingual content, multi-column layouts).

**Structured data representation is non-unique**: The same table can be represented in Markdown, LaTeX, or HTML, and such inconsistency itself constitutes noise.

**RAG is sensitive to noise**: Prior work (InfoRAG, RAAT) has shown RAG to be sensitive to input noise, but only at the chunk-level retrieval stage—**noise introduced during OCR has been entirely overlooked**.

**Existing evaluation gaps**:
   - Existing RAG benchmarks do not assess the impact of OCR.
   - Existing OCR benchmarks do not evaluate downstream RAG effects.
   - No dataset covers the full range of OCR challenges (scanned documents, handwriting, multilingual content, historical documents).

Core thesis: OCR noise undergoes **cascading amplification**—OCR errors cause retrieval errors, which in turn cause generation errors, with the compounded effect far exceeding any single-stage impact.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

OHRBench consists of three components:
1. **Document dataset**: PDFs from 7 domains, along with ground-truth structured data and QA pairs.
2. **OCR noise modeling**: Definitions and perturbation generation for Semantic Noise and Formatting Noise.
3. **Three-level evaluation protocol**: Separate assessment of OCR's impact on retrieval, generation, and end-to-end RAG performance.

### Key Designs

1. **Document Data Collection (7-Domain Coverage)**:

    - Textbook, Law, Finance, Newspaper, Manual, Academic, and Administration.
    - Sources: DUDE, OmniDocBench, FinanceBench, CUAD, GNHK, and other publicly available resources.
    - Covers 9 types of OCR challenge characteristics: handwriting, scanning, historical documents, multilingual content, tables, formulas, charts, multi-column layouts, and watermarks.
    - Comprises 1,261 PDFs and 8,561 pages; ground truth is initially extracted by Mathpix and subsequently corrected by expert annotators.

2. **QA Pair Generation and Quality Control**:

    - Organized around 5 evidence source types: plain text (41.5%), tables (27.8%), formulas (14.9%), charts (9.0%), and reading order (8.1%).
    - Task types: comprehension (71.9%) and reasoning (28.1%); single-page (90.1%) and multi-page (9.9%).
    - Generated using GPT-4o and filtered through a three-stage process:
        - **RAG compatibility**: Questions must be context-dependent and not answerable by the model's internal knowledge.
        - **Task definition fidelity**: Questions must align with the defined task types.
        - **Correctness**: Verified via repeated sampling with oracle context.
    - A final set of 8,498 high-quality QA pairs is selected from 15,317 candidates.

3. **Two Categories of OCR Noise: Definitions and Perturbations**:

    - **Semantic Noise**: OCR prediction errors that alter the semantic content.
        - Generated by applying 8 types of image-level perturbations (background noise, watermarks, dilation/erosion, etc.) to document images, then re-running OCR with MinerU/GOT/Qwen2.5-VL.
        - Realism is ensured through iterative cross-validation: one annotator adds perturbations while another blindly judges distinguishability.
        - Three severity levels (mild/moderate/severe) are produced.
    - **Formatting Noise**: Style commands (spaces, bold, italic) and format inconsistencies (conversions among MD/LaTeX/HTML).
        - Introduced through heuristic rules by adding, removing, or converting formatting commands.
        - Three severity levels are similarly produced.

4. **Noise Level Quantification**:

    - Traditional edit distance does not accurately reflect OCR's actual impact on RAG.
    - The metric $r_{noise}$ is defined as the proportion of QA pairs affected by OCR noise (LCS < 0.95 is considered affected).

### Loss & Training

This paper presents a benchmark and does not involve model training. Evaluation metrics include:
- OCR quality: edit distance
- Retrieval performance: LCS (Longest Common Subsequence)
- Generation performance: F1 score
- Retrievers: BGE-M3 (dense) + BM25 (sparse)
- LLMs: Qwen2-7B, Qwen2-72B, Llama-3.1-8B

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (Impact of Different OCR Solutions on RAG)

| OCR Solution | Edit Distance↓ | Retrieval ALL↑ | Generation ALL↑ | End-to-End ALL↑ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground Truth | - | 70.0 | 43.9 | 36.1 |
| Qwen2.5-VL-72B (VLM) | **0.18** | **59.2** | **37.5** | **31.1** |
| Marker (Pipeline) | 0.28 | 56.6 | 35.9 | 29.5 |
| MinerU (Pipeline) | 0.24 | 50.1 | 36.7 | 30.0 |
| InternVL2.5-78B (VLM) | 0.28 | 55.8 | 35.8 | 29.6 |
| GOT (End-to-end) | 0.27 | 45.4 | 27.8 | 24.6 |
| Nougat (End-to-end) | 0.34 | 40.9 | 25.5 | 14.5 |

### Ablation Study (Impact of Semantic Noise vs. Formatting Noise)

| Noise Type | Severity | Impact on Retrieval | Impact on Generation | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic Noise | mild→severe | Linear decline of ~50% | Comprehensive decline; more severe for multimodal questions | Consistently strong impact across all RAG stages |
| Formatting Noise | mild→severe | 12.7% decline for multimodal questions | Limited impact on small models; larger models more sensitive | Primarily affects table/formula/chart-related questions |

### Impact of Table Format

| Format | Retrieval (BGE-M3)↑ | Generation (Qwen2-72B)↑ | End-to-End↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Markdown | **Best** | Weaker (no merged cell support) | Best (BGE+Qwen2-72B) |
| LaTeX | Moderate | Comparable to HTML | Moderate |
| HTML | Worst | Comparable to LaTeX | Worst |

### Key Findings

- **Best OCR still leaves a 14% performance gap**: Even Qwen2.5-VL-72B, the top-performing solution, achieves an end-to-end F1 5 points below GT (31.1 vs. 36.1).
- **Edit distance is a poor OCR-for-RAG metric**: MinerU achieves a lower edit distance than Marker yet performs worse on RAG retrieval.
- **Semantic noise is the primary threat**: Its impact is persistent and consistent; neither dense nor sparse retrievers are immune.
- **The impact of formatting noise varies by model**: Smaller LLMs are surprisingly unaffected (due to inherently weaker information integration), while larger LLMs are more sensitive.
- **Reading order is a weakness across all solutions**: VLM and end-to-end OCR approaches score only ~5 points on reading order tasks, while pipeline OCR performs slightly better through rule-based strategies.
- **Chart parsing fails across the board**: With the exception of VLMs, virtually all other OCR solutions are unable to parse charts.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Systematic identification of a critical overlooked problem**: The RAG research community focuses on improving retrievers and LLMs while neglecting the "garbage in, garbage out" problem of knowledge base quality.
- **The decoupled analysis of two noise types** has significant practical value: Semantic noise requires improving OCR accuracy itself, while formatting noise calls for standardized output formats or noise-robust retrievers.
- **The perturbation generation methodology** (iterative cross-validation for realism) is generalizable to other data augmentation scenarios.
- **Findings on table format impact** directly guide deployment decisions: BGE-M3 better understands Markdown, while LLMs handle merged cells in HTML/LaTeX more effectively.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Only Chinese and English are covered; languages with greater OCR challenges, such as Arabic and Japanese, are excluded.
- The perturbation methodology operates at the image level and does not account for noise in native PDF text extraction (non-OCR pipelines).
- Retrieval evaluation uses only top-2 chunks; the effect of varying top-$k$ settings is not assessed.
- The latest multimodal RAG approaches (e.g., VisRAG, which bypasses OCR via direct image retrieval) are not evaluated.

## Related Work & Insights

- For RAG system builders: "OCR quality is the ceiling of your RAG system"—ensure knowledge base quality before optimizing retrievers and LLMs.
- For OCR researchers: "RAG-aware" OCR metrics need to be developed, as edit distance is insufficient.
- The two-noise framework is generalizable to error propagation analysis in other pipeline-based AI systems.
