# CausalCLIP: Causally-Informed Feature Disentanglement and Filtering for Generalizable Detection of Generated Images

**Conference**: AAAI 2026
**arXiv**: [2512.13285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13285)  
**Code**: Unavailable  
**Area**: Image Generation
**Keywords**: Generated image detection, causal reasoning, CLIP, feature disentanglement, adversarial training

## TL;DR

CausalCLIP is proposed to disentangle CLIP features into causal and non-causal subspaces via Gumbel-Softmax masking and HSIC constraints, combined with adversarial masking and counterfactual intervention to preserve stable forensic cues, achieving a 6.83% accuracy improvement in cross-generator generalization.

## Background & Motivation

The rapid advancement of generative models (GANs, diffusion models) has substantially lowered the barrier to producing high-quality forged images, creating an urgent need for detection methods that generalize across diverse generators. Existing approaches face the following challenges:

**Generator-specific detection**: Early CNN-based classifiers rely on **generator-specific artifacts** such as upsampling traces and frequency-domain anomalies, resulting in poor generalization to unseen generators.

**Entangled CLIP feature space**: Recent methods leveraging pretrained vision-language models such as CLIP (UnivFD, CLIPping, C2P-CLIP) show improvement but still operate in highly entangled feature spaces where causal forensic cues are mixed with spurious patterns.

**Coarse-grained filtering**: VIB-Net suppresses irrelevant features via an information bottleneck but does not explicitly disentangle causal from non-causal components, resulting in coarse filtering that may discard task-relevant features.

Root cause: Achieving cross-generator generalization requires first **explicitly separating causal forensic cues from spurious artifacts**, and then performing filtering in the disentangled space (disentangle-then-filter), rather than applying coarse suppression in an entangled space.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

CausalCLIP follows the **disentangle-then-filter** paradigm:

1. A frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder extracts image features.
2. **Factorization Module**: Disentangles CLIP features into causal and non-causal components.
3. **Adversarial Masking Module**: Suppresses the influence of non-causal features via an adversarial mechanism.
4. The disentangled causal features are fed into a lightweight classifier for real/fake prediction.

### Key Designs

#### 1. Factorization Module

It is assumed that an image $X$ is generated by a structural causal model containing two independent factors: $Z_c$ (causal features, derived from a generator-agnostic content factor G) and $Z_{nc}$ (non-causal features, derived from a generator-specific style/artifact factor C).

Given the CLIP embedding $E = \text{CLIP}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, a feature mask $M \in [0,1]^d$ is learned to perform separation:

$$\tilde{Z}_c = M \odot E, \quad \tilde{Z}_{nc} = (1-M) \odot E$$

The mask $M$ is parameterized via **Gumbel-Softmax**:

$$M = \sigma((\text{MLP}(E) + g) / \tau), \quad g \sim \text{Gumbel}(0,1)$$

The temperature $\tau$ controls the sparsity of feature selection. This differentiable feature selection mechanism ensures the purity of the causal subspace $\tilde{Z}_c$.

#### 2. Adversarial Masking Module

Even after factorization, residual non-causal signals may still influence the classifier. A minimax game is therefore introduced:

- **Classifier h**: Predicts real/fake based on causal features $\tilde{Z}_c$.
- **Adversary d**: Attempts to predict real/fake from non-causal features $\tilde{Z}_{nc}$.

The classifier and mask are optimized to ensure $\tilde{Z}_{nc}$ contains no discriminative information, forcing the model to rely solely on $\tilde{Z}_c$.

**Mask regularization**:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} = \lambda_1 \|M\|_1 + \lambda_2 \widehat{\text{HSIC}}(\tilde{Z}_c, \tilde{Z}_{nc})$$

- The $\ell_1$ norm encourages sparse feature selection.
- The empirical HSIC term encourages statistical independence between the causal and non-causal subspaces (Gaussian kernel with median heuristic bandwidth selection).

#### 3. Counterfactual Intervention

Random masking is applied to causal features to simulate distributional perturbations:

$$\tilde{Z}_c^{CF} = \tilde{Z}_c \odot (1 - B), \quad B \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$$

Prediction consistency is enforced via KL divergence:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} = \text{KL}(h(\tilde{Z}_c) \| h(\tilde{Z}_c^{CF}))$$

This compels the classifier to rely on stable causal semantics rather than generator-dependent cues.

### Loss & Training

The overall objective is:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} - \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} + \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}$$

Training alternates among: minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}$ for h, maximizing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}$ for d, and minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ for M.

Implementation details: Adam optimizer, lr=1e-4, batch=256, center-cropped input at 224×224, trained on a V100 GPU.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

**Table 1: AP on 15 test sets, trained on Diffusion source (SDv1.4)**

| Method | SD1.4 | ADM | GLIDE | ProGAN | CycleGAN | StyleGAN | GauGAN | Mean AP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CNNSpot | 99.98 | 51.10 | 58.80 | 53.15 | 50.23 | 49.79 | 47.07 | 63.24 |
| UnivFD | 96.04 | 66.34 | 93.73 | 51.77 | 63.42 | 75.81 | 54.12 | 75.31 |
| VIB-Net | 100.00 | 95.49 | 97.13 | 96.59 | 98.44 | 97.17 | 84.31 | 94.60 |
| **CausalCLIP** | **100.00** | **95.37** | **99.47** | **97.33** | **98.35** | **96.29** | **98.62** | **96.92** |

**Table 2: ACC on 15 test sets, trained on Diffusion source**

| Method | Mean ACC |
|---|---|
| CLIPping | 83.03 |
| VIB-Net | 85.83 |
| **CausalCLIP** | **90.45** |

CausalCLIP achieves +2.32% mean AP and +4.62% ACC over VIB-Net. On unseen GAN-based generators, it yields an additional +6.83% ACC and +4.06% AP improvement.

**Tables 3–4: Cross-diffusion generalization, trained on GAN source (ProGAN)**

| Method | Mean AP | Mean ACC |
|---|---|---|
| VIB-Net | 93.04 | 82.97 |
| **CausalCLIP** | **94.27** | **86.23** |

CausalCLIP maintains superiority under the more challenging GAN→Diffusion distribution shift.

| ✗ | ✗ | 65.37 | 60.42 | 75.31 | 67.09 |

The factorization module alone yields +14.05% ACC; the masking module alone yields +5.36%; their combination achieves the best overall performance.

### Key Findings

1. **UMAP visualization**: Raw CLIP features are highly entangled between real and fake images; VIB achieves only partial separation, while CausalCLIP achieves clear separation on both seen and unseen generators.
2. **Robustness**: Under JPEG compression and Gaussian blur perturbations, CausalCLIP maintains the most stable performance.
3. The improvement from the factorization module substantially exceeds that of the masking module, confirming that **explicit disentanglement is the critical factor**.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Theory-driven design**: The relationship between causal and non-causal features is formalized using a structural causal model, rather than treated as simple feature selection.
- **Gumbel-Softmax + HSIC combination**: The former ensures differentiable sparse selection; the latter ensures statistical independence between causal and non-causal subspaces, grounding the method in sound theoretical principles.
- **Lightweight**: Only downstream modules are trained on top of frozen CLIP, with computational overhead comparable to UnivFD but substantially improved performance.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. The method relies on fixed features from CLIP-ViT-L/14 and may be sensitive to artifact types not well represented in CLIP's pretraining data.
2. The binary classification setting (real/fake) does not support generator attribution.
3. The mask $M$ operates as an element-wise mask over the entire embedding dimension; finer granularity (e.g., patch-level disentanglement) may be preferable.
4. The selection of the Bernoulli probability $p$ for counterfactual intervention lacks theoretical justification.

## Related Work & Insights

- Compared to VIB-Net's information bottleneck approach, CausalCLIP's advantage lies in explicit disentanglement prior to filtering, avoiding the loss of useful features that can occur in entangled spaces.
- The successful application of causal representation learning (CausalVAE, DEAR) to image forensics provides a reference for other distribution-shift scenarios.
- The disentangle-then-filter paradigm is extensible to deepfake video detection, machine-generated text detection, and related domains.
