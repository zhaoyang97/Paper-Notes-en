# Pixels Don't Lie (But Your Detector Might): Bootstrapping MLLM-as-a-Judge for Trustworthy Deepfake Detection and Reasoning Supervision

**Conference**: CVPR 2026
**arXiv**: [2602.19715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19715)  
**Code**: Available (dataset, model, and code are fully open-sourced)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: Deepfake Detection, Reasoning Supervision, MLLM-as-a-Judge, Visual Forensics, Bootstrapping, VLM Evaluation

## TL;DR

This paper proposes the DeepfakeJudge framework, which scales human-annotated reasoning supervision into large-scale structured scoring data via a bootstrapped generator-evaluator pipeline. The framework trains 3B/7B vision-language models as automatic judges for deepfake detection reasoning quality, achieving high human alignment in both pointwise and pairwise evaluation settings.

## Background & Motivation

**Threats from advances in generative models**: Synthetic images produced by diffusion/Transformer models such as Stable Diffusion, DALL·E 2, and Imagen have approached photorealism, rendering traditional detection methods based on frequency-domain inconsistencies or blinking patterns ineffective against modern generative pipelines.

**Classification accuracy is insufficient; interpretable reasoning is needed**: Existing deepfake detectors focus solely on classification accuracy, yet reliable detection requires interpretable and visually grounded reasoning. Textual explanations from prior methods (e.g., SIDA, FakeShield) frequently suffer from hallucination or disconnection from visual evidence.

**VLMs over-rely on textual priors**: Studies show that vision-language models tend to exploit textual priors and world knowledge rather than genuinely examining image content, leading to unreliable reasoning (e.g., predicting four legs even when a cow in the image has only three).

**Conventional text metrics cannot measure reasoning quality**: Metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and BERTScore capture only lexical overlap or semantic similarity and fail to assess the factuality and visual groundedness of reasoning, exhibiting poor correlation with human judgments.

**Lack of OOD benchmarks**: Existing datasets (FaceForensics++, DFDC) primarily target older generative models and lack coverage of the latest text-to-image synthesis and image-editing forgeries.

**No scalable solution for reasoning supervision**: Human annotation of reasoning is costly and difficult to scale; a method is needed to efficiently amplify limited human annotations into large-scale training data.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The DeepfakeJudge framework consists of three stages:

1. **Dataset Construction**: An OOD benchmark is built from real images (Open-Images V7) and forged images (T2I + editing), accompanied by human-annotated visual reasoning.
2. **Bootstrapping Reasoning Supervision**: A generator-evaluator iterative pipeline expands limited human annotations into large-scale reasoning-scoring data covering five quality levels.
3. **DeepfakeJudge Training**: Qwen-2.5-VL-3B/7B models are fine-tuned on bootstrapped data, supporting both pointwise (single-sample scoring) and pairwise (comparative selection) evaluation modes.

### Key Designs

**Dataset Construction**:

- **Real images**: 1,000 diverse samples are selected from Open-Images V7 using a seeded stochastic greedy set-cover algorithm.
- **T2I forgeries**: Photorealistic prompts are filtered from a large-scale diffusion prompt dataset, corrected by GPT-4o-mini, and then used to generate 250 images each with Gemini and SeedDream (500 total); images are manually reviewed to ensure photorealism.
- **Edited forgeries**: 800 real images receive captions and editing instructions generated by GPT-4o-mini; approximately 167 images per editing model (Gemini-Nano Banana, Flux-Kontext-Max, Qwen-Edit-2509) are produced, totaling 500 images.
- **Human annotation**: Six annotators label visual artifact flags, bounding boxes, and anomaly descriptions for 1,500 forged images; Cohen's κ = 0.71 indicates strong inter-annotator agreement.

**Bootstrapping Pipeline**:

- **Initial generation**: For each triplet (image $I$, label $g$, gold reasoning $y^*$), generator $G$ produces one reasoning sample at each scoring level $r \in \{1, \ldots, 4\}$.
- **Evaluation and feedback**: Evaluator $E$ scores each generated reasoning as $\hat{r}$ and provides feedback; samples with $|r - \hat{r}| \leq \alpha$ are accepted; otherwise, refinement iterations are initiated.
- **Refinement iteration**: Evaluator feedback is fed back into the generator to improve the reasoning, for up to $T$ rounds until score agreement is reached.
- **Paraphrase diversification**: All validated reasoning samples (including gold reasoning) are each paraphrased five times to ensure stylistic diversity while preserving semantic consistency (BERTScore 0.92, BLEU 0.39), preventing the evaluator from memorizing linguistic patterns.

**Training Setup**:

- Pointwise mode: The model receives (image, label, reasoning) and outputs a score from 1–5 with justification.
- Pairwise mode: The model receives (image, label, reasoning A, reasoning B) and outputs the preferred option; option order is randomized to mitigate position bias.
- Backbone models: Qwen-2.5-VL-3B and 7B, each trained for 2 epochs.

### Loss & Training

Standard negative log-likelihood (NLL) is adopted as the objective, modeling each token autoregressively over the target sequence:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{|t_i|}\log P_\theta(t_{i,j} \mid t_{i,<j}, I_i, g_i, y_i)$$

where $t_{i,j}$ denotes the $j$-th token of the target sequence and $M$ is the number of samples.

### Ablation Study

1. **Failure of conventional metrics**: All models yield BLEU-3 < 0.1 and ROUGE-2 < 0.06, with near-zero correlation with human judgments, confirming that conventional text metrics cannot assess reasoning quality.
2. **Effectiveness of bootstrapping**: BERTScore/BLEU analysis verifies that bootstrapped data produces linearly decreasing quality levels across scoring grades (Figure 5).
3. **Smaller models outperform larger ones**: DeepfakeJudge-3B/7B substantially surpass models up to 30× larger (e.g., Qwen-3-VL-235B) on both pointwise and pairwise evaluations.
4. **Positive correlation between reasoning and detection**: The model with the highest detection accuracy (Qwen-3-VL-235B) also receives the highest DeepfakeJudge reasoning score (3.59), indicating that visually grounded reasoning is closely associated with detection performance.
5. **User study**: Among 10 participants, 70% prefer reasoning generated by DeepfakeJudge (vs. Qwen-235B 20%, SIDA 5%, GPT-4o-mini 5%); binomial test $p \approx 0.015$ is statistically significant.

## Highlights & Insights

- **First work to systematically introduce reasoning fidelity as a quantifiable dimension in deepfake detection**, moving beyond pure classification accuracy.
- **Efficient bootstrapping pipeline**: Large-scale multi-level supervision data can be generated from limited human annotations, with paraphrasing ensuring stylistic diversity.
- **Small models, large impact**: The 3B/7B models outperform models 30× larger on evaluation tasks, demonstrating the substantial potential of domain-specific fine-tuning.
- **Comprehensive dataset design**: The benchmark jointly covers T2I and editing forgeries, OOD and in-distribution scenarios, and both detection and reasoning evaluation, forming a complete assessment framework.
- **Fully open-sourced**: Dataset, models, and code are all publicly released, facilitating community reproduction and extension.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Reasoning supervision relies on GPT-4o-mini to generate gold-standard reasoning, introducing biases and stylistic tendencies from a closed-source model that may limit generalizability.
- Although the OOD dataset covers multiple generative models, it comprises only approximately 1,000 + 924 images, a scale that may be insufficient to represent the diversity of real-world scenarios.
- Bootstrapping requires high-quality human annotations as seeds, which remain non-trivial in cost (six annotators labeling 1,500 images).
- Only Qwen-2.5-VL-based backbone models are evaluated; whether alternative architectures (e.g., InternVL, LLaVA) yield superior results remains unexplored.
- The framework addresses only static image scenarios and does not handle video deepfakes.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Deepfake detection**: The field has evolved from early frequency-domain/blinking-pattern methods to CNN/RNN-based learning and then Transformer/diffusion-based detectors; this paper adds a reasoning evaluation dimension on top of detection.
- **Explainability methods**: SIDA, GenBuster++, and FakeShield attempt to generate textual explanations but suffer from hallucination; this paper proposes VLM-as-Judge to directly assess reasoning quality from images.
- **LLM-as-Judge**: The paradigm of using LLMs as automatic evaluators, borrowed from NLP, is extended to the multimodal domain, incorporating visual inputs to evaluate reasoning groundedness.
- **Evaluation benchmarks**: Unlike classification-focused benchmarks such as FaceForensics++ and DFDC, this paper simultaneously evaluates both detection and reasoning dimensions.
