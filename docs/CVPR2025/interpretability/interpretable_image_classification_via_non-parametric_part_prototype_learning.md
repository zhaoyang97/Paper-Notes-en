# Interpretable Image Classification via Non-parametric Part Prototype Learning

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.10247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10247)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/zijizhu/proto-non-param](https://github.com/zijizhu/proto-non-param)  
**Area**: Interpretability  
**Keywords**: Interpretable Classification, Prototype Learning, Non-parametric Methods, Self-supervised ViT, Part Discovery

## TL;DR

This paper proposes an interpretable image classification framework based on non-parametric prototype learning. It discovers semantically distinct object part prototypes by performing optimal transport clustering on self-supervised ViT features, addressing prototype redundancy issues in existing ProtoPNet methods, while introducing two new metrics, Distinctiveness and Comprehensiveness, to quantify explanation quality.

## Background & Motivation

Prototypical Part Networks (ProtoPNet) simulate human visual reasoning processes by learning prototypes that represent object parts, achieving interpretable image classification. However, existing methods train prototypes as **learnable parameters**, which often leads to multiple prototypes of the same class focusing on **the same object regions**—for example, 3 out of the 4 most active prototypes might all focus on a bird's wing, ignoring the legs and tail. This results in explanations that are neither comprehensive nor diverse. 

**Key Challenge**: The learning process of parametric prototypes makes it difficult to force different prototypes to focus on different semantic parts.  
**Key Insight**: Leveraging the idea of non-parametric prototypes from semantic segmentation, prototypes are discovered through **clustering**. Since each prototype acts as the empirical mean of its corresponding cluster, it naturally guarantees that different prototypes represent distinct semantic concepts. Combining this with the strong part representation capabilities of self-supervised ViT (DINO) allows for the discovery of diverse object parts without part-level annotations.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The framework consists of two training phases: **Phase 1**—discovers non-parametric part prototypes for each class via optimal transport clustering on the frozen self-supervised ViT backbone; **Phase 2**—fixes the prototypes and fine-tunes the backbone network using Block Expansion with cross-entropy loss and patch-prototype contrastive loss, adapting to the target domain and improving classification accuracy. During inference, interpretable classification decisions are generated by calculating the similarity between input image patches and the prototypes.

### Key Designs

1. **Non-parametric Prototype Discovery (Optimal Transport Clustering)**:
    - **Function**: Learns $K$ semantically distinct part prototypes for each class.
    - **Mechanism**: Treats all foreground patch tokens of the same class in a batch as a "bag of features" and calculates their similarity matrix $\mathbf{S}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ with the current prototypes. Then, an assignment matrix $\mathbf{A}^c$ is solved via optimal transport (OT) to maximize the total patch-prototype similarity: $\max_{\mathbf{A}^c} \text{Tr}((\mathbf{A}^c)^\top \mathbf{S}^c)$. The key constraints are **unique assignment** (each patch is assigned to only one prototype) and **equipartition** (each prototype receives roughly the same number of patches), solved efficiently using the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm. Prototypes are momentum-updated as their cluster means: $\mathbf{P}_k^c \leftarrow \mathbf{P}_k^c + (1-\beta)\bar{\mathbf{f}}^{c,k}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: The equipartition constraint forces each prototype to represent different semantic regions, fundamentally avoiding the issue of multiple prototypes focusing on the same part. Compared to the regularization "guidance" in parametric methods, the "constraints" of non-parametric methods are more direct and effective.

2. **Block Expansion Fine-tuning Strategy**:
    - **Function**: Adapts to the target domain without destroying the structural integrity of the DINO feature space.
    - **Mechanism**: Inserts an **identity-initialized block** (setting the weights of the attention projection layer and the second MLP layer to zero) after every $m$ transformer blocks of the ViT backbone, keeping the initial output unchanged. During training, the original weights and prototypes are frozen, and only the newly added blocks and the modulation vector $w$ are trained.
    - **Design Motivation**: Directly fine-tuning the entire ViT degrades the high-quality semantic feature space learned by DINO, whereas Block Expansion offers an efficient adaptation mechanism.

3. **Patch-Prototype Contrastive Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPC}}$)**:
    - **Function**: Anchors features to the learned prototypes during the fine-tuning phase.
    - **Mechanism**: Intra-class contrastive loss—pulls each patch toward its assigned prototype and pushes it away from other prototypes of the same class: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPC}} = \sum_c \frac{\exp(\mathbf{f}_i^{c\top} \mathbf{P}_{k_i}^c)}{\exp(\mathbf{f}_i^{c\top} \mathbf{P}_{k_i}^c) + \sum_j \exp(\mathbf{f}_i^{c\top} \mathbf{P}_{k_j}^c)}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Although pure cross-entropy fine-tuning improves classification accuracy, it degrades consistency and stability. The PPC loss guides the feature space to maintain correspondence with prototypes while adapting to the target domain.

### Loss & Training

- Phase 1: Non-parametric update, no traditional loss, using momentum update with $\beta=0.99$ and Sinkhorn smoothing parameter $\kappa=0.05$.
- Phase 2: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{ce}} + \lambda_{\text{PPC}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{PPC}}$, with $\lambda_{\text{PPC}}=0.8$.
- Phase 1 is trained for 1 epoch, and Phase 2 is trained for 5 epochs using the Adam optimizer.
- Learning rate is 0.0001 for the backbone and $1 \times 10^{-6}$ for the modulation vectors.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (CUB-200-2011, DINOv2 ViT-B/14, K=5)

| Method | Consistency (%) | Stability (%) | **Distinctiveness (%)** | Classification Accuracy (%) |
|------|----------|----------|-------------|------------|
| ProtoPNet | 1.30 | 63.84 | 11.99 | 88.16 |
| Deformable ProtoPNet | 2.83 | 80.43 | 10.34 | 88.14 |
| ProtoPool | 29.27 | 57.49 | 89.62 | 89.90 |
| TesNet | 58.60 | 74.60 | 81.93 | 90.35 |
| EvalProtoPNet | 67.80 | 74.40 | 72.12 | 90.06 |
| **Ours** | **66.40** | **82.97** | **94.45** | **90.82** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Consistency | Stability | Distinctiveness | Classification Accuracy |
|------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Unconstrained clustering | 4.09 | 75.50 | 49.47 | 9.25 |
| Without foreground extraction | 28.40 | 82.33 | 92.69 | 64.29 |
| Without feature fine-tuning (Phase 1 only) | 63.74 | 83.37 | 96.07 | 77.96 |
| Without PPC loss (CE only) | 55.61 | 77.67 | 80.41 | 90.78 |
| **Full Model** | **66.40** | **82.97** | **96.74** | **91.02** |

### Key Findings

- **Distinctiveness score leads by a large margin**: At K=5, the proposed method (94.45%) outperforms the best baseline TesNet (81.93%) by 12.52%, indicating that the prototypes indeed focus on distinct object parts.
- **Phase 1 already holds decent classification capabilities**: Achieving 77.96% accuracy using only the non-parametric prototypes on the frozen DINO features verifies the semantic quality of the self-supervised ViT feature space.
- **Lack of constraints leads to collapse**: Removing the equipartition and unique assignment constraints drops classification accuracy to 9.25% (near random), showing that OT constraints are core to the method.
- **PPC loss is critical for fine-tuning**: Without PPC, distinctiveness decreases from 96.74% to 80.41%, indicating that pure CE fine-tuning disrupts the correspondence between features and prototypes.
- **Concept learning adaptation**: Adapting the framework to the Concept Bottleneck Model yields a concept trustworthiness of 47.04% and a classification accuracy of 88.18% on CUB.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Fundamental advantage of non-parametric vs. parametric approaches**: Parametric prototypes rely on regularization to "guide" dispersion without hard constraints; non-parametric methods **force** each prototype to correspond to distinct semantic regions via equipartition constraints.
- **Out-of-the-box capability of DINO features**: Self-supervised ViT patch tokens already encode rich part semantics, making them a natural choice for prototype learning.
- **Two new metrics filling the evaluation gap**: Distinctiveness Score quantifies prototype overlap, and Comprehensiveness Score quantifies foreground coverage, enabling quantifiable comparison of interpretability.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Relies on the quality of the foreground extraction module—may not be applicable to scenes with complex backgrounds.
- The equipartition constraint assumes that the number of patch tokens per part is roughly equal, which may not be optimal for uneven part distributions.
- The number of prototypes per class, K, needs to be preset; adaptively determining K is an open problem.
- Validated only on fine-grained classification datasets; generic image classification scenarios remain untested.

## Related Work & Insights

- Inspired by non-parametric prototype methods such as ProtoSeg and Deep Nearest Centroids, transferring segmentation concepts to interpretable classification.
- The Block Expansion fine-tuning strategy comes from the field of parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
- The Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm is also used for uniform assignment in SwAV self-supervised learning, here applied to part prototype assignment.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The concept of non-parametric prototypes combined with OT clustering is novel within the ProtoPNet framework, although individual components (DINO, Sinkhorn, Block Expansion) have precedents.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Comprehensive ablation, introducing new quantitative metrics, but datasets are limited to fine-grained classification (3 datasets).
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear methodological descriptions, but the transition between Phase 1 and Phase 2 could be more compact.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A significant breakthrough in interpretability (distinctiveness +12%), making an important contribution to the explainable AI community.
