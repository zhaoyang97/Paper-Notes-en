# Think Outside the Policy: In-Context Steered Policy Optimization

**Conference**: ACL 2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2510.26519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26519)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Celine-hxy/ICPO)  
**Area**: LLM Reasoning / Reinforcement Learning  
**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, In-Context Steered, Policy Optimization, Exploration Enhancement, Mathematical Reasoning

## TL;DR

This paper proposes ICPO (In-Context Steered Policy Optimization), which utilizes the In-Context Learning (ICL) capability of Large Language Models as implicit expert guidance to expand the policy exploration space during RLVR training, eliminating the reliance on reasoning trajectories from external stronger models.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), particularly the GRPO algorithm, has become the dominant paradigm for enhancing the mathematical reasoning capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). However, GRPO relies on on-policy sampling, where all trajectories are drawn from the current policy distribution, leading to limited exploration diversity.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) On-policy exploration is confined to the current policy distribution, resulting in insufficient trajectory diversity and a tendency to fall into local optima; (2) Existing methods for expanding exploration space (such as LUFFY) rely on reasoning trajectories generated by stronger LRMs as off-policy samples, but these advanced models are computationally expensive and not always accessible; (3) Directly introducing external trajectories may introduce noise, affecting training stability.

**Key Challenge**: RLVR requires sufficient exploration diversity to discover superior policies, but on-policy sampling naturally restricts the exploration range; while introducing external expert trajectories is effective, it introduces dependency on external resources.

**Goal**: Design an RLVR framework that does not rely on external stronger models to expand the exploration space and improve training effectiveness by leveraging the model's own capabilities.

**Key Insight**: ICL is essentially an implicit expert-conditioned reasoning process—by providing examples in the input, the model shifts its reasoning distribution closer to the expert region without parameter updates. Incorporating these ICL-steered trajectories into GRPO training enables "Implicit Expert Forcing."

**Core Idea**: Use examples from existing datasets (such as the MATH training set) as ICL demonstrations to guide the model in generating off-policy trajectories, requiring no external stronger model. Simultaneously, use rejection sampling and annealed reward shaping to ensure training stability.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

ICPO introduces three components to the standard GRPO: (1) Mixed-Policy GRPO + Implicit Expert Forcing (IEF): Utilizes ICL to generate off-policy trajectories for expanding the exploration space; (2) Expert Region Reject Sampling (ERRS): Filters low-quality off-policy trajectories; (3) Annealed Expert Bonus Reward Shaping (RS): Balances early expert guidance with later autonomous optimization. Each prompt generates 8 trajectories (7 on-policy + 1 off-policy ICL-steered).

### Key Designs

1.  **Mixed-Policy GRPO with IEF**:
    - **Function**: Mixes on-policy and ICL-steered off-policy trajectories within GRPO rollout groups to expand the exploration space.
    - **Mechanism**: For each prompt $q$, examples $\mathcal{D}$ randomly sampled from the MATH dataset are concatenated as $x_{\mathrm{exp}}=[\mathcal{D};q]$. An ICL-steered trajectory $\tau_{\mathrm{exp}} \sim \pi_\theta(\tau|x_{\mathrm{exp}})$ is generated. From the perspective of ICL hypothesis classes, the Transformer maps demonstrations to a task vector $\vartheta = A(\mathcal{D})$, which is equivalent to implicitly introducing an expert prior. Group-relative advantages are then recalculated over the mixed rollout groups.
    - **Design Motivation**: Although all trajectories originate from the same model $\pi_\theta$, ICL conditioning changes the input distribution, biasing trajectories toward expert-aligned regions—an input-conditioned off-policy approach that requires no additional models.

2.  **Expert Region Reject Sampling (ERRS)**:
    - **Function**: Filters low-quality ICL-steered trajectories to prevent noise from polluting policy updates.
    - **Mechanism**: Defines the expert region as $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}} = \{(x_{\mathrm{exp}}, \tau_j) | R(\tau_j) \geq \delta\}$, where ICL-steered trajectories are included in training only if their reward exceeds the threshold $\delta=1.0$ (i.e., the answer is correct). A reject sampling operator $\rho$ ensures only high-reward trajectories participate in the update.
    - **Design Motivation**: ICL guidance does not always produce correct answers. Using all off-policy trajectories directly would introduce misleading gradients; ERRS ensures the reliability of training signals.

3.  **Annealed Expert Bonus Reward Shaping**:
    - **Function**: Strengthens expert guidance during early training and gradually relaxes it to promote autonomous optimization.
    - **Mechanism**: Adds a linearly decaying reward bonus to correct trajectories within the expert region: $R_{\mathrm{shaped}}(\tau) = R(\tau) + \alpha \cdot \gamma(t)$, where $\gamma(t) = 1 - t/T$ is a linear decay scheduler. This causes early training to mimic expert behavior more closely while transitioning to autonomous exploration later.
    - **Design Motivation**: Fixed expert reward bonuses can lead to over-reliance on expert behavior; the annealing design achieves a smooth transition from "following the expert" to "autonomous reasoning."

### Loss & Training

The final objective $\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{ICPO}}(\theta)$ consists of on-policy and off-policy components, with the off-policy part subjected to rejection sampling and importance ratio adjustments. Regularized importance sampling $f(x) = x/(x+\lambda)$ ($\lambda=0.01$) is used for policy shaping of off-policy trajectories. A KL regularization term is maintained to prevent excessive policy shift.

### Key Findings
- ICL-steered trajectories not only improve accuracy but also enhance trajectory diversity (larger edit distances) and distribution quality (higher "flip" ratios—from incorrect to correct).
- ICPO maintains higher policy entropy throughout training, reflecting broader policy support and more thorough exploration.
- ICPO is robust to the choice of expert data—consistent improvements are achieved even when using cross-domain data in Program-of-Thought (PoT) format.
- The ICPO† variant with reward shaping performs better on OOD benchmarks, suggesting that the annealing strategy aids generalization.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Theoretical Perspective of ICL as Implicit Expert Forcing**: Connecting the hypothesis class decomposition of ICL with expert forcing provides an elegant theoretical explanation.
- **Zero Additional Model Dependency**: Compared to methods like LUFFY, ICPO requires no external stronger models, only using existing datasets as ICL demonstrations.
- **Plug-and-Play Framework**: The target policy distribution can be flexibly adjusted by changing expert data, offering excellent scalability.
- **Visualization of Training Dynamics**: Reward and entropy curves clearly demonstrate ICPO's advantages over standard GRPO.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Experiments primarily focus on mathematical reasoning; cross-domain generalization (e.g., code generation, common sense reasoning) has not been fully verified.
- The quality of ICL guidance depends on the quality of demonstrations, which may have limited effectiveness for extremely difficult problems.
- Only one off-policy trajectory is used per prompt (7+1 configuration); more flexible ratio strategies are worth exploring.
- Future work could integrate ICPO with other exploration enhancement techniques (e.g., temperature scheduling, replay buffers).

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs LUFFY**: LUFFY requires reasoning trajectories from stronger LRMs as off-policy samples. ICPO replaces external models with ICL, outperforming LUFFY by +3.3 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B.
- **vs GRPO + Extra Rollouts**: Simply increasing the number of rollouts yields limited gains; ICPO's ICL guidance provides more effective exploration signals.
- **vs ReLIFT**: ReLIFT alternates between RL and SFT, introducing training instability; ICPO unifies SFT signals and RL optimization within a single framework.
