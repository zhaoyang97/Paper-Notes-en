# C-LoRA: Contextual Low-Rank Adaptation for Uncertainty Estimation in Large Language Models

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2505.17773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17773)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/ahra99/c_lora)  
**Area**: Model Compression
**Keywords**: LoRA, uncertainty estimation, Bayesian fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, LLM, variational inference, data-dependent

## TL;DR

This paper proposes C-LoRA, which introduces a lightweight contextual module to condition the distribution of LoRA low-rank matrices on the input data, enabling sample-level heteroscedastic uncertainty estimation and significantly improving calibration quality in few-shot fine-tuning scenarios.

## Background & Motivation

- **Background**: Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA have become the dominant paradigm for adapting LLMs to downstream tasks. Recent works including BLoB and Laplace-LoRA attempt to incorporate Bayesian methods into LoRA for uncertainty quantification (UQ).
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing Bayesian LoRA methods (e.g., BLoB) impose fixed variational distributions over adapter parameters that do not vary with the input sample, thereby neglecting aleatoric uncertainty arising from the data itself (epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty remain entangled).
- **Key Challenge**: In few-shot fine-tuning scenarios, LLMs are prone to overfitting and overconfident predictions. Existing methods model only epistemic uncertainty and cannot capture differences in predictive variability induced by distinct inputs.
- **Goal**: To efficiently model sample-level, data-dependent heteroscedastic UQ within the LoRA fine-tuning framework while maintaining low parameter overhead.
- **Key Insight**: A low-dimensional matrix $\mathbf{E}$ is inserted between the LoRA matrices $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{A}$; the distributional parameters of $\mathbf{E}$ are generated by a lightweight input-driven contextual module, confining Bayesian inference to the low-dimensional $r \times r$ space.
- **Core Idea**: Contextual LoRA — upgrading the stochasticity of LoRA weights from "sampling from a fixed distribution" to "sampling from an input-conditioned distribution," which amounts to performing amortized variational inference over LoRA.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

C-LoRA inserts a low-dimensional matrix $\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ between the standard LoRA matrices $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$, so that the forward pass becomes $\mathbf{h} = (\mathbf{W}_0 + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}$. Here $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{A}$ are learned as deterministic parameters, while the distributional parameters (mean and variance) of $\mathbf{E}$ are produced per layer by a contextual module conditioned on the input. Training maximizes the ELBO objective; at inference, either the posterior mean ($M=0$) or Monte Carlo sampling ($M=10$) can be used.

### Key Design 1: Lightweight LoRA Factorization

- **Function**: Extends the standard LoRA decomposition $\Delta\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{BA}$ to $\Delta\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{BEA}$ by inserting $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ in the middle.
- **Mechanism**: Reduces the target of Bayesian inference from the $d$-dimensional space (where matrix $\mathbf{A}$ has $r \times d$ parameters) to the $r^2$-dimensional space (where matrix $\mathbf{E}$ has only $r \times r$ parameters), fully decoupling complexity from model dimension $d$.
- **Design Motivation**: BLoB performs variational inference over the entire $\mathbf{A}$ matrix, so parameter count grows linearly with $d$. By inserting $\mathbf{E}$, the Bayesian component has a constant $r^2$ parameters, making data-dependent Bayesian inference computationally tractable.

### Key Design 2: Contextual Module

- **Function**: Each layer is equipped with a two-layer fully connected network $h_\varphi^l$. Its input is $\mathbf{z}^l = \mathbf{A}^l \mathbf{x}^{l-1} \in \mathbb{R}^r$ (reusing features from the main model), and its output is the distributional parameters $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathbf{E}}^l, \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{E}}^l)$ of $\mathbf{E}$.
- **Mechanism**: Through amortized inference, the variational posterior $q_\phi(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}}|\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{x}), \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{E}}^2(\mathbf{x}))$ varies with the input, achieving sample-level heteroscedastic UQ.
- **Design Motivation**: In conventional Bayesian methods, parameter distributions are identical for all samples and cannot reflect uncertainty differences across inputs. The contextual module enables the model to produce high-confidence predictions for "easy" inputs and low-confidence predictions for "hard" inputs.

### Key Design 3: Cross-Layer Autoregressive Factorization and Feature Reuse

- **Function**: The $\mathbf{E}$ distributions across layers are factorized autoregressively as $q_\phi(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}}|\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{l=1}^{L} q_\phi(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}}^l | \mathbf{x}^{l-1})$, and the contextual module takes the low-dimensional vector $\mathbf{z}^l = \mathbf{A}^l \mathbf{x}^{l-1}$ as input rather than raw high-dimensional features.
- **Mechanism**: The $\mathbf{A}$ matrix of LoRA is reused as a natural dimensionality-reduction mapping, compressing the $d$-dimensional intermediate representation to $r$ dimensions before feeding it into the contextual network.
- **Design Motivation**: Learning contextual modules directly in the $d$-dimensional feature space would be prohibitively expensive and prone to overfitting. By reusing the projection of $\mathbf{A}$, the additional computational cost of the contextual module is only $\mathcal{O}(r^4)$, far smaller than the $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ cost per layer in the main model.

## Loss & Training

- **Objective**: Maximize the ELBO = expected log-likelihood − per-sample KL divergence:
$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi} \log p_\theta(y_i|\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}_i}) - \text{KL}(q_\phi(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}_i}|\mathbf{x}_i) \| p(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}_i}))]$$
- **Decoupled Parameter Updates**: Parameters $\mathbf{B}$/$\mathbf{A}$ are updated using only NLL gradients (excluding the KL term), ensuring clean supervision signals and reducing gradient noise; contextual module parameters $\phi$ are updated with the full ELBO.
- **Reparameterization Trick**: $\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}}^l = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathbf{E}}^l + \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{E}}^l \odot \mathcal{E}^l$, where $\mathcal{E}^l \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$.
- **Training Efficiency**: C-LoRA requires only 1,500 iterations (baseline methods require 5,000), with batch size 4, applied to the query/value/output projection layers of LLaMA2-7B.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: In-Distribution Results on 6 Commonsense Reasoning Datasets (Table 1)

| Method | WG-S ACC↑ | ARC-C ACC↑ | WG-S ECE↓ | ARC-C ECE↓ | ARC-E ECE↓ | WG-M ECE↓ |
|--------|-----------|------------|-----------|------------|------------|-----------|
| MAP | 69.37 | 67.67 | 29.76 | 30.60 | 13.49 | 23.01 |
| Deep Ensemble | 68.98 | 68.57 | 28.72 | 27.75 | 11.87 | 18.67 |
| BLoB (M=10) | 66.55 | 66.66 | 11.23 | 10.77 | 4.29 | 4.52 |
| **C-LoRA (M=10)** | 66.21 | 67.79 | **6.86** | **8.83** | **4.27** | **3.71** |
| **C-LoRA (M=0)** | 67.16 | 69.02 | 7.16 | 12.28 | 5.75 | 6.07 |

**Key Findings**: C-LoRA incurs only a 1–2% drop in accuracy while reducing ECE from BLoB's 10.77% to 8.83% (ARC-C) and from 11.23% to 6.86% (WG-S). Even with $M=0$ (no sampling), C-LoRA outperforms BLoB at $M=10$.

### Ablation Study: Effect of the Contextual Module (Table 4)

| Method | WG-S ECE↓ | ARC-C ECE↓ | ARC-E ECE↓ | OBQA ECE↓ | BoolQ ECE↓ |
|--------|-----------|------------|------------|-----------|------------|
| FE (M=10) w/o context | 13.06 | 11.09 | 5.75 | 5.58 | **1.28** |
| **C-LoRA (M=10)** | **6.86** | **8.83** | **4.27** | **4.00** | 1.62 |

**Key Findings**: Removing the contextual module (FE) degrades ECE from 6.86% to 13.06% on WG-S and from 8.83% to 11.09% on ARC-C, confirming that the contextual module is the primary source of calibration improvement.

### OOD Robustness (Table 3, trained on OBQA → tested on ARC/Chem/Phy)

| Method | ARC-C ECE↓ | Chem ECE↓ | Phy ECE↓ | Chem NLL↓ | Phy NLL↓ |
|--------|------------|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|
| MAP | 25.54 | 29.73 | 36.22 | 1.81 | 1.86 |
| BLoB (M=10) | 9.77 | 12.63 | 17.56 | 1.35 | 1.46 |
| **C-LoRA (M=10)** | **8.83** | **12.49** | 18.16 | **1.31** | **1.42** |

**Key Findings**: On the Chem/Phy datasets with large distribution shift, C-LoRA achieves NLL of 1.31/1.42, outperforming all baselines (BLoB: 1.35/1.46).

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Clear and Compelling Core Innovation**: Confining Bayesian inference within the $r \times r$ low-dimensional space of LoRA and introducing input dependence is an elegant and practical design — it essentially implements amortized variational inference within the PEFT framework.
2. **High Training Efficiency**: Only 30% of the iterations required by baseline methods (1,500 vs. 5,000), benefiting from the stronger prior information provided by the contextual module.
3. **M=0 Already Competitive**: Using the posterior mean alone, calibration already surpasses BLoB at $M=10$, indicating that the contextual module learns means with inherent adaptive uncertainty awareness, eliminating the need for sampling overhead during deployment.
4. **Strong OOD Performance**: ECE and NLL advantages are maintained under distribution shift, suggesting that data-dependent UQ generalizes more robustly.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Validation Limited to 7B Models**: All experiments are conducted on LLaMA2-7B; scalability to 13B/70B or larger models has not been verified.
2. **Accuracy Trade-off**: C-LoRA achieves 1–3% lower accuracy than MAP and Deep Ensemble on nearly all tasks, which may be undesirable in accuracy-critical settings.
3. **Narrow Task Coverage**: Validation is restricted to multiple-choice and binary classification tasks; UQ on generation tasks (e.g., open-domain QA, summarization, translation) has not been explored.
4. **Contextual Module Hyperparameters**: The hidden size $C=64$ is fixed without a systematic hyperparameter search or sensitivity analysis.
5. **Insufficient Computational Analysis**: Although low overhead is claimed, concrete comparisons of FLOPs, memory, and training time are absent.

## Related Work & Insights

| Method | Key Similarities and Differences | Advantage of C-LoRA |
|--------|----------------------------------|---------------------|
| **BLoB** (Wang et al., 2024) | Applies mean-field VI to matrix $\mathbf{A}$; distribution is input-independent | C-LoRA performs data-dependent VI in $r^2$ space; ECE reduced by 30–40% |
| **Laplace-LoRA** (Yang et al., 2024) | Laplace posterior approximation; post-hoc method | C-LoRA is end-to-end trained with lower NLL; Laplace-LoRA ECE is extremely poor on some tasks (ARC-E: 45.85%) |
| **Deep Ensemble** | Trains multiple independent LoRA models and averages predictions | C-LoRA achieves competitive results with a single model and fewer parameters; DE remains far behind Bayesian methods on ECE |

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Inserting a low-dimensional matrix between LoRA factors and making it input-dependent for Bayesian inference is a novel and natural design; applying amortized inference within PEFT is a pioneering contribution.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐ — Coverage across 6 datasets, OOD evaluation, and ablation studies is relatively comprehensive, but is limited to a 7B model and classification tasks, lacking analysis of generative tasks and computational efficiency.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Theoretical derivations are clear; Figure 1 provides an intuitive comparison between BLoB and C-LoRA; tables are informative.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Provides a practical and efficient solution for uncertainty-aware fine-tuning of LLMs; the competitiveness of $M=0$ makes it deployment-friendly.
