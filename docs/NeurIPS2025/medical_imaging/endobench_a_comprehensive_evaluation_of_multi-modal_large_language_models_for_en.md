# EndoBench: A Comprehensive Evaluation of Multi-Modal Large Language Models for Endoscopy Analysis

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2505.23601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23601)  
**Code**: Public (benchmark + code)  
**Area**: Medical Imaging
**Keywords**: Endoscopy, MLLM Evaluation, Benchmark, VQA, Clinical Workflow

## TL;DR

This paper introduces EndoBench, the first comprehensive MLLM evaluation benchmark covering 4 endoscopic scenarios, 12 clinical tasks, and 5 levels of visual prompt granularity, comprising 6,832 clinically validated VQA pairs. Evaluation of 23 MLLMs reveals that commercial models generally outperform open-source and medical-specific counterparts, yet all remain below human expert performance.

## Background & Motivation

**Clinical Demand for Endoscopy AI**: Gastrointestinal and urological diseases represent major global health challenges, and endoscopy is a critical diagnostic and therapeutic tool. The growing demand for endoscopic procedures necessitates AI assistance to improve accuracy and efficiency.

**Limitations of Existing Benchmarks**: (a) Narrow scenario coverage — Surgical-VQA evaluates only surgical scenes, and ColonINST covers only colonoscopy; (b) Limited task dimensions — Kvasir-VQA focuses solely on basic recognition tasks; (c) Absence of comprehensive evaluation across multiple capability dimensions.

**Real-World Clinical Requirements**: In practice, clinicians operate across multiple modalities (gastroscopy / colonoscopy / capsule endoscopy / surgical endoscopy) and require multi-level competencies ranging from anatomical recognition to surgical intervention. Existing benchmarks are insufficient to measure whether MLLMs possess such comprehensive capabilities.

**Key Challenge**: Despite the rapid development of MLLMs in endoscopy, the evaluation ecosystem remains fragmented and incomplete, failing to accurately reflect clinical utility.

## Method

### Benchmark Construction Pipeline

EndoBench is constructed in three steps:

**Step 1: Data Collection**
- Aggregates 20 public + 1 private endoscopic datasets
- Covers both diagnostic imaging (gastroscopy / colonoscopy / capsule endoscopy) and interventional imaging (surgical endoscopy)
- All data undergo privacy de-identification

**Step 2: QA Standardization**
- In collaboration with clinical physicians, 12 specialized tasks across 4 major categories are designed
- Five to eight distinct question templates are created per task
- Spatial understanding tasks adopt a unified coordinate format of $[x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2]$
- Plausible distractors are generated by creating alternative bounding boxes with similar dimensions but overlap below a defined threshold
- This process yields 446,535 image-text pairs, constituting the EndoVQA-Instruct dataset (currently the largest endoscopy instruction-tuning set)

**Step 3: Data Filtering**
- Entity distribution balancing → DINO-V2 visual embedding extraction → K-center clustering for representative sample selection
- GPT-4o-mini is used to paraphrase questions to assess linguistic adaptability
- Two certified clinicians conduct clinical review to remove low-quality samples
- The final benchmark contains 6,832 clinically validated VQA pairs

### Multi-Dimensional Capability Evaluation Framework

Twelve tasks across 4 major categories are designed to mirror clinical workflows:

1. **Anatomical Structure Recognition**: Organ identification, anatomical landmark recognition
2. **Lesion Analysis and Grading**: Lesion quantification, lesion type identification, lesion severity grading
3. **Spatial Localization and Region Understanding**: Visual grounding, region selection, region recognition
4. **Surgical Workflow and Procedural Analysis**: Preoperative assessment, macro-phase recognition, micro-action analysis, instrument management

### Visual Prompt Granularity

A 5-level granularity design is adopted: image-level, bounding-box-level, contour-level, multi-box, and multi-contour — contours and boxes are derived from segmentation masks to evaluate fine-grained perception capabilities.

## Key Experimental Results

### Evaluation Configuration

- **Total Models**: 23 (13 open-source general + 5 medical-specific + 5 commercial)
- **Open-Source Models**: LLaVA, LLaVA-Next, CogVLM, ShareGPT-4v, Qwen2.5-VL (3B–72B), Janus-Pro, InternVL2.5, QvQ
- **Commercial Models**: GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Deepseek-V3 (vision), Grok-3
- **Medical Models**: MedDr, LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, ColonGPT
- **Human Baseline**: 2 certified clinicians, 5–15 samples per sub-task, with average scores serving as reference

### Main Results

**Overall Accuracy by Category**:

| Model Type | Anatomical Recognition | Lesion Analysis | Spatial Localization | Surgical Workflow | Overall |
|------------|----------------------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|---------|
| Commercial (best) | Highest | Highest | Highest | Highest | **Leading** |
| Open-source general (best) | Second | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Second |
| Medical-specific | Moderate | High | Lower | Moderate | Third |
| Human clinicians | — | — | — | — | **Highest** |

### Key Findings

1. **Commercial Models Lead Overall**: Commercial MLLMs outperform open-source and medical-specific models across all categories, yet still fall short of human expert performance.
2. **Medical Fine-Tuning Is Effective**: Domain-specific SFT substantially improves task-specific accuracy, demonstrating the value of domain adaptation.
3. **Prompt Sensitivity**: Model performance is sensitive to prompt format and task complexity, with notable variation across visual prompt granularity levels.
4. **Weak Spatial Understanding**: All models perform worst on spatial localization and region understanding tasks, representing a primary shortcoming of current MLLMs.
5. **Scaling Effect**: Larger model sizes generally yield better performance (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B substantially outperforms the 3B variant).

## Highlights & Insights

- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Comprehensiveness**: The first benchmark covering 4 endoscopic scenarios × 12 tasks × 5 granularity levels.
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Clinical Alignment**: The evaluation framework is rigorously aligned with clinical workflows, progressing from anatomical recognition to surgical intervention.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Human Baseline**: Certified clinicians are introduced as a reference standard, enabling direct comparison between MLLMs and human capability.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Large-Scale Training Set**: The accompanying 446K EndoVQA-Instruct instruction-tuning dataset can be directly used for model training.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Rigorous Quality Control**: A three-stage quality control pipeline comprising DINO-V2 clustering, GPT-4o-mini paraphrasing, and physician review.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Evaluation Metric Limitation**: Exact match is the sole evaluation metric, limiting the assessment of open-ended generation capabilities.
2. **Scenario Imbalance**: Surgical endoscopy accounts for nearly 40% of samples, resulting in an uneven scenario distribution.
3. **Static Evaluation**: Video sequence understanding is not considered, despite the fact that real endoscopic examinations involve continuous video streams.
4. **Monolingual Scope**: Only English QA is supported, limiting applicability in non-English-speaking clinical settings.
5. **Absence of Generative Tasks**: Evaluation is primarily conducted in multiple-choice format, without covering clinically relevant generative tasks such as report generation and clinical dialogue.

## Rating ⭐⭐⭐⭐

EndoBench represents a significant contribution to endoscopic MLLM evaluation, with comprehensiveness and clinical alignment as its primary strengths. The benchmark design is systematic and rigorous, and the large-scale evaluation of 23 models provides a valuable community reference. However, the evaluation methodology (multiple-choice questions with exact match) and the absence of video understanding assessment are notable limitations. EndoBench is well-suited to serve as a standard evaluation platform for endoscopic MLLM research.
