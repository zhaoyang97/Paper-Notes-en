# FIPER: Factorized Features for Robust Image Super-Resolution and Compression

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2410.18083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18083)
**Code**: [Project Page](https://jayisaking.github.io/FIPER/)
**Area**: Image Restoration / Super-Resolution and Compression
**Keywords**: super-resolution, image compression, factorized features, basis-coefficient decomposition, coordinate transformation, multi-frequency modulation, multi-frame compression

## TL;DR

This paper proposes a Factorized Features representation that decomposes images into learnable non-uniform bases and spatially variant coefficients, augmented with sawtooth coordinate transformation and multi-frequency modulation. The approach achieves a 204.4% relative PSNR gain at 4× super-resolution (HAT-L-F vs. SwinIR) and a 21.09% BD-rate reduction over VTM in image compression.

## Background & Motivation

**Evolution of super-resolution**: The field has progressed from CNNs (EDSR, RCAN) to GANs (SRGAN) to Transformers (SwinIR, HAT, DAT). Existing methods focus primarily on network architecture design—windowed attention, sparse attention, dense skip connections—while largely overlooking the optimization of image content representation itself.

**Limitations of frequency decomposition methods**: Existing decomposition approaches (e.g., Learnable Fourier Series, Factor Fields) model frequency components using fixed sinusoidal functions, but suffer from two core issues: (a) the uniform distribution of fixed harmonic functions limits adaptability to non-uniform visual patterns; (b) the higher energy of low-frequency components causes models to bias toward low frequencies during training, sacrificing high-frequency details and resulting in severely asynchronous convergence between high and low frequencies.

**Shared nature of super-resolution and compression**: Both tasks fundamentally require recovering fine details from degraded inputs while maintaining perceptual fidelity—super-resolution enhances resolution, while compression reconstructs from lossy encodings. This commonality motivates a unified representation.

**Redundancy in multi-frame compression**: Conventional methods encode each frame independently, ignoring frequency components shared across images, leading to low coding efficiency.

**Core Problem**: Can a unified image representation be designed that goes beyond simple feature map outputs, enabling accurate detail reconstruction for super-resolution and efficient coding for compression simultaneously?

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The core of FIPER is the Factorized Features representation (Eq. 7), which reconstructs images as factorized products of basis functions $b_i$ and coefficients $c_{ij}$, enhanced by coordinate transformation $\gamma$ and multi-frequency modulation $(\alpha_j, \psi)$:

$$\hat{I}(x) = \mathcal{P}\left(\text{Concat}_{i=1}^{N} {}_{j=1}^{K} \left\{ c_{ij}(x) \odot \psi(\alpha_j \cdot b_i(\gamma_i(x))) \right\}\right)$$

The framework consists of three core modules:
- **Coefficient Backbone** $F_{\text{coeff}}$: extracts features $X_{\text{coeff}}$ from low-resolution input to generate spatially variant coefficient maps
- **Basis Swin Transformer** $F_{\text{basis}}$: generates multi-scale learned bases $\{b_1, \ldots, b_N\}$ from $X_{\text{coeff}}$
- **Factorized Features Reconstruction**: combines bases and coefficients according to Eq. 7 and projects to RGB output

### Key Designs

**1. Spatially Variant Coefficients**

Frequency characteristics vary significantly across image regions (smooth regions do not require high-frequency components), so coefficients $c_i(x)$ are designed to be spatially variant:

$$f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_i(x) \cdot b_i(x)$$

These are generated by the Coefficient Backbone via convolution and PixelShuffle upsampling, enabling adaptive weighting across spatial positions.

**2. Learned Non-uniform Basis**

Conventional methods construct basis functions by learning frequency parameters $u, v$ and phase $\xi$, but the fixed sinusoidal form limits local flexibility, and high-frequency components converge significantly slower than low-frequency ones. FIPER directly generates complete basis maps $b_i = M_i \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$ via the Basis Swin Transformer. Experiments (Fig. 2) demonstrate that learned non-uniform bases yield more synchronized and stable convergence.

**3. Sawtooth Coordinate Transformation**

A coordinate transformation $\gamma(x) = x \bmod k$ is introduced to periodically remap coordinates before sampling the basis functions. The sawtooth transformation folds the continuous space into piecewise-linear triangular waves, explicitly enforcing the model to learn patch-level periodic repetition patterns (e.g., window grids on buildings, periodic fabric textures). Visualizations (Fig. 3b) show that compared to a plain basis-coefficient field (Fig. 3a), coordinate transformation causes features to exhibit clear block-periodic structures.

**4. Multi-frequency Modulation**

Neural networks exhibit spectral bias, tending to learn low-frequency content first. Multi-frequency modulation uses different frequency scalars $\alpha_j \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$ and periodic functions $\psi \in \{\sin, \cos\}$ to force each basis function to contribute to both high- and low-frequency components simultaneously.

Larger $\alpha_j$ values cause $\psi$ to oscillate more rapidly, compelling basis functions to maintain accuracy in the high-frequency domain—if a basis is only accurate at low frequencies, its high-frequency output will contain unwanted noise, forming an implicit regularization.

**5. Coefficient-First Pipeline Order**

Unlike conventional Fourier analysis which proceeds basis-first, FIPER first extracts features via the Coefficient Backbone and then generates bases from those features. The ablation variant HAT-F-Basis-First demonstrates that reversing this order causes significant performance degradation, since the Coefficient Backbone effectively serves as a feature extraction module whose refined features benefit downstream basis generation.

**6. Mergeable Bases for Multi-frame Compression**

Different images frequently share similar frequency components. FIPER employs a Transformer $F_{\text{merge}}$ to fuse the per-frame bases $b_i^n$ from $M$ frames into a single shared basis:

$$b_i(h,w) = F_{\text{merge}}(\{b_i^n(h,w) \mid n \in 1, \ldots, M\})$$

The shared basis is transmitted once, while each frame encodes only its own coefficients, reducing coding redundancy by exploiting inter-frame mutual information. As $M$ increases, the shared basis becomes increasingly denoised and generalized.

### Loss & Training

- **Super-resolution**: $L_1$ loss is used for optimization. ImageNet pretraining for 300k iterations (lr=2e-4, batch=32), followed by DF2K fine-tuning for 200k iterations (lr=1e-5).
- **Image compression**: Rate-distortion loss $L = R(\hat{y}) + R(\hat{z}) + \lambda \cdot D(x, \hat{x})$, where $R$ denotes bitrate cost, $D$ denotes distortion, and $\lambda$ controls the trade-off. The SR prior is trained first with the compression module frozen, followed by end-to-end joint fine-tuning.
- Number of basis-coefficient pairs $N=6$; frequency scalars $\alpha_j \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$.

### Key Findings

- **204.4% relative PSNR gain**: Using SwinIR as baseline and HAT-L as reference, HAT-L-F improves Urban100 PSNR from 27.56 → 29.51, far surpassing the reference model's 28.60.
- **Every component is critical**: Removing the coordinate transformation (Fig. 3a vs. 3b), multi-frequency modulation (Fig. 3c vs. 3d), or the coefficient-first ordering (HAT-F vs. HAT-F-Basis-First) each causes notable performance degradation.
- **Scalability of multi-frame shared bases**: As the number of merged frames $M$ increases from 1 to 24, BD-rate improves from +27.96% to -20.97%, with the shared basis becoming increasingly generalized.
- **Parameter efficiency**: DAT-F (40M) outperforms DAT-L (43M); HAT-F (46M) outperforms HAT-L (41M), demonstrating that Factorized Features is more efficient than simply scaling up the model.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Representation over architecture**: The core contribution of FIPER lies not in novel network modules but in redefining the image output representation—from simple feature maps to factorized basis-coefficient decompositions. This provides an improvement pathway orthogonal to network design.
- **Elegance of sawtooth coordinate transformation**: $\gamma(x) = x \bmod k$ imposes patch-level periodicity constraints with only $O(1)$ computation, elegantly guiding the model to learn repetitive structural patterns.
- **Transfer of SR priors to compression**: A pretrained SR model is directly reused as the synthesis transform in the compression decoder, fully exploiting the shared nature of the two tasks.
- **Scalability of multi-frame compression**: Increasing the number of shared bases yields consistent BD-rate improvements, while decoding time actually decreases as $M$ grows (due to the denoising effect of the shared basis).

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Computational overhead**: The Basis Swin Transformer introduces additional parameters and inference time (TCM-HAT-L-F decoding: 0.264s vs. 0.15s for TCM), limiting applicability in real-time decoding scenarios.
- **Absence of semantic information**: The current method operates purely at the pixel/frequency level without leveraging semantic priors, which may limit performance in complex scenes.
- **Diminishing returns in multi-frame scaling**: BD-rate improvement from $M=16$ to $M=24$ is marginal (-19.88% to -20.97%), suggesting an upper bound on the capacity of the shared basis.
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Evaluation is restricted to standard academic benchmarks (Set5/14, Urban100, Kodak, etc.) without validation of robustness across diverse real-world scenarios.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Relationship to SwinIR/HAT/DAT**: These methods serve as plug-and-play Coefficient Backbones that are directly enhanced, demonstrating the generality of Factorized Features.
- **Connection to Factor Fields/TensoRF**: FIPER transfers the tensor decomposition ideas from 3D scene modeling to 2D low-level image vision tasks.
- **Distinction from Fourier Feature Mapping**: The latter uses fixed Fourier bases to map coordinates, whereas FIPER learns non-uniform bases augmented with coordinate transformation.
- **Broader implications**: This representation-first philosophy is generalizable to other low-level vision tasks such as denoising, deblurring, and inpainting; the multi-frequency modulation mechanism may also offer insights for temporal frequency modeling in video super-resolution.
