# How Far are AI-generated Videos from Simulating the 3D Visual World: A Learned 3D Evaluation Approach

**Conference**: ICCV 2025
**arXiv**: [2406.19568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19568)
**Code**: Not released
**Area**: 3D Vision / Video Generation Evaluation / 3D Consistency
**Keywords**: Video Evaluation, 3D Coherence, Monocular 3D Cues, Contrastive Learning, Deepfake Detection

## TL;DR
This paper proposes Learned 3D Evaluation (L3DE), an objective and quantifiable evaluation framework based on monocular 3D cues (motion, depth, appearance) and contrastive learning, designed to measure the gap between AI-generated videos and real videos in terms of 3D visual consistency, without requiring manual annotation of artifacts or quality labels.

## Background & Motivation

Video diffusion models (e.g., Sora, Kling) can generate highly realistic videos, yet the extent to which these videos simulate the real 3D world has not been systematically studied. Existing evaluations rely either on subjective user studies (time-consuming and non-scalable) or on metrics such as FVD and IS (which fail to capture 3D spatial consistency).

A **natural evaluation strategy** is 3D scene reconstruction — high-quality reconstruction indicates good 3D consistency. However, this approach faces significant challenges:
- Camera pose estimation for in-the-wild videos is unreliable; methods such as COLMAP frequently fail on generated videos.
- The absence of multi-view cues makes large-scale reconstruction-based evaluation impractical.

The authors therefore turn to **monocular 3D cues** as a proxy: optical flow (motion), depth (geometry), and DINOv2 features (appearance). These cues can be reliably extracted from foundation models and reflect the 3D structure and motion consistency of a video without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction.

The core challenge is how to **quantify** the 3D simulation gap between real and generated videos using these proxy cues while providing **interpretable** localization of failure regions.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
1. **Data Preparation**: 80,000 real/synthetic video pairs are collected (real videos from Pexels; synthetic counterparts generated by SVD conditioned on the first real frame), maximizing content alignment to isolate 3D consistency differences.
2. **3D Proxy Extraction**: Three categories of monocular cues are extracted from each video.
3. **Classifier Training**: A 3D convolutional network with contrastive learning is trained to distinguish real from synthetic videos.
4. **Evaluation and Visualization**: Confidence scores quantify the gap; Grad-CAM localizes inconsistent regions.

### 3D Visual World Proxies

Three dimensions characterize the authenticity of the 3D visual world:

- **Appearance**: Per-frame visual features extracted by DINOv2, capturing cross-frame appearance consistency (better suited to represent high-level semantic alignment than raw RGB).
- **Motion**: Inter-frame optical flow extracted by RAFT, reflecting differences in motion patterns.
- **Geometry**: Per-frame metric depth maps extracted by UniDepth, encoding occlusion relationships, spatial structure, scale, and other 2.5D geometric cues (metric depth ensures cross-frame scale consistency).

### Classifier Training and L3DE Score

A 3D convolutional network (multi-layer 3D Conv + ReLU) is built on the 3D proxies to predict the confidence score of whether an input sample belongs to real or synthetic video.

A **contrastive loss** enhances discriminability:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{contrastive}} = \sum_i \exp\left(-\|\mathbf{f}_{\text{gen}}^{(i)} - \mathbf{f}_{\text{real}}^{(j(i))}\|_2^2\right)$$

where $\mathbf{f}_{\text{real}}^{(j(i))}$ is the real video feature nearest to $\mathbf{f}_{\text{gen}}^{(i)}$. The contrastive loss pushes generated video features away from their nearest real video counterparts.

**Total loss**:
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{contrastive}}$$

### Feature Fusion

Scores from the three aspects are fused via feature concatenation:
$$\mathbf{f}_{\text{fused}} = \text{Concat}(\mathbf{f}_{\text{app}}, \mathbf{f}_{\text{mot}}, \mathbf{f}_{\text{geo}})$$

The resulting L3DE Fusion score provides an overall 3D visual consistency assessment that complements individual aspect scores.

### Grad-CAM Visualization
Gradients are back-propagated to the final convolutional layer to generate class-discriminative localization maps, highlighting spatiotemporal regions that drive model decisions and pinpointing 3D-inconsistent locations.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Correlation of L3DE with 3D Reconstruction Quality

| Evaluation Method | Fusion | Appearance | Motion | Geometry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3D Reconstruction Quality (Spearman ρ) | **0.7566** | 0.7181 | 0.6669 | 0.3142 |
| Human Ratings (Spearman ρ) | **0.6460** | 0.5643 | 0.4617 | 0.3479 |

- The Fusion model achieves the highest correlation under both validation protocols.
- Inconsistent regions localized by L3DE align closely with regions of high 3D reconstruction error.

### Ablation Study: Comparison with Existing Metrics

| Metric | Source | Spearman ρ with Human Ratings |
|---|---|---|
| Subject Consistency | VBench | 3.90 |
| Background Consistency | VBench | 20.68 |
| Motion Smoothness | VBench | 19.99 |
| Temporal Consistency | EvalCrafter | 13.85 |
| **L3DE Fusion Score** | **L3DE** | **64.84** |

The L3DE Fusion score substantially outperforms existing metrics in correlation with human judgments.

### Generative Model Benchmark

| Generator | Fusion | Appearance | Motion | Geometry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kling 2.1 | **0.8904** | 0.8129 | 0.6735 | 0.7623 |
| Sora | 0.8895 | **0.8394** | 0.6467 | 0.7458 |
| MiniMax | 0.7932 | 0.7714 | 0.6098 | 0.7251 |
| Kling 1.5 | 0.7518 | 0.7247 | 0.5926 | 0.6927 |
| CogVideoX-5B | 0.6104 | 0.5893 | 0.6203 | 0.7539 |
| Luma 1.6 | 0.5062 | 0.4950 | 0.5853 | 0.6800 |
| **Real Videos** | **0.9999** | 0.9950 | 0.8321 | 0.8435 |

**Key Findings**:
- Sora and Kling 2.1 achieve the strongest appearance simulation (>0.81), yet all models exhibit a substantial gap in motion consistency (real: 0.83 vs. best: 0.67).
- Geometry consistency is generally superior to motion consistency across all models, indicating that motion realism remains the primary weakness of current generative models.

## Highlights & Insights
1. **First systematic 3D visual consistency evaluation framework**: Covers appearance, motion, and geometry simultaneously.
2. **Reconstruction-free evaluation**: Monocular foundation models serve as 3D proxies, circumventing the bottleneck of pose estimation failure.
3. **Strong interpretability**: Grad-CAM provides spatiotemporal localization of failure regions.
4. **Broad applicability**: L3DE can be directly applied as a deepfake detector (>0.7 accuracy) and can improve video quality by guiding the repair of flagged regions.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Training relies on SVD-generated paired data, potentially inheriting SVD's generative biases.
- Monocular 3D cues are ultimately proxy indicators and cannot fully replace ground-truth 3D reconstruction validation.
- The geometry proxy shows relatively low correlation with human ratings (0.35), suggesting that depth prediction may be inaccurate in certain scenarios.
- Semantic-level 3D consistency (e.g., physical plausibility of object interactions) is not considered.

## Related Work & Insights
- **Video generation evaluation**: VBench and EvalCrafter provide general benchmarks but lack a 3D evaluation dimension.
- **3D reconstruction**: NeRF and 3DGS can serve as validation tools but are unsuitable for large-scale evaluation.
- **Depth/optical flow foundation models**: UniDepth and RAFT provide reliable monocular 3D cue extraction capabilities.
