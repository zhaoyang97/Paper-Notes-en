# From Scoring to Explanations: Evaluating SHAP and LLM Rationales for Rubric-based Teaching Quality Assessment

**Conference**: ACL2026 Findings  
**arXiv**: [2606.05180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05180)  
**Code**: Not provided in cache  
**Area**: Educational NLP / Explainability / Automated Rubric Scoring  
**Keywords**: SHAP, LLM Explanation, Teaching Quality Assessment, Sentence-level Attribution, Deletion Test  

## TL;DR
This paper proposes a sentence-level evaluation framework for automated rubric scoring. By comparing fine-tuned PLMs, prompted LLMs, SHAP attribution, and LLM rationales in the task of feedback quality scoring for classroom teaching, the authors find that fine-tuned PLMs are more accurate, while SHAP provides more faithful and transferable explanations than LLM-generated rationales.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: Automated rubric scoring has been applied to open-ended performance assessments such as essays, peer feedback, classroom transcripts, and clinical notes. Models typically output a scalar score to indicate whether the text meets a specific rubric dimension.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Providing only a score is insufficient, especially in high-stakes educational scenarios. Teachers, students, and administrators need to know why a model assigned a specific score to trust, contest, or improve the feedback. While LLMs can generate plausible natural language explanations, research suggests these justifications are often merely "plausible" rather than "faithful."

**Key Challenge**: Rubric scoring requires interpretability and accountability, yet the internal decision-making processes of powerful models are difficult to observe directly. LLM rationales are readable but may not reflect the actual computational basis of the score. Attribution methods like SHAP are closer to causal influence but lack systematic comparison in the context of long classroom transcripts and multi-level rubrics.

**Goal**: The authors aim to answer three questions: whether PLMs or LLMs are better suited for scoring the "Quality of Feedback" (QoF); whether SHAP or LLM sentence ranking is more effective at identifying sentences that truly influence the score; and whether explanations generated by one type of model can transfer to another.

**Key Insight**: The paper defines an explanation as a sentence-level evidence ranking rather than free-text justification. This allows for a unified deletion test to measure faithfulness: if removing the top-k sentences selected by an explanation method significantly alters the model's prediction, those sentences are deemed to truly influence the scoring.

**Core Idea**: Utilizing a protocol of "Sentence-level SHAP / LLM ranking → Delete top-10 sentences → Rescore → Compare prediction changes," the authors systematically test if rubric scoring explanations are faithful and further conduct cross-model transfer tests.

## Method
The methodology serves as an evaluation framework rather than a single new model. The authors first train or invoke two types of scorers, then use two explanation methods to identify important sentences, and finally evaluate explanation quality through the change in scores after deleting those sentences.

### Overall Architecture
Data is derived from NCTE elementary mathematics classroom transcripts, comprising over 1,600 lessons segmented into 6,005 15-minute clips, expert-annotated using the CLASS framework. This study focuses on the "Quality of Feedback" dimension under Instructional Support, with scores ranging from 1 to 7. The dataset consists of 4,775 training segments and 1,230 test segments, with no class overlap between splits. For each transcript segment, the model outputs a QoF score; the explanation method ranks the most important sentences; the system iteratively deletes the top-10 sentences and rescores, using the average consecutive prediction change $\overline{\Delta}$ to measure whether the explanation accurately identifies the evidence the model relies on.

### Key Designs
1.  **PLM and LLM Dual Scorer Setup**:
    - **Function**: To compare the differences between task-specific fine-tuned models and general-purpose instruction-tuned LLMs in rubric scoring.
    - **Mechanism**: On the PLM side, base and large versions of BERT, ALBERT, RoBERTa, and DeBERTaV3 are fine-tuned. Each transcript segment is encoded by sentence, using the `[CLS]` representation for each, followed by a trainable attention mechanism to obtain a document representation, and a linear regression head to predict the QoF scalar. On the LLM side, open-source instruction models like Llama 3.1, Mixtral, Qwen3, and Mistral are used for direct scoring via few-shot prompting.
    - **Design Motivation**: PLMs represent traditional supervised rubric scoring, while LLMs represent zero-training deployable solutions; setting them side-by-side allows for determining if explanation methods are model-specific.

2.  **Sentence-level Explanation and Deletion Test**:
    - **Function**: To convert the question of "explanation faithfulness" into measurable prediction changes.
    - **Mechanism**: For PLMs, SHAP is run on the document-level regression output, treating each sentence embedding as a feature to obtain a sentence Shapley value. For LLMs, models are prompted zero-shot to return the indices of the 10 most influential sentences. Subsequently, sentences are deleted in order of rank, calculating $\Delta_i=f(x_{-r_{i-1}})-f(x_{-r_i})$, and reporting the top-10 average change $\overline{\Delta}$. A larger change indicates that the sentences selected by the explanation are more likely to be the basis of the model's prediction.
    - **Design Motivation**: Free-text explanations are hard to verify, while sentence deletion establishes an observable link between explanations and model predictions. A random deletion baseline is included to rule out the confounding effect of text perturbation.

3.  **Cross-model Consistency Evaluation**:
    - **Function**: To check whether evidence found by one model/explanation method is also important to another category of model.
    - **Mechanism**: The authors select 3 PLMs (BERT large, DeBERTaV3 large, ALBERT base) and 3 LLMs (Qwen3 235B, Mistral Small, Llama 3.1 8B). They use sentences selected by LLMs to perturb PLMs and use SHAP-selected sentences from PLMs to perturb LLMs, comparing the prediction change trajectories.
    - **Design Motivation**: If an explanation only affects its source model, it reflects model-specific behavior; if it generates stable perturbations across architectures, it likely captures general rubric-relevant evidence.

### Loss & Training
PLM fine-tuning utilizes Mean Squared Error (MSE) to optimize for the 1-7 QoF regression target. At the input level, each sentence is truncated to 128 tokens, with a maximum of 263 sentences per document, covering 98% of sentence lengths and 90% of document lengths. LLMs are not task-finetuned; few-shot scoring and zero-shot sentence ranking are performed with deterministic decoding and 4-bit nf4 quantization for local inference. Explanation evaluation is standardized using the same sentence splitting, deletion rules, and top-10 settings.

## Key Experimental Results

| SHAP → LLM transfer | Strong initial drop | The first SHAP sentence often causes immediate LLM prediction shifts. |
| LLM rationale → PLM transfer | Lower $\Delta$, non-monotonic | LLM rationales transfer poorly to PLM decision bases. |

### Key Findings
- While fine-tuned PLMs have better scoring accuracy, they compress predictions around the 3-5 range, making extreme classroom quality difficult to identify.
- LLMs can output the full 1-7 distribution but have higher MSE. Furthermore, LLM sentence-ranking outputs are unstable; some models fail to return exactly 10 sentences consistently even after retries.
- SHAP explanations are more stable in both self-model and cross-model deletions. LLM rationales may look like "reasons," but their actual impact on model predictions is limited.
- Teacher utterances are the primary source of evidence: LLMs select teacher utterances 79.5% of the time, and PLMs 74.0%, aligning with the QoF rubric definition.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Shifting from "Plausibility" to "Prediction Change"**: In education, it is easy to be swayed by the readability of natural language rationales. This paper uses the deletion test to remind researchers that explanations must be coupled with model behavior.
- **Asymmetry between SHAP and LLM Rationales**: LLM-selected sentences often have little impact on PLMs, yet SHAP-selected sentences significantly perturb LLMs. This suggests traditional attribution methods remain indispensable in high-stakes NLP.
- **Misalignment between Accuracy and Faithfulness**: DeBERTaV3 large is the strongest scorer but not the most sensitive explanation target. This indicates model selection should not rely solely on MAE.
- **Framework Portability**: As long as a task involves long-text rubric scoring where parts can be deleted, this protocol can be reused for essay scoring, peer feedback, or medical record assessment.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Dataset scale and label skew: Although QoF is relatively balanced compared to other dimensions, 81% of labels fall in the 3-5 range, leading to PLM label compression.
- The study relies solely on text. Actual CLASS scoring depends on audio (tone, rhythm) and visual cues which text-only models miss.
- Single-expert annotation makes it impossible to distinguish model error from human subjectivity or estimate inter-rater reliability.
- Deletion disrupts discourse flow, which may disproportionately affect LLMs. Future work should involve human-gold rationales or construct-relevance labels.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs Attention-based explanation**: Since attention's status as an explanation is debated, this paper opts for SHAP and deletion tests, which are closer to the "faithfulness" standard.
- **vs LIME / SHAP in essay scoring**: While earlier works applied attribution to essays, this study scales it to long transcripts and hierarchical PLM/LLM comparisons.
- **vs LLM Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: CoT may improve readability but not necessarily faithfulness. The ranking results here suggest LLM rationales require independent verification in high-stakes scoring.

## Rating
- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ The deletion test and SHAP are not new, but the unified protocol for PLM/LLM rubric scoring and cross-model transfer is highly valuable.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ Wide model coverage; however, it is limited to one dimension and one dataset without human sentence-level gold evidence.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ Clear research questions and solid experimental narrative.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ Provides a critical warning for using LLM explanations in high-stakes settings, highlighting that fluent rationales are not always faithful rationales.
