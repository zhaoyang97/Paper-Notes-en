# DeClotH: Decomposable 3D Cloth and Human Body Reconstruction from a Single Image

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.19373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19373)  
**Code**: [https://hygenie1228.github.io/DeClotH/](https://hygenie1228.github.io/DeClotH/) (Project Page)  
**Area**: Diffusion Models/3D Reconstruction  
**Keywords**: 3D Garment Reconstruction, Human Reconstruction, Decomposable, Template Regularization, Garment Diffusion Models

## TL;DR

This work proposes DeClotH, which reconstructs decomposable 3D clothing and human body meshes from a single image. It leverages 3D templates (SMPLicit+SMPL) as geometric priors to mitigate occlusion issues and trains a dedicated ClothDiffusion model to provide garment-specific texture and geometric guidance.

## Background & Motivation

1. **Background**: Significant progress has been made in 3D clothed human reconstruction using diffusion models (e.g., SiTH, TeCH, HumanSGD). However, reconstructing the clothed body as a holistic entity prevents the decomposition of separate garments and the human body.
2. **Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Severe occlusion of the body by clothing makes it difficult to infer the geometry and texture of occluded regions; (2) The clothing contour in the input image is incomplete due to occlusions, leading the reconstruction to easily overfit to imperfect observations; (3) Standard diffusion models (StableDiffusion) always generate hybrid clothing+human images, which are unsuitable for guiding independent clothing reconstruction.
3. **Key Challenge**: Severe mutual occlusion between clothing and the body makes decomposable reconstruction extremely challenging, and the guidance obtained from standard diffusion models via SDS loss is inapplicable to separate garments.
4. **Goal**: To achieve separate, high-quality 3D reconstruction of garments and the body, supporting downstream applications such as virtual try-on.
5. **Key Insight**: Using parametric 3D templates (SMPLicit for clothing, SMPL for the body) to provide strong geometric priors, and a customized diffusion model to provide garment-specific appearance priors.
6. **Core Idea**: Template regularization + ClothDiffusion (a garment-dedicated diffusion model) + two-stage optimization.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Input image → extract normal map $N$, silhouette $S$, 3D template meshes (ClothNet→$M^t_{cloth}$, BodyNet→$M^t_{body}$) → represent 3D garments and the body utilizing DMTet → geometry stage (template regularization + normal/silhouette loss + SDS_norm) → texture stage (SDS_rgb + RGB reconstruction loss) → output decomposed 3D meshes.

### Key Designs

1. **Template Regularization Loss (Template Regularization)**

    - **Function**: Utilizes the shape priors of 3D garment/body templates to constrain the optimization process, preventing incorrect reconstructions caused by occlusion.
    - **Mechanism**: ClothWild is used to predict the garment template $M^t_{cloth}$ (parameterized by SMPLicit), and PIXIE is used to predict the body template $M^t_{body}$ (parameterized by SMPL). During optimization, the rendered silhouettes of the DMTet meshes are constrained to match the rendered silhouettes of the templates: $L_{cloth-t} = \|\mathcal{R}_{sil}(M_{cloth}, k) - \mathcal{R}_{sil}(M^t_{cloth}, k)\|_2$. These templates are trained on in-the-wild datasets and are robust to occlusions.
    - **Design Motivation**: Instead of relying entirely on potentially incomplete image evidence, data-driven template priors are utilized to provide a reliable baseline.

2. **ClothDiffusion (Garment-dedicated Diffusion Model)**

    - **Function**: Provides garment-specific appearance priors to guide 3D garment reconstruction, replacing the unsuitable StableDiffusion.
    - **Mechanism**: Finetuned based on the ControlNet+StableDiffusion architecture, training data includes: (1) garment images segmented by SAM, (2) garment template silhouettes + human skeletons as conditional inputs, (3) garment text descriptions generated by BLIP. After training, ClothDiffusion generates only garment images (excluding the human body), and the generation results can be controlled to be consistent with the actual pose through the silhouette and skeleton conditions.
    - **Design Motivation**: Standard diffusion models cannot generate garments separated from the human body. ClothDiffusion addresses this issue via specialized data and conditional controls.

3. **Decomposed Dual-path SDS Loss**

    - **Function**: Optimizes the geometry and textures of the garments and human body separately.
    - **Mechanism**: Cloth SDS loss utilizes ClothDiffusion, conditioned on garment silhouettes + human skeletons; Human SDS loss utilizes ControlNet (conditioned on skeleton) to simultaneously optimize the isolated human body rendering and the joint clothing-body rendering. An interpenetration penalty $L_{pen}$ is also employed to prevent mesh intersection. A two-stage optimization is conducted: geometry first (normal SDS + template regularization + normal/silhouette loss), and then texture (RGB SDS + RGB reconstruction loss).
    - **Design Motivation**: Garments and the body require different diffusion priors—ClothDiffusion for garments and standard ControlNet+skeleton for the body.

### Loss & Training

Geometry stage: $L_{geo} = L_{cloth-t} + L_{body-t} + L_{cloth-SDS}^{norm} + L_{human-SDS}^{norm} + L_{recon}^{geo}$ (including normal, silhouette, and interpenetration penalties). Texture stage: $L_{tex} = L_{cloth-SDS}^{rgb} + L_{human-SDS}^{rgb} + L_{recon}^{tex}$ (including L2 and LPIPS reconstruction losses).

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Method | 4D-DRESS(cloth) CD↓ | 4D-DRESS(cloth) NC↓ | 4D-DRESS(cloth+human) CD↓ |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| BCNet | 4.387 | 0.046 | 3.925 |
| SMPLicit | 4.080 | 0.038 | 3.605 |
| ClothNet+BodyNet | 4.100 | 0.038 | 3.526 |
| **Ours** | **3.902** | **0.037** | **3.292** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | CD↓(cloth) | NC↓(cloth) |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| w/o Template Reg. | 4.45 | 0.042 |
| w/o ClothDiffusion (using SD) | 4.21 | 0.040 |
| w/o Human SDS | 4.10 | 0.039 |
| **Full model** | **3.902** | **0.037** |

### Key Findings

- Template regularization contributes the most (CD increases by 0.55 without it), indicating that the occlusion problem is indeed the core challenge.
- ClothDiffusion vs. StableDiffusion: Using the dedicated diffusion model reduces the CD by 0.31, validating the importance of garment-specific priors.
- Generalizability is also validated on THuman2.0, consistently outperforming the baselines.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Using 3D templates as a "safety net" is highly practical**: Although parametric templates lack fine details, they provide a reasonable global shape to prevent optimization from deteriorating. This template-guided optimization paradigm can be transferred to other single-view 3D reconstruction tasks.
- **The design of ClothDiffusion is highly targeted**: Utilizing SAM to automatically segment garments and BLIP to generate descriptions establishes a highly automated pipeline for creating training data.
- The combination strategy of templates and SDS enables decomposable reconstruction without requiring 3D ground truth.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The optimization process is time-consuming (requiring multiple SDS iteration steps), making it unsuitable for real-time applications.
- For highly complex multi-layered clothing scenarios (e.g., jacket + sweater + shirt), it only handles a single layer.
- The representational capacity of the templates is limited—template quality may be insufficient for unconventional garments (e.g., skirts, hats, etc.).
- Texture generation relies on the diversity of the diffusion model, which sometimes leads to inconsistent textures.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs SiTH/TeCH**: These methods reconstruct the clothed human body as a whole, whereas DeClotH is the first to achieve decomposable garment + human body reconstruction.
- **vs GALA**: GALA decomposes garments from 3D scans, while DeClotH operates directly from a single image, which is more challenging.
- Garment decomposition has direct value for applications such as virtual try-on and garment editing.
