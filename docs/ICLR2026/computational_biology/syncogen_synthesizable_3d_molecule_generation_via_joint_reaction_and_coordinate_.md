# SynCoGen: Synthesizable 3D Molecule Generation via Joint Reaction and Coordinate Modeling

**Conference**: ICLR 2026
**arXiv**: [2507.11818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11818)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/andreirekesh/SynCoGen)  
**Area**: Medical Imaging / Molecule Generation
**Keywords**: synthesizable molecule generation, 3D conformation generation, masked graph diffusion, flow matching, drug discovery

## TL;DR

SynCoGen proposes a multimodal generative framework combining masked graph diffusion and flow matching to jointly sample molecular building-block reaction graphs and 3D atomic coordinates, achieving high-quality 3D molecule generation while guaranteeing synthetic feasibility.

## Background & Motivation

Generative molecular design holds significant value in drug discovery, yet a critical bottleneck is **synthetic accessibility**—generated molecules are often difficult to synthesize in the laboratory. Existing approaches suffer from two major shortcomings:

**Template-based synthesis methods** (e.g., GFlowNet, Transformer) generate only 2D molecular graphs and cannot model 3D geometric structure, which is essential for the chemical and biological properties of drugs.

**3D molecule generation methods** (e.g., SemlaFlow, MiDi) can generate atomic coordinates but disregard synthetic routes, yielding molecules that are frequently unsynthesizable.

SynCoGen aims to **bridge the gap between 3D molecule generation and synthetic feasibility**, jointly generating synthesizable molecular graphs and physically plausible 3D conformations within a unified framework.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

SynCoGen represents each molecule as a triple $(X, E, C)$:
- $X \in \{0,1\}^{N \times (|\mathcal{B}|+1)}$: building block identity encoding
- $E \in \{0,1\}^{N \times N \times (|\mathcal{R}|V_{\max}^2+2)}$: inter-building-block chemical reaction and reaction center encoding
- $C \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M \times 3}$: 3D coordinates of all atoms

Training unifies two diffusion schemes on a shared time axis: discrete absorption (masking) diffusion over $(X, E)$ and continuous, visibility-aware flow matching over $C$.

### Key Designs

1. **SynSpace Dataset Family**: Constructs a dataset comprising 1.2 million synthesizable molecules and 7.5 million low-energy conformations, using 93 low-cost building blocks and 19 high-yield reaction templates (core set) and 378 building blocks with 26 reactions (extended set). Molecules are generated by iteratively coupling building blocks, and multiple 3D conformations are computed via a semi-empirical method (GFN2-xTB).

2. **Joint Masked Graph Diffusion + Flow Matching**: Discrete reaction graphs are handled with absorption diffusion following the MDLM framework, while continuous coordinates employ conditional flow matching (CFM); both share a unified time step. A key innovation is a **visibility mask** $S_t$ that, during training, consistently hides coordinate information for atoms belonging to masked building blocks, enabling the model to correctly handle variable atom counts.

3. **Chemistry-Informed Training Constraints**:

    - **No self-loop diagonal**: prevents a building block from reacting with itself
    - **Edge count limit**: at most $n-1$ edges for $n$ building blocks
    - **Compatibility mask**: once a reaction edge is determined, the connected nodes are restricted to chemically compatible building blocks, ensuring chemical validity

4. **SE(3)-Equivariant Backbone**: Adapted from the SemlaFlow architecture to simultaneously predict building-block logits $L_t^X, L_t^E$ and coordinate estimates $\hat{\tilde{C}}_0^t$. For pharmacophore-conditioned generation, pharmacophore points are introduced as "virtual atoms" that interact with real atoms in the equivariant–invariant dynamics module.

### Loss & Training

The total loss is a weighted sum:
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{graph}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}}$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{graph}}$: cross-entropy loss over $(X, E)$
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}$: mean squared error on masked coordinates
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}}$: short-range pairwise distance regularization to ensure 3D structural plausibility

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Method | Valid.↑ | AiZyn.↑ | Synth.↑ | GFN-FF↓ | xTB↓ | PB↑ | FCD↓ |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|------|-----|------|
| **SynCoGen** | **96.7** | **50** | **72** | **3.01** | **-0.91** | **87.2** | **2.91** |
| SemlaFlow | 93.3 | 38 | 36 | 5.96 | -0.72 | 87.2 | 7.21 |
| JODO | 91.1 | 38 | 31 | 4.72 | -0.74 | 84.1 | 4.22 |
| EQGAT-diff | 85.9 | 37 | 24 | 4.89 | -0.73 | 78.9 | 6.75 |
| MiDi | 74.4 | 33 | 31 | 4.90 | -0.74 | 63.0 | 6.00 |

SynCoGen achieves substantial improvements in synthetic feasibility (AiZyn +12, Synth +36 vs. SemlaFlow) and conformation energy quality.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Key Metric | Remark |
|--------------|-----------|--------|
| w/o chemistry constraints | Large drop in Valid. | Chemistry-aware graph constraints are the largest contributor |
| w/o self-conditioning | Drop in Valid./FCD | Self-conditioning contributes significantly to quality |
| SemlaFlow retrained on SynSpace | Valid. 72.0 | Confirms gains stem from training strategy, not data/architecture alone |
| SynSpace-L (larger search space) | Maintains high quality | Scales to larger building-block vocabularies |

### Key Findings

- **Fragment Linking**: On 3 FDA-approved drug targets, molecules linked by SynCoGen achieve docking scores comparable to or better than native ligands, with retrosynthetic feasibility rates of 58–79% (vs. 0% for DiffLinker).
- **Pharmacophore-Conditioned Generation**: Across 10 PDB/LIT-PCBA targets, SynCoGen achieves the best docking score on 8/10, with retrosynthetic feasibility rates 15–65% higher than all baselines.
- **Zero-Shot Conformation Generation**: Given a random reaction graph, the model attains conformation quality approaching ETKDG (RDKit).

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Paradigm shift of "synthesis as generation"**: Synthetic constraints are encoded directly into the generative process rather than applied as post-hoc filters, fundamentally guaranteeing synthesizability.
2. **Multi-resolution multimodality**: Discrete diffusion at the building-block level and continuous flow matching at the atomic level—the "dual-resolution, unified time" design is particularly elegant.
3. **Zero-shot conditional design**: A single amortized model accomplishes multiple drug design tasks without target-specific retraining.
4. **Significant data contribution**: SynSpace contains 1.2M+ synthesizable molecules and 7.5M+ conformations, representing an important public resource for the field.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Limited building-block vocabulary**: The largest vocabulary comprises only 378 building blocks, restricting chemical diversity.
2. **No macrocycle support**: Edge-count constraints during training preclude macrocycle generation.
3. **Absence of wet-lab validation**: Generated molecules have not been physically synthesized or tested for biological activity.
4. **Upper bound on retrosynthetic success rate**: AiZynthFinder itself resolves only 50–70% of known drug molecules, potentially underestimating true synthetic feasibility.

## Related Work & Insights

- Compared to **CGFlow** (GFlowNet + flow matching), SynCoGen requires neither per-target retraining nor external reward functions.
- **SemlaFlow** provides the equivariant backbone architecture but lacks synthetic constraints.
- **DiffLinker** is a dedicated fragment linking model, yet generated molecules are entirely unsynthesizable.
- The work offers important insights for both computational chemistry and AI-driven drug discovery: synthetic constraints can be intrinsically embedded into generative models.
