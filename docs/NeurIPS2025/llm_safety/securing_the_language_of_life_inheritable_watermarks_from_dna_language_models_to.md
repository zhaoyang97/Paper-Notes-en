# Securing the Language of Life: Inheritable Watermarks from DNA Language Models to Proteins

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2509.18207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18207)  
**Code**: Unavailable  
**Area**: Medical Imaging
**Keywords**: DNA watermarking, biosecurity, language models, synonymous codon substitution, central dogma

## TL;DR

This paper proposes DNAMark and CentralMark, two watermarking schemes for embedding robust watermarks in sequences generated by DNA language models. DNAMark achieves function-preserving watermarks via synonymous codon substitution, while CentralMark realizes inheritable watermarks that propagate from DNA to protein through the central dogma.

## Background & Motivation

DNA language models (e.g., Evo, Evo2) have achieved revolutionary advances in understanding and designing DNA sequences, with applications in therapeutics, synthetic biology, and gene editing. However, this capability simultaneously introduces serious dual-use risks — models could be misused to engineer pathogens or biological weapons.

Existing LLM watermarking techniques (e.g., KGW) cannot be directly transferred to DNA language models for three reasons: (1) DNA has only four nucleotides (A, C, T, G), making the vocabulary extremely small and severely limiting green/red list approaches; (2) DNA is susceptible to natural mutations, synthesis errors, and sequencing inaccuracies, which can easily destroy watermarks; (3) the functional integrity of protein-coding sequences must be preserved. Therefore, watermarking schemes specifically designed for DNA sequences are required.

The core motivation is to establish a provenance mechanism for DNA language model outputs — enabling traceability of AI-generated DNA sequences without compromising biological function — thereby balancing innovation with biosecurity.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The system comprises two complementary watermarking schemes: DNAMark targets the DNA sequence itself (modifying only the third base of each codon), while CentralMark targets information transfer from DNA to protein via the central dogma (modifying the second codon base), enabling inheritable watermarks detectable in both the DNA and the translated protein.

### Key Designs

1. **Evo2 Embedding-Based Watermark Model (DNAMark)**: A trained watermark model obtains functional embeddings of the current sequence via Evo2, then transforms them into watermark logits that are superimposed onto the original logits. The watermark model is trained to optimize two objectives: an alignment loss $\mathcal{L}_a$ that ensures the similarity of watermark logits is consistent with that of Evo2 embeddings (using $\tanh$ normalization), and a normalization loss $\mathcal{L}_n$ that constrains the watermark logits to have zero mean and uniform absolute values across all directions. The core intuition is that DNA embeddings are naturally robust to small mutations, preserving semantic and functional integrity.

2. **Synonymous Codon Substitution (SCS)**: DNAMark specifically injects watermarks at the third base position of codons in coding regions. Due to the degeneracy of the genetic code, synonymous codons encode the same amino acid. For a synonymous codon set $\mathcal{S}$, the base with the highest watermark logit is assigned to the green list and the remaining bases to the red list. For example, for histidine (CAT/CAC), T is green and C is red. Watermarking is skipped when $|\mathcal{S}|=1$ (e.g., methionine ATG).

3. **Adaptive Watermark Strength and Entropy-Guided Strategy**: Watermark strength $\delta$ is dynamically adjusted via EMA, adapting within $[z_{\min}, z_{\max}]$ based on the current z-score:

$$\delta_{t+1} = (1-\beta)\delta_t + \beta \cdot \max(\delta_{\min}, \min(\delta_{\max}, \delta_t + \kappa \cdot \text{adj}(z_t)))$$

Watermarking is additionally skipped in low-entropy subsequences ($H(s) < H_{\text{threshold}}$) to avoid disrupting critical functional elements such as regulatory motifs.

4. **CentralMark Inheritable Watermark**: Unlike DNAMark, CentralMark modifies the second codon base (which primarily determines the chemical properties of amino acids), using ESM protein embeddings in place of Evo2 embeddings. By partitioning the second base into green/red lists ($\mathcal{G}_b = \{C, G\}$), it achieves a near-non-overlapping green/red partition at the amino acid level:

$$\mathcal{G}_a = \{a \mid \text{translate}(b_1, b_2, b_3) = a, b_2 \in \mathcal{G}_b\}$$

The resulting watermark is detectable not only in the DNA sequence but also independently in the translated protein sequence.

### Loss & Training

The total loss of the watermark model is the sum of the alignment loss and the normalization loss. Watermark detection employs the z-score method from KGW. Due to the specific design of DNAMark and CentralMark, the expected proportion of green tokens $\gamma$ is set to 0.3559, 0.5, and 0.55 respectively — differing from the standard 0.5 used in general LLM watermarking.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Evaluation on a therapeutic DNA benchmark (400 sequences) using Evo2-7B:

| Attack Type | Method | TPR@1%FPR | F1@1%FPR | TPR@10%FPR | F1@10%FPR |
|-------------|--------|-----------|----------|------------|-----------|
| No attack | KGW-1 | 0.765 | 0.862 | 0.805 | 0.845 |
| No attack | DNAMark | 0.845 | 0.911 | 0.915 | 0.908 |
| No attack | CentralMark(DNA) | **0.875** | **0.928** | **0.920** | **0.911** |
| Synonymous substitution | KGW-1 | 0.580 | 0.729 | 0.756 | 0.815 |
| Synonymous substitution | DNAMark | 0.820 | 0.896 | 0.896 | 0.898 |
| Synonymous substitution | CentralMark(Protein) | **0.860** | **0.920** | 0.904 | 0.902 |
| Nucleotide substitution | DNAMark | 0.808 | 0.902 | 0.886 | 0.892 |
| Indel | CentralMark(DNA) | 0.765 | 0.862 | 0.850 | 0.872 |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Detection F1 | Note |
|---------------|-------------|------|
| Full DNAMark | 0.911 | Baseline |
| w/o adaptive δ + EMA | Significant drop | Adaptive strength is critical for detection |
| w/o watermark model | Significant drop | Watermark model is a core component |
| w/o entropy guidance | Slight drop | Entropy guidance primarily preserves sequence quality |
| w/o alignment + normalization loss | Moderate drop | Loss functions contribute to robustness |

### Key Findings

- DNAMark and CentralMark achieve detection F1 above 0.85 under all attack conditions, substantially outperforming the KGW baseline.
- Sequence quality is well preserved: sequence similarity exceeds 60% and degradation scores remain below 15% compared to unwatermarked generation.
- DNAMark generalizes across multiple DNA models (megaDNA to Evo2-40B), with F1 ranging from 0.851 to 0.919.
- A CRISPR-Cas9 case study demonstrates that CentralMark does not affect protein structure (TM-score = 0.6802).
- Watermarking introduces approximately 30% time overhead, while the watermark model itself remains compact.

## Highlights & Insights

- **High novelty of problem formulation**: This is the first systematic application of watermarking to DNA language models, representing pioneering work at the intersection of biosecurity and AI safety.
- **Elegant inheritable watermark design**: CentralMark leverages the central dogma to propagate watermarks through the DNA-to-protein information transfer, a concept that stands apart in the watermarking literature.
- **Synonymous codon substitution balances function and watermarking**: By exploiting the degeneracy of the genetic code, the method achieves genuinely function-preserving watermarks.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Validation is limited to coding regions; the impact on non-coding regions (e.g., UTRs) remains insufficiently explored.
- Wet-lab experimental validation is absent; evaluation is purely computational.
- Performance degrades noticeably under indel attacks, leaving room for improved robustness.
- The scheme relies on green/red list partitioning; partition-free alternatives could be explored in future work.

## Related Work & Insights

Compared to general LLM watermarking methods (KGW, EWD, etc.), this work faces the unique challenges of an extremely small vocabulary and biological functional constraints. Relative to protein watermarking work (FoldMark), this paper is the first to cover the full pipeline of the central dogma. A key insight is that when domain constraints are stringent (e.g., preservation of biological function), deep domain knowledge must be integrated into watermark design rather than naively adapting general-purpose methods.
