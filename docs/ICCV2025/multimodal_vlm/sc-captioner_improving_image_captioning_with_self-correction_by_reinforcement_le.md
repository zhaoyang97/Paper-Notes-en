# SC-Captioner: Improving Image Captioning with Self-Correction by Reinforcement Learning

## Metadata
- **Conference**: ICCV 2025
- **arXiv**: [2508.06125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06125)
- **Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/zl2048/SC-Captioner)
- **Area**: Multimodal / Image Captioning
- **Keywords**: Image Captioning, Self-Correction, Reinforcement Learning, LVLM, Evaluation Metrics

## TL;DR

SC-Captioner proposes a multi-turn reinforcement learning framework based on policy gradient. By designing a correction reward function that incorporates correctness bonuses and mistake punishments, it enables large vision-language models to acquire self-correction capabilities for image captioning, while also introducing an improved CAPTURE evaluation metric.

## Background & Motivation

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) face a precision–recall trade-off in image captioning tasks:
- **Precision**: Generated content may include descriptions of objects absent from the image (hallucination).
- **Recall**: Longer captions improve recall but are more prone to hallucination.

A natural question arises: **Can LVLMs improve initial captions through self-correction?**

Prior work has shown that intrinsic self-correction in LLMs (without additional training) rarely improves outputs effectively. In image captioning, directly prompting a model to self-correct may cause it to remove correct descriptions while introducing hallucinations. Dedicated training strategies are therefore necessary to activate the self-correction capability.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

SC-Captioner employs multi-turn reinforcement learning:
1. The policy model generates a first-round caption $y_1$ conditioned on the image and an initial instruction.
2. The first-round dialogue is concatenated with a self-correction instruction to generate a second-round caption $y_2$.
3. A correction-based reward is computed, and the model is updated via policy gradient.

### Training Objective

$$L = -R(y_1, y_2, y^*) \cdot \log \pi_\theta(y_2 | [I, x_1, y_1, x_2]) + \beta L_{\text{KL}}$$

- When the reward is positive, the generation probability of $y_2$ is increased (encouraging correct corrections).
- When the reward is negative, the generation probability of $y_2$ is decreased (suppressing erroneous corrections).
- The KL loss $L_{\text{KL}}$ preserves the initial captioning capability.

### Key Designs: Reward Function

The reward function is based on scene graph parsing, decomposing captions into three sets: **objects, attributes, and relations**.

**Correctness Bonus**:
- The FACTUAL parser extracts object/attribute/relation sets.
- Set differences between $y_1$ and $y_2$ are computed to identify added and removed elements.
- These elements are matched against the ground-truth set to compute soft and hard scores.

For added elements: $\mathbf{S_a} = \{\max_{o^* \in \mathbf{O}_{y^*}} s(o_a, o^*) \mid o_a \in \mathbf{O}_{y_2} \setminus \mathbf{O}_{y_1}\}$

The soft score $\sum_{s \in \mathbf{S_a}}(s - \tau_a)$ measures the effectiveness of additions; the hard score $\sum_{s \in \mathbf{S_a}} \mathbf{1}_{\{s > \tau_a'\}}$ rewards correct additions.

**Mistake Punishment**:
- Adding hallucinations (present in $y_2$ but absent from $y_1 \cup y^*$): reward is penalized.
- Removing correct descriptions (present in $y_1 \cup y^*$ but absent from $y_2$): reward is penalized.
- No punishment is applied to relations due to the complexity of matching.

### Improved Evaluation Metrics

Three improvements are made to the CAPTURE metric:

1. **Object Precision**: Additional objects generated by GPT-4o are incorporated for precision computation, avoiding false-positive penalties caused by incomplete ground truth.
2. **Attribute Matching**: Attributes are matched only between the same or similar objects (CAPTURE's global matching erroneously matches similar attributes across different objects).
3. **Relation Evaluation**: Tuple-based matching is replaced by a question-answering (QA) approach, where 5 relation questions per image are generated and answered by an LLM.

### Dataset: RefinedCaps

- 6.5K images sampled from the COCO 2017 training set.
- Initial captions generated by GPT-4o.
- Expert annotators corrected hallucinations and added missing descriptions.
- At least 80% of objects in each image are ensured to appear in the caption.

### Key Findings

1. **Self-correction is effective**: SFT+Ours consistently improves performance on both test sets, with CAPTURE scores improving by more than 1.3.
2. **DPO is suboptimal**: DPO activates self-correction behavior but yields high recall at low precision — the model corrects but does so inaccurately.
3. **Mistake punishment is critical**: Removing mistake punishment leads to a significant drop in precision.
4. **Components are complementary**: Object, attribute, and relation rewards individually improve their respective metrics, with the best results achieved in combination.
5. **Correction behavior analysis**: SC-Captioner performs more insertions than deletions relative to SFT, indicating that the model learns to effectively add missing content.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **First application of self-correction training to image captioning**: Prior work on self-correction was largely confined to mathematics and code generation.
2. **Elegant reward function design**: Element-level rewards based on scene graphs directly reflect the quality of corrections, proving more effective than using holistic metrics as reward signals.
3. **Practical metric improvements**: The three key deficiencies of CAPTURE are addressed, particularly global attribute matching and simplistic tuple-based relation matching.
4. **RefinedCaps dataset**: 6.5K high-quality, manually refined captions, where SFT alone already yields substantial gains.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. The reward function depends on the quality of the FACTUAL scene graph parser; parsing errors propagate directly into training signals.
2. When ground-truth captions are incomplete, mistake punishment may penalize correct additions not mentioned in the ground truth.
3. Experiments are conducted only on 7B models; generalization to larger or smaller models remains unverified.
4. Relation evaluation relies on the QA paradigm, requiring GPT-4o for test set construction and an LLM for answer generation.

## Related Work & Insights

- **SCoRe**: A multi-turn RL self-correction framework for mathematical reasoning; this paper extends its principles to the vision-language domain.
- **CAPTURE**: A scene-graph-based caption evaluation framework; this paper addresses three of its key deficiencies.
- **DPO**: Serves as a comparative baseline, demonstrating that preference optimization alone is insufficient for learning effective self-correction strategies.

## Rating

⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The problem formulation is novel, the reward function design is well-motivated, and the experiments are thorough. However, the dependence on scene graph parsing quality and the handling of incomplete ground truth leave room for further improvement.
