# Forecasting in Offline Reinforcement Learning for Non-stationary Environments

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2512.01987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01987)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning
**Keywords**: Offline Reinforcement Learning, Non-stationary Environments, Diffusion Models, Time Series Forecasting, State Estimation

## TL;DR
This paper proposes Forl, a framework that fuses multimodal candidate states generated by a conditional diffusion model with shift predictions from a zero-shot time-series foundation model via Dimension-wise Closest Matching (DCM). Forl enables deployment-time adaptation to non-stationary observation functions that shift episodically, without retraining, achieving substantial average score improvements on D4RL benchmarks.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Offline RL trains policies on pre-collected static datasets, avoiding the cost and risk of online interaction. Existing robust offline RL methods primarily address test-time perturbations such as sensor noise or adversarial disturbances.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: A common form of non-stationarity in real-world deployment is **an unknown, episode-constant offset added to the observation function**—e.g., daily calibration drift in industrial robots, sensor drift, or systematic biases in data aggregation. Such offsets violate the smooth/Gaussian noise assumptions of standard methods and evolve across episodes in non-Markovian temporal patterns.

**Key Challenge**: During training, the agent operates in a fully observable stationary MDP; at test time, it faces a sequence of POMDPs where each episode's observation $o_t = s_t + b^j$ contains an unknown and inaccessible offset $b^j$. A single observation cannot disentangle the true state $s_t$ from the offset $b^j$, as infinitely many pairs $(s', b')$ satisfy $o_t = s' + b'$.

**Goal**: (a) How can models be trained without knowledge of future non-stationary patterns? (b) How can within-episode action-effect history and cross-episode offset predictions be fused to estimate the true state? (c) Can zero-shot adaptation be achieved without retraining or online exploration?

**Key Insight**: Two complementary information sources are leveraged: (i) within-episode $(\Delta o, a)$ sequences encode positional constraints (e.g., hitting a wall implies the agent is likely a few steps south of it) → captured via a conditional diffusion model for multimodal belief representation; (ii) temporal patterns in historical offset values → predicted by a zero-shot time-series foundation model.

**Core Idea**: A conditional diffusion model performs *retrospective* multimodal state estimation, while a time-series foundation model performs *prospective* offset prediction; DCM fuses the two to yield an optimal state estimate.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
During training, a conditional diffusion model (Forl-Dm) and a standard offline RL policy (e.g., Diffusion-QL) are trained on stationary offline data. During testing, a zero-shot time-series model predicts offsets $\hat{b}^j$ for the next $P$ episodes every $P$ episodes. Within each episode, once a trajectory buffer has accumulated $(\Delta o, a)$ sequences, the diffusion model generates candidate states, which are fused with the predicted offsets via DCM to produce the final state estimate that is fed into the pre-trained policy for action selection.

### Key Designs

1. **Forl Conditional Diffusion Model**:

    - **Function**: Generates multimodal candidate true states from within-episode action-effect history.
    - **Mechanism**: Defines trajectory subsequences $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{(t,w)} = [(\Delta o_{t-w+1}, a_{t-w}), \cdots, (\Delta o_t, a_{t-1})]$ and trains a noise prediction network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\boldsymbol{s}_t^{(n)}, \boldsymbol{\tau}_{(t,w)}, n)$ with loss:
      $$\mathcal{L}_p(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{n, \boldsymbol{\tau}, \boldsymbol{s_t}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\boldsymbol{s}^{(n)}, \boldsymbol{\tau}_{(t,w)}, n)\|^2\right]$$
    - The forward diffusion process adds noise to the true state $s_t$: $s_t^{(n)} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}(n)} s_t + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}(n)} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$
    - At inference, $k$ candidate states $\{s_t^{(0)}\}_k$ are generated in parallel.
    - **Design Motivation**: The distribution of possible states under an unknown offset is highly multimodal—e.g., after hitting a wall in a maze, the agent could be at one of four distinct locations. Diffusion models are naturally suited to capturing such multimodal distributions.
    - **Key Insight**: The model is trained exclusively on unshifted stationary data, yet $\Delta o_t = s_t - s_{t-1}$ is invariant to constant offsets, so the conditioning variable $\tau$ remains valid under shifted observations—providing the theoretical foundation for training-free adaptation.

2. **Zero-Shot Time-Series Foundation Model Prediction**:

    - **Function**: Predicts offsets for the next $P$ episodes from the history of observed offsets.
    - **Mechanism**: A probabilistic zero-shot time-series model (e.g., Lag-Llama) takes the past $C$ offset values $(b^{j-C}, \dots, b^{j-1})$ as context and generates $l$ offset samples. Each dimension is predicted independently, as Lag-Llama is a univariate model.
    - **Design Motivation**: Offsets may follow arbitrary real-world temporal patterns (possibly non-Markovian); zero-shot models require no training on specific patterns.

3. **Dimension-wise Closest Match (DCM)**:

    - **Function**: Fuses the multimodal diffusion candidates with the unimodal time-series predictions.
    - **Mechanism**: Given $\mathcal{D}_{\text{diffusion}} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_k\}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\text{timeseries}} = \{\mathbf{y}_1, \dots, \mathbf{y}_l\}$, the estimate is constructed as $z_d = y_{j^*(d), d}$, where $j^*(d) = \arg\min_j (\min_i |x_{i,d} - y_{j,d}|)$—for each dimension, the time-series sample that has the closest match among diffusion candidates is selected.
    - **Design Motivation**: Using diffusion samples directly risks selecting an incorrect mode; using time-series predictions directly may yield high bias. DCM uses the constraints provided by the diffusion model to filter the most credible time-series estimate—non-parametric, lightweight, and hyperparameter-free.

4. **Within-Episode Adaptive Procedure**:

    - First $w$ steps (warm-up window): Only the time-series prediction mean is used: $\tilde{s}_t = o_t - \overline{\hat{b}^j}$.
    - After $w$ steps: The diffusion model has sufficient context to generate meaningful candidates, and DCM fusion is activated.
    - **Design Motivation**: The diffusion model requires a minimum history length to effectively constrain candidates; the prediction model's prior is used in the early phase.

## Key Experimental Results

|------|-----|-------------------|----------|----------|

| maze2d-large (avg) | 3.5 | 7.1 | 12.1 | **38.8** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | antmaze-umaze Avg. Normalized Score | Note |
|------|--------------------------|------|
| Forl (full) | **75.1** | Diffusion model + time-series prediction + DCM |
| DQL + Lag-$\bar{s}$ (time-series mean only) | 44.9 | No retrospective diffusion correction |
| Dmbp + Lag (diffusion + backpropagation optimization) | 51.4 | Non-DCM fusion strategy |
| DQL (no adaptation) | 16.1 | Fully degraded by offset |
| Forl-F (FQL policy replacing DQL) | 45.7 (cube-single) | Policy-agnostic: compatible with different offline RL policies |

### Key Findings
- **Forl consistently outperforms all baselines across all environments and all real-world time-series datasets**, with particularly large gains in maze2d and antmaze settings (average +30–60 points).
- **The multimodal capacity of the diffusion model is central**: time-series prediction alone (DQL+Lag) corrects only the "expected offset" and fails in high-uncertainty scenarios.
- **DCM fusion outperforms backpropagation-based optimization** (Dmbp+Lag), as DCM is non-parametric and does not overfit to inaccurate predictions.
- Forl is **policy-agnostic** and can be used as a plug-in module with any offline RL policy.
- Improvements are limited in high-dimensional environments such as kitchen—likely because diffusion model landmark constraints weaken in higher dimensions.

## Highlights & Insights
- **The insight that $\Delta o$ is invariant to the offset $b^j$** is the theoretical cornerstone of the entire method—constant offsets cancel in state differences, allowing the diffusion model trained on unshifted data to generalize to shifted deployment scenarios elegantly.
- **The simplicity of DCM**: dimension-wise nearest-neighbor matching achieves effective fusion of multimodal and unimodal distributions, approximating a product distribution under ideal sampling conditions.
- **Dual-channel "retrospective + prospective" design**: within-episode action-effect history supports retrospective inference (constraining possible positions), while cross-episode offset sequences support prospective prediction (anticipating future offsets)—the two channels are complementary.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Strong offset assumptions**: Only episode-constant additive offsets are considered; multiplicative, time-varying, or dynamics-level non-stationarities are not addressed.
- **Bottleneck of time-series prediction accuracy**: Inaccurate predictions from the zero-shot foundation model (Lag-Llama) on complex temporal patterns degrade overall performance (e.g., real-data-E).
- **Scalability to high-dimensional state spaces**: Limited improvements on kitchen and cube environments suggest that the diffusion model requires more samples or stronger architectures in high dimensions.
- **Assumption on ground-truth offset availability**: The method assumes that true past offsets can be obtained every $P$ episodes for time-series prediction, which may be unrealistic in some settings.
- **Validation limited to D4RL benchmarks**; end-to-end experiments on real robots or industrial systems are absent.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Standard Robust Offline RL**: Prior methods typically assume bounded random noise or adversarial perturbations, whereas this paper addresses episode-level constant offsets with non-Markovian cross-episode evolution—a fundamentally different non-stationarity regime.
- **vs. Diffuser/Decision Diffuser**: These works use diffusion models for planning or policy generation; Forl uses diffusion models for state estimation and belief updating—a distinct application of the same tool.
- **vs. Online POMDP Methods**: POMDP approaches typically require partial observability to be incorporated at training time, whereas Forl trains under an MDP and deploys under a POMDP—achieving zero-shot transfer.
- The framework has practical relevance for any RL deployment scenario involving sensor drift or calibration bias.

## Rating
- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Novel problem formulation (episode-level non-stationary observations); the combination of conditional diffusion, time-series prediction, and DCM is elegant.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Five real-world time-series datasets × eight D4RL environments with thorough ablations; real robot validation is lacking.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Problem motivation and method description are clear; figures are intuitive (especially the maze example in Fig. 3).
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Addresses a gap in non-stationary observation adaptation for offline RL deployment; the modular framework is readily extensible.
