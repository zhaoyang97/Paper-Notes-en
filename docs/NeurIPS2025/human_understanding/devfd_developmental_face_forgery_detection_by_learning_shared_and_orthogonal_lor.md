# DevFD: Developmental Face Forgery Detection by Learning Shared and Orthogonal LoRA Subspaces

**Conference**: NEURIPS2025
**arXiv**: [2509.19230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19230)
**Code**: To be confirmed
**Area**: Human Understanding
**Keywords**: face forgery detection, continual learning, mixture of experts, LoRA, orthogonal subspace

## TL;DR
This paper proposes DevFD—a developmental MoE architecture that models the common characteristics of real faces via a shared Real-LoRA, incrementally captures new forgery types via a sequence of orthogonal Fake-LoRAs, and mitigates catastrophic forgetting by integrating orthogonal gradient constraints into an orthogonal loss. DevFD achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and the lowest forgetting rate in continual learning for face forgery detection.

## Background & Motivation
- Generative models such as GANs and diffusion models evolve rapidly, continuously introducing new face forgery techniques that existing detectors struggle to generalize to.
- Naively retraining on an expanded dataset that includes new forgery data incurs high computational cost and causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned forgery types.
- **Key observation**: Real face data is abundant and captured through a uniform pipeline (camera imaging), resulting in a compact cross-dataset distribution; in contrast, forged faces generated by different techniques form multiple discrete clusters (verified via t-SNE visualization).
- This asymmetry motivates separate modeling: a single shared expert learns the commonalities of real faces, while an expandable sequence of orthogonal experts incrementally captures the distinctive features of different forgery types.

## Core Problem
1. **Catastrophic forgetting under continual learning**: How to retain previously acquired detection capabilities when learning new forgery types?
2. **Distributional asymmetry between real and forged faces**: How to exploit the compact distribution of real faces and the dispersed distribution of forged faces for a more efficient architecture?
3. **Incompleteness of orthogonal subspace constraints**: In existing orthogonal LoRA methods, gradients can still interfere with established subspaces during early training when orthogonality conditions are not yet satisfied.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
- The pretrained ViT backbone is frozen; LoRA modules are introduced into the FFN layers of each Transformer block as MoE experts.
- The architecture grows with tasks (developmental): each new forgery type triggers the expansion of a new LoRA branch.

### 1. Developmental MoE Architecture
- **Real-LoRA** ($\mathbf{A}_0\mathbf{B}_0$): A shared expert that remains trainable across all tasks, dedicated to learning and refining general knowledge of real faces.
- **Fake-LoRA sequence** ($\{\mathbf{A}_j\mathbf{B}_j\}_{j=1}^{t}$): An orthogonal expert sequence where a new branch is added per task and previous branches are frozen.
- Output formulation: $\mathbf{e} = \text{FFN}(\mathbf{x}) + \sum_{j=0}^{t} \text{LLB}(\mathbf{A}_j\mathbf{B}_j\mathbf{x})$

### 2. Integrated Orthogonal Loss
- **Problem analysis**: Constraining only subspace orthogonality ($\|\mathbf{B}_t^T\mathbf{B}_i\|^2$) is insufficient—when orthogonality conditions are not yet met in early training, new-task gradients can still corrupt knowledge in old subspaces.
- **Orthogonal gradient constraint**: Exploiting the property that gradient updates of a linear layer lie in the span of its input vectors, SVD is applied to the input matrix $\mathbf{H}_t$, and the top-$r$ right singular vectors $(\mathbf{V}_t^T)_r$ are used as an estimate of the gradient subspace.
- **Integrated loss**: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ort}} = \frac{1}{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(\lambda_1\sum\|\mathbf{O}_{i,t}\|^2 + \lambda_2\sum\|\mathbf{G}_{i,t}\|^2)$
  - The first term enforces subspace orthogonality; the second enforces gradient-space orthogonality, providing dual protection against forgetting.

### 3. Label-Guided Localized Balancing (LLB)
- A response matrix $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{(t+1)\times n}$ is constructed to measure each expert's response intensity for each sample.
- A balancing coefficient matrix $\mathbf{C}$ is constructed based on labels: when an expert type matches the sample label, the coefficient is $1-\delta$ (encouraging stronger response); otherwise it is $1+\delta$ (suppressing response).
- The LLB loss constrains the dispersion of the weighted response matrix: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{llb}} = \sigma^2(\mathbf{I}\circ\mathbf{C}) / \mu(\mathbf{I}\circ\mathbf{C})$
- At inference, all LoRA experts contribute jointly to the final decision without hard routing.

### Total Loss
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{ort}} + \lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{\text{llb}}$$

## Key Experimental Results

### Dataset-Incremental Protocol (FF++ → DFDC-P → DFD → CDF2)

| Method | Final Avg Acc (%) | Final AF (%) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| LwF | 76.68 | 18.21 |
| DFIL | 85.49 | 7.01 |
| DMP | 89.99 | 4.08 |
| **DevFD** | **89.82** | **4.03** |

### Forgery-Type-Incremental Protocol (Hybrid → FR → FS → EFS)

| Method | Final Avg AUC (%) | Final AF (%) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| DFIL | 79.29 | 26.01 |
| HDP | 78.26 | 22.65 |
| SUR-LID | 94.33 | 2.99 |
| **DevFD** | **94.90** | **2.44** |

### Ablation Study
- Using $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}$ alone: final AF = 23.93%; adding orthogonal subspace constraint reduces it to 10.06%; further adding orthogonal gradient constraint reduces it to **7.91%**.
- Introducing Real-LoRA simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces forgetting; replacing Real-LoRA with an orthogonal sequence yields no significant gain, validating the rationale of using a single shared expert for real faces.
- In the Task10 long-sequence experiment (DF40 dataset, 10 tasks), DevFD maintains the highest average AUC and the lowest forgetting rate, while DFIL and HDP exhibit unstable catastrophic forgetting.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Insight into real/fake distributional asymmetry**: The design of "shared Real-LoRA + orthogonal Fake-LoRA" is substantiated by both t-SNE visualization and ablation experiments on orthogonal gradient loss.
- **Integrated orthogonal gradient constraint**: The paper theoretically analyzes the insufficiency of subspace orthogonality alone and proposes a complementary constraint from the gradient-space perspective, effectively mitigating interference during early training.
- **Strict data isolation**: DevFD achieves competitive or superior performance over replay-based methods (DFIL, SUR-LID) without any replay mechanism, demonstrating the strength of the architectural design itself.
- **Label-guided soft routing**: The balancing coefficient matrix implements "encourage matching, suppress mismatching" while preserving joint inference across all experts.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The number of LoRA branches grows linearly with the number of tasks; parameter count and inference overhead warrant attention in extremely long task sequences.
- The selection of LoRA rank $r$ relies on grid search; adaptive rank allocation across different tasks remains to be explored.
- The orthogonal subspace dimension is limited ($r \ll \min(d_O, d_I)$), theoretically bounding the number of tasks by the feature space dimensionality.
- Experiments focus on face forgery detection; the generalizability of the method to broader continual learning scenarios (e.g., class-incremental learning) has yet to be verified.
- The label-guided strategy cannot leverage label information at inference time; response aggregation alone may underperform on boundary samples.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. O-LoRA / InfLoRA**: Both employ orthogonal LoRA, but DevFD additionally introduces gradient-space orthogonal constraints and differentiates Real/Fake LoRA.
- **vs. DFIL / SUR-LID**: These methods rely on replay mechanisms, yet DevFD achieves superior or comparable performance under strict data isolation.
- **vs. MoEFFD**: MoEFFD applies MoE+LoRA to forgery detection but not in a continual learning setting; DevFD is specifically designed for incrementally arriving forgery types.
- **vs. DMP**: The two methods perform comparably under the dataset-incremental protocol (89.99 vs. 89.82), but DevFD shows a more pronounced advantage under the forgery-type-incremental protocol.

## Related Work & Insights (Broader)
- **Generalizability of developmental architectures**: The design of Real-LoRA + orthogonal Fake-LoRA can be generalized to other detection scenarios characterized by stable normal samples and diverse anomalous samples (e.g., anomaly detection, network intrusion detection).
- **Inspiration from gradient-space constraints**: Using input SVD as an estimate of the gradient subspace is a theoretically insightful idea that can be incorporated into other continual learning methods to mitigate forgetting.
- **Compatibility with adapter tuning**: The framework is naturally compatible with various parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods; replacing LoRA with Adapters or Prefix Tuning variants is a promising direction.
