# Teaching Language Models to Evolve with Users: Dynamic Profile Modeling for Personalized Alignment

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2505.15456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15456)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/XingYuSSS/RLPA)  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning
**Keywords**: Personalized Alignment, User Profile Modeling, Reinforcement Learning, Multi-turn Dialogue, Cold Start

## TL;DR

This paper formulates personalized dialogue alignment as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process and proposes the RLPA framework, enabling LLMs to dynamically infer and maintain user profiles through online interaction with simulated users, and to generate personalized responses accordingly.

## Background & Motivation

Alignment techniques for large language models (e.g., RLHF) have enabled models to serve population-level preferences well, yet real-world users differ substantially in their needs, goals, and communication styles. Existing personalization approaches suffer from two fundamental limitations:

**Prompt-based methods** (profile/RAG injection) are straightforward to implement but achieve only shallow personalization — template-based information injection cannot flexibly represent user preferences, and is further constrained by context window limits that preclude long-term memory. **Offline optimization methods** (SFT/DPO) depend on large-scale annotated data, making them infeasible in cold-start scenarios, and their static training prevents real-time adaptation to users' evolving needs during interaction.

The root cause lies in the fact that personalization is inherently a **dynamic and evolving** process — user profiles must be built incrementally from scratch (cold start) and continuously updated over time (long-term maintenance), yet all existing methods are static in nature. The paper's starting point is to model personalized alignment as a **multi-turn MDP**, using reinforcement learning to train the model to infer, maintain, and leverage user profiles through online interaction.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

RLPA (Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Alignment) formalizes personalized dialogue as a five-tuple MDP $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma)$:

- **State** $s_t = \{u_1, r_1, \dots, u_t\}$: current dialogue history
- **Action** $a_t$: the response $r_t$ generated by the model at turn $t$
- **Transition** $\mathcal{T}$: a simulated user generates the next message $u_{t+1}$ based on dialogue history
- **Reward** $R_t$: a two-level reward composed of Profile Reward + Response Reward

The objective is to learn a dialogue policy $\pi(a_t|s_t)$ that maximizes:

$$J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} \gamma^{t-1} R_t\right]$$

### Key Designs

1. **Simulated User Model**: An LLM (GPT-4o-mini) serves as the simulated user, whose behavior is conditioned on a predefined user profile $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n\}$ (e.g., preferences, personality, goals). A critical design choice is **progressive disclosure** — the simulated user is instructed to reveal profile information gradually across turns rather than all at once. This forces the dialogue model to accumulate and refine its understanding of the user profile through multi-turn reasoning, and is motivated by the goal of simulating realistic cold-start scenarios.

2. **Profile Reward**: Guides the model to accurately infer the user profile. The user profile adopts a slot-value structured format. After each dialogue turn, the model outputs its current profile estimate $\hat{\mathcal{P}}_t$, which is matched against the ground-truth profile $\mathcal{P}$ at the slot level:

$$R_t^{\text{profile}} = \frac{2 \cdot |\hat{\mathcal{P}}_t \cap \mathcal{P}|}{|\hat{\mathcal{P}}_t| + |\mathcal{P}|}$$

This is essentially an F1-score that penalizes both missed and spurious predictions, encouraging the model to progressively infer a complete profile.

3. **Response Reward**: Ensures that generated responses faithfully reflect the inferred profile. An external reward model (GPT-4o-mini) scores responses along four dimensions (preference expression, style consistency, goal alignment, personality coherence), combined with five binary quality criteria:

$$R_t^{\text{response}} = N \cdot R \cdot L \cdot G \cdot F$$

where N = naturalness, R = relevance, L = logical consistency, G = engagement, F = informativeness. The multiplicative form ensures that only responses meeting all criteria receive a positive reward.

### Loss & Training

The total reward is the sum of the two-level rewards: $R_t = R_t^{\text{profile}} + R_t^{\text{response}}$. The policy is optimized using the PPO algorithm. Training is conducted on 8×A100 80GB GPUs using the OpenRLHF + vLLM framework with Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct as the base model, yielding Qwen-RLPA.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

Evaluated on the ALOE benchmark under two settings (Vanilla: same-format generalization / Extended: cross-format generalization):

| Model | Method | Vanilla AVG↑ | Vanilla N-R²↑ | Extended AVG↑ | Extended N-R²↑ |
|------|------|-------------|---------------|--------------|----------------|
| GPT-4o | Self-Critic | 74.59 | 0.380 | 54.66 | 0.070 |
| Claude-3.5-Sonnet | Self-Critic | 69.19 | 0.792 | 35.62 | 0.266 |
| Qwen-2.5-3B | SFT | 44.32 | 0.628 | 24.38 | 0.159 |
| Qwen-2.5-3B | DPO | 45.27 | 0.389 | 27.26 | 0.037 |
| **Qwen-2.5-3B** | **RLPA** | **73.38** | **0.855** | **52.74** | **0.498** |

After RLPA training, the 3B model surpasses GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet in alignment scores, and achieves substantially higher N-R² than all baselines (0.855 vs. 0.380/0.500), demonstrating stronger response–profile consistency.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | AVG↑ | N-IR↑ | N-R²↑ | Note |
|------|------|-------|-------|------|
| RLPA (full) | 73.38 | 0.090 | 0.855 | Optimal with both rewards |
| Profile Reward only | 45.54 | 0.015 | 0.019 | Profile inferred but not reflected in responses |
| Response Reward only | 66.19 | 0.088 | 0.524 | Responses personalized but profile unstable |

### Key Findings

- **Long-term stability**: Over 70-turn conversations, the profile inference score rises steadily from 9.26 at turn 1 to 52.54 at turn 50, approaching the theoretical upper bound, demonstrating RLPA's capability for long-term profile maintenance.
- **Preference conflict handling**: When a preference shift is introduced at turn 6, RLPA recovers quickly after a brief dip (82.43 at k=9), whereas DPO drops sharply from 71.62 to 25.68 and fails to recover.
- **Reasoning efficiency**: Compared to reasoning-oriented LLMs such as DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ-32B, Qwen-RLPA achieves higher alignment scores with fewer reasoning tokens per turn (QwQ-32B uses 300+ tokens per turn yet performs worse).

## Highlights & Insights

- The paper reframes **personalized alignment** as a dynamic MDP rather than a static fitting problem, capturing the essential characteristic of personalization — its **evolutionary nature**.
- The two-level reward design is elegant: Profile Reward provides explicit profile supervision, while Response Reward provides implicit behavioral supervision; the two are complementary and mutually indispensable.
- A 3B model trained with RLPA can match or surpass much larger models such as GPT-4o, demonstrating that the **right training paradigm** matters more than model scale.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The current work assumes single-user, single-session interaction, without considering multi-user, multi-session, or cross-domain scenarios.
- A potential sim-to-real gap may exist between the behavior distributions of simulated and real users.
- The theoretical convergence properties of long-term alignment have not been thoroughly characterized.
- Reliance on GPT-4o-mini as the reward model raises concerns regarding cost and controllability.

## Related Work & Insights

- The work is related to persona-based dialogue but goes further: rather than generating responses given a profile, RLPA infers the profile from the dialogue itself.
- The RLPA framework is transferable to other settings requiring learning user preferences from interaction, such as recommender systems and educational AI.
- The two-level reward design (process reward + outcome reward) shares conceptual parallels with PRM/ORM designs in mathematical reasoning RL.
