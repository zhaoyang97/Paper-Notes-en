# OmniStyle: Filtering High Quality Style Transfer Data at Scale

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.14028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14028)  
**Code**: [https://wangyephd.github.io/projects/cvpr25_omnistyle.html](https://wangyephd.github.io/projects/cvpr25_omnistyle.html) (Project Page)  
**Area**: Image Generation  
**Keywords**: Style Transfer, Large-scale Dataset, Quality Filtering, Diffusion Transformer, End-to-End Training

## TL;DR

Built the first million-scale paired style transfer dataset OmniStyle-1M (1 million content-style-stylized triplets, 1000 styles), designed the OmniFilter multidimensional quality filtering framework to screen high-quality data, and trained an end-to-end style transfer model OmniStyle based on the DiT architecture. It supports both instruction-guided and reference-guided style transfer, comprehensively outperforming existing methods.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Style transfer has evolved from optimization methods (Neural Style Transfer) to arbitrary style transfer (AdaIN, etc.) and then to diffusion model methods. Recent diffusion model methods are divided into tuning-based (requiring fine-tuning for each style) and tuning-free (achieving style transfer via attention injection), but both rely on iterative inference, resulting in slow speeds.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Existing methods lack generalization ability and can only handle limited style categories; (2) Most of them adopt non-end-to-end architectures (optimization-based or inversion-based), requiring hundreds or even thousands of iterations, which is extremely costly computationally; (3) There is a lack of large-scale high-quality paired datasets; for example, IMAGStyle only contains 14 style categories and relies on a single model to generate data; (4) Training is unsupervised, lacking precise control over the stylized output.

**Key Challenge**: High-quality end-to-end style transfer requires large-scale paired data for training, but paired data is extremely expensive to obtain; meanwhile, existing automatically generated data is of mixed quality, lacking effective quality-control means.

**Goal**: (1) Construct a sufficiently large, highly diverse, and quality-controlled paired style transfer dataset; (2) Train a fast and efficient end-to-end style transfer model based on this dataset.

**Key Insight**: Since the generation quality from a single style transfer model is unstable, six SOTA models are used collectively to generate candidate results, and then a multidimensional automated evaluation system is utilized to filter out the best results. Supervised training replaces unsupervised style alignment.

**Core Idea**: Construct high-quality paired data at scale using a "multi-model generation + intelligent filtering" paradigm, making end-to-end supervised training possible and fundamentally solving the efficiency and quality issues of style transfer.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The method consists of three core components: (1) OmniStyle-1M dataset construction—generating 2000 content images using FLUX, selecting 1000 style images from Style30K, and generating million-level triplets using 6 SOTA style transfer models; (2) OmniFilter quality filtering framework—evaluating and filtering high-quality triplets from three dimensions: content preservation, style consistency, and aesthetic appeal; (3) OmniStyle model—an end-to-end style transfer framework based on the FLUX-dev DiT architecture, supporting both instruction-guided and reference-guided modes.

### Key Designs

1. **OmniStyle-1M Dataset Construction Pipeline**:

    - **Function**: Provide a large-scale, highly diverse paired dataset for style transfer training.
    - **Mechanism**: For content images, 2000 prompts across 20 categories are generated by ChatGPT, and then copyright-free images are generated using FLUX. For style images, 1000 fine-grained styles are selected from Style30K. For each content image, 100 styles are randomly selected, and stylized results are generated by six models: StyleID, ArtFlow, StyleShot, AesPANet, CSGO, and CAST, ultimately producing over 1 million triplets. Text descriptions and instruction-based prompts are attached to each triplet.
    - **Design Motivation**: Multi-model generation avoids the bias and limitations of a single model. The 1000 styles cover an extremely rich range of genres such as comics, ink wash, oil painting, vector illustration, etc., while the 20 content categories ensure a balanced distribution.

2. **OmniFilter Multidimensional Quality Filtering Framework**:

    - **Function**: Automatically evaluate stylized image quality across three dimensions to filter the best results.
    - **Mechanism**: **Content preservation evaluation**—combines CLIP image-text similarity (semantic consistency $S_{semantic}$) and DINOv2 embedding similarity (structural integrity $S_{structural}$), weighted as $C_{score} = 0.5 \cdot S_{semantic} + 0.5 \cdot S_{structural}$. **Style consistency evaluation**—fine-tunes the CLIP image encoder via contrastive learning on Style30K data so that images of the same style cluster in the feature space, and then calculates the style consistency score $S_{score}$. **Aesthetic evaluation**—defines 40 visual attributes (composition, color harmony, lighting, etc.), generates attribute descriptions using InternVL2, and trains an aesthetic scoring model $A_{score}$ on the AVA + BAID datasets. The final composite score is $Score = 0.2 \cdot C + 0.6 \cdot S + 0.2 \cdot A$, where style has the highest weight. For each content-style pair, the highest-scoring output among the 6 models is selected, ultimately filtering out OmniStyle-150K.
    - **Design Motivation**: Existing evaluation methods focus only on partial dimensions (e.g., style loss only considers style). The three-dimensional joint evaluation of OmniFilter ensures that content is preserved, styles are aligned, and aesthetic quality is guaranteed. Style consistency is given the highest weight (0.6) because the core goal of style transfer is style alignment.

3. **OmniStyle Model Architecture**:

    - **Function**: Efficient end-to-end style transfer based on DiT.
    - **Mechanism**: Based on the FLUX-dev model, only the Diffusion Transformer part is fine-tuned while keeping other components frozen. **Reference-guided mode**: VAE is used to extract visual features of the style and content images, which are concatenated in space with the noise latent and text tokens before being fed into MM-DiT. Style and content features use different positional encodings. **Instruction-guided mode**: Content features are treated as text tokens, distinguished using `[img]/[/img]` tags. Reference-guide mode uses `[img1]/[img2]` tags to distinguish style and content images.
    - **Design Motivation**: Fine-tuning based on a powerful DiT foundation model like FLUX-dev inherits its strong generative capabilities and achieves end-to-end style transfer via large-scale paired data, eliminating the need for iterative optimization.

### Loss & Training

Trained on 8×NVIDIA H100, batch size 1/GPU, learning rate 1e-4. Random cropping and horizontal flipping are applied to the input style images to enhance the robustness of style learning. Only the DiT part is fine-tuned, while the VAE and text encoders are frozen. Training data is from the OmniStyle-150K high-quality subset filtered by OmniFilter.

### Key Findings

- In the **instruction-guided task**, OmniStyle dominates by a landslide: style consistency reaches 0.6441, significantly outperforming the runner-up OmniGen's 0.5487, and its 86.9% first-choice rate in user studies far exceeds all competitors.
- In the **reference-guided task**, the advantage is similarly obvious: the Style Loss of 0.1086 is the lowest among all methods, and the user first-choice rate of 41.22% exceeds the runner-up StyleShot's 18.63%.
- OmniStyle achieves balanced high performance across all four evaluation dimensions, unlike CAST (high content preservation but poor style consistency) or CSGO (high style consistency but high style loss), which suffer from uneven performance.
- Dataset quality is key: OmniFilter's three-dimensional filtering ensures the high quality of the training data. Compared to directly using all data, training on the filtered 150K subset yields better results.

## Highlights & Insights

- **The "multi-model generation + intelligent filtering" data construction paradigm**: This approach is not limited to style transfer and can be generalized to any generative task requiring high-quality paired data. Leveraging the collective intelligence of multiple weak models to filter high-quality training data is a highly practical engineering methodology.
- **OmniFilter's three-dimensional evaluation system**: Decoupling and evaluating content preservation, style consistency, and aesthetic appeal is more comprehensive than a single style loss or FID. In particular, fine-tuning CLIP via contrastive learning to evaluate style consistency and using MLLM visual attribute descriptions to evaluate aesthetics are highly creative solutions.
- **End-to-end replacing iteration**: Through supervised training on large-scale paired data, style transfer is transformed from an iterative optimization problem into a feedforward inference problem, yielding massive speedups.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Dataset construction relies on the generation quality of 6 existing style transfer models; if all models fail on certain extreme styles, high-quality training data cannot be obtained.
- The evaluation criteria of OmniFilter (especially the style consistency weight of 0.6) are set manually and might not be applicable to all style types.
- The model is fine-tuned based on FLUX-dev, which has a large parameter size and high inference costs (requiring 8×H100 training); real-world deployment may require further distillation.
- Content images are generated by FLUX rather than being real photos, which may introduce domain bias.
- Future work could consider collaborating with professional artists to annotate "perfectly paired" datasets, providing more objective evaluation baselines.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs IMAGStyle**: IMAGStyle has only 14 styles and relies on a single model (B-LoRA) to generate 210,000 samples. OmniStyle-1M has 1000 styles, utilizes 6 models, and contains 1 million triplets, placing its diversity and scale in an entirely different league.
- **vs StyleShot/CSGO**: These methods are strong competitors in the reference-guided task, but they are non-end-to-end. OmniStyle is superior in both style consistency and style loss.
- **vs DiffStyler**: DiffStyler only supports text-guided style transfer and has limited style understanding, failing to handle fine-grained styles (e.g., "American comic style"). OmniStyle supports both text and image guidance.
