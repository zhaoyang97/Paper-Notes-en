# Synthetic Face Datasets Generation via Latent Space Exploration from Brownian Identity Diffusion

**Conference**: ICML 2025  
**arXiv**: [2405.00228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00228)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Image Generation  
**Keywords**: Synthetic Face Data, Latent Space Exploration, Brownian Motion, Identity Diffusion, Face Recognition

## TL;DR
Inspired by the Brownian motion of soft particles in physics, this paper proposes an identity sampling method driven by stochastic forces in the latent space (Langevin, Dispersion, and DisCo algorithms) to generate large-scale, diverse synthetic face datasets for training face recognition models while preventing training data leakage.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Face recognition models require large-scale real data for training, but face severe privacy and ethical concerns. Replacing real data with synthetic data has become a trend, but whether generative models can produce sufficiently diverse data remains uncertain.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Face datasets generated by GANs and diffusion models suffer from insufficient identity diversity—different "identities" might be too similar, or variations within a single identity might be insufficient. More critically, diffusion models have been proven to memorize training data, leading to privacy leakage in synthetic data.

**Key Challenge**: Diversity vs. Controllability—needing to sample sufficiently diverse identities in the latent space while ensuring each identity is a distinguishable real face in the sense of face recognition.

**Goal**: Design principled latent space exploration methods to generate large-scale, highly diverse, and data-leakage-free synthetic face datasets under constraints.

**Key Insight**: Analogizing identity sampling to constrained Brownian motion—identity points behave like particles moving in the latent space, where repulsive forces ensure diversity.

**Core Idea**: Use stochastic Brownian forces to push "identity particles" across a wider range in the latent space, while using repulsive forces to prevent identity collapse.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

- **Input**: Latent space of a pretrained diffusion/GAN model
- **Process**: Obtain diverse identity embedding points in the latent space using physics-inspired sampling algorithms
- **Output**: Each identity embedding is decoded into a set of face images, forming a complete synthetic face dataset

### Key Designs

1. **Langevin Algorithm**:

    - Identity points in the latent space follow Langevin dynamics: $\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = \mathbf{z}_t + \eta \nabla_{\mathbf{z}} E(\mathbf{z}_t) + \sqrt{2\eta} \xi_t$
    - The energy function $E$ includes repulsive potentials between identities (ensuring diversity) and latent space constraints (ensuring validity)
    - **Design Motivation**: Simulate the equilibrium distribution of soft particles in potential fields

2. **Dispersion Algorithm**:

    - After initializing a set of identity points, gradually "diffuse"/disperse them to larger regions of the latent space
    - Use nearest-neighbor repulsive forces to ensure a uniform distribution
    - Constrain each point to remain within the valid output range of the generative model
    - **Design Motivation**: Cover high-dimensional latent space more efficiently

3. **DisCo (Dispersion + Constraints) Algorithm**:

    - An enhanced version of Dispersion with explicit identity constraints
    - Verify using a pretrained face recognition model during sampling: whether the newly sampled identity is indeed a distinguishable new face
    - Rejection sampling ensures no identity overlaps
    - **Design Motivation**: Directly incorporate downstream task requirements during the diffusion process

### Loss & Training

- No training required, standard sampling + verification pipeline
- Energy function includes a repulsive term + latent space boundary constraints
- Verify identity distinguishability using a face recognition model after generation
- Privacy protection: Leakage detection ensures synthetic images do not match real individuals in the training set

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Dataset | Training Data | LFW↑ | AgeDB↑ | CFP-FP↑ |
|--------|---------|------|--------|---------|
| Real Data (CASIA) | 0.5M | Baseline | Baseline | Baseline |
| GAN Synthetic Data | 0.5M | Lower | Lower | Lower |
| Prev. Diffusion Synthetic | 0.5M | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Ours (DisCo)** | **0.5M** | **Higher** | **Competitive** | **Competitive** |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Key Metric | Description |
|------|---------|------|
| Random Sampling (No Brownian Force) | Low Diversity | Identity Clustering |
| Langevin only | Medium | Effective but insufficient dispersion |
| Dispersion only | Better | Good dispersion effect |
| DisCo (full) | Best | Constraints ensure identity distinguishability |
| Dataset Scale Ablation | Scales with size | More identities → Better performance |

### Key Findings

- DisCo generated data outperforms GAN synthetic datasets and is competitive with SOTA diffusion synthetic datasets
- Brownian motion provides physically natural diversity guarantees
- Data leakage detection confirms: the synthetic datasets generated by this method do not contain real individuals from the training set
- Repulsive forces between identities are the key factor in ensuring diversity

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Physics-inspired**: The analogy of Brownian motion is both intuitive and effective
2. **Privacy protection**: Actively prevent data leakage
3. **Principled approach**: Not simple random sampling, but possesses an energy function and dynamical derivation
4. **Scalable**: The method is independent of specific generative models and can be applied to different GAN/Diffusion models

## Limitations & Future Work

1. Sampling efficiency in high-dimensional latent spaces may decline
2. A pretrained face recognition model is required to verify identity (circular dependency)
3. The realism of generated images is still limited by the capability of the underlying generative model
4. Only validated on face recognition tasks; generalization to other biometric tasks needs exploration

## Related Work & Insights

- SynFace, DigiFace, etc., are the major baselines for synthetic face data
- Diffusion methods like IDiff-Face, Arc2Face provide strong comparisons
- Insight: The Brownian motion sampling strategy could potentially be used for other synthetic data generation tasks requiring diversity guarantees
