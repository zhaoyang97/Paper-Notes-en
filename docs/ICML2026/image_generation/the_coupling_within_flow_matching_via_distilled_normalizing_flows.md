# The Coupling Within: Flow Matching via Distilled Normalizing Flows

**Conference**: ICML 2026  
**arXiv**: [2603.09014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09014)  
**Code**: https://github.com/apple/ml-nfm  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / Generative Models / Flow Matching  
**Keywords**: Flow Matching, Normalizing Flow, coupling, Distillation, TarFlow

## TL;DR
This paper proposes NFM (Normalized Flow Matching), which utilizes the "quasi-deterministic data $\to$ noise bijection" generated by a pre-trained TarFlow (an autoregressive Normalizing Flow) as the noise-data coupling for Flow Matching. This advances FM convergence speed and few-step FID to new levels, while simultaneously accelerating inference by several orders of magnitude compared to the NF teacher.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Flow Matching (FM) has become the mainstream training paradigm for large-scale generative models—using $x_t=(1-t)x+t\epsilon$ to perform linear interpolation between data and noise, followed by regressing the velocity $v_t=\epsilon-x$. During inference, an ODE solver is used to revert from $x_1\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ to $x_0$. A key design determining performance is the "noise-data coupling." Independent coupling is the simplest but suffers from slow training and high inference curvature; thus, OT-based coupling (e.g., SD-FM) has become the dominant refinement direction.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: OT-based methods are essentially model-agnostic preprocessing based on geometric distance, failing to truly exploit the "data representations learnable by a model." Conversely, Normalizing Flows (NF)—particularly the recent TarFlow—can directly learn a data $\leftrightarrow$ Gaussian bijection. Theoretically, once this pairing is fixed, velocity regression has zero error and permits single-step sampling; however, NF autoregressive sampling is prohibitively slow.

**Key Challenge**: FM offers fast inference but employs coarse coupling; NF provides perfect coupling but suffers from slow sampling. Both have structural weaknesses, and prior work has seldom combined them.

**Goal**: (i) Replace random/OT coupling in FM with NF-learned (near-bijective) pairings to provide the FM student with better "aligned" training pairs; (ii) Verify that this "distilling NF into FM" approach accelerates FM convergence and enables the student to outperform the NF teacher in terms of FID.

**Key Insight**: The authors view NF as a "learned, model-dependent approximation of Optimal Transport." Even if the NF mapping is not OT-optimal in the likelihood sense (due to network capacity), its ability to stably map each data point to a specific Gaussian representation is sufficient to serve as a high-quality coupling.

**Core Idea**: During FM student training, the noise $\epsilon$ is replaced by the encoding $z_{\epsilon'}=f_{\text{NF}}(x+\eta\epsilon',c)/\sigma_f$ from the pre-trained NF teacher. The velocity target is changed to $v_t=z_{\epsilon'}-x$, while other FM procedures remain unchanged.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
NFM is a two-stage distillation process: The first stage trains a TarFlow teacher $f_{\text{NF}}$ to learn an invertible mapping $x\mapsto z$ via maximum likelihood. The second stage freezes the teacher and trains an FM student $g$ (arbitrary architecture, non-invertibility permitted). The student sees training pairs $(x, z_{\epsilon'})$ instead of $(x, \epsilon)$, where $z_{\epsilon'}=f_{\text{NF}}(x+\eta\epsilon',c)/\sigma_f$, $\eta$ is the input perturbation magnitude used during teacher training, and $\sigma_f$ is a scalar used to normalize teacher outputs to unit variance. The student is trained with the standard FM loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}=\|g((1-t)x+tz_{\epsilon'},c,t)-(z_{\epsilon'}-x)\|_2^2$, and inference is identical to standard FM.

### Key Designs

1. **Replacing Random Noise with $z$ from the NF Teacher**:
    - **Function**: Binds each data $x$ to a near-Gaussian $z_{\epsilon'}$ determined by the NF teacher. The FM student no longer learns a many-to-many mapping from "arbitrary noise to arbitrary data" but a near-deterministic mapping from "specific $z$ to specific $x$."
    - **Mechanism**: Training involves sampling $z_{\epsilon'}=f_{\text{NF}}(x+\eta\epsilon',c)/\sigma_f$, where $\sigma_f^2=\mathbb{E}[f_{\text{NF}}(x+\eta\epsilon',c)^2]$ ensures $z$ has a variance of approximately 1, making the overall distribution close to $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Consequently, the FM student can sample starting from standard Gaussian noise. Notably, in variance-preserving notation, the TarFlow perturbation $\eta=0.05$ corresponds to a maximum FM noise level of $t=\eta/(1+\eta)\approx0.0476$, significantly smaller than the standard $t=1$.
    - **Design Motivation**: A core difficulty in FM is the high conditional variance of $x_t$ at large $t$, where the velocity target $v_t=\epsilon-x$ carries variance information mainly from endpoints. Replacing $\epsilon$ with $z_{\epsilon'}$ significantly reduces $\text{Var}(v_t|x_t,t)$, resulting in more stable gradients and straighter trajectories, directly improving FID at lower NFE.

2. **TarFlow Teacher + Input Perturbation $\eta$**:
    - **Function**: TarFlow is selected as the teacher because it rivals diffusion models in image generation; the small $\eta\epsilon'$ perturbation during teacher training ensures the mapping is smooth in the data neighborhood.
    - **Mechanism**: TarFlow is an autoregressive flow implemented with Transformers, generating patches autoregressively within meta-blocks. While sampling is slow, invertibility is strong. NFM retains the perturbation $\eta$ used during training, which ensures smoothness of $z$ in small neighborhoods and naturally compresses the FM student's effective noise level to $\sim\eta/(1+\eta)$.
    - **Design Motivation**: The teacher's perturbation $\eta$ serves two roles in NFM: it prevents $z$ from collapsing into a purely deterministic mapping (retaining some stochasticity) and implicitly controls the maximum noise level seen by the FM student. Experiments show that larger $\eta$ yields the best FID at higher NFE, while smaller $\eta$ performs better in few-step sampling.

3. **Student Architectural Freedom + FM-Equivalent Objective**:
    - **Function**: The student $g$ can be a standard ViT/CNN and does not need to be invertible, allowing it to be smaller than TarFlow and allowing for adjustable inference steps.
    - **Mechanism**: By replacing $\epsilon$ with $z_{\epsilon'}$, the FM form—regressing velocity along linear interpolation—and time weighting remain unchanged. This allows seamless integration into any FM training pipeline (using SiT-XL in experiments). Inference uses Euler (NFE $\le$ 5) or Heun (NFE $\ge$ 5), with step sizes following a $t^2$ schedule $\{1, (1-\delta t)^2,\ldots\}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Maintaining the FM training form avoids introducing new hyperparameters or breaking existing code stacks. Not requiring student invertibility unlocks arbitrary architecture choices, making inference orders of magnitude faster than the invertible TarFlow teacher.

### Loss & Training
The student is trained using $\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}=\|g((1-t)x+tz_{\epsilon'},c,t)-(z_{\epsilon'}-x)\|_2^2$. Class labels are randomly nulled with probability $p=0.1$ to support classifier-free guidance. Time $t$ is sampled from $\text{lognorm}(-0.2,1)$. The teacher is trained on 512 MiB samples (approx. 420 epochs), whereas the student is trained on only 256 MiB (approx. 210 epochs).

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Dataset / Teacher / NFE | FM | SD-FM | NFM (256 MiB) | NFM vs FM (Gain) |
|----------------------|----|-------|---------------|-----------|
| ImageNet64, SiT-XL/4, 31 | 2.57 | 2.68 | 1.78 | -0.79 |
| ImageNet64, 15 | 4.80 | 3.15 | 2.15 | -2.65 |
| ImageNet64, 7 | 13.01 | 6.41 | 3.23 | -9.78 |
| ImageNet64, Euler-5 | 21.05 | 12.18 | 3.92 | -17.13 |
| ImageNet256, SiT-XL/2, 31 | 2.30 | – | 2.29 | -0.01 |
| ImageNet256, 7 | 12.41 | – | 3.43 | -8.98 |

The student's FID reaches 1.78 on ImageNet64, outperforming the TarFlow teacher (FID=1.98) with equivalent parameters. On ImageNet256, NFM (FID 2.29) significantly outperforms the teacher (FID 3.96).

### Ablation Study

| Configuration / Phenomenon | Result | Description |
|------------|------|------|
| Heun($t^2$), 31 NFE | FM 2.57 $\to$ NFM 1.78 | NFM advantage is smaller but stable at large NFE |
| Euler($t$), 128 NFE Curvature $\kappa$ | FM 0.0386 / SD-FM 0.0289 / NFM 0.0181 | NFM paths are significantly straighter, requiring fewer NFE |
| Heun, 5 NFE | 17.56 $\to$ 9.29 $\to$ 4.01 | NFM provides the highest relative gain at extremely low NFE |
| Large $\eta$ vs Small $\eta$ ($z$-space structure) | Larger $\eta \to$ closer $z$ for different images | Provides weaker "quasi-determinism" to FM; few-step FID degrades |

### Key Findings
- **Student outperforming teacher**: The authors attribute this to the combination of "near-deterministic coupling," "student architectural flexibility," and "EMA"—the teacher is limited by the low capacity of invertibility constraints, which the student lacks.
- **Unexpected $z$-space structure**: Projections of the same image under different $\eta\epsilon'$ values are not nearest neighbors in $z$-space; instead, different images under the same noise are closer. This suggests NF entangles "image identity" and "noise vectors" deeply in $z$-space. Nonetheless, NFM works, indicating the FM student learns endpoint correspondences rather than neighborhood structures.
- **Gain distribution**: In the typical deployment range (NFE = 7), NFM reduces FM's FID to 1/4 and SD-FM's by 50%. This is specifically where OT coupling in SD-FM fails to cover.

## Highlights & Insights
- Treating NF as a "model-learned OT approximation" is a powerful perspective: the core value of OT is "coupling," not "geometric optimality." NFM proves that geometric optimality is unnecessary if the coupling is sufficiently deterministic.
- "Distilling NF using FM" can be interpreted as both distillation (NF $\to$ FM) and a hybrid approach (NF + FM). The student being faster and better than the teacher is a rare and notable result.
- The method does not modify FM training objectives, samplers, guidance, or time scheduling. The engineering cost is minimal—one only needs to replace $\epsilon$ with $z_{\epsilon'}$ in existing FM pipelines.

## Limitations & Future Work
- Requires pre-training a high-quality NF teacher, which is expensive. If the teacher's FID is poor, the student may also suffer; the boundaries of this sensitivity remain unexplored.
- Experiments are restricted to ImageNet64/256 and class-conditional settings. Text-to-image, video, and high-resolution (1024+) scenarios have not been verified, and TarFlow's stability in those settings remains an open question.
- The counterintuitive $z$-space structure is not yet fully understood, potentially posing long-term risks for distillation stability.
- NFM is not inherently incompatible with OT-based coupling methods like SD-FM. The authors suggest combining them in the future.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs SD-FM / OT-CFM**: Both modify coupling, but OT methods approximate discrete optimal transport. This work uses NF-learned deterministic maps, producing significantly better results on ImageNet256, especially in low-NFE regimes.
- **vs DiffFlow / DiNof**: These works jointly train NF and diffusion. NFM follows a simpler "frozen teacher, distilled student" route that is easier to replicate.
- **vs Consistency Models / Rectified Flow**: CM and RF aim to shorten ODE paths to reduce NFE. NFM focuses on "better endpoints," essentially improving path curvature from the source (halving $\kappa$ in experiments).
- **vs General Distillation** (e.g., Progressive Distillation): Traditional distillation forces students to mimic sampling trajectories; NFM has the student learn FM on a newly defined target distribution, avoiding per-NFE alignment and offering better generalization.

## Rating
- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Using NF as a coupling source for FM is a refreshing and natural perspective; the cross-family distillation is cleanly executed.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Covers multiple NFE/solvers, different $\eta$, and curvature analysis; compared against FM, SD-FM, and TarFlow.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear reasoning; features honest reporting of counterintuitive $z$-space findings.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Provides a low-cost FID improvement for existing FM models, with significant impact in practical few-step sampling ranges.
