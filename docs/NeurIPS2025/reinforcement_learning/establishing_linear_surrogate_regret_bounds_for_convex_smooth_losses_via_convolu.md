# Establishing Linear Surrogate Regret Bounds for Convex Smooth Losses via Convolutional Fenchel–Young Losses

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.09432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09432)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning  
**Keywords**: surrogate regret bound, convex smooth loss, Fenchel–Young loss, infimal convolution, discrete prediction

## TL;DR

By constructing a Fenchel–Young loss based on convolutional negentropy, this work proves for the first time that convex and smooth surrogate losses can simultaneously possess linear surrogate regret bounds, shattering the long-held belief in the community that smoothness and linear regret rates are mutually exclusive.

## Background & Motivation

In machine learning, target losses (such as the 0-1 loss) are typically discrete and difficult to optimize directly, necessitating the design of surrogate losses. A desirable surrogate loss should simultaneously satisfy three properties: **convexity** (ensuring optimization feasibility), **smoothness** (accelerating optimization and estimation convergence), and **linear regret rate** (losslessly translating surrogate regret to target regret).

However, prior work suggests that these three properties are mutually exclusive:
- **Hinge loss**: Convex but non-smooth $\rightarrow$ possesses a linear regret rate
- **Logistic loss**: Convex and smooth $\rightarrow$ only achieves a square-root regret rate $\psi(r) = O(\sqrt{r})$
- **Sigmoid loss**: Smooth but non-convex $\rightarrow$ possesses a linear regret rate

Existing works such as poly_regret further prove that locally strongly convex and smooth losses **must** suffer from a square-root regret rate. The central contribution of this work is bypassing this limitation to construct a class of surrogate losses that are both convex and smooth while simultaneously enjoying linear regret rates.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

This work builds the construction based on the **Fenchel–Young loss** framework. The Fenchel–Young loss generated by generalized negentropy $\Omega$ is defined as $L_\Omega(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{p}) = \Omega(\boldsymbol{p}) + \Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \langle\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{p}\rangle$, which is inherently convex. The core mechanism consists of three steps:

1. Selecting a strongly convex **base negentropy** $\Omega$ (such as Shannon entropy)
2. Encoding the structure of the target loss $\ell$ into the negentropy via the **negative Bayes risk** $T(\boldsymbol{p}) = -\min_{t} \langle \boldsymbol{p}, \boldsymbol{\ell}^{\rho}(t)\rangle$, constructing $\Omega_T = \Omega + T$
3. Deriving convex and smooth surrogate losses and matching prediction links using the infimal convolution structure

### Key Designs

**Convolutional negentropy and infimal convolution**: The conjugate of convolutional negentropy $\Omega_T = \Omega + T$ takes the form of an infimal convolution:

$$\Omega_T^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \inf_{\boldsymbol{\pi} \in \Delta^N} \Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta} + \mathcal{L}^{\rho}\boldsymbol{\pi})$$

where $\mathcal{L}^{\rho}$ is the loss matrix. This is the **core** of the proposed method's success, enabling additive decomposition of the surrogate regret to achieve a linear lower bound.

**$\boldsymbol{\pi}$-argmax link**: Unlike the standard argmax, the link is defined as $\Pi(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \arg\min\{\Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta} + \mathcal{L}^{\rho}\boldsymbol{\pi}):\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Delta^N\}$, where the prediction corresponds to the class of the maximum component of $\pi_t$. This link distorts raw scores through optimized information, incorporating target loss structures.

**Convexity and smoothness guarantee**: When $\Omega$ is strongly convex, $L_{\Omega_T}(\cdot,y)$ is convex and smooth on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Crucially, this loss is **not locally strongly convex**, thereby bypassing the known lower bound of square-root regret.

### Loss & Training

**Convolutional Fenchel–Young Loss**:
$$L_{\Omega_T}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, y) = \min_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Delta^N}\Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathcal{L}^{\rho}\boldsymbol{\pi}) + \Omega_T(\boldsymbol{\rho}(y)) - \langle\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\rho}(y)\rangle$$

**Linear Regret Bound (Theorem 13)**: $\text{Regret}_\ell(\varphi(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\boldsymbol{\eta}) \leq N \cdot \text{Regret}_{L_{\Omega_T}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\eta})$

**Constant Improvement (Corollary 16)**: Leveraging low-dimensional decomposition, the constant is reduced from $N$ to $\text{affdim}(\mathcal{L}^{\rho})+1$, which drops from $2^d$ to $d+1$ under multi-label classification.

**Gradient Computation**: Via the envelope theorem, $\nabla L_{\Omega_T}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y)=\nabla\Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathcal{L}^{\rho}\boldsymbol{\pi})-\boldsymbol{\rho}(y)$, which boils down to finding $\Pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})$. In multi-class classification instances, Algorithm 1 solves this via sorting in $O(K\ln K)$ time.

**Fisher-Consistent Probability Estimator (Theorem 17)**: $\nabla\Omega^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*+\mathcal{L}^{\rho}\boldsymbol{\pi})$ yields a consistent estimate of $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\rho}(y)]$, which can be utilized for uncertainty quantification and classification with a rejection option.

### Ablation Study

- **Randomized link**: Employing $\Pr[\tilde{\varphi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=t]=\pi_t(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ improves the regret constant from $N$ to **1** (dimension-independent).
- **Low-dimensional vs. standard decomposition**: Utilizing binary encoding for multi-label classification reduces the optimization dimension from $2^d$ to $d$.
- **$\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\log}$-argmax equivalence**: The link is equivalent to the standard argmax in multi-class classification (Proposition 19), incurring zero overhead during testing.

### Key Findings

1. **Convex smoothness and linear regret can coexist**, and the key lies in the fact that the loss is not locally strongly convex.
2. Training overhead only introduces an additional sorting step $O(K\ln K)$, while the inference/testing matches standard classifiers exactly.
3. It naturally provides a probability estimator, refuting the prior hypothesis that "linear regret is incompatible with probability estimation."

## Highlights & Insights

- **Elegant Convex Analysis**: Leveraging infimal convolution transfers the "additive structure" from the negentropy space to the loss space, allowing additive decomposition of the regret.
- **No Violation of Known Lower Bounds**: The lower bounds from prior work (e.g., poly_regret) require local strong convexity, whereas the proposed loss is not strongly convex on the boundary (as $T$ is non-differentiable), cleverly bypassing these theoretical limits.
- **Unified Framework**: Applicable to any discrete target losses—including multi-class, multi-label, top-k, and structured prediction.

## Limitations & Future Work

- **Purely theoretical work, lacking empirical experiments**: The convergence speed comparison between this loss and cross-entropy in actual training remains unverified.
- Solving $\Omega_T^*$ in high-dimensional structured prediction may still incur heavy computational overhead.
- The constant of the linear regret bound might be large in certain scenarios, hindering practical utility.
- Whether fast rates can be achieved under low-noise assumptions remains an open question.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Polyhedral losses** (Mao et al.): Convex, non-smooth linear regret losses; this work can be viewed as their "smoothed" dual.
- **Fenchel–Young losses** (Blondel et al.): This work introduces a convolutional structure to this framework, expanding its scope of applicability.
- **SELF decomposition**: Provides low-dimensional label encoding for structured prediction, which is leveraged in this work to reduce regret constants.
- For online learning/bandit problems, this loss can be combined with the OCO framework to achieve highly efficient online predictions.
