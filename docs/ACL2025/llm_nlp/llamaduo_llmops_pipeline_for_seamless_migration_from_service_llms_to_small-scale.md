# LlamaDuo: LLMOps Pipeline for Seamless Migration from Service LLMs to Small-Scale Local LLMs

**Conference**: ACL2025  
**arXiv**: [2408.13467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13467)  
**Code**: [deep-diver/llamaduo](https://github.com/deep-diver/llamaduo)  
**Area**: LLM/NLP  
**Keywords**: LLMOps, Knowledge Distillation, Synthetic Data, Small Model Fine-tuning, Service Migration, QLoRA

## TL;DR
Proposes LlamaDuo, an automated LLMOps pipeline that iteratively fine-tunes small models using synthetic data generated by service LLMs. This enables 2B-8B local models to approximate or match the performance of large models like GPT-4o on specific downstream tasks, significantly reducing long-term deployment costs.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Cloud-based large language models (such as GPT-4, Gemini, Claude) occupy approximately 80% of the enterprise market share, but introduce operational dependencies, privacy risks, and the requirement for continuous internet connectivity.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Service disruptions, risks of data privacy leaks, degradation of user experience due to mismatched prompts from PoC (Proof of Concept) to deployment, and inconsistencies caused by model updates from service providers.

**Key Challenge**: Enterprises require locally controllable small models, but directly employing small models fails to match the quality of service LLMs on specific tasks.

**Goal**: To design an automated pipeline that seamlessly transfers knowledge from service LLMs to small-scale local models without manual intervention.

**Key Insight**: Leveraging the service LLM itself to generate synthetic data, combined with automated LLM-as-Judge evaluation, to form a closed-loop iterative optimization.

**Core Idea**: Ensuring that the small model eventually achieves the task performance of the service LLM through multi-round "synthetic data generation $\rightarrow$ fine-tuning $\rightarrow$ evaluation" loops.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
A three-phase pipeline: **Development/PoC Phase** (users interact with the service LLM to collect high-quality prompt-response pairs as the coverage dataset) $\rightarrow$ **Alignment Phase** (iteration: fine-tuning the local LLM $\rightarrow$ batch inference $\rightarrow$ LLM-as-Judge evaluation $\rightarrow$ if performance does not meet the threshold, generating more synthetic data for further fine-tuning) $\rightarrow$ **Deployment Phase** (deploying models that satisfy the threshold to resource-constrained environments).

### Key Designs

#### Key Design 1: Coverage Dataset and Synthetic Data Generation
- **Function**: Utilizing high-quality prompt-response pairs from real-world user scenarios as seeds, and leveraging the service LLM to generate large-scale synthetic instruction data.
- **Mechanism**: The seed data is split into train/test subsets by a ratio $\Phi$. Using the train subset as seeds, the Self-Instruct framework is employed to generate new samples via the service LLM. Deduplication and data decontamination (excluding samples similar to the test set) are performed using LSH MinHash and ROUGE.
- **Design Motivation**: Maintaining distributional consistency between synthetic data and real-world scenarios, while preventing data leakage from compromising the fairness of evaluation.

#### Key Design 2: Service LLMs-as-Judge Evaluation
- **Function**: Leveraging the service LLM to perform automated quality evaluation on responses generated by the local model.
- **Mechanism**: For each test prompt, the local model generates $K=4$ responses. The service LLM acts as the judge to assign scores across two dimensions: precision (response accuracy) and similarity (relevance to the ground truth). Each sample is evaluated $M=10$ times to take the average, while the coverage percentage is calculated.
- **Design Motivation**: Replacing manual evaluation to support flexible, task-specific evaluation metrics; multi-round evaluation reduces bias and improves reliability.

#### Key Design 3: Iterative Closed-Loop Fine-Tuning
- **Function**: If the local model's evaluation score does not meet the threshold $\varepsilon$, a new round of synthetic data is automatically generated for cumulative fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: The $t$-th round of fine-tuning performs SFT using the coverage train set along with all historical synthetic data $\{D_{synth}^{(1)}, ..., D_{synth}^{(t-1)}\}$. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning is conducted using QLoRA to reduce computational costs.
- **Design Motivation**: Guaranteeing continuous improvement of the small model until it matches the performance of the service LLM; QLoRA enables fine-tuning on consumer-grade GPUs.

### Loss & Training
Standard SFT loss: $\mathcal{L}_{SFT}(\pi^{(t)}, \mathcal{D}^{(t)}) = -\mathbb{E}[\log P_{\pi^{(t-1)}}(\mathcal{R}^{(t)} | \mathcal{I}^{(t)})]$, which minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the generated response on the accumulated training dataset.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (128K Synthetic Data, Generated by GPT-4o, Evaluated by Claude 3 Sonnet, Mean Precision)

| Task | GPT4o | Claude 3 Sonnet | Gemma 2B | Gemma 7B | Mistral 7B | LLaMA3 8B |
|------|-------|----------------|----------|----------|------------|-----------|
| Summarization | 93.25 | 93.39 | 74.89 | 86.19 | 86.20 | **87.02** |
| Classification | 87.50 | **92.89** | 69.98 | 78.78 | 76.01 | **89.20** |
| Coding | 94.57 | 93.82 | 80.92 | **90.47** | 81.25 | 83.97 |
| Closed QA | 97.21 | 97.60 | 80.22 | 88.83 | **88.25** | 86.03 |

### Ablation Study: Impact of Synthetic Data Volume on Gemma 7B Performance

| Data Volume | Trend |
|--------|------|
| 1K-4K | Performance improves slowly, with minimal impact from the evaluator type |
| 8K-32K | Performance improves rapidly |
| 64K-256K | Reaches performance saturation, approaching or matching the service LLM |

### Deployment Cost Comparison (Gemma 7B vs GPT-4o, Monthly Operational Expense)

| Scenario | Gemma 7B (12 Months) | GPT-4o (12 Months) |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| Light Load | $3,699 | $23,400 |
| Heavy Load | $23,992 | $117,000 |

| Task | Optimal Local Model | Number of Winning Scenarios |
|------|------------|-----------|
| Summarization | Mistral 7B | 7/12 |
| Classification | LLaMA3 8B | 12/12 |

### Key Findings
1. The fine-tuned 7B-8B models achieve performance matching capability (P-Match) of approximately 85%-105% of the service LLM on specific tasks.
2. Synthetic data generated by Claude 3 Sonnet typically yields the optimal model performance, followed by GPT-4o, with Gemini 1.5 Flash performing the worst.
3. The severity of different evaluators varies significantly: Gemini 1.5 Flash is the most stringent (especially yielding lower similarity scores) > GPT-4o > Claude 3 Sonnet (more lenient).
4. Even the 2B model achieves reasonable performance; for example, Gemma 2B reaches 80.22 on the Closed QA task (compared to 88.83 for Gemma 7B), indicating the performance gap is not unacceptable.
5. The operational costs of local models fall below GPT-4o API call costs after just 2 months of deployment; a heavy-load scenario saves approximately $93K over 12 months.
6. When the data volume is sparse (1K-4K), the type of evaluator has a minimal impact on results, indicating that the model's sensitivity to the choice of the judge increases with the volume of training data.

## Highlights & Insights
1. **End-to-End Automation**: The entire process operates without manual intervention, establishing a closed-loop system from data generation, through evaluation, to iterative fine-tuning.
2. **Rigorous Cost Justification**: Detailed cost comparisons demonstrate the financial viability of long-term local deployment of small models (saving 5 to 6 times the cost over 12 months).
3. **Model-Agnostic Nature**: The pipeline is universally applicable across various service and local LLM combinations, without dependencies on specific models.
4. **Comprehensive Experimental Coverage**: Extensive cross-validated results covering 3 service LLMs × 4 local LLMs × 4 task categories × 3 evaluation judges ensure reliable and robust conclusions.

## Limitations & Future Work
1. Synthetic data may inherit potential biases and safety flaws from the service LLM.
2. Dependence on service LLM APIs for data generation exposes the pipeline to API rate limits and associated transaction costs.
3. Iterative SFT may encounter performance plateaus, lacking advanced optimization paradigms such as RLHF or DPO.
4. Evaluation is restricted to 4 task categories, leaving complex scenarios such as multi-turn dialogues or logical reasoning unaddressed.
5. The LLM-as-Judge setup struggles with inherent preferential biases (e.g., scoring discrepancies among different evaluators are substantial).

## Related Work & Insights

### vs. Knowledge Distillation (Alpaca/Vicuna, etc.)
Traditional distillation utilizes a one-off generation and fine-tuning paradigm, whereas LlamaDuo introduces an **iterative closed loop**: failed evaluation $\rightarrow$ generate more data $\rightarrow$ continue fine-tuning, which guarantees quality convergence. Additionally, it provides a comprehensive end-to-end LLMOps pipeline rather than mere fine-tuning scripts.

### vs. Direct API Deployment
API-based deployment requires zero upfront investment but incurs high and uncontrollable long-term operational costs. LlamaDuo demonstrates that the investment for localized deployment pays off within just 2 months, while offering privacy preservation and offline runtime capabilities.

### Insights
- Scaling law of synthetic data: performance saturates around 64K-256K samples, after which diminishing returns are observed.
- The reliability of relying on service LLMs as judges demands caution, as the choice of evaluator heavily impacts conclusions.
