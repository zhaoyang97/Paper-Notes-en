# Molmo and PixMo: Open Weights and Open Data for State-of-the-Art Vision-Language Models

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2409.17146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17146)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/allenai/molmo](https://github.com/allenai/molmo)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM  
**Keywords**: Vision-Language Models, Open Data, Pointing Capability, Image Captioning, Multimodal Pre-training

## TL;DR

This paper proposes the Molmo family of VLMs and the PixMo dataset. Completely independent of synthetic data from closed-source VLMs, they construct high-quality training data from scratch through innovative data collection methods (voice descriptions of images, interactive Q&A annotation, and 2D pointing annotation). Its 72B model outperforms Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini 1.5 Pro on academic benchmarks and human evaluations, ranking second only to GPT-4o.

## Background & Motivation

Current leading VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude) are all closed-source models. Open-source models either lag in performance (like early LLaVA) or rely heavily on synthetic data generated by closed-source models (such as ShareGPT4V using GPT-4V to generate captions), which is essentially distillation from closed-source VLMs. This leaves the academic community lacking fundamental knowledge on "how to build high-performance VLMs from scratch." 

*   **Key Challenge**: High-quality multimodal data collection is extremely costly, and the quality of crowdsourced annotations is difficult to guarantee.
*   **Key Insight**: Utilize an innovative data collection method (a modality conversion trick—asking annotators to "speak" rather than "write" descriptions) to obtain high-quality data at low cost.
*   **Core Idea**: SOTA-level VLMs can be trained using high-quality original data without relying on distillation.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Molmo adopts a standard VLM architecture: a pre-trained ViT image encoder + a vision-language connector + a pre-trained decoder LLM. Training consists of two stages: (1) pre-training all parameters on PixMo-Cap to learn detailed image captioning; (2) fine-tuning on a mixture of the PixMo dataset and open-source academic datasets to acquire capabilities such as Q&A, pointing, and counting. The model family spans various scales from 1B (MolmoE) to 72B (based on Qwen2-72B).

### Key Designs

1. **PixMo-Cap Speech-to-Text Description Collection**:
    - **Function**: Collect high-quality, detailed descriptions (averaging 196 words) for 712k images to serve as pre-training data.
    - **Mechanism**: Have annotators **verbally describe the image for 60-90 seconds** instead of typing captions. The speech is transcribed to text and then formatted into high-quality captions by a text-only LLM.
    - **Design Motivation**: Asking annotators to directly type long captions yields poor results—they tend to focus only on a few salient elements, typing long paragraphs is time-consuming, and they might copy-paste outputs from closed-source VLMs. The "modality conversion trick" of the speech modality naturally encourages annotators to provide richer details while leaving an audio "receipt" proving that VLMs were not used.

2. **PixMo-Points 2D Pointing Data**:
    - **Function**: Collect 2.3M pointing annotations to enable the model to localize objects, count, and explain answers by pointing to pixels.
    - **Mechanism**: Annotators point out all instances of a certain object class in an image (using points instead of bounding boxes). The model learns to output normalized coordinates $x, y \in [0, 100]$. During counting, a chain-of-thought approach is used—pointing out each target one by one before reporting the final count.
    - **Design Motivation**: Point annotations are much faster to collect than bounding boxes and segmentation masks, and they naturally support counting (by pointing sequentially) and visual explanation. This can empower VLM-driven agents (e.g., robot navigation, UI operations) in the future.

3. **Overlapping Multi-Crop Strategy**:
    - **Function**: Process high-resolution images while ensuring each patch retains neighborhood context.
    - **Mechanism**: The image is divided into multiple square crops that are allowed to overlap, but only the patch features from the non-overlapping parts are fed into the LLM. 12 crops are used during training, and 36 crops are used during inference.
    - **Design Motivation**: In standard tiling strategies, boundary patches lack neighborhood context (e.g., a bicycle brand name being cut off). Overlapping resolves this issue. Ablation studies show that overlapping improves the 11-benchmark average from 75.7 to 76.9.

### Loss & Training

- **Two-Stage Simplification**: Skip the common stage of "freezing the LLM to train the connector first," and directly pre-train all parameters end-to-end. The connector uses a higher learning rate (2e-4 vs. 2e-5 for the LLM) and a shorter warmup (200 steps vs. 2000 steps) to compensate.
- **Text-Only Dropout**: Apply residual dropout only to text tokens during the pre-training stage, leaving image tokens untouched, forcing the model to rely more on visual inputs rather than language priors.
- **Efficient Training with Multi-Annotations**: Pack multiple annotations for the same image into a single long sequence, using attention masks to isolate them and avoid redundant encoding of the image. This reduces image processing by 2/3 and cuts training time by more than half.
- **Style Tags**: Prefix academic datasets with tags (e.g., "vqa2:") to teach the model to use short-answer styles only during evaluations, while maintaining a natural style in user interactions.

### Key Findings

- SOTA-level performance can be achieved solely through high-quality original data, without using any closed-source VLM synthetic data.
- Speech description produces much more detailed captions than written descriptions (196 words vs. 11 words in COCO).
- The pointing capability enables counting accuracy that significantly outperforms all other models (85.2 on PixMo-Count vs. 59.6 for GPT-4o).
- MolmoE-1B (a Mixture-of-Experts model) approaches GPT-4V performance with extremely few parameters.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Innovative Data Collection**: The "modality conversion" trick of speech description is an exceptionally clever, low-cost, high-quality solution. It prevents annotators from taking shortcuts while providing audio verification against cheating.
- **Pointing as Explanation**: Using 2D points instead of bounding boxes for visual localization significantly lowers annotation costs while opening up a pointing-based interaction paradigm for VLMs acting as agents.
- **Fully Open High-Performance VLM**: It leads by a wide margin in the "open weights + open data" category, proving that top-tier performance can be achieved without distilling closed-source models.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Reasoning capabilities (MMMU, MathVista) are significantly weaker than GPT-4o and the Qwen series, as the training data lacks high-level reasoning-related content.
- Pointing capability generalizes poorly when changing the number of crops, requiring additional high-resolution post-training.
- The speech description scheme relies on English-speaking annotators, requiring extra consideration for multilingual expansion.
- The model architecture itself has no novelty; the core contribution is on the data side.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. LLaVA-OneVision**: The LLaVA series relies on distilled data like ShareGPT4V, whereas Molmo is built entirely on self-constructed data and delivers stronger performance.
- **vs. Qwen2-VL**: Qwen2-VL slightly outperforms in OCR-related tasks but lags far behind Molmo in human evaluation (Elo 1037 vs. 1077).
- **vs. PaliGemma**: As another open model, PaliGemma's performance is far lower than Molmo's, indicating that data quality is more critical than architecture.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ No architectural innovations, but the data collection methods (speech description + pointing annotation) are highly novel.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 11 academic benchmarks + 325k human evaluations + extensive ablations.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Detailed and clear, with extremely comprehensive project details and ablation analyses.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Provides a complete roadmap for the open VLM community to build SOTA models from scratch.
