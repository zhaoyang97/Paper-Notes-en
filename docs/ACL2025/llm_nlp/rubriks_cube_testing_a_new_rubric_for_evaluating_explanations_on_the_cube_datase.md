# Rubrik's Cube: Testing a New Rubric for Evaluating Explanations on the CUBE Dataset

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.23899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23899)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/RubriksCube/rubriks_cube)  
**Area**: Other  
**Keywords**: explanation evaluation, rubric, LLM explanations, CUBE dataset, quality assessment, education-inspired

## TL;DR
This paper proposes Rubrik—an explanation quality evaluation rubric inspired by educational assessment, based on a three-tier nested typology ($Commentary \subseteq Justification \subseteq Argument$) and 8 quality dimensions. Along with the CUBE dataset (containing 26K explanations generated by humans and 6 LLMs), it is discovered that the primary cause of low-quality LLM explanations is a lack of conciseness rather than coherence.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: LLMs are increasingly deployed in tasks requiring explanations (e.g., automated scoring, problem-solving, diagnostic reasoning). However, LLM-generated explanations remain unreliable, suffering from hallucinations, misleading arguments, and poor reasoning.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (a) There is a lack of unified standards for evaluating explanation quality—human evaluators are often untrained, leading to inconsistent evaluation criteria across individuals. (b) It is difficult to diagnose "why an explanation is poor" in a fine-grained manner (e.g., is it incoherent, inaccurate, or too verbose?).

**Key Challenge**: How to establish a task-agnostic, fine-grained, and theoretically grounded standard for evaluating explanation quality?

**Key Insight**: Leverage assessment rubrics from the field of education—a mature tool designed for complex/subjective task evaluations (such as IELTS writing assessment), following the best practices outlined by Dawson (2017).

**Core Idea**: The Rubrik hierarchical rubric (typology $\rightarrow$ components $\rightarrow$ dimensions) combined with the CUBE multi-task, multi-model explanation dataset offers a standardized evaluation framework for explanation quality.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Consisting of two parts: (1) The Rubrik rubric, which defines the structure (typologies and components) and quality (dimensions) of explanations; (2) The CUBE dataset, which employs 4 tasks $\times$ 7 producers (6 LLMs + humans) to generate 26K explanations, annotated for quality using Rubrik.

### Rubrik Rubric Design

1. **Three-tier Nested Explanation Typology**:
    - **Commentary**: The most basic tier, requiring Action (what was done) + Reason (why).
    - **Justification**: Inherits components of Commentary + Evidence.
    - **Argument**: Inherits Justification + Affective appeal & Qualifiers.
    - Hierarchical relationship: $Commentary \subseteq Justification \subseteq Argument$.
    - **Design Motivation**: A consensus in cognitive science, social sciences, and education indicates that explanations serve different purposes (comprehension, persuasion) and exhibit escalating structural complexity.

2. **8 Quality Dimensions (Categorized into Language and Content)**:
    - **Language Dimensions**: Grammaticality, Word Choice, Cohesion, Conciseness.
    - **Content Dimensions**: Appropriateness, Coherence, Plausibility, Stance Clarity.
    - **Design Motivation**: LLMs can sometimes be "superficially fluent but factually incorrect"—necessitating separate evaluations of linguistic quality and content substance.

3. **Scoring Process**:
    - Determine typology first: Check Commentary components $\rightarrow$ Check Justification components $\rightarrow$ Check Argument components.
    - Evaluate dimensions for each typology next: All dimensions passed = Good (✓), any failed = Poor (✗).
    - Result: Typology (None/Commentary/Justification/Argument) + Quality (Good/Poor).

### CUBE Dataset

| Task | Description | No. of Instances |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| C - Commonsense Reasoning | CSQA-based | 1000 |
| U - Logical Fallacies | LOGIC-based | 1000 |
| B - Reading Comprehension | QuAIL-based | 1000 |
| E - Essay Scoring | ASAP-based | 1000 |

- 7 Producers: Humans + GPT-4o + Claude Sonnet 3.5 + Command R+ + Gemma 2 + Llama 3.1 + Mixtral.
- Total of ~26K explanations.
- Custom agreement metric: Accounts for the hierarchical nested nature of the rubric.

## Key Experimental Results

### Explanation Quality Diagnostics

| Dimension | Main cause of low-quality LLMs? | Details |
|------|------------|------|
| **Conciseness** | **Yes (Primary)** | LLM verbosity is the leading cause of low quality |
| Evidence | Partial | Sometimes suffers from a lack of sufficient evidence |
| Cohesion | No | Coherence of LLM-generated text is generally good |
| Word Choice | No | Vocabulary selection meets expectations |
| Grammaticality | No | LLM grammar is almost flawless |

### Model Comparison

| Category | Performance |
|------|------|
| Closed-source (GPT-4o, Claude) | Higher overall quality |
| Open-source (Llama, Gemma, Mixtral) | Lower quality |
| Human | **More concise** but with weaker evidence |

### Key Findings
- **Explanation typology depends on tasks and perceived difficulty**: Reasoning tasks tend to produce Justification/Argument, while simpler questions lean towards Commentary.
- **The primary driver of poor LLM explanations is a lack of conciseness**—rather than a lack of coherence or poor vocabulary selection. LLMs tend to generate explanations that are overly verbose, repetitive, and contain unnecessary information.
- **Humans are more concise but provide weaker evidence**—whereas LLMs do the opposite, providing stronger evidence but being too verbose.
- **Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude) significantly outperform open-source ones**: Demonstrating more balanced performance across all dimensions.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Educational Methodology $\rightarrow$ NLP**: Rubrics are well-proven in the educational domain, and introducing them to LLM evaluation serves as a highly valuable cross-disciplinary transfer. Adhering to Dawson's (2017) 10 design elements ensures the systematicity of the rubric.
- **"Lack of conciseness is the primary driver"** provides direct guidance for LLM development: Training should prioritize conciseness, which is more urgent than fixing coherence or grammatical errors.
- **Task-Agnostic Framework**: Rubrik can be applied to any task requiring explanations, not limited to the current four tasks.
- **Hierarchical nested typology system** elegantly manages the diversity of explanations—different explanations serve distinct purposes, thereby having varying structural requirements and quality standards.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **English-Only**: Quality of multi-lingual explanations might be influenced by cultural and rhetorical norms.
- **Four tasks may lack diversity**: Specialized fields such as code explanation and mathematical reasoning are not covered.
- **Unexplored rubric dimension weights**: All dimensions are treated equally, but different scenarios might require different priorities.
- **Evaluation still requires human/AI evaluators**: Rubrik itself is not an automated scoring tool, but a structured guideline.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. G-Eval (Liu et al.)**: G-Eval employs LLMs to evaluate generation quality, whereas Rubrik provides human-actionable structured criteria.
- **vs. MQM (Multidimension Quality Metric)**: MQM inspired the hierarchical design of Rubrik, but Rubrik is designed for explanations rather than translation.
- **Insight**: Rubrik could be integrated into RLHF rewards—specifically targeting and penalizing non-concise explanations.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Cross-domain transfer of educational rubrics to NLP, featuring a novel hierarchical typology.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 26K explanations $\times$ 7 producers $\times$ 4 tasks, providing a comprehensive analysis.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear and systematic, with transparent design decisions.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A standardized tool for explanation quality evaluation with practical utility for LLM assessment.
