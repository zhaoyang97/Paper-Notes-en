# Demo: Guide-RAG: Evidence-Driven Corpus Curation for Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Long COVID

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025 (GenAI for Health Workshop)  
**arXiv**: [2510.15782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15782)  
**Code**: None  
**Area**: Medical Imaging / Medical NLP  
**Keywords**: RAG, Long COVID, corpus curation, LLM-as-a-judge, clinical QA

## TL;DR
This paper systematically evaluates six RAG corpus configurations for Long COVID clinical QA. The GS-4 configuration—combining clinical guidelines with high-quality systematic reviews—consistently outperforms both single-guideline and large-scale literature retrieval baselines across faithfulness, relevance, and comprehensiveness. The authors further introduce the Guide-RAG framework and the LongCOVID-CQ evaluation dataset.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: AI chatbots are rapidly being adopted in clinical medicine; however, designing effective clinical decision support AI for complex, heterogeneous, and evidence-limited emerging diseases such as Long COVID poses significant challenges.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
   - Pure LLMs without retrieval are prone to hallucination and citation errors.
   - Retrieval from large-scale databases such as PubMed introduces substantial low-quality or irrelevant content (information overload) and suffers from the "lost-in-the-middle" effect.
   - Relying solely on a single clinical guideline is overly narrow and fails to capture rapidly evolving research evidence.
   - Existing medical QA benchmarks predominantly use multiple-choice formats, which are inadequate for evaluating clinical decision support capabilities.

**Key Challenge**: In emerging disease settings characterized by rapidly evolving evidence and lack of consensus, there exists a fundamental tension between retrieval scope that is too narrow (missing evolving evidence) and too broad (introducing noise).

**Key Insight**: Using Long COVID as a case study, the paper systematically compares the effect of different corpus curation strategies on RAG performance.

**Core Idea**: Combining clinical guidelines with a small number of high-quality systematic reviews (GS-4) achieves an optimal balance between narrow consensus documents and unfiltered primary literature.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Six corpus configurations are constructed and compared using GPT-4o as the backbone LLM. LongCOVID-CQ (20 expert clinical questions) serves as the evaluation set. Answer quality is assessed via an LLM-as-a-judge pairwise comparison framework.

### Key Designs

1. **Six Corpus Configurations**

    - **NR-0**: GPT-4o without retrieval (control group)
    - **G-1**: AAPM&R Long COVID multidisciplinary guideline only (single document)
    - **GS-4**: Guideline + 3 high-quality systematic reviews (4 documents total; primary configuration)
    - **R-110**: Full texts of 110 references cited in the guideline
    - **PM**: Full PubMed retrieval (hybrid sparse-dense retrieval)
    - **WS**: GPT-4o web search (restricted to peer-reviewed literature)
    - **Design Motivation**: Spanning the complete spectrum from "narrowest" (G-1) to "broadest" (PM) to empirically validate the trade-off between curation quality and coverage breadth.

2. **LongCOVID-CQ Evaluation Dataset**

    - Twenty clinical questions generated by Long COVID specialists, covering diagnosis, management strategies, and mechanisms.
    - Employs open-ended QA rather than multiple-choice format, better reflecting real-world clinical information needs.

3. **Three-Dimensional LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation**

    - **Faithfulness**: Whether responses are grounded in and traceable to retrieved documents.
    - **Relevance**: Whether responses directly address the question without redundant content.
    - **Comprehensiveness**: Whether responses provide adequate coverage (Long COVID has 200+ known symptoms).
    - **Scoring mechanism**: GPT-4o performs pairwise comparisons with randomized presentation order to mitigate position bias and explicit length-bias control; win = 100, loss = 0, tie = 50 each.

### Loss & Training
This work presents an evaluation framework rather than a trained model; no loss function is involved. Key technical choices include dense retrieval for small corpora (G-1, GS-4, R-110) and hybrid sparse-dense retrieval for PubMed.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Pairwise Win Rate (%)

| Configuration | vs G-1 | vs R-110 | vs PM | vs WS | Average Overall Win Rate |
|------|--------|----------|-------|-------|----------------------|
| **GS-4** | ~60 | ~62 | ~60 | ~65 | **57.5–65** |
| G-1 | — | 57.5 (faithfulness) | ~55 | ~58 | ~55 |
| R-110 | 42.5 | — | ~50 | ~52 | ~48 |
| PM | ~40 | ~50 | — | ~50 | **Best relevance** |

GS-4 (guideline + 3 systematic reviews; only 4 documents) ranks first in overall, faithfulness, and comprehensiveness.

### Ablation Study: Performance by Dimension

| Dimension | Best Configuration | Notes |
|------|---------|------|
| Faithfulness | GS-4 | Curated documents provide high-quality, citable sources |
| Relevance | PM | Large-scale corpus yields broader relevant content |
| Comprehensiveness | GS-4 | Systematic reviews inherently synthesize evidence with broad coverage |
| Overall | GS-4 | Optimal across all three dimensions |

### Key Findings
- GS-4 with only 4 documents outperforms both R-110 (110 references) and the full PubMed corpus, demonstrating that **corpus quality far outweighs quantity**.
- Synthesized guidelines outperform their constituent references (G-1 > R-110 in faithfulness), indicating that synthesized documents provide better retrieval anchoring.
- PubMed shows marginally better relevance but underperforms in faithfulness and comprehensiveness—large-scale retrieval tends to produce overconfident responses with weak evidentiary grounding.
- Expert review reveals that R-110 and PM generate responses that appear specific but are potentially misleading (e.g., recommending exercise training for Long COVID patients, which specialists explicitly advise against).

## Highlights & Insights
- **Counterintuitive "less is more" finding**: Four curated documents outperform 39M+ PubMed records and 110 references, demonstrating that curation quality is paramount for RAG in emerging diseases—a principle generalizable to other rapidly evolving evidence domains.
- **Rigorous three-dimensional evaluation framework**: Faithfulness ensures trustworthiness, relevance prevents information overload, and comprehensiveness ensures completeness; this evaluation paradigm is reusable for other medical RAG systems.
- **Compelling qualitative expert analysis**: Beyond quantitative win rates, concrete case studies illustrate how large-scale corpora can produce authoritative-seeming but clinically dangerous recommendations.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The study focuses exclusively on Long COVID, leaving generalizability to other emerging diseases unvalidated.
- Only GPT-4o is used as the judge, with no comparison against human evaluation.
- The evaluation set contains only 20 questions, limiting statistical robustness.
- Different retrieval strategies (dense vs. hybrid) across corpora may introduce confounding retrieval method bias.
- No systematic ablation is performed over retrieval parameters such as chunk size, embedding model, or reranking threshold.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Medical Graph RAG**: The latter employs knowledge graph–structured retrieval, while Guide-RAG relies on curated corpora; the two approaches are complementary—combining structured knowledge graphs with curated documents may yield superior results.
- **vs. RAGAS Framework**: RAGAS provides automated RAG evaluation metrics; Guide-RAG extends this with clinically specific dimensions (comprehensiveness for 200+ symptoms).
- **vs. General Medical QA Benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA)**: These benchmarks use multiple-choice questions, whereas LongCOVID-CQ comprises open-ended expert questions that more faithfully reflect real clinical settings.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐ — The systematic comparison is valuable, but no new model or algorithm is proposed; this is primarily an empirical study.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐ — Six-configuration comparison is comprehensive, but the evaluation set is small and human evaluation is absent.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Motivation is clear, experimental design is sound, and expert analysis is substantive.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Provides practical design principles for corpus curation in RAG systems targeting emerging diseases.
