# Adapting Psycholinguistic Research for LLMs: Gender-Inclusive Language in a Coreference Context

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2502.13120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13120)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/marionbartl/GIL-coref-context](https://github.com/marionbartl/GIL-coref-context)  
**Area**: NLP Understanding  
**Keywords**: Gender-inclusive language, Coreference resolution, LLM bias, Psycholinguistics, German

## TL;DR

By adapting the psycholinguistic experiment of Tibblin et al. (2023) from French to English and German LLMs, this work measures coreferent word probabilities and analyzes generated content. The findings show that: English LLMs generally maintain antecedent-coreference gender consistency, but singular *they* is rarely used and an underlying masculine bias persists. The German Leo Mistral 7B model exhibits a stronger masculine bias that dominates all 8 gender-inclusive strategies; nevertheless, these inclusive strategies still increase the probability of feminine/neutral gender occurrences, aligning with the results of human psycholinguistic experiments.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Gender-inclusive language (e.g., chairman $\rightarrow$ chairperson, he/she $\rightarrow$ they) aims to eliminate the "masculine-by-default" bias. Psycholinguistics has demonstrated that gender-inclusive expressions can increase the mental representation of female and non-binary genders. Since LLMs are widely used as writing assistants and content generators, the language they generate can influence users' linguistic habits.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Although gender bias in LLMs has been extensively studied, research on how LLMs process gender-inclusive language is extremely scarce. The processing of singular *they* in English LLMs has only begun to be explored, while the processing of gender-inclusive language in German LLMs remains entirely uninvestigated. Due to German's grammatical gender system (where nouns, articles, and adjectives are marked for gender), its inclusive strategies are highly complex, encompassing various forms such as capital-I (Binnen-I), gender asterisk (*), colon (:), underscore (_), and coordinate feminine-masculine form (splitting).

**Key Challenge**: Gender-inclusive language aims to promote gender-neutral interpretation, but do LLMs actually interpret these expressions neutrally? If the language generated by LLMs remains male-biased, their role as writing tools may instead reinforce the masculine-by-default bias.

**Goal**: To quantify whether English and German LLMs generate gender-consistent coreferents or reflect model bias when encountering gender-inclusive antecedents.

**Key Insight**: To adapt the French psycholinguistic experimental paradigm of Tibblin et al. (2023) (the gender relationship between the antecedent and its coreferent in sentence pairs) into an LLM evaluation methodology, while comparing the effectiveness of 8 German inclusive strategies.

**Core Idea**: Transforming the psycholinguistic coreference resolution experimental design into an LLM gender bias probe, systematically evaluating the effectiveness of 8 gender-inclusive strategies in German LLMs for the first time.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Two types of experiments were designed: (1) measuring the conditional probability of specific coreferents—the probability distribution of specific gender coreferents given the antecedent, and (2) analyzing the genders that appear in the LLM's free-generation continuations—observing actual model generation behavior. English (6 models) and German (1 model) were evaluated separately.

### Key Designs

1. **Dataset Construction**:

    - Function: Constructing sentence pair templates based on Tibblin et al. (2023), where Sentence 1 contains an antecedent (gendered/neutral) and Sentence 2 contains a coreferent (men/women/people or he/she/they).
    - Mechanism—English:
        - Plural condition (PL): 34 antecedent triplets (e.g., swordsman/swordswoman/fencer) $\times$ 3 coreferent words (men/women/people) $\times$ 44 templates = 13,464 instances.
        - Singular condition (SG): 37 triplets $\times$ 3 pronouns (he/she/they) $\times$ 44 templates = 14,652 instances.
    - Mechanism—German: 10 antecedents $\times$ 8 gender strategies (masculine/feminine/pairing masculine first/pairing feminine first/capital-I/asterisk/colon/underscore) $\times$ 3 coreferent words (Männer/Frauen/Personen) $\times$ 44 templates = 10,560 instances.
    - Design Motivation: To maintain comparability with the original psycholinguistic design while leveraging LLM characteristics (ability to precisely measure probabilities) to expand the dimensions of analysis.

2. **Coreferent Probability Measurement**:

    - Function: Measuring the predictive probability of specific coreferents after a given antecedent in LLMs.
    - Mechanism: Using LLMs to predict the joint probability of the sentence pair up to the coreferent position, and extracting the log-probability $\log(p)$ of the coreferent. For multi-token coreferent words, the probability of the first token is extracted to avoid self-prediction inflation in subsequent tokens.
    - Statistical Analysis: Two-way ANOVA was utilized to test the interaction effect of antecedent gender $\times$ coreferent gender, followed by Tukey post-hoc tests for specific pairwise comparisons.

3. **Coreferent Generation Analysis**:

    - Function: Prompting the LLM to freely generate continuations and annotating the gender of the coreferents appearing in the generated content.
    - Mechanism: English was set to generate 8-token continuations, and German was set to generate 10-token continuations. Human annotators annotated the gender mentioned in the generation and whether it functioned as a coreference.
    - English Annotation: Three annotators completed the task, reaching Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.757$ (gender) and $0.671$ (coreference), indicating "substantial agreement" for both metrics.
    - German Annotation: One of the authors, as a native speaker, annotated the pilot study.

### Evaluated Models

English: GPT-2 (baseline), gender-neutral fine-tuned GPT-2, OLMo-1B/7B/13B, Qwen-2.5-32B  
German: Leo Mistral 7B

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (English Qwen-2.5 Coreferent Probability, ANOVA)

| Effect | PL Condition | SG Condition |
|------|---------|---------|
| Main effect of antecedent gender | $F(2,13455)=138.59, p<.001, \eta^2=0.02$ | Significant |
| Main effect of coreferent gender | $F(2,13455)=178.33, p<.001, \eta^2=0.03$ | Significant |
| **Interaction effect** | $F(4,13455)=809.94, p<.001, \eta^2=0.19$ (large) | Significant |

### Ablation Study (German Leo Mistral 7B, Comparison of 8 Inclusive Strategies)

| Strategy | Feminine Coreferent Probability Rank | Neutral Coreferent Probability Rank | Explanation |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|------|
| Generic Masculine (Baseline) | Lowest | Lowest | Strongest masculine bias |
| Feminine Form | **Highest** | Medium | As expected |
| Asterisk (*) | Second highest | Medium | Contains the "-innen" suffix |
| Pairing forms (both) | Medium | Medium | Moderate effect |
| Capital-I / Colons / Underscores | Medium | Medium | Weaker effect than asterisk |

### Key Findings

- **Underlying masculine bias in English**: The probability of a masculine coreferent after a neutral antecedent is 3 times higher than that of a feminine coreferent (Tukey: $e^{1.107} \approx 3.03, p<.001$).
- **Singular *they* is rarely accepted**: Under the singular condition with neutral antecedents, the probability of *he* is 88% higher than *they* ($e^{-2.16} \approx 0.12$), showing that models do not treat *they* as a singular pronoun.
- **Dominant masculine bias in German**: Regardless of which inclusive strategy is employed, the probability of *Männer* (men) coreferents remains consistently the highest. Unlike in English, in German, the gender of the coreferent itself (rather than the interaction effect) is the strongest predictor ($\eta^2 = 0.33$).
- **Inclusive strategies are partially effective**: Although they fail to eliminate the masculine bias, all inclusive strategies increase the probability of feminine and neutral coreferents, consistent with human psycholinguistic findings in Tibblin et al. (2023).
- **Generation matches probability**: English LLMs generally maintain antecedent-coreferent gender consistency when generating coreferents, but the frequency of generating coreferents after neutral antecedents is the lowest.

## Highlights & Insights

- First systematic study on the processing of gender-inclusive language in German LLMs—the comparison of the 8 strategies provides empirical support for the German NLP community.
- Clear and effective methodological translation from psycholinguistics to NLP—probability measurement and generation analysis complementarily validate each other, providing a new experimental paradigm for bias research.
- The finding that "singular *they* usage is extremely low" has direct implications for English NLP system design—if writing assistants do not actively use singular *they*, they might erase the linguistic representation of non-binary individuals.
- The finding that inclusive strategies are "partially effective" is both theoretically significant (bias in LLMs can be partially mitigated) and practically valuable (recommending the use of inclusive expressions in LLM inputs).

## Limitations & Future Work

- Limited model scale (up to 32B); performance might differ in larger models (>100B).
- Only one German model (Leo Mistral 7B) was tested; validation on more models is required.
- Templated sentence pairs may not reflect natural co-reference patterns in real-world texts.
- Only cross-sentence coreference resolution between two sentences was considered—actual LLM interactions usually involve longer context.
- The category of coreferents was fixed (men/women/people), and other candidate coreferent words were not tested.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs Watson et al. (2023)**: Tested role noun acceptability judgments on BERT; ours tests coreferent probabilities and actual generation in generative LLMs.
- **vs Brandl et al. (2022)**: Tested the processing difficulty of Swedish neopronouns; ours systematically compares 8 inclusive strategies in German.
- **vs Winograd Schema Bias Evaluation**: Within-sentence pronoun resolution evaluation; ours evaluates cross-sentence coreferent relationships.

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Seamlessly adapts the psycholinguistic coreference paradigm to LLM evaluation for the first time, filling the blank in German inclusive language strategy research.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Double validation via both probability and generation, rigorous ANOVA statistical analysis, but only a single-model pilot of German.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Clear description of the experimental design, standardized statistical reporting (effect sizes and confidence intervals).
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Methodological contribution to LLM fairness and multilingual bias research, with practical significance in promoting the use of inclusive language.
