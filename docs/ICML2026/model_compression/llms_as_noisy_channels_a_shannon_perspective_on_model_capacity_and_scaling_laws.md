# LLMs as Noisy Channels: A Shannon Perspective on Model Capacity and Scaling Laws

**Conference**: ICML 2026  
**arXiv**: [2605.23901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23901)  
**Code**: TBD  
**Area**: Model Compression / Scaling Laws / Information Theory  
**Keywords**: Shannon Capacity, Scaling Laws, U-shaped Loss, Catastrophic Overtraining, Quantization Degradation

## TL;DR
Ours reinterprets LLM training as a Shannon-Hartley noisy channel—where parameter count corresponds to bandwidth, training tokens to signal power, and data/model noise to channel noise. Derived from this framework, the Shannon Scaling Law $C_{\text{LLM}} = aN^\alpha \log_2(1 + bD^\beta / (c(DN)^\gamma + dD^\delta + e))$ provides a unified explanation for both classical monotonic scaling and recently discovered U-shaped degradation (catastrophic overtraining, quantization-induced degradation). It achieves an $R^2 = 0.847$ when extrapolating from $\le$6.9B data to a 12B model with 307B tokens on Pythia/OLMo2.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: The development of LLMs is guided by scaling laws—OpenAI's law uses $L = [(N_c/N)^{\alpha_N/\alpha_D} + D_c/D]^{\alpha_D}$ while the Chinchilla law uses the additive form $L = A/N^\alpha + B/D^\beta + E$. Both assume that performance improves monotonically with computation. This motivated trillion-parameter models (DeepSeek-V4 1.6T, Kimi K2.6 1T) and massive pretraining datasets.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: A series of recent findings challenge the monotonicity assumption:
- **Catastrophic Overtraining** (Springer 2025): Excessive pretraining can actually harm downstream SFT.
- **Quantization-induced Degradation (QiD)** (Kumar 2024, Ouyang 2024): Larger or longer-trained models become more sensitive to quantization.
These phenomena produce **U-shaped loss curves**—where the loss decreases and then rises. Existing perturbation-aware scaling laws (Ouyang 2024, Kumar 2024) merely append degradation terms $\Delta_q L$ or $\delta_{\text{PTQ}}$ to the base law, lacking a unified theory.

**Key Challenge**: Monotonic power laws assume infinite signal gain, but actual LLM training involves both signal (learned knowledge) and noise (data noise, model noise, quantization perturbations, etc.). Performance improves as long as signal growth outpaces noise, but when noise dominates past a critical point, a U-shape emerges. A unified framework is needed to represent the interaction between signal and noise.

**Goal**: (1) Establish a unified scaling law capable of describing both monotonic returns (high SNR regime) and U-shaped degradation (low SNR regime). (2) Derive this law from first principles of information theory rather than empirical curve fitting. (3) Enable extrapolation to larger models and longer training durations outside the training distribution.

**Key Insight**: LLM training is analogous to Shannon communication—pretraining $=$ channel modulation (encoding information into weights), and inference $=$ transmission (transferring from context to output). The Shannon-Hartley theorem $C = B \log_2(1 + S/\mathcal{N})$ naturally describes the capacity upper bound of "signal vs. noise." At high $S/\mathcal{N}$, capacity is approximately $B \log_2(S/\mathcal{N})$ (monotonic), while high noise compresses capacity—exactly corresponding to the U-shape.

**Core Idea**: Parameter count $N \to$ bandwidth $B$; training tokens $D \to$ signal power $S$; data noise + model noise + perturbations $\to$ noise $\mathcal{N}$. This yields the Shannon Scaling Law $C_{\text{LLM}} = aN^\alpha \log_2(1 + bD^\beta / (c(DN)^\gamma + dD^\delta + e))$, which covers both monotonic and U-shaped behaviors.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Mapping Shannon-Hartley physical quantities to LLMs:
- **Bandwidth $B_{\text{LLM}} \propto N^\alpha$**: Parameter count defines the width of the "feature spectrum" the model can carry.
- **Signal $S_{\text{LLM}} \propto D^\beta$**: Knowledge carried by training tokens, scaling according to a power law.
- **Three sources of noise $\mathcal{N}$**: Data noise $d D^\delta$ (typos/ambiguity accumulating with tokens), model interaction noise $c(DN)^\gamma$ (fitting noise generated by the interaction between the model and training data), and irreducible perturbation $e$.

Complete law:
$$C_{\text{LLM}} = aN^\alpha \log_2\left(1 + \frac{bD^\beta}{c(DN)^\gamma + dD^\delta + e}\right)$$

### Key Designs

1. **Three-source Noise Decomposition**:
    - **Function**: Decomposes noise into data noise, model-data interaction noise, and irreducible perturbations, corresponding to different degradation mechanisms in LLM training.
    - **Mechanism**:
        - Data noise $d D^\delta$: Pretraining data contains typos, ambiguities, and contradictions; more tokens lead to more accumulation $\to$ explains catastrophic overtraining.
        - Model interaction noise $c(DN)^\gamma$: Training acts as denoising, where the interaction between model and data generates noise; the $DN$ term reflects the "large model $\times$ big data" interaction $\to$ explains QiD (larger models trained on more data are more fragile to quantization).
        - Irreducible perturbation $e$: Inherent to hardware or algorithms.
    - **Design Motivation**: A single noise term cannot distinguish between different sources of degradation; this three-layer decomposition makes fitted parameters interpretable.

2. **Unification of U-shape and Monotonicity**:
    - **Function**: Covers two types of counter-intuitive behaviors under a single formula.
    - **Mechanism**: The shape of $\log_2(1 + S/\mathcal{N})$ determines the behavior—when $S \gg \mathcal{N}$, $\log_2$ approximates $\log_2(S/\mathcal{N})$, and capacity increases monotonically with $D$. When the growth of $\mathcal{N}$ catches up to or exceeds $S$ (e.g., dominated by the $D^\delta$ term), capacity decreases—the U-shape emerges naturally.
    - **Design Motivation**: Previous perturbation-aware laws were additive "base law + degradation term" patches. The Shannon law is derived from a single principle; monotonicity and U-shapes are simply two limits of the same formula in different SNR regimes.

3. **Unified Encoding of Multi-source Perturbations**:
    - **Function**: Incorporates various perturbations such as Gaussian noise, quantization, and SFT into the framework.
    - **Mechanism**: All perturbations act on the $\mathcal{N}$ term. Additive Gaussian noise directly increases $e$ or $d$; quantization reduces effective SNR; SFT on OOD data introduces new interaction noise $c'(DN)^{\gamma'}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: While classical scaling laws model each perturbation separately, increasing parameter complexity, the Shannon framework reuses the same formula, requiring only different parameter fits.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Fitting Quality ($R^2$)

| Experimental Setting | OpenAI law | Chinchilla | Perturbation-aware (Ouyang) | **Shannon** |
|----------------------|------------|------------|-----------------------------|-------------|
| Pythia Standard Pretraining | 0.91 | 0.93 | 0.93 | **0.95** |
| Pythia + Gaussian Noise | 0.42 | 0.45 | 0.78 | **0.91** |
| Pythia + Quantization | 0.38 | 0.41 | 0.82 | **0.93** |
| OLMo2 + math SFT | 0.31 | 0.34 | 0.67 | **0.89** |
| OLMo2 + QA SFT | 0.28 | 0.30 | 0.69 | **0.88** |
| OLMo2 + code SFT | 0.35 | 0.37 | 0.71 | **0.90** |

The Shannon law leads significantly in all perturbation scenarios, especially in SFT scenarios where classical laws collapse.

### Extrapolation Ability (Key Test)

| Training Data | Test Model | Test Tokens | OpenAI $R^2$ | Chinchilla $R^2$ | **Shannon $R^2$** |
|---------------|------------|-------------|--------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Pythia $\le$6.9B, $\le$180B token | Pythia 12B | 180B | 0.65 | 0.71 | **0.93** |
| Pythia $\le$6.9B, $\le$180B token | Pythia 12B | 240B | 0.42 | 0.48 | **0.89** |
| Pythia $\le$6.9B, $\le$180B token | Pythia 12B | 307B | **−0.18** | **−0.05** | **0.847** |

Classical laws produce negative $R^2$ (worse than a constant fit) at 307B tokens, while the Shannon law maintains 0.847—demonstrating true extrapolation outside the training distribution.

### U-shape Capture
In Pythia quantization experiments, as bit-width decreases from 4 to 2, the loss remains stable before surging. OpenAI/Chinchilla entirely fails to capture this critical point, Ouyang’s law partially captures it with some offset, while the Shannon law precisely identifies the location of the loss basin.

### Key Findings
- **Unified framing is superior to formula stitching**: The Shannon law is not just another perturbation-aware law but is derived from first principles. Its deep unification results in better extrapolation.
- **Interpretable three-source noise**: Fitted $c, d, e$ allow quantitative analysis of various degradation sources (e.g., determining whether a failed training run was due to data or model noise).
- **Extrapolation to 12B / 307B holds**: Training only up to 6.9B / 180B tokens still predicts results at much larger scales, proving the Shannon law captures underlying laws rather than curve-fitting tricks.
- **Consistent across perturbation types**: Gains are observed across Gaussian, quantization, and three types of SFT, proving the framework's generality.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Information theory as a profound perspective**: While previous scaling laws were empirical fits, the Shannon law is derived from communication theory; this first-principles approach provides a template for future scaling research.
- **Interpretability of noise decomposition**: Quantitatively diagnosing failure by mapping issues to data quality, model interaction, or irreducible factors provides a practical diagnostic tool.
- **Engineering significance of $R^2 = 0.847$**: The primary value of scaling laws is predication. Ours remains accurate outside the training distribution, allowing small-scale experiments to predict large-scale performance and saving massive compute.
- **Analogy to the Shannon-Weaver model**: Viewing inference as channel transmission allows analysis of inference optimizations (like KV cache or speculative decoding) within the same framework.

## Limitations & Future Work
- It remains an empirical fitting law involving 9 parameters ($a, b, c, d, e, \alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$) that require data estimation. Exploring whether these can be derived purely from channel coding theory is worthwhile.
- Validation is limited to Pythia and OLMo2 families; performance on closed-source models (GPT/Claude) is untested.
- Architectural differences (e.g., attention heads, layer width) are not explicitly modeled, relying only on total parameters $N$.
- Capacity during inference (context length, KV cache, etc.) is not yet incorporated; inference scaling laws represent a separate research direction.
- The Shannon-Hartley theorem assumes AWGN, whereas LLM training noise is likely more complex; generalized capacity formulas could be considered.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. OpenAI / Chinchilla**: Those assume monotonicity and are falsified by U-shaped phenomena; the Shannon law is a strict superset (reducing to power laws in high SNR regimes).
- **vs. Ouyang QiD law / Kumar precision law**: Those patch base laws with additive degradation terms; the Shannon law naturally yields these behaviors from a single principle.
- **vs. Information Bottleneck theory (Tishby)**: That theory uses mutual information to analyze DNN representations, whereas Ours uses channel capacity to analyze training—a parallel perspective.
- **Insight**: Re-evaluating empirical scaling laws through information theory may reveal unified principles; this "first principle scaling" approach is worth applying to RL, multimodal, and agents.
