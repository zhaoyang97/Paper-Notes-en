# Explaining Puzzle Solutions in Natural Language: An Exploratory Study on 6×6 Sudoku

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2505.15993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15993)  
**Code**: Pending  
**Area**: Model Compression  
**Keywords**: LLM reasoning, Sudoku, explanation generation, human-AI collaboration, puzzle solving  

## TL;DR

Evaluating the capabilities of five LLMs in solving and explaining $6\times6$ Sudoku puzzles reveals that even though o1-preview can solve 65% of the puzzles, its generated explanation chains remain severely lacking in faithfulness, clarity, and educational value.

## Background & Motivation

**Problem Definition:** The effectiveness of LLMs in human-AI collaborative decision support scenarios depends not only on their ability to provide correct answers but also on whether they can provide step-by-step, consistent, and trustworthy explanations. This paper investigates the explanation generation capabilities of LLMs in multi-step reasoning tasks using $6\times6$ Sudoku puzzles as a controlled experimental environment.

**Limitations of Prior Work:** Existing work (such as PuzzleLM) achieves correct answer generation by incorporating symbolic solvers (Z3) but pays little attention to whether LLMs can provide useful natural language explanations for the resolution process. Although symbolic reasoners can generate correct and faithful reasoning steps, their outputs are typically presented as formal proofs, which are difficult for non-expert users to comprehend.

**Core Motivation:** A good reasoner should be able to clearly expose its deductive process. The authors argue that reasoning explanations should simultaneously exhibit faithfulness, provide sufficient justification for each decision, and convey generalizable puzzle-solving strategies. As a logical puzzle requiring deductive reasoning and rule-based elimination, Sudoku provides an ideal testbed.

**Research Questions:** (1) Can LLMs solve $6\times6$ Sudoku? (2) Can LLMs successfully explain the reasoning steps to arrive at a given solution?

## Method

### Overall Architecture

This paper is an evaluation study conducted in two stages:
1. **Solving Capability Evaluation:** Testing the solving accuracy of five LLMs on 2,293 Z3-generated $6\times6$ Sudoku puzzles.
2. **Explanation Capability Evaluation:** Selecting a subset of 20 puzzles covering different difficulty levels, with three experts evaluating the quality of explanations generated by o1-preview.

### Key Designs

1. **Minimal Dataset Construction:** Using the Z3 solver to generate Sudoku puzzles; starting with 11 clues (given numbers), clues are incrementally removed until removing any further number would violate the uniqueness of the solution. Ultimately, approximately 96% of the puzzles contain 9–11 clues. The Explainer Rating (ER) system is employed to quantify difficulty, covering five levels from Easy (ER 1.0–1.2, 73.4%) to Diabolical (ER 6.2+, 1.5%).
2. **Multi-granularity Accuracy System:** Evaluation is conducted progressively across five levels: cell-wise $\rightarrow$ row-wise $\rightarrow$ column-wise $\rightarrow$ box-wise $\rightarrow$ complete solution, enabling precise localization of the models' failure modes in constraint integration.
3. **Three-dimensional Explanation Quality Assessment:** Three experts used a three-point Likert scale (Yes / Maybe / No) to independently score explanations across three dimensions: Justification (whether justification is provided for the solution), Clarity (logical clarity, consistency of terminology), and Educational Value (whether generalizable puzzle-solving strategies are conveyed).

### Evaluation Metrics

Weighted Kappa was used to measure inter-annotator agreement, yielding $\kappa=0.6$ for educational value, $\kappa=0.6$ for justification, and $\kappa=0.4$ for clarity, indicating moderate to good agreement. The Spearman correlation coefficient between human annotators' difficulty ratings and the ER on the 20 puzzles reached 0.87.

### Key Findings

1. **Open-source models struggle significantly to solve:** Even when the cell accuracy reaches 40-50%, the row and column constraint satisfaction rate is only around 7%, and the completely correct rate is near 0%, indicating a fundamental difficulty in integrating global constraints.
2. **Sharp contrast between the solving and explaining capabilities of o1-preview:** o1-preview achieves 100% accuracy on Easy/Medium difficulties, solving 59% overall, but its explanation quality is extremely poor—while the model places correct numbers, it almost never explains "why" those placements were chosen.
3. **Lack of systematic explanations:** Evaluators consistently reported that explanations lacked logical progression, failed to justify reasoning steps, suffered from inconsistent terminology, and did not clearly convey the solving path.
4. **Difficulty sensitivity:** On the Diabolical level (ER 6.2+), the completely correct rate of o1-preview drops to 40%, and cell-wise accuracy drops to 57.2%.

## Highlights & Insights

- This work presents the first systematic study on the **explanation generation capabilities** of LLMs in multi-step reasoning tasks, shifting the research focus from "can they solve" to "can they explain".
- The benchmark dataset is constructed using Z3 with theoretical guarantees (unique solutions, minimality), ensuring the scientific rigor of the evaluation.
- The human evaluation is well-designed, incorporating multi-dimensional scoring, inter-annotator agreement verification, and difficulty calibration.
- A future research direction combining LLMs and logical reasoners is proposed: using symbolic reasoners to generate reliable reasoning chains, which are then translated into human-comprehensible natural language by the LLM.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Only $6\times6$ Sudoku is used, which has limited complexity and may underestimate the limitations of LLMs on standard $9\times9$ Sudoku.
- The evaluation scale of o1-preview is relatively small (100 puzzles for solving + 20 puzzles for explanation), limiting statistical sufficiency.
- The impact of fine-tuning on explanation capabilities is not explored.
- Newer versions of reasoning models (such as the official o1, o3, etc.) have not been tested.

## Related Work & Insights

- **LLM puzzle solving:** PuzzleVQA (Chia et al., 2024) diagnoses multimodal reasoning, PuzzleLM (Mittal et al., 2024) integrates SMT solvers.
- **Reasoning capability evaluation:** GSM-Symbolic (Mirzadeh et al., 2024) reveals the mathematical reasoning limitations of LLMs.
- **LLM + formal reasoning:** DeepSeek-Prover (Xin et al., 2024) explores the combination of LLMs with Lean 4, Draft-Sketch-Prove (Jiang et al., 2022) uses informal proofs to guide formal provers.
- **Unfaithful reasoning:** Turpin et al. (2024) demonstrate that the reasoning chains of LLMs under CoT prompting may not reflect their actual reasoning process.
