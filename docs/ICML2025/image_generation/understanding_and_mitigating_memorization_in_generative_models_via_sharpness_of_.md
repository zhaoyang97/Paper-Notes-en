# Understanding and Mitigating Memorization in Generative Models via Sharpness of Probability Landscapes

**Conference**: ICML2025 Spotlight  
**arXiv**: [2412.04140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04140)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Dongjae0324/sharpness_memorization_diffusion)  
**Authors**: Dongjae Jeon, Dueun Kim, Albert No
**Area**: Image Generation  
**Keywords**: Diffusion Models, Memorization, Sharpness, Hessian, Score Function, Privacy

## TL;DR

An analytical geometric framework for memorization in diffusion models is established via the Hessian curvature (sharpness) of log-probability density, introducing a new metric to detect memorization at the early stages of generation and designing a retraining-free initial noise optimization strategy, SAIL, to mitigate memorization.

## Background & Motivation

Diffusion Models have achieved tremendous success in image generation, but suffer from severe **memorization** issues—the models sometimes directly replicate training samples instead of generating novel outputs. This causes significant privacy risks when models are trained on sensitive data, leading to various real-world lawsuits.

Prior analytical methods for memorization exhibit the following limitations:

**LID (Local Intrinsic Dimensionality) methods** (Ross et al., 2024): Can only detect memorization at the final generation step ($t \approx 0$) and fail to provide early warning.

**Training data comparison-based methods** (Carlini et al., 2023): Require access to the training set, incur heavy computational costs, and are unsuitable for real-time detection.

**Score-difference metric by Wen et al. (2024)**: Effective but lacks theoretical justification.

**Prompt modification strategies**: Mitigate memorization at the cost of degraded generation quality.

Therefore, the core motivation of this paper is: **Can a unified theoretical framework be established from the perspective of probability density geometry to explain, detect, and mitigate memorization?**

## Method

### Core Idea: Memorization = Sharp Regions in the Probability Landscape

The authors propose a key observation: memorized samples correspond to **sharp peaks** in the learned log-probability density $\log p(\mathbf{x})$. This sharpness can be quantified by the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix:

$$H(\mathbf{x}_t) \coloneqq \nabla^2_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$$

- **Large negative eigenvalues** $\rightarrow$ Sharp and isolated probability peaks $\rightarrow$ Memorization
- **Small/positive eigenvalues** $\rightarrow$ Smooth and broad distribution $\rightarrow$ Good generalization

The key difference from LID methods is that sharpness analysis can be performed across **all timesteps**, rather than being restricted to the final step.

### Score Norm as a Sharpness Metric (Lemma 4.1)

Directly computing the eigenvalues of a high-dimensional Hessian is computationally prohibitive. The authors prove that, under Gaussian assumptions, the norm of the score function is equivalent to the trace of the Hessian:

$$\mathbb{E}[\|s(\mathbf{x})\|^2] = -\text{tr}(H(\mathbf{x}))$$

where $H(\mathbf{x}) \equiv -\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}$. This means that the **score norm** can serve as an efficient approximation of sharpness—avoiding expensive eigenvalue decomposition. This relationship is particularly accurate in the intermediate-to-high noise stages of the diffusion process, where the latent distribution is close to Gaussian.

### Theoretical Foundation for Wen's Metric (Lemma 4.2)

The memorization detection metric proposed by Wen et al. (2024) is the norm of the difference between the conditional and unconditional scores:

$$\|s_\theta^\Delta(\mathbf{x}_t)\| \coloneqq \|s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, c) - s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t)\|$$

The authors prove that this metric essentially measures the **eigenvalue difference** between the conditional and unconditional Hessians:

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim p(\mathbf{x}|c)}[\|s(\mathbf{x},c) - s(\mathbf{x})\|^2] = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{(\lambda_i - \lambda_{i,c})^2}{\lambda_{i,c}}$$

(under the assumption that the covariance matrices commute and their means are identical). This provides a sharpness-based theoretical explanation for Wen's metric.

### New Sharpness Metric via Hessian Amplification (Section 4.4)

Wen's metric is insufficiently sensitive in the initial stage of generation ($t = T-1$). The authors propose using the Hessian-score product to amplify high-curvature directions:

$$\|H_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, c) \cdot s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, c)\|^2 \quad \text{近似} \quad -\text{tr}(H_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, c)^3)$$

This third-moment statistic is more sensitive to large negative eigenvalues ($\lambda^3$ amplification effect), thereby effectively distinguishing memorized from non-memorized samples at the very first sampling step.

### SAIL: Sharpness-Aware Initialization for Latent Diffusion

**Sharpness-Aware Initialization for Latent Diffusion (SAIL)** is the mitigation strategy proposed in this paper. The core idea is to optimize the initial noise $\mathbf{z}_T$ during inference to steer it away from high-sharpness regions:

- Using the proposed sharpness metric as a regularization term
- Searching for initialization points with low sharpness in the initial noise space
- Guiding the diffusion process to sample towards smooth probability regions

**Key Advantages**:
- **No Retraining**: Only adjusts the initial noise during inference.
- **Preserves Prompts**: Does not alter user-provided prompts, unlike prompt modification strategies.
- **Model-Agnostic**: Applicable to various diffusion model architectures.

## Key Experimental Results

### Experimental Setup

- **Datasets**: 2D toy dataset, MNIST, Stable Diffusion v1.4 / v2.0
- **Memorization Categories**: Exact Memorization (EM), Partial Memorization (PM), Non-memorized
- **MNIST Experiments**: Induced memorization by replicating a single "9" image, using the "3" class as a general control group.

### Key Findings

1. **Eigenvalue Distribution Differences**: Memorized samples exhibit significantly more and larger negative eigenvalues at both the initial and final steps, whereas non-memorized samples have eigenvalues close to zero or positive.
2. **Early Detection**: The Hessian-score product metric can detect memorization at $t = T-1$ (the first step), substantially outperforming the final-step limitation of LID.
3. **Conditional vs. Unconditional Hessian Discrepancies**: Memorized samples demonstrate a significant discrepancy between conditional and unconditional Hessian eigenvalues, whereas this difference is close to zero for non-memorized samples.
4. **SAIL Mitigation Effectiveness**: SAIL successfully mitigates memorization while preserving generation quality (without significant degradation in FID or CLIP scores).

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Theoretical Elegance**: Formulates the memorization problem as a curvature analysis of the probability landscape, connecting seemingly distinct concepts like the score function, Hessian, and LID.
2. **Practical Value**: Detects memorization in only 1 inference step with minimal computational overhead.
3. **Theoretical Foundation for Wen's Metric**: Provides a rigorous mathematical justification for existing heuristic methods.
4. **Third-Moment Amplification Trick**: The Hessian-score product leverages the $\lambda^3$ amplification effect, elegantly addressing the lack of sensitivity at the initial steps.
5. **SAIL Design Philosophy**: No model retraining or prompt modification, only optimizing the initial noise—this minimally invasive mitigation strategy is highly feasible for practical deployment.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Limitations of Gaussian Assumption**: The core theory relies on Gaussian assumptions (Lemma 4.1, 4.2). Although the score norm remains empirically effective in later stages of the diffusion process when the distribution deviates from Gaussian, the theoretical guarantees are weakened.
2. **Computational Overhead of SAIL**: Initial noise optimization requires additional forward passes to evaluate sharpness, and its efficiency for large-scale deployment warrants further evaluation.
3. **Scope of Memorization Definition**: Primarily focuses on text-conditional settings; the applicability to unconditional or other conditional generation tasks is not fully discussed.
4. **Lack of Large-Scale Experiments**: Validated mainly on SD v1.4/v2.0, without exploring newer models such as SDXL or Flux.
5. **Hessian Approximation Accuracy**: Arnoldi iteration yields only a subset of eigenvalues, and the approximation quality for the 16,384-dimensional latent space requires further in-depth analysis.
6. **Impact of SAIL on Generation Diversity**: Although claimed to maintain quality, whether systematically avoiding high-sharpness regions reduces generation diversity remains to be investigated.

## Related Work & Insights

- **LID Methods** (Ross et al., 2024; Kamkari et al., 2024): Sharpness is a generalization of LID and can detect memorization at arbitrary timesteps.
- **Score Difference Metric of Wen et al. (2024)**: Ours provides its theoretical foundation.
- **Manifold Learning Hypothesis** (Fefferman et al., 2016): Exact memorization corresponds to data points on zero-dimensional manifolds.
- **SAM Optimization** (Foret et al., 2021): Sharpness-aware concepts are transferred from training to inference.
- **Differentially Private Training**: SAIL offers a complementary, retraining-free privacy protection scheme.
