# PROSPERO: Active Learning for Robust Protein Design Beyond Wild-Type Neighborhood

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2505.22494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22494)
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/szczurek-lab/ProSpero)
**Area**: Medical Imaging / Protein Engineering
**Keywords**: protein design, active learning, sequential Monte Carlo, pretrained generative model guidance, surrogate model robustness

## TL;DR

This paper proposes ProSpero, an active learning framework that discovers high-fitness and novel protein sequences even under surrogate model mismatch, via inference-time sampling of a frozen pretrained generative model (EvoDiff) guided by a surrogate, a targeted masking strategy, and biologically-constrained SMC sampling.

## Background & Motivation

- **Background**: Protein sequence design faces a fundamental tension: exploring high-fitness sequences beyond the wild-type neighborhood requires venturing outside the training distribution, where surrogate model accuracy degrades sharply (surrogate mismatch), while sequences far from the wild type tend to lose biological plausibility.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: PEX is limited to local mutations; global exploration methods such as GFN suffer severely from surrogate mismatch; GFN-AL-δCS uses random masking that may corrupt conserved residues; iterative fine-tuning of pretrained generative models is impractical.
- **Key Challenge**: Pretrained generative models naturally encode biological priors but are expensive to fine-tune repeatedly in iterative optimization loops.
- **Goal**: Enable reliable global exploration of protein sequence space using a frozen pretrained generative model, guided at inference time by an iteratively updated surrogate, while maintaining biological plausibility.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

ProSpero follows an active learning loop: at each round, (1) a surrogate model $f_\theta$ is trained on the current dataset; (2) fitness-relevant residues are identified and masked via targeted masking; (3) EvoDiff generates new candidate sequences through biologically-constrained SMC sampling guided by the surrogate; (4) candidates are evaluated by an oracle and the dataset is updated. The key innovation is that the generative model remains frozen throughout—surrogate feedback is incorporated solely via inference-time guidance.

### Key Designs

1. **Targeted Masking**: Inspired by alanine scanning, a batch of mutant sequences is generated by substituting randomly selected positions with alanine. Each mutant is scored using the surrogate's UCB (mean plus uncertainty). Positions corresponding to the highest-UCB mutations—those that are mutated yet do not immediately decrease fitness while exhibiting high uncertainty—are deemed functionally relevant and replaced with [MASK] tokens, while the remaining wild-type residues are preserved. This avoids the risk of random masking inadvertently corrupting critical conserved residues.

2. **Biologically-Constrained SMC Sampling**: To mitigate surrogate mismatch, amino acid charge class is used as an explicit biological prior to constrain sampling. Masked positions are partitioned according to the wild-type residue's charge into positively charged (R, K, H), negatively charged (D, E), and neutral groups; proposals at each position are restricted to amino acids of the same charge class. The SMC procedure proceeds residue by residue: (i) an amino acid is proposed from the constrained base model $\mathcal{P}_{RAA}$; (ii) the partially filled sequence is completed by the base model and scored via the surrogate UCB, normalized by the unconstrained model perplexity to obtain importance weights; (iii) sequences are resampled according to normalized weights, discarding low-quality candidates.

3. **Inference-Time Guided Active Learning Loop**: The generative model (EvoDiff-OADM) remains frozen at all times; surrogate model signals enter only through the SMC weighting mechanism. After each active learning round, the surrogate is updated on additional data, expanding its effective support and providing increasingly reliable guidance signals in subsequent rounds.

### Loss & Training

The surrogate model (an ensemble of three 1D CNNs) is trained with MSE loss; all baselines share the same architecture for fair comparison. Sequence selection uses the UCB acquisition function $f_\theta(x) = \mu_\theta(x) + k \cdot \sigma_\theta(x)$, with $k=1$ during masking (exploration-biased) and $k=0.1$ during SMC sampling (exploitation-biased). Each round evaluates $K=128$ sequences with $N=10$ rounds; SMC batch size is $B=256$. The alanine scanning batch size is $S=16$; for short sequences ($L \approx 100$), 3–10 positions are mutated, and for long sequences, 5–15. The final $n_{keep}=10$ SMC rollout steps also contribute to candidate selection.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | Effect | Note |
|---------------|--------|------|
| Remove charge constraint | Fitness decreases | Unconstrained sampling more prone to mismatch |
| Replace targeted masking with random masking | Fitness decreases | May corrupt critical residues |
| Remove active learning (single round) | Significant decrease | Surrogate cannot expand effective support |
| Increase surrogate noise | ProSpero most robust | Charge constraint provides an additional safety net |

### Key Findings

- ProSpero and PEX are the only two methods that improve upon wild-type fitness across all 8 landscapes.
- In terms of novelty (Hamming distance), ProSpero's top-100 sequences are on average farther from the wild type than those of PEX.
- ProSpero discovers high-fitness sequences earlier than baselines on half of the tasks.
- Under surrogate mismatch conditions (covariate shift and prediction noise), ProSpero consistently maintains the best or near-best performance.
- Diversity of top-100 sequences (mean pairwise Hamming distance) remains within a reasonable range.
- Biological plausibility analysis confirms that the charge constraint effectively preserves structural integrity.
- Four baseline methods fail to improve upon the wild type on any task, highlighting the difficulty of global exploration.

## Highlights & Insights

- The "frozen generative model + inference-time guidance" design avoids costly fine-tuning while naturally leveraging the biological priors encoded in the pretrained model.
- Charge-class-constrained sampling is an elegant and practical way to inject biological priors, more tractable than complex structural constraints.
- The technique of correcting constrained sampling bias via perplexity within SMC is a transferable methodological insight.
- Targeted masking, inspired by alanine scanning, elegantly translates an established experimental biology technique into a computational strategy.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The charge grouping is coarse; finer physicochemical property groupings (e.g., volume, polarity) merit exploration.
- Residue-by-residue sequential SMC decoding incurs substantial computational cost; parallel decoding strategies could be investigated.
- Evaluation relies solely on the TAPE oracle to simulate wet-lab experiments; validation in real experimental settings is lacking.
- Protein structural information is not incorporated; integration with structure prediction tools such as AlphaFold may further improve performance.

## Related Work & Insights

- PEX excels at local optimization but cannot explore distant regions; ProSpero broadens the exploration range while maintaining reliability.
- GFN-AL-δCS employs random masking, whereas ProSpero's targeted masking is more principled.
- Compared to the twisted SMC approach of Amin et al., ProSpero does not require training on a specific clonal family.
- The pretrained model plus inference-time guidance paradigm generalizes to other discrete sequence optimization problems (e.g., DNA and RNA design).
- RL-based methods such as DyNaPPO and LatProtRL possess strong global exploration capacity but are less robust than ProSpero across most tasks.
- CbAS and GFN-AL fail to improve upon the wild type on most of the 8 tasks, exposing the fragility of global methods.
- AdaLead's greedy mutation-and-recombination strategy is robustly performant but lacks the biological priors of pretrained models.
- MLDE directly proposes candidates using ESM-2 but lacks the iterative refinement capability of active learning.
- Bayesian optimization methods perform well on certain landscapes but lack consistency.
- EvoDiff's order-agnostic property affords greater flexibility in designing the sampling order within SMC.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ First work to combine inference-time guidance with active learning for protein design; the three components are elegantly co-designed.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Comprehensive evaluation across 8 protein tasks, 10 baselines, and surrogate mismatch robustness analysis.
