# T5Score: A Methodology for Automatically Assessing the Quality of LLM Generated Multi-Document Topic Sets

**Conference**: ACL 2025  
**arXiv**: [2407.17390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17390)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/itamartrainin/Tpower5Score)  
**Area**: LLM/NLP  
**Keywords**: Topic Extraction Evaluation, Free-Text Topics, LLM-as-Judge, Inter-Annotator Agreement, Multi-Document Analysis

## TL;DR

The authors propose T5Score, a methodology that decomposes the quality of LLM-generated free-text topic sets (FT-topics) into five quantifiable dimensions (interpretability, topic coverage, document coverage, non-overlap, inner-order). This approach achieves high inter-annotator agreement through simple labeling tasks and validates that LLMs can serve as automated evaluators to replace human effort.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: With the rise of LLMs, an increasing number of studies use them for multi-document topic extraction to produce topic descriptions in free-text format (e.g., "Transportation to Concentration Camps"), rather than traditional word-distribution topics (WD-topics) generated by LDA.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing evaluation methods (e.g., word intrusion test, BLEU/ROUGE) are either only applicable to word-distribution topics (WD-Topics) or focus solely on lexical similarity while ignoring semantic depth. Crucially, current practices exhibit **extremely low inter-annotator agreement (IAA)**, making it impossible to reliably evaluate the quality of topic sets.

**Key Challenge**: Asking human annotators to directly evaluate the overall quality of an entire topic set imposes an excessive cognitive load (Hoyle et al., 2021), leading to high variance and low IAA. However, without reliable evaluation, the validity of LLM-generated topics cannot be verified.

**Goal**: To establish an indirect yet reliable evaluation methodology for LLM-generated free-text topic sets that achieves both high IAA and automated execution.

**Key Insight**: Decomposing the vague question of "whether a topic set is good" into five distinct quality dimensions measurable via simple annotation tasks.

**Core Idea**: Deconstruct complex topic set evaluation into three simple annotation tasks (Interpretability $I$, Relevance $R$, Overlap $O$), and subsequently derive five quality scores from them.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Given a document set $D$ and a set of $N$ FT-topics $T_f$ generated by system $f$, T5Score computes five quality scores ($C_I, C_T, C_D, V_T, K_T$) based on three annotator measurements (Interpretability, Relevance, Overlap). These annotations can be performed either by humans or automatically by an LLM.

### Key Designs

1. **Interpretability $C_I$**: Annotating $I(t) \in [0,1]$ for each topic $t$ to measure whether annotators can infer the underlying theme from the topic text. For example, "sadness" is too ambiguous in the Holocaust context to be interpretable. $C_I$ is defined as the average of $I(t)$.
2. **Topic Coverage $C_T$**: Annotating the relevance $R(t,d) \in [0,1]$ for each pair of $(topic, document)$. $C_T$ is the mean of all $R(t,d)$, measuring whether the topic set covers the primary themes of the document set. A high $C_T$ indicates that the extracted topics successfully "tell the story" of the document collection.
3. **Document Coverage $C_D$**: $C_D = \min_d \max_t R(t,d)$, representing the best matching score for the most poorly covered document. A low $C_D$ implies that important topics have been missed.
4. **Non-Overlap $V_T$**: Combining definitional overlap $O(t, t')$ (whether two topics describe the same theme) and coverage overlap (whether two topics cover the same documents), $V_T$ measures the redundancy within the topic set. Higher $V_T$ indicates less overlap among topics.
5. **Inner-Order $K_T$**: Using Kendall's $\tau$ to compare the default order of topics in the set with the order sorted by their average relevance $r_t$, measuring whether important topics are ranked at the front.

### Loss & Training

This work does not involve model training. During the automated evaluation phase, an LLM (such as GPT-4, LLaMA-3 70B, etc.) is used as a judge. Prompts are designed for each of the three measurement tasks to perform zero-shot annotation. Continuous scores (0-100 scale) are utilized instead of discrete labels.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Measurement Task | Annotation Items | Number of Annotators | Krippendorff's $\alpha$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpretability $I(t)$ | 550 | 3 | 0.66 |
| Relevance $R(t,d)$ | 1583 | 4 | 0.67 |
| Overlap $O(t,t')$ | 464 | 2 | 0.78 |

→ All three measurements achieve high IAA ($\alpha > 0.6$), far surpassing existing topic evaluation methods.

### Ablation Study (LLM as Automated Evaluator)

| Model | Quantization | Relevance $\rho$ | Overlap $\rho$ | Interpretability $\rho$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4 | - | **0.66** | 0.86 | 0.63 |
| GPT-3.5 | - | 0.50 | 0.79 | **0.73** |
| GPT-4o Mini | - | 0.61 | **0.87** | 0.61 |
| LLaMA-3 70B | None | 0.62 | 0.87 | 0.66 |
| LLaMA-3 70B | 4-bit | 0.48 | 0.24 | 0.29 |
| Mixtral 8x7B | None | 0.50 | 0.73 | 0.65 |

→ GPT-4 performs best overall; LLaMA-3 70B (unquantized) is the best open-source alternative; 4-bit quantization severely disrupts performance.

### Key Findings

- T5Score achieves high IAA in human evaluation, resolving the long-standing issue of low consistency in FT-topics evaluation.
- The Spearman correlation between the LLM judge and human annotations generally exceeds 0.6, supporting its adoption for automation.
- In generation system ranking experiments, T5Score correctly reflects the expected system rankings: GPT-4 > GPT-3.5 > LDA+GPT > LDA+Prefix > Random.
- There is a significant trade-off between coverage and non-overlap: high-level topics improve coverage but increase overlap.
- The T5Score performance of human-generated topic sets is comparable to that of GPT-4, validating the effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Decomposed Evaluation Philosophy**: Breaking down the ambiguous "quality" question into five orthogonal dimensions, with each dimension measured by a simple task—this mindset offers broad inspiration for other NLG evaluation tasks.
- **Dual-mode Applicability**: The same methodology supports both human evaluation (yielding high IAA) and LLM-based automated evaluation (yielding high correlation), demonstrating high practicality.
- **Discovery of the Coverage-Overlap Trade-off**: Combined methods might score exceptionally high on a single dimension but perform poorly overall, whereas LLM-based methods are more balanced—providing guidance for the design of topic generation systems.
- **Clever Dataset Selection**: Utilizing Holocaust survivor testimonies (providing similar yet unique experiences) is inherently suited for multi-document topic analysis.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Specificity of the USC-SF dataset: The same experienced events in testimonies might be scattered across different fragments, which potentially leads to incoherent context after merging.
- Lack of cross-domain validation: Multi-News is the only non-USC-SF dataset, indicating limited domain coverage.
- The aggregation method of the five dimensions has not been deeply researched; the paper acknowledges that the weights of different dimensions vary significantly across scenarios.
- The performance of the LLM judge drops sharply under 4-bit quantization, which is unfriendly to resource-constrained scenarios.
- The sensitivity of evaluation results to topic granularity (the value of $N$) has not been explored.

## Related Work & Insights

- **WD-Topic Evaluation** (Chang et al., 2009; intrusion test): Only applicable to word-distribution topics and unsuitable for FT-topics → T5Score fills the gap in FT-topics evaluation.
- **Semantic Similarity Metrics** (BERTScore, Zhang et al., 2019): Not sufficiently reliable in the context-poor FT-topics scenario.
- **LLM-as-Judge** (Fu et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023): T5Score adopts this paradigm but incorporates specific designs tailored for topic evaluation tasks.
- Insights: Decomposed evaluation can be generalized to tasks such as summarization, KG construction, and claim verification.

## Rating

- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Decomposing FT-topics evaluation into five quantifiable dimensions is a novel and practical contribution.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Complete experimental chain containing an IAA study, LLM judge comparison, generative system ranking validation, and human-generated set validation.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The formal definitions are clear, but the abundance of formulas slightly increases reading overhead.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Provides the first reliable methodological framework for FT-topics evaluation, which is highly practical for the topic modeling community.
