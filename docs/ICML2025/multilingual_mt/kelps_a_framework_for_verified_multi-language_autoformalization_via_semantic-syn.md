# KELPS: A Framework for Verified Multi-Language Autoformalization via Semantic-Syntactic Alignment

**Conference**: ICML2025  
**arXiv**: [2507.08665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08665)  
**Code**: Provided in supplementary material  
**Area**: Autoformalization  
**Keywords**: Autoformalization, Knowledge Equation, Assertional Logic, Lean4, Coq, Isabelle, Multi-Language Translation, Data Synthesis

## TL;DR

Proposed an intermediate representation based on assertional logic, called Knowledge Equation (KE), to achieve rule-based translation from natural language mathematical statements to multiple formal languages (Lean4/Coq/Isabelle), achieving an 88.9% pass@1 syntax accuracy on MiniF2F and outperforming DeepSeek-V3 and Herald.

## Background & Motivation

- **Core Problem**: Automatically translating informal natural language mathematical statements into machine-verifiable formal theorems (autoformalization). Existing methods rely on direct LLM translation, which is limited by the scarcity of high-quality parallel NL-FL corpora and typically only supports a single target formal language.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**:
    1. The cost of annotating large-scale NL-FL data is extremely high;
    2. The quality of formal statements directly generated by LLMs is unstable, and semantic alignment is difficult to guarantee;
    3. Existing works rarely support simultaneous translation into multiple formal languages (Lean, Coq, Isabelle).
- **Design Motivation**: To design a controllable intermediate representation layer (Knowledge Equation) such that the "natural language $\to$ intermediate representation $\to$ multiple formal languages" pipeline can guarantee structure and semantic consistency through rules, while reducing dependence on the volume of parallel corpora.

## Method

### 3.1 Assertional Logic and Knowledge Equation

The theoretical foundation of KELPS is **Assertional Logic (AL)**—an extended first-order logic system whose core idea is to express all mathematical knowledge uniformly in the form of assertions:

$$a = b$$

where $a, b$ are terms, which can be atomic individuals $a \in \mathcal{I}$, or composite terms $O(a_1, \ldots, a_n)$ formed by operators acting on individuals. The syntactic structure of AL is defined as a triple $\langle \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{O} \rangle$:

- $\mathcal{I}$: Set of individuals (corresponding to elements in set theory)
- $\mathcal{C}$: Set of concepts (corresponding to sets in set theory)
- $\mathcal{O}$: Set of operators (corresponding to functions/operations)

Building on this, the **Knowledge Equation (KE)** decomposes a mathematical problem into three components:

1. **Declaration**: `var : ConceptType`, declaring the concept type to which a variable belongs.
2. **Fact**: Recording known conditions in assertion form, unifying propositions and equations by utilizing the equivalence $A \equiv (A = \text{True})$.
3. **Query**: Isomorphic to Fact, but representing the proposition to be proved or solved.

### 3.2 Rule-based Translation from KE to Formal Languages

The core translation rules are one-to-one mappings of concepts and operators:

$$C_{\text{KE}} \mapsto C_{\text{TL}}, \quad O_{\text{KE}} \mapsto O_{\text{TL}}$$

A BNF grammar parser is implemented via ANTLR4 to automatically convert KE into target languages such as Lean4, Coq, and Isabelle. Unlike methods like GFLean that directly parse natural language, KELPS only needs to process structurally simple assertions, concepts, and operators, reducing the complexity of rule design.

### 3.3 Three Phases of the KELPS Framework

1. **Semantic Parsing**: DeepSeek-Math-7B-Base is used as the base model and fine-tuned on 1,200 human-annotated samples. Then, 7 rounds of expert iteration are performed, successfully parsing over 50K verified samples in total.
2. **Syntax Validation**: Grammar checking is first performed using the ANTLR4 KE parser, followed by final validation using the target language compiler. Approximately 80-90% of KEs pass compilation.
3. **Semantic Validation**: DeepSeek-V3 is utilized as an LLM judge to evaluate semantic alignment between the formal statements and the original natural language expressions on a 0-5 scale. A sample@3 majority voting mechanism is applied, where at least two perfect scores are required for a translation to be considered semantically correct.

### 3.4 Data Synthesis Strategy

- **Mathematical Ontology Construction**: Covers K12 to undergraduate mathematics, including 6 major domains, 40 core concepts, and 180 operators.
- **Data Sources**: Approximately 70K formalizable problems are filtered from the Numina dataset, yielding 50K problems after 7 rounds of iterative translation.
- **Template Synthesis**: LLM 3-shot learning is utilized to generate templates based on concept-operator combinations, and a composition template strategy is applied to enhance problem diversity and complexity. Over 50 high-quality templates are constructed, resulting in a final corpus of over 60,000 NL-FL parallel pairs.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results: Comparison with Baseline Models (pass@1)

| Model | MiniF2F Syntax | MiniF2F Semantics | Numina-Hard Syntax | Numina-Hard Semantics | FormalMATH Syntax | FormalMATH Semantics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek-V3 (671B) | 81.0% | 4.00 | 82.0% | 4.02 | 58.3% | 2.90 |
| Herald (7B) | 81.3% | 3.58 | 85.5% | 3.30 | 73.8% | 3.11 |
| LLaMA-3 (8B) | 61.4% | 2.63 | 62.3% | 2.58 | 37.7% | 1.56 |
| **KELPS (7B)** | **88.9%** | **4.05** | **94.3%** | **4.49** | **74.3%** | **3.29** |

### Ablation Study: Impact of Synthetic Data Ratio

| Real:Synthetic | Data Volume | MiniF2F Syntax | Numina-Hard Syntax | FormalMATH Syntax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:0 | 14K | 81.6% | 91.7% | 60.1% |
| 1:0.5 | 21K | 87.6% | 94.5% | 70.3% |
| 1:1 | 28K | 88.4% | 93.4% | 70.9% |
| 1:1.5 | 35K | **88.9%** | **94.3%** | **74.3%** |

### Key Findings

- KELPS (7B) outperforms DeepSeek-V3 with 671B parameters across all benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the structured intermediate representation.
- Adding only 7K of synthetic data yields a significant improvement (MiniF2F syntax: 81.6% $\to$ 87.6%), though marginal returns diminish beyond ~28K.
- KELPS maintains consistently high performance across Lean4 and Isabelle, validating the stability of cross-formal-language translation.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Novel Intermediate Representation Design**: The Knowledge Equation is based on assertional logic, unifying all mathematical knowledge into the form of $a = b$, which dramatically simplifies the rule design for translating into multiple target formal languages.
2. **Small Model Beats Large Model**: KELPS with 7B parameters completely outperforms DeepSeek-V3 with 671B parameters in syntactic accuracy, proving the value of structured prior knowledge.
3. **Multi-Language Support**: It is the first autoformalization framework supporting Lean4, Coq, and Isabelle simultaneously, rather than being restricted to a single target language.
4. **Controllable Data Synthesis**: The template synthesis strategy based on concept-operator ontology allows precise control over the type and difficulty of the generated problems at the atomic level.
5. **Complete Verification Pipeline**: Double safeguards for data quality are provided via syntax validation (compiler) + semantic validation (LLM judge + majority voting).

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Limited Ontology Coverage**: Currently covers only K12 and partial undergraduate mathematics, with no support yet for advanced mathematics (such as algebraic topology or category theory).
2. **Ceiling of Template Synthesis**: Synthetic data is constrained by template diversity, showing limited improvement on competition-level complex problems (such as FormalMATH).
3. **Semantic Validation Dependency on LLMs**: Relying on DeepSeek-V3 as a judge introduces bias and cannot completely replace human verification.
4. **Focus on Statement Autoformalization Only**: The framework only handles statement autoformalization and does not address the autoformalization of proofs (proof autoformalization).
5. **Rule Maintenance Cost**: Adding new formal languages or extending operators requires human design of mapping rules, presenting an upper bound on scalability.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Herald (Gao et al., 2025)**: Synthesizes data through FL$\to$NL back-translation and excels on FormalMATH, but does not support multi-language translation.
- **GFLean (Pathak, 2024)**: Directly parses natural language to Lean based on grammatical frameworks, but its coverage is limited by syntactic rules.
- **Numina (Li et al., 2024)**: The largest open-source K12 mathematics dataset, which serves as the foundation for building the training corpus in this work.
- **Key Insight**: The paradigm of intermediate representation + rule-based translation can be generalized to formalization tasks in other domains (such as program verification and formalization of legal statutes).

## Rating

- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The design concept of using the Knowledge Equation as an intermediate representation is novel, and the introduction of assertional logic provides theoretical depth.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Evaluation across multiple benchmarks and ablation studies are comprehensive, though cross-comparisons of pass@k (k>1) are missing.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The structure is clear, with rich tables and figures, and the definitions and formal specifications are rigorous.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — This is the first multi-formal-language autoformalization framework, with both the dataset and code open-sourced, showing great potential for practical applications.
