# Stealing That Free Lunch: Exposing the Limits of Dyna-Style Reinforcement Learning

**Conference**: ICML 2025  
**arXiv**: [2412.14312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14312)  
**Code**: [CLeARoboticsLab/STFL](https://github.com/CLeARoboticsLab/STFL)  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning / Model-Based RL  
**Keywords**: Dyna, Model-Based RL, MBPO, ALM, Benchmark, DeepMind Control  

## TL;DR

This paper reveals that Dyna-style model-based reinforcement learning algorithms (MBPO, ALM), despite performing exceptionally well on OpenAI Gym, **fail catastrophically** in the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC). Through a systematic analysis of factors including model error, overestimation bias, and loss of plasticity, it shows that even with a perfect model, MBPO cannot consistently outperform SAC, demonstrating that there is "no free lunch."

## Background & Motivation

The **Dyna architecture** (Sutton, 1991) is a core paradigm in model-based RL: learning an environment dynamics model $p_\theta(s', r | s, a)$ and augmenting training with synthetic data generated by the model. Representative algorithms include:

- **MBPO** (Janner et al., 2019): Adds model ensembles on top of SAC to generate synthetic rollouts, ~1100 citations.
- **ALM** (Ghugare et al., 2022): Jointly learns latent representations and a world model on top of DDPG.

These methods substantially outperform model-free methods on the **OpenAI Gym** benchmark. However, this paper discovers a **startling fact**:

> On the **DeepMind Control Suite (DMC)**, the "Dyna augmentation" of MBPO and ALM not only fails to improve performance but actually **prevents any policy improvement**—the performance remains at the level of a random policy.

These two benchmarks utilize the **same MuJoCo physics engine** and offer **similar tasks** (hopper, walker, humanoid, etc.). However, despite having around 1100 citations, MBPO has almost never been evaluated on DMC.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

This work does not propose a new algorithm but instead systematically diagnoses the failure of Dyna-style methods on DMC. The research methodology consists of:

1. **Re-implementing MBPO**: Highly efficient JAX-based implementation, speeding up training by ~40$\times$ (from 100+ GPU-days down to ~4 GPU-days).
2. **Cross-benchmark Comparison**: Comparing MBPO vs SAC, and ALM vs DDPG on 6 Gym + 15 DMC environments.
3. **Systematically ruling out potential failure modes**.

### Key Designs

**Experiment 1: Basic Performance Comparison**
- MBPO consistently outperforms SAC across all 6 Gym environments.
- MBPO shows **absolutely no policy improvement** in 6/15 DMC environments.

**Experiment 2: Model Error Analysis**
Defines the **percentage model error**: $\frac{\|\hat{y} - y\|_2}{\|y\|_2} \times 100$

- Gym: Model error converges to <25%.
- DMC hopper: Model error >100%.
- Other failed DMC tasks: >25%.

Introduces the **synthetic-to-real data ratio $S$**: a continuum from SAC ($S→0$) to MBPO ($S→0.95$).
- Increasing $S$ consistently degrades the episodic return.
- humanoid-stand: Catastrophic degradation occurs even with a tiny fraction of synthetic data.

**Experiment 3: Perfect Model Experiment**
Replacing the learned model with the ground-truth simulator, taking environment states directly from the replay buffer to perform real rollouts:
- The perfect model enables policy improvement in some of the environments that previously failed to show improvement.
- However, **in 4/6 environments, MBPO with the perfect model still fails to outperform SAC**.

$→$ Conclusion: Model error is not the sole culprit.

**Experiment 4: Q-value Divergence Analysis**
- The critic of MBPO **severely underestimates** or predicts zero returns (compared to SAC).
- Dyna augmentation **amplifies overestimation** in quadruped tasks.
- Mitigating this using Layer Normalization still fails to consistently outperform SAC.

**Experiment 5: Loss of Plasticity**
- Periodic resetting of model parameters: No significant improvement.
- Periodic resetting of Actor/Critic/temperature parameters:
    - Quadruped tasks: MBPO improves substantially, even outperforming SAC.
    - Other tasks: Still no improvement.
    - However, SAC + reset is equally applicable and generally superior.

### Loss & Training

The core components of MBPO remain unchanged:
- **World Model**: An ensemble of probabilistic neural networks predicting $p_\theta(s', r | s, a)$ as a Gaussian distribution.
- **SAC Loss**: $J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}[\alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s)) + Q(s,a)]$
- Training is performed on a mix of synthetic and real data, with a replay ratio = 20 (compared to 1 for SAC).

ALM (a DDPG-based Dyna method) also **fails completely** in the same 6 DMC environments, but DDPG works normally once the Dyna augmentation is removed.

| Implementation | Seconds per environment step |
|------|-----------|

Speedup: ~40$\times$ vs. original MBPO, and ~4$\times$ vs. ALM.

### Ablation Study

1. **Synthetic data ratio $S$**: The higher the $S$, the worse the performance, confirming that synthetic data acts as poison.
2. **Hyperparameter Search**: Comprehensive searching of model-related hyperparameters on hopper-stand and humanoid-stand yielded no improvement.
3. **Periodic Reset of Actor/Critic**: Only effective on quadruped, ineffective on other tasks.
4. **DreamerV3**: Belongs to the same Dyna family but achieves SOTA on DMC, indicating the existence of "successful subclasses" and "failed subclasses."

## Highlights & Insights

1. **"No Free Lunch"**: Dyna's core promise—accelerating learning using synthetic data—does not hold up across broader benchmarks; synthetic rollouts can indeed be detrimental.
2. **Benchmark Overfitting**: The community's heavy reliance on a limited set of benchmarks (OpenAI Gym) has led to an overestimation of the robustness of Dyna-style methods.
3. **Even a Perfect Model is Insufficient**: Even when excluding model error, MBPO still underperforms compared to SAC, indicating a deeper issue—possibly related to learning dynamics under a high replay ratio.
4. **Two Categories of Dyna Methods**: DreamerV3 succeeds while MBPO/ALM fail, where the difference likely lies in **latent-space learning + decoupled training** versus **state-space synthetic data + joint training**.
5. **Reproducibility Crisis**: The original MBPO required over 100 GPU-days to reproduce key experiments, which this work reduces to 4 GPU-days.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Incomplete Exploration of All Possible Fixes**: Does not test every possible solution to mitigate these issues.
2. **Incomplete Explanation of Why DreamerV3 Succeeds**: Merely identifies the existence of successful vs. failed Dyna subclasses without exhaustive explanation.
3. **Limited to Proprioceptive Observations**: Does not evaluate visual observations (pixel inputs).
4. **MBPO as a Proxy**: Uses MBPO as a representative for the "failed subclass," meaning some conclusions might not generalize to all Dyna variants.
5. **Difficulty in Attributing Benchmark Differences**: The differences between Gym and DMC span multiple facets (reward structures, termination conditions, physical parameters), complicating attribution.

## Related Work & Insights

- **Dyna Architecture**: Sutton (1991), MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), ALM (Ghugare et al., 2022)
- **DreamerV3**: Hafner et al. (2025), a Dyna variant that succeeds on DMC
- **High Replay Ratio**: D'Oro et al. (2023), Nikishin et al. (2022)
- **Loss of Plasticity**: Lyle et al. (2022), Qiao et al. (2023)
- **Critic Divergence**: Thrun & Schwartz (1993), Nauman et al. (2024), Hussing et al. (2024)

## Rating

⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

An important "negative results" paper that exposes an unsettling blind spot in the RL community. The accelerated JAX implementation provides a practical tool for researchers. The diagnosis is systematic, though no algorithmic solution is proposed.
