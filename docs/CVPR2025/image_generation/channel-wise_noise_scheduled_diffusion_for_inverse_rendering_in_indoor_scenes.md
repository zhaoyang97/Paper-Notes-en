# Channel-wise Noise Scheduled Diffusion for Inverse Rendering in Indoor Scenes

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2503.09993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09993)  
**Code**: None (not mentioned)  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / 3D Vision  
**Keywords**: Inverse Rendering, Channel-wise Noise Scheduling, Diffusion Models, Pixel-wise Lighting, Indoor Scenes

## TL;DR
Proposed a channel-wise noise scheduling method that enables a single diffusion model architecture to achieve two inverse rendering modes: accuracy-first (SDM, $T=4$) and diversity-first (PDM, $T=1000$) through different noise schedules. Concurrently, an implicit lighting representation (ILR) is introduced to support pixel-wise environment map reasoning and real object insertion.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Single-image inverse rendering decomposes an RGB image into geometry (normal, depth), material (albedo, roughness), and lighting. Deterministic methods (Li et al., IRISformer, Zhu et al.) trained on large-scale synthetic datasets can provide accurate solutions but ignore the ambiguity of the solution space. LDM-based methods (RGB↔X, Kocsis et al.) leverage pre-trained priors to generate diverse solutions, but overlook the trade-off between diversity and accuracy, and predict each modality independently without handling dependencies.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Inverse rendering is inherently an ill-posed problem, as the same radiance can be generated by different combinations of material, lighting, and geometry. There is a need to possess both the ability to "provide a precise solution" and "express multiple plausible solutions," but these two objectives are contradictory. In addition, existing LDM-based methods rely on pre-trained three-channel autoencoders and cannot handle high-dimensional pixel-wise environment maps, limiting applications such as object insertion.

**Key Challenge**: The total steps $T$ of a diffusion model control the accuracy-diversity trade-off (large $T$ $\rightarrow$ high diversity but low accuracy, small $T$ $\rightarrow$ high accuracy but low diversity). Moreover, when multiple modalities are generated simultaneously, dependencies exist between different modalities (geometry $\rightarrow$ material $\rightarrow$ lighting), which a unified noise schedule fails to exploit.

**Goal**: How to achieve both accuracy and diversity in inverse rendering using diffusion models, while handling high-dimensional pixel-wise lighting.

**Key Insight**: Geometry has the lowest uncertainty (easiest to determine) while lighting has the highest. Applying different noise schedules to different modality channels (generating geometry first, then lighting) can leverage cross-modality dependencies to improve quality. The trade-off between accuracy and diversity can be optimized separately through the choice of $T$.

**Core Idea**: Apply noise schedules with different rates to three categories of channels (geometry, material, and lighting) to exploit modality dependencies, and use large $T$ (PDM) and small $T$ (SDM) to optimize diversity and accuracy respectively.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
Inputting a single RGB image, the framework outputs the normal map, depth map, albedo map, roughness map, and pixel-wise environment maps. First, all modalities are generated in a low-resolution ($60\times 80$) pixel space using a conditional diffusion model (rather than a latent space, since LDM autoencoders do not support environment maps). They are then upsampled to $240\times 320$ using an RGB-guided super-resolution model (SRM). Two variants are introduced: PDM ($T=1000$, progressive generation, diversity-first) and SDM ($T=4$, switchable generation, accuracy-first).

### Key Designs

1. **Channel-wise Noise Scheduling**

    - **Function**: Set different noise decay rates for different modality groups (geometry/material/lighting) to exploit cross-modality dependencies.
    - **Mechanism**: Divide the output $\mathbf{z}_0$ into three groups: geometry ($N, D$), material ($A, R$), and lighting ($\mathbf{f}$). A parameterized cosine scheduler $\bar{\alpha}_t = \cos(((b-s)\cdot t+s)\cdot 2\pi)^{2\tau}$ is used to control the noise of each group, where a larger $\tau$ leads to slower noise decay (later generation). PDM sets $\tau=(0.9, 1.2, 1.5)$ corresponding to geometry, material, and lighting, meaning geometry clears up first, and lighting clears up last. SDM ($T=4$) utilizes a binary noise schedule table: step 0 has geometry + material clean, step 1 has geometry + lighting clean, step 2 has material + lighting clean, and step 3 starts with full noise. This achieves the workflow where "one modality is predicted at each step, and the already generated modalities serve as conditions for the next step".
    - **Design Motivation**: Ablation studies verify that the generation order of geometry $\rightarrow$ material $\rightarrow$ lighting is optimal (with the reverse order being the worst), because geometry constrains material, and material constrains lighting.

2. **Implicit Lighting Representation (ILR) Improvements**

    - **Function**: Compress high-dimensional pixel-wise environment maps ($3\times 128\times H\times W$) into a compact 96-dimensional feature vector.
    - **Mechanism**: Design an MLP autoencoder where the encoder encodes the environment map into $\mathbf{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{96}$ after applying a log1p transformation and batch normalization. Three decoders separately reconstruct the environment map, shading, and specular radiance, while reconstruction employs expm1 inverse transformation back to HDR values during decoding. Compared to the original ILR of MAIR++ (which infers from material/geometry instead of encoding directly from the environment map), this ILR preserves ground-truth lighting information more accurately.
    - **Design Motivation**: Diffusion models directly handling HDR environment maps are unstable due to the extremely large scale of value range, and the three-channel autoencoder of LDMs cannot handle it. ILR provides a compact intermediate representation.

3. **Accuracy-Diversity Dual Model Design (PDM + SDM)**

    - **Function**: Utilize the same architecture but different $T$ values to achieve two complementary objectives.
    - **Mechanism**: Ablation studies show that as $T$ decreases from 1000 to 1, the MSE monotonically decreases (accuracy increases) but the variance approaches 0 (diversity is lost). Hence, PDM ($T=1000$, progressive noise scheduling, sampling 10 times to take the mean/best) is designed to display multiple plausible solutions, while SDM ($T=4$, switchable noise scheduling, deterministic inference) is designed to provide a single optimal solution. The two are complementary in different scenarios: SDM is superior in scenes with low ambiguity, whereas PDM excels in highly ambiguous scenarios.
    - **Design Motivation**: Inverse rendering needs to satisfy both the demand for "precise decomposition" (e.g., object insertion requires accurate lighting) and "expressing uncertainty" (e.g., several material-lighting combinations are all plausible).

### Loss & Training
The diffusion models are trained using the velocity prediction loss. The ILR autoencoder is trained with environment map reconstruction loss and rendering loss. SRM is trained with standard super-resolution loss. Training is conducted on the OpenRooms FF dataset, using CLIP image encoder features as cross-attention conditions.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (OpenRooms FF Synthetic Data, MSE $\times 10^{-2}$)

| Method | Normal↓ | Depth↓ | Albedo↓ | Lighting↓ | Re-render↓ |
|------|---------|--------|---------|-----------|------------|
| Li et al. | 3.782 | 0.135 | 0.869 | 15.49 | 0.491 |
| Zhu et al. | 1.738 | 0.062 | 0.528 | *31.15 | *3.565 |
| **SDM** | **1.502** | **0.039** | **0.349** | **13.35** | **0.156** |
| PDM(Mean) | 1.831 | 0.047 | 0.394 | 14.08 | 0.150 |

### Ablation Study

| Generation Order $(\tau_{\text{geo}}, \tau_{\text{mat}}, \tau_{\text{light}})$ | Normal MSE↓ | Albedo MSE↓ | Lighting MSE↓ |
|----------------------------------|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| **(0.9, 1.2, 1.5) Geometry $\rightarrow$ Material $\rightarrow$ Lighting** | **Best** | **Best** | **Best** |
| (1.5, 1.2, 0.9) Lighting $\rightarrow$ Material $\rightarrow$ Geometry | Worst | Worst | Worst |

User study (object insertion): SDM selection rate is **32.2%**, which is close to the ground truth of 34.8% and significantly outperforms the baselines (15-17%).

### Key Findings
- Channel-wise noise scheduling significantly outperforms unified scheduling. The generation order validates the intuition: geometry $\rightarrow$ material $\rightarrow$ lighting.
- SDM ($T=4$) achieves the highest accuracy among all single-view methods, with its albedo MSE even surpassing the multi-view method MAIR.
- The improved ILR yields a re-rendering MSE (0.156) that is over 3$\times$ better than Li et al. (0.491), demonstrating the critical importance of HDR lighting representation.
- Accuracy-diversity trade-off of the $T$ value: $T=1000$ yields a variance of 0.183 but an MSE of 2.236, whereas $T=1$ yields a variance of 0 but an MSE of 1.817.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Channel-wise Noise Scheduling** is a general technique for multimodal diffusion: when multiple output modalities possess a natural dependency order, different schedules can be employed to "generate stable modalities first, then uncertain ones", thereby reducing error accumulation.
- The observation of **separating accuracy and diversity via $T$** is simple yet practical: two complementary models can be obtained from the same architecture using two different $T$ values.
- **ILR's log-space MLP autoencoder** effectively solves the encoding challenge of HDR environment maps.

## Limitations & Future Work
- It is necessary to maintain two independent models (PDM + SDM), and a unified solution is still lacking.
- Pre-trained LDM priors are not reused (trained from scratch on synthetic data), causing the generalization ability to real-world scenes to be limited by the training data.
- Operating at a low resolution ($60\times 80$) followed by super-resolution may lead to a loss of fine details.
- The training data is limited to the OpenRooms FF synthetic dataset.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs Li et al. / Zhu et al.**: Deterministic methods that do not consider the ambiguity of solutions. SDM comprehensively outperforms them in accuracy, while PDM provides diversity.
- **vs RGB↔X**: LDM-based methods that predict each modality independently and do not support environment maps. Ours considers cross-modality dependencies and supports pixel-wise lighting.
- **vs MAIR++**: A multi-view method that utilizes ILR but does not encode directly from environment maps. The proposed ILR is more accurate.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The channel-wise noise scheduling and SDM designs are innovative, though the overall framework is somewhat engineering-heavy.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Synthetic, real-world, user study, and ablation experiments are comprehensive, but evaluation on real-world scenes is relatively limited.
