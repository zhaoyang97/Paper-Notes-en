# MCMoE: Completing Missing Modalities with Mixture of Experts for Incomplete Multimodal Action Quality Assessment

**Conference**: AAAI 2026
**arXiv**: [2511.17397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17397)
**Code**: [https://github.com/XuHuangbiao/MCMoE](https://github.com/XuHuangbiao/MCMoE)
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: Incomplete multimodal learning, action quality assessment, mixture of experts, modality completion, cross-modal fusion

## TL;DR

This paper presents the first exploration of incomplete multimodal action quality assessment (AQA), proposing the MCMoE framework. An Adaptive Gated Modality Generator (AGMG) completes missing modalities, while a Mixture of Experts (MoE) module with soft routing dynamically fuses unimodal and cross-modal joint representations within a unified single-stage training paradigm. MCMoE achieves state-of-the-art performance under both complete and incomplete modality settings across three public AQA benchmarks, with only 4.90M parameters.

## Background & Motivation

Multimodal AQA leverages complementary cues from RGB, optical flow, and audio to achieve fine-grained discrimination among highly similar action sequences. In practice, however, modality unavailability frequently arises due to sensor failures, environmental constraints, or privacy concerns. Existing multimodal models assume all modalities are available at both training and inference time, and fail catastrophically when any modality is absent.

Prior solutions fall into two categories: (1) *modality completion* methods that reconstruct missing data using heavy generative models such as diffusion models, VAEs, or GANs, incurring high computational cost; and (2) *cross-modal joint representation learning* methods that extract joint features but typically rely on two-stage training pipelines, increasing training complexity.

The root cause of the challenge is: how to efficiently learn both modality-specific and cross-modal shared knowledge within a single-stage training framework, without relying on heavy generative models, so as to compensate for the interference caused by missing modalities.

The core idea of this paper is to exploit the complementarity between Multimodal Modality Completion (MMC) and Mixture of Experts (MoE): MMC supplies high-confidence features to support unimodal and joint learning for MoE, while MoE in turn dynamically refines and repairs the features generated by MMC, reducing dependence on high-fidelity heavy generators.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

**Input**: Video features from three modalities — RGB ($v$), optical flow ($f$), and audio ($a$) — extracted by frozen pretrained encoders (VST/I3D/AST). During training, all modalities are visible, and random masking simulates incomplete scenarios; at inference, missing modalities are initialized with zero vectors.

**Pipeline**: Feature extraction → Shared Temporal Enhancement Module (STEM) → Adaptive Gated Modality Generator (AGMG) for missing modality completion → Mixture of Experts (MoE) with soft router for dynamic fusion → Cross-modal Fusion Module (CFM) → rank-based regression network → score prediction.

### Key Designs

1. **Shared Temporal Enhancement Module (STEM)**:

    - **Function**: Maps heterogeneous modality features into a shared latent space to bridge cross-modal semantic gaps.
    - **Mechanism**: A stack of 3-layer Transformer encoders (2-head self-attention) processes each modality's features with shared parameters to capture cross-modal commonalities.
    - **Design Motivation**: Avoids direct cross-modal feature interaction at an early stage, laying the groundwork for subsequent unimodal learning while enhancing temporal context modeling.

2. **Adaptive Gated Modality Generator (AGMG)**:

    - **Function**: Dynamically generates feature representations for missing modalities from available ones.
    - **Mechanism**: A 2-layer, 4-head cross-attention mechanism is employed — available modality features are concatenated as Key/Value, while the zero-initialized missing modality serves as Query, iteratively refined over $L$ layers. A gating layer then dynamically weights and fuses the outputs, adjusting fusion weights according to the completeness of the current input.
    - **Design Motivation**: (1) Cross-attention progressively completes missing modalities using available information; (2) the gating layer suppresses error propagation from imperfect generation; (3) the design is lightweight and efficient compared to heavy GAN/diffusion models.

3. **Mixture of Experts (MoE) + Soft Router**:

    - **Function**: Dedicated unimodal experts (lightweight 2-layer MLPs) are designed for each modality, and all expert knowledge is dynamically mixed to extract cross-modal joint representations.
    - **Mechanism**: For features of modality $v$, each of the visual, optical flow, and audio experts processes the input independently. A soft router (2-layer MLP + Softmax) dynamically estimates the importance weight of each expert given the input, and a weighted sum yields the final feature.
    - **Design Motivation**: (1) Each expert mines modality-specific knowledge while collectively capturing cross-modal patterns; (2) the soft router adapts to diverse missing modality combinations; (3) an alignment loss forces the joint representation to align with unimodal representations, enabling unified single-stage learning.

4. **Cross-modal Fusion Module (CFM)**:

    - **Function**: Captures inter-modal correlations and maps them to a task-specific latent space.
    - **Mechanism**: A simple two-layer convolutional block suffices for effective fusion, as MoE-processed features already carry rich cross-modal semantics.
    - **Design Motivation**: Leverages the cross-modal information already captured by MoE, avoiding complex fusion architectures.

### Loss & Training

A weighted sum of four loss functions:
- **Reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$**: MSE, supervising AGMG to generate high-fidelity features.
- **Alignment loss $\mathcal{L}_{align}$**: KL divergence, aligning joint representations with unimodal representations to drive unified single-stage learning.
- **Diversity loss $\mathcal{L}_{div}$**: Triplet loss, ensuring that patterns of different quality ranks attend to distinct action characteristics.
- **Task loss $\mathcal{L}_{task}$**: MSE, for regression score prediction.

Random masking is applied during training to simulate incomplete scenarios. The Adam optimizer is used with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule.

### Key Findings
- AGMG is the largest contributor to performance gains, demonstrating that dynamic modality completion is critical for bridging the semantic gap caused by missing data.
- A significant complementarity exists between MMC and MoE: MMC provides high-confidence inputs to MoE, while MoE provides dynamic refinement for MMC.
- All three auxiliary losses contribute independently; removing any one degrades performance, with the reconstruction loss having the greatest impact.
- Performance peaks at rank count $N=4$; MCMoE requires only 4.90M parameters and 1.34 GFLOPs, far below competing methods.
- t-SNE visualizations clearly demonstrate that MCMoE effectively separates action quality levels of different grades.

## Highlights & Insights

- **MMC–MoE Complementary Design**: The core insight of the paper; the two components mutually reinforce each other. MMC reduces the difficulty of MoE processing zero matrices, while MoE relaxes the requirement for high-fidelity generation, forming a virtuous cycle.
- **Single-Stage Unified Learning**: The alignment loss elegantly bypasses the complexity of two-stage training pipelines.
- **Extreme Lightweight Design**: Only 4.90M parameters and 1.34 GFLOPs, far below competing methods (e.g., GCNet: 8.78M/1191 GFLOPs).
- **Gating Design in AGMG**: The gating layer suppresses error propagation from imperfect generation, yielding greater robustness than directly using generated features.
- **Scalability**: The framework can be flexibly extended to an arbitrary number of modalities.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Validation is limited to three modalities (RGB, optical flow, audio); additional modalities such as text and skeleton have not been explored.
- The framework is AQA-task-specific; generalizability to other incomplete multimodal tasks remains uncertain.
- AGMG generation quality degrades under extreme modality scarcity (e.g., audio only).
- Scenarios where modality absence also occurs during training have not been investigated.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. MoMKE**: MoMKE is a two-stage method; MCMoE achieves unified single-stage learning with fewer parameters.
- **vs. ActionMAE**: ActionMAE reconstructs missing modalities via MAE in a single stage but cannot fully exploit cross-modal fusion.
- **vs. PAMFN**: PAMFN performs best under complete modality settings but cannot handle incomplete scenarios.
