# IDProtector: An Adversarial Noise Encoder to Protect Against ID-Preserving Image Generation

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2412.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11638)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/showlab/IDProtector](https://github.com/showlab/IDProtector)  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / AI Security  
**Keywords**: Face Privacy Protection, Adversarial Perturbation, Identity-Preserving Generation, Feed-forward Attack, Encoder Attack

## TL;DR

IDProtector proposes the first feed-forward adversarial noise encoder that adds imperceptible adversarial perturbations to face photos via a single forward pass. It can simultaneously defend against multiple encoder-driven identity-preserving generation methods such as InstantID, IP-Adapter, and PhotoMaker, while remaining robust to transformations like JPEG compression and resizing.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Recent zero-shot identity-preserving generation methods (e.g., InstantID, IP-Adapter) extract identity information from a single portrait photo using pre-trained face encoders, generating identity-preserving images with just a single inference. These methods make unauthorized face tampering extremely easy, posing severe privacy and security threats.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing defense methods (e.g., Anti-DreamBooth, AdvDM, PhotoGuard) primarily target fine-tuning-based methods (DreamBooth/LoRA/Textual Inversion) and are almost completely ineffective against encoder-driven zero-shot methods. They also suffer from four key limitations: (1) lack of universality (attackers can easily bypass them by switching methods); (2) based on PGD step-by-step optimization per image, taking several minutes per photo and failing to handle large-scale photo protection on social media; (3) lack of robustness against image transformations; (4) high visibility of perturbations.

**Key Challenge**: Encoder-driven methods have evolved from domain-agnostic encoders (CLIP) to domain-specific encoders (ArcFace), resulting in complex and diverse attack surfaces. It is necessary to simultaneously attack the feature spaces of multiple encoders while balancing efficiency, universality, robustness, and imperceptibility.

**Goal**: Design a feed-forward face privacy protection scheme that meets the EURI criteria (Efficiency, Universality, Robustness, Imperceptibility).

**Key Insight**: Reformulate the defense problem from "per-image optimization" to "learning a universal noise encoder", which achieves universal protection by jointly attacking key feature layers of multiple encoders during training.

**Core Idea**: Train a ViT encoder to predict adversarial perturbations in a single forward pass, achieving universal protection against multiple identity-preserving methods by jointly attacking the key embedding layers of ArcFace and CLIP encoders.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

Given a face image of any size, it is first resized to 224×224 and fed into the ViT-S/8 adversarial noise encoder to output a three-channel perturbation, which is then resized back to the original size and added to the original image. During training, the protected image passes through the face feature extraction pipeline of various identity-preserving methods, and learning is performed by minimizing the cosine similarity between the original and protected images in multiple encoder feature spaces.

### Key Designs

1. **ViT Adversarial Noise Encoder**:

    - **Function**: Generate adversarial perturbations for any face image via a single forward pass.
    - **Mechanism**: Using a ViT-S/8 architecture, it takes a 224×224 4-channel image (RGB + face localization mask) as input and outputs a 3-channel perturbation. The mask channel is generated by the InsightFace pipeline based on facial landmarks, identifying the location of the face region. The perturbation is normalized to $[-1,1]$ using tanh, scaled to $[-\epsilon, \epsilon]$, and then resized back to the original image size to be added.
    - **Design Motivation**: PGD per-image optimization takes several minutes and is unsuitable for large-scale protection on social media. The feed-forward approach enables real-time protection. The additional mask channel relieves the network from the burden of learning face localization, reducing training difficulty.

2. **Joint Attack Objectives and Selection of Attack Surfaces**:

    - **Function**: Simultaneously disrupt key features of CLIP and ArcFace encoders to achieve universal protection against various identity-preserving methods.
    - **Mechanism**: Analyze the feature extraction pipelines of InstantID (using ArcFace), IP-Adapter (using CLIP output), IP-Adapter-Plus (using the penultimate layer features of CLIP), and PhotoMaker (using CLIP output). Select attack target embeddings based on three principles: (a) block all information pathways—ensuring each information flow passes through at least one attacked embedding; (b) select earlier layers where possible to shorten the backpropagation path; (c) select semantically dense embeddings for easier optimization. The final adversarial loss is the weighted sum of individual model losses: $L_{adv} = \sum_i \alpha_i \cdot \text{cossim}(e_i, e_i')$
    - **Design Motivation**: An attacker can switch methods at any time, making a single-target attack worthless. By carefully selecting attack surfaces, simultaneous defense against four mainstream methods is realized at a limited optimization cost.

3. **Enhancing Robustness to Affine Transformations**:

    - **Function**: Maintain effectiveness of adversarial perturbations against common image processing such as JPEG compression, scaling, and affine transformations.
    - **Mechanism**: Leverage the existing face-alignment step in InstantID—precompute the affine transformation matrix $A$ for each image, and add small Gaussian noise during training to simulate transformations: $\tilde{p}' = (A + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma I))\tilde{p}$, where $\sigma=0.003$. This augmentation is applied only to the InstantID branch, but experiments show other branches also gain robustness improvements from it.
    - **Design Motivation**: Traditional methods stack various differentiable augmentations (differentiable JPEG, random cropping, etc.) during training, which imposes a heavy learning burden. Adding noise by reusing the existing face alignment pipeline achieves robustness to multiple transformations at minimal cost.

### Loss & Training

The total loss is $L = L_{adv} + L_{reg}$, where the regularization statement $L_{reg} = \beta_1 \|\delta\|_1 + \beta_2 \|\delta - \text{clip}_{\pm\epsilon}(\delta)\|_1$ controls the visibility of the perturbation. A three-stage curriculum learning is adopted: gradually shrinking the $\epsilon$-ball (0.05→0.04→0.035) and adjusting the weights of each model's loss. It is trained on 4 H100 GPUs with a batch size of 112 for 10 days.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results

| Protection Method | InstantID ISM ↓ | IP-Adapter ISM ↓ | IP-Adapter-Plus ISM ↓ | PhotoMaker ISM ↓ |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| No Protect | 0.775 | 0.176 | 0.240 | 0.265 |
| Anti-DB | 0.753 | 0.155 | 0.222 | 0.255 |
| PhotoGuard | 0.758 | 0.152 | 0.223 | 0.259 |
| SimAC | 0.752 | 0.162 | 0.217 | 0.253 |
| **IDProtector** | **0.231** | **0.060** | **0.114** | **0.123** |
| IDProtector (PGD) | -0.166 | 0.024 | 0.072 | 0.094 |

A lower ISM (Identity Similarity Match score) indicates better protection. IDProtector significantly outperforms the baselines across all methods, particularly bringing the ISM of InstantID down from 0.775 to 0.231.

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | InstantID ISM ↓ | Explanation |
|------|-----------------|------|
| Full model | 0.231 | Full model (CelebA test set) |
| PGD upper bound | -0.166 | Performance upper bound of step-by-step optimization per image |
| No Protect | 0.775 | Unprotected |
| Affine augmentation ablation | Robustness degraded significantly | Removing affine noise augmentation |

### Key Findings

- Existing defense methods targeting fine-tuning-based approaches (Anti-DB, AdvDM, etc.) are almost completely ineffective against encoder-driven methods, where InstantID ISM is only reduced from 0.775 to around 0.75.
- IDProtector remains effective on the unseen VGG Face dataset, demonstrating cross-dataset generalization ability.
- It also shows protective effects on closed-source commercial models (Midjourney, Jing Gou), proving the universality of the selected attack surfaces.
- The PGD version (per-image optimization) performs better but is several orders of magnitude less efficient, while the feed-forward version achieves a good balance between efficiency and effectiveness.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Methodology of attack surface analysis is highly instructive**: By analyzing the encoder pipelines shared by multiple methods, it identifies key embedding layers for joint attacks, reflecting the philosophy of "attacking commonalities rather than individualities". This analysis can be transferred to other multi-model defense scenarios.
- **The elegance of affine noise augmentation**: Instead of stacking various differentiable augmentations, it simply introduces tiny noise to the existing face alignment pipeline, achieving robustness to multiple transformations at extremely low cost.
- **Advancing from theory to practice**: The feed-forward encoder pushes adversarial protection from the "laboratory level" to the "deployable level", bringing practical value for privacy protection on social media.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The perturbation upper bound $\epsilon=9/255 \approx 3.5\%$, although scarcely perceptible to the naked eye, still has detectability, and more extreme compression might weaken the protection.
- In some cases, the Face Detection Rate suffers a drop (which in itself is a form of protection), but it may affect normal usage scenarios.
- Trained only on CelebA, face diversity is limited (mostly celebrities), so its protection effectiveness on general people requires further verification.
- As new identity-preserving methods emerge (e.g., using new encoders), retraining or extending target attacks might be necessary.
- High training cost (4×H100 training for 10 days), leaving room for improvement in reducing training overhead.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs Anti-DreamBooth**: Anti-DB designs PGD attacks targeted at fine-tuning methods and is ineffective against encoder methods. IDProtector specifically analyzes encoder attack surfaces, successfully defending against zero-shot methods.
- **vs PhotoGuard/AdvDM**: These methods attack the generation process of diffusion models, whereas IDProtector attacks the more upstream feature extraction process, offering better universality.
- **vs Glaze**: Glaze protects artistic style while IDProtector protects face identity. Although their attack targets differ, their methodologies can complement each other—the design concept of feed-forward encoders can be transferred to style preservation fields.
