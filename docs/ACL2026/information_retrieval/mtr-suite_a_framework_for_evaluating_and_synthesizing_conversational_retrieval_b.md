# MTR-Suite: A Framework for Evaluating and Synthesizing Conversational Retrieval Benchmarks

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2605.20729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20729)  
**Code**: https://github.com/rangehow/mtr-suite  
**Area**: Information Retrieval / Conversational Retrieval  
**Keywords**: Conversational Retrieval, RAG Evaluation, Synthetic Benchmarks, LLM-as-a-Judge, Greedy Traversal Clustering  

## TL;DR
MTR-Suite proposes a complete framework encompassing benchmark auditing, conversational data synthesis, and retrieval evaluation. It utilizes MTR-Eval to diagnose annotation quality and MTR-Pipeline to generate MTR-Bench, a high-difficulty multi-turn retrieval benchmark, at approximately 1/400 of the cost of human labor.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: The factual upper bound of RAG is largely determined by the retrieval module. If the retriever fails to find the correct evidence, even the strongest generator cannot provide a reliable answer. As production systems enter multi-turn dialogue scenarios, conversational retrieval benchmarks become increasingly critical.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Human-annotated benchmarks are constrained by cognitive boundaries. Annotators typically see only local documents and struggle to identify if other documents in the corpus could also answer the current query, leading to annotation sparsity and false negatives. Although automated synthesis methods are cost-effective, many rely on static heuristics (e.g., rewriting Wikipedia section headers into questions), resulting in unnatural dialogues and unstable alignment between queries and gold documents.

**Key Challenge**: High-quality conversational retrieval benchmarks require a global perspective, natural language interaction, multi-turn contextual interference, and low-cost scalability. Human annotation is high-quality but expensive and local, while rule-based automation is cheap but rigid and may inherit local perspective issues.

**Goal**: To construct a unified framework capable of auditing the query-evidence/answer-evidence quality of existing benchmarks and automatically synthesizing multi-turn retrieval data that closely mirrors production RAG environments, while evaluating the actual shortcomings of modern retrievers using this data.

**Key Insight**: The paper decomposes the system into three components: MTR-Eval for evaluating benchmark quality, MTR-Pipeline for automated synthesis, and MTR-Bench, a general-domain benchmark generated by the pipeline. The core methodology includes LLM ensemble auditing, knowledge base cleaning, greedy traversal clustering, and three-agent dialogue generation.

**Core Idea**: Transitioning from local heuristics to global-aware automated annotation. This ensures that synthetic queries are not just based on a single document but are checked against global candidates and hard negatives to ensure the evidence is unique, complete, and credible.

## Method
The methodology of MTR-Suite can be summarized as "auditing what constitutes a good benchmark first, then synthesizing benchmarks according to those standards." MTR-Eval uses LLM-as-a-Judge to assess four types of quality issues in existing data. MTR-Pipeline utilizes high-quality document snippets, semantic path clustering, and multi-agent generation to create natural multi-turn dialogues. Finally, MTR-Bench stress-tests retrievers using more complex topic switching, verbose answers, and recent knowledge bases.

### Overall Architecture
MTR-Eval takes a conversational retrieval benchmark as input, where each turn consists of conversation history $H_i$, current query $q_i$, and a gold document set $G_i$. The system evaluates whether the gold documents truly support the query, whether more suitable evidence was missed, if the answer is faithful to the evidence, and the linguistic quality of the answer itself.

MTR-Pipeline starts from a raw corpus, performing non-text cleaning, recursive chunking, and MinHash-LSH deduplication, followed by filtering high-information snippets using an NVIDIA quality classifier and FineWeb-EDU scorer. Subsequently, greedy traversal clustering constructs continuous semantic paths in the embedding space, segmented by a fixed cluster size. Finally, three agents generate the dialogue: the Questioner simulates user inquiries and topic switches, the Responder generates strictly grounded answers, and the Polisher adds anaphora, ellipsis, and natural expressions.

### Key Designs
1. **MTR-Eval Four-Dimensional Audit**:
    - **Function**: Quantifies the annotation quality of retrieval benchmarks rather than just evaluating model scores.
    - **Mechanism**: Query-Evidence Alignment checks if the gold document answers the query; Evidence Completeness performs discriminability testing via a hard negative pool; Answer-Evidence Faithfulness checks if answers are supported by evidence; Answer Quality independently evaluates linguistic quality.
    - **Design Motivation**: Achieving high recall on benchmarks with sparse annotations or high noise is meaningless; auditing the benchmark itself is necessary to interpret model scores.

2. **Greedy Traversal Clustering**:
    - **Function**: Constructs semantically related but non-redundant document sequences for multi-turn dialogues.
    - **Mechanism**: Starting from a random point, each step selects the nearest unvisited neighbor to form a continuous semantic path, which is then divided into clusters every $k$ nodes. Each document is visited only once, and cluster size remains controllable.
    - **Design Motivation**: K-means or DBSCAN can produce uncontrollable cluster sizes, and threshold-based neighbor methods often lead to overlapping clusters. A greedy path is better suited to simulate users browsing progressively along links or topics.

3. **Three-Agent Dialogue Synthesis**:
    - **Function**: Generates multi-turn retrieval data that is both gold-grounded and resembles real user interactions.
    - **Mechanism**: The Questioner generates queries based on document clusters and history; the Responder answers based only on specified gold documents; the Polisher rewrites the dialogue to include anaphora, ellipsis, natural topic switching, and production-style verbose answers.
    - **Design Motivation**: Single-agent generation tends to be rigid or fails to maintain grounding. Multi-agent division of labor balances naturalness, evidence alignment, and controllability.

### Loss & Training
This work focuses on benchmark synthesis and evaluation and does not involve training retrieval models. MTR-Eval employs a multi-LLM ensemble and pointwise scoring to mitigate self-preference bias and position bias. Document order is randomly shuffled during Discriminability Testing. The generation cost of MTR-Pipeline is estimated at approximately $0.005 per dialogue, which is about 1/400 of the $1.50-$2.00 cost for crowdsourced benchmarks like Doc2Dial.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results
MTR-Bench is constructed based on a Wikipedia 2025-01 dump to prevent models from relying directly on old knowledge memorization. The scale of the data is shown below.

### Key Findings
- In main retrieval experiments, existing retrievers often achieve 90+ Recall@20 on old benchmarks but show significant declines on MTR-Bench. The paper reports that average Recall@20 on prior benchmarks is 43.54 points higher than on MTR-Bench.
- Expanding the retrieval window on MTR-Bench yields limited returns: while old benchmarks see an average improvement of 15.06 points moving from R@5 to R@20, MTR-Bench only improves by 8.68 points, indicating that gold evidence is harder to retrieve into the candidate set.
- Representative results show gte-modernbert-base achieving 50.29 / 59.31 for R@5 / R@20 on MTR-Bench, gte-Qwen2-7B at 39.75 / 53.23, and Dragon-ChatQA at 43.84 / 50.96.
- Extended Recall@k shows that even at $k=1000$, full recall is not reached: bge-large-en-v1.5 is at 47.0, ChatQA-Context at 67.4, and gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct at 82.2.
- Oracle query rewriting brings 20%-40% R@5 improvement, suggesting that the difficulty arises primarily from the complexity of conversational language (anaphora, ellipsis, topic switching, verbose history) rather than the 2025 knowledge base being completely unretrievable.

## Highlights & Insights
- The core contribution of MTR-Suite is not just "another benchmark" but the systematization of benchmark quality auditing. This helps explain why certain datasets yield high recall: it could be strong models, or it could be overly simple data or noisy annotations.
- Greedy Traversal Clustering is a highly pragmatic yet effective design that simultaneously addresses cluster size, repeated sampling, and natural topic flow.
- The ablation of the Polisher is convincing: without it, human accuracy in identifying machine-generated questions rose from 62% to 79%, proving that naturalistic rewriting effectively reduces synthetic traces.
- The "w/o Filter" setting shows higher recall but a "easier" benchmark, suggesting that evaluation sets are not necessarily better if they are easier to retrieve; high-quality benchmarks should possess both reliable annotations and sufficient discriminability.

## Limitations & Future Work
- MTR-Bench focuses explicitly on the retrieval component rather than end-to-end generation; while this keeps diagnostics clear, it does not directly measure the quality of the final response.
- E2E metrics like EM or BLEU conflict with the long-answer style of real-world RAG. Consequently, the paper avoids them, but future work is needed for end-to-end evaluations capable of handling long answers, evidence chains, and factuality.
- Wikipedia is relatively clean as a primary corpus. Although internal financial corpus validation was performed, proprietary domains, low-resource languages, and noisy knowledge bases require more public experimentation.
- Synthetic data remains dependent on LLM safety alignment and prompt constraints. Continuous auditing for generation bias, hallucinations, and potentially sensitive content will be required in the long term.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs QuAC / CoQA / Doc2Dial**: These manual or semi-manual datasets laid the foundation for multi-turn QA but suffer from human local perspective and cost issues. MTR-Suite scales this via global auditing and automated generation.
- **vs CORAL**: CORAL relies on Wikipedia's structural heuristics for synthesis, often resulting in rigid dialogues. MTR-Pipeline generates more natural query flows using multiple agents and semantic trajectories.
- **vs TREC CAsT / QReCC**: These datasets emphasize conversational retrieval, but sparse annotations and historical formats may not match production RAG. MTR-Bench explicitly includes verbose answers and hard topic switches.
- **Insight**: Enterprise RAG systems can use MTR-Pipeline as a continuous regression testing tool, automatically generating new benchmarks after every knowledge base update to detect if the retriever is keeping pace with data evolution.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ The combination of MTR-Eval + MTR-Pipeline + MTR-Bench is complete, and the benchmark auditing perspective is particularly valuable.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ Includes main evaluation, industrial financial domain validation, filter and Polisher ablations, and extended recall analysis.
- Writing Quality: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ The system structure is clear, with a tight correspondence between data design and experimental conclusions.
- Value: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Highly practical for RAG retriever evaluation, automated benchmark synthesis, and regression testing for enterprise retrieval systems.
