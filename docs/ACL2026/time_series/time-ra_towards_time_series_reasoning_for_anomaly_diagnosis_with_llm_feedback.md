# Time-RA: Towards Time Series Reasoning for Anomaly Diagnosis with LLM Feedback

**Conference**: ACL 2026
**arXiv**: [2507.15066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15066)  
**Code**: [yyysjz1997/Time-RA](https://github.com/yyysjz1997/Time-RA)  
**Area**: Time Series Analysis / LLM Reasoning
**Keywords**: Time series anomaly detection, anomaly reasoning diagnosis, multimodal benchmark, LLM fine-tuning, AI feedback annotation

## TL;DR

This paper introduces Time-RA, a new task that upgrades time series anomaly detection from binary classification to generative reasoning diagnosis (detection + classification + causal explanation). It constructs RATs40K, the first multimodal benchmark comprising ~40K samples across 10 domains and 20 anomaly types, and validates the feasibility of this paradigm through an AI feedback annotation pipeline and LLM fine-tuning.

## Background & Motivation

**State of the Field**: Time series anomaly detection (TSAD) is critical in finance, healthcare, AIOps, and industrial systems. Current deep learning methods predominantly treat it as a binary classification task (normal vs. anomalous), lacking fine-grained anomaly categorization and causal explanation.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (1) Traditional TSAD produces only binary labels without specific anomaly categories or diagnostic reasoning needed for root cause analysis; (2) existing benchmark datasets lack interpretive reasoning annotations and fine-grained anomaly classification; (3) multimodal TSAD datasets are mostly synthetic or narrowly scoped, failing to capture real-world complexity; (4) the reasoning capabilities of multimodal LLMs remain underexplored in the time series domain.

**Root Cause**: Anomalies are "detected" but not "explained"—understanding root causes is essential for preventive or corrective action, yet current methods stop at detection.

**Paper Goals**: (1) Define the Time-RA task, upgrading TSAD from discriminative to generative reasoning diagnosis; (2) construct a large-scale multimodal benchmark dataset to support this task; (3) systematically evaluate LLM/MLLM capabilities in time series reasoning diagnosis.

**Starting Point**: Time series anomaly diagnosis is structured as a three-stage Observation–Thought–Action workflow, aligned with the diagnostic reasoning process of human analysts, enabling LLMs to acquire this diagnostic capability through structured prompting.

**Core Idea**: Reframe TSAD as a multi-objective generative task (detection + classification + reasoning), and train LLMs to perform human-like diagnostic reasoning via a multimodal dataset (numerical + textual + visual) combined with an AI feedback annotation pipeline.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The Time-RA workflow consists of: (1) **Data Collection**—~40K time series segments are collected from 10 real-world scenarios, accompanied by textual descriptions and visualization charts; (2) **Reasoning Annotation**—initial annotations are generated by a pool of four strong models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, DeepSeek-R1, Llama-3.3-70B), then refined through GPT-4 preference ranking and critique feedback; (3) **Model Fine-tuning**—LLMs/MLLMs are fine-tuned with LoRA using structured prompts that include anomaly definitions and few-shot examples; (4) **Multi-dimensional Evaluation**—binary classification accuracy, multi-class classification accuracy, and text reasoning quality are assessed separately.

### Key Designs

1. **Fine-grained Anomaly Taxonomy**:

    - Function: Provides a standardized classification reference for time series anomalies.
    - Mechanism: Synthesizing the literature, 14 univariate anomaly types (point anomalies, trend drift, nonlinear pattern anomalies, etc.) and 6 multivariate anomaly types (trend deviation, joint contextual anomalies, etc.) are formally defined. Each type is accompanied by a formal definition, example time series, and real-world scenario description. Each sample is annotated with a binary label and a specific anomaly category.
    - Design Motivation: Traditional datasets provide only binary labels, which cannot support root cause analysis; fine-grained classification enables LLMs to distinguish different anomaly patterns and provide targeted explanations.

2. **AI Feedback Annotation Pipeline**:

    - Function: Generates high-quality reasoning annotations at low cost.
    - Mechanism: A three-stage process—(a) *Reasoning completion sampling*: four model pool instances generate diagnostic reasoning and anomaly classification for each sample; (b) *Preference annotation*: GPT-4 ranks model pool outputs using a Likert scale and selects the best result; (c) *Critique generation*: GPT-4 provides specific improvement suggestions for the best result, which are integrated into the final annotation. In total, 158K model outputs and 150K+ feedback data points are generated.
    - Design Motivation: Manually annotating 40K reasoning explanations is infeasible; the layered refinement of multi-model pooling, preference ranking, and critique improvement ensures annotation quality approaching expert level.

3. **Structured Diagnostic Prompting**:

    - Function: Guides LLMs to perform time series diagnosis in a manner consistent with human analyst reasoning.
    - Mechanism: Prompts adopt a role specification ("time series anomaly detection expert") and decompose the task into three stages—Observation (input time series and domain knowledge), Thought (analyzing behavioral patterns, variable relationships, and deviation patterns), and Action (outputting anomaly categories). Prompts include a complete anomaly category list with natural language definitions and few-shot examples.
    - Design Motivation: Structured prompting simulates the human diagnostic reasoning process, ensuring clarity, consistency, and interpretability of outputs.

### Loss & Training

Standard SFT objective: $\max_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(x,y) \sim \mathcal{D}} [\log P_\theta(y|x)]$, where input $x = \{T, D, V\}$ consists of time series data, textual description, and visual chart, and output $y = \{y_l, a, r\}$ consists of the detection label, anomaly category, and reasoning explanation. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning is performed using LoRA (rank=8, alpha=32).

### Key Findings

- SFT fine-tuning substantially improves LLM anomaly detection and reasoning capabilities, validating the effectiveness of the Time-RA task paradigm.
- The visual modality (time series charts) contributes positively to diagnostic accuracy, with multimodal input outperforming text-only input.
- Fine-tuned models exhibit strong plug-and-play transfer to unseen real-world datasets, outperforming conventional TSAD baselines.
- Performance degrades more significantly in scenarios with more complex anomaly types and more extensive cross-variable relationships, indicating that Time-RA remains an open frontier.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Paradigm Redefinition of TSAD**: The shift from binary classification to a three-layer output of "detection + classification + reasoning" fundamentally upgrades anomaly detection to anomaly diagnosis, better reflecting the needs of real-world operations analysts. This task formulation is itself a significant contribution.
- **Engineering Value of the AI Feedback Annotation Pipeline**: The three-stage pipeline of multi-model pooling, preference ranking, and critique improvement constitutes a reusable large-scale, high-quality annotation paradigm applicable to any scenario requiring reasoning annotations.
- **Scale and Diversity of RATs40K**: With 10 real-world domains, 20 anomaly types, and ~40K samples, the dataset fills a critical data gap in time series reasoning diagnosis.

## Limitations & Future Work

- Despite GPT-4 refinement, AI feedback annotations may still contain biases; expert validation covers only a subset of samples.
- Absolute performance on binary and multi-class classification tasks still has considerable room for improvement.
- The anomaly ratio is high (83.7%), which may bias models toward predicting anomalies.
- Future directions include: online/streaming anomaly detection settings, integration of domain-specific knowledge graphs to enhance reasoning, and development of interactive diagnostic systems.

## Related Work & Insights

- **vs. Traditional TSAD (e.g., Anomaly Transformer)**: Traditional methods output only binary labels; this work extends them with anomaly classification and reasoning explanations, providing actionable diagnostic information.
- **vs. LLMAD/AnomLLM**: These prior works explore LLM-based TSAD but suffer from small data scale, sparse annotations, and absence of reasoning; RATs40K comprehensively surpasses them in scale, diversity, and annotation depth.
