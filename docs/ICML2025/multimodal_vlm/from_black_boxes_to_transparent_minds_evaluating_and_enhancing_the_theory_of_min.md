# From Black Boxes to Transparent Minds: Evaluating and Enhancing the Theory of Mind in Multimodal Large Language Models

**Conference**: ICML2025  
**arXiv**: [2506.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14224)  
**Code**: [Project Page](https://annaisavailable.github.io/GridToM)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM  
**Keywords**: Theory of Mind, Multimodal Large Language Models, Interpretability, Attention Intervention, GridToM  
**Authors**: Xinyang Li, Siqi Liu, Bochao Zou, Jiansheng Chen, Huimin Ma

## TL;DR

This work evaluates the Theory of Mind (ToM) capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) from an interpretability perspective, constructs a 2D grid world-based multimodal ToM dataset named GridToM, and proposes a training-free attention head activation intervention method to significantly enhance the ToM performance of MLLMs.

## Background & Motivation

**Core Problem**: Do Large Language Models (LLMs) or Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) truly possess the Theory of Mind (ToM) — the capability to infer others' beliefs, intentions, and cognitive states?

**Limitations of Prior Work**:

**Unimodal Limitations**: Most ToM assessments are restricted to a single modality, either text or video, lacking integrated multimodal evaluations.

**Black-Box Evaluation**: Existing assessment methods rely almost entirely on the accuracy of question-answering (QA) outputs to judge whether a model possesses ToM, overlooking whether the model establishes internal cognitive representations that distinguish between different perspectives.

**Lack of Perceptual Control**: Real-world video datasets (e.g., MMToM-QA) cannot precisely control whether a character "sees" a specific object, making the evaluation accuracy susceptible to the quality of perceptual information.

**Hallucination Bias**: MLLMs are prone to hallucinations, wherein a model may "understand" a concept but still produce incorrect answers. Consequently, QA accuracy does not fully reflect genuine ToM capabilities.

**Design Motivation**: Initiated from the model's internal mechanisms, this work utilizes interpretability methods to investigate whether MLLMs form internal representations in their intermediate layers that distinguish belief states from different perspectives. Based on these insights, targeted interventions are introduced to boost ToM performance.

## Method

### 1. GridToM Dataset Construction

A 2D grid world dataset is built leveraging the Multigrid/Minigrid libraries:

- **Scale**: 1,296 video-text pairs with a resolution of 294×420, approximately 40 frames per video.
- **Scenario**: A 10×7 grid, containing 3 rooms and 2 agents.
- **Configuration Diversity**: 27 maps $\times$ 2 initial positions $\times$ 2 orientations $\times$ 6 movement sequences $\times$ true/false belief pairings.
- **Task Types**: Initial belief test, first-order belief test, and second-order belief test.
- **Key Designs**: Prompt-blocking information behind closed doors to simulate realistic perceptual boundaries; providing complete physical perspective information for each agent.
- **Test set of 500 samples**, with a training/validation set of 148 samples (split 75%/25%).

The defining difference from previous datasets is that GridToM offers controllable, multimodal visual-linguistic causal narratives with full perceptual details for all characters. Additionally, the 2D grid world abstracts away high-level semantic distractions, allowing the evaluation to focus strictly on core ToM reasoning.

### 2. Attention Feature Extraction

The model concatenates the visual inputs $V=\{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_m\}$ and textual inputs $X=\{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\}$ into a single unified sequence $T = \text{concat}(V, X) \in \mathbb{R}^{(m+n) \times DH}$, which is processed through $L$ Transformer layers.

Multi-head attention is approximated as:

$$T_{l+1} = T_l + \sum_{h=1}^{H} Attn_l^h(P_l^h T_l) \cdot W_l^o$$

where $P_l^h \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times DH}$ projects activations into the $D$-dimensional attention head space, and $W_l^o$ represents the output projection matrix.

The activations $X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times H \times D}$ are extracted at the last token position for each attention head across every layer, and are mapped to two belief-related labels:
- $Y_p$: Correctness of the protagonist's perspective belief.
- $Y_o$: Correctness of the omniscient perspective belief.

The label design reconciles both **perspective separation** and **belief reasoning**: $Y_p = \{Y_p^{TB} \cap Y_p^{FB}\}$.

### 3. Probing

An independent logistic regression binary probing classifier is trained for each attention head:

$$f_l^h = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(x\theta + b)}}$$

where $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the weight vector, and $b \in \mathbb{R}$ is the bias, optimized by minimizing cross-entropy loss.

**Key Findings**:
- A substantial number of attention heads, spanning from intermediate to final layers, accurately encode the belief states of the protagonist.
- The four belief combinations (TB/FB $\times$ True/False) form distinct, non-overlapping clusters within the representation space.
- The belief representations exhibit high **linear separability**, demonstrating that MLLMs indeed establish internal intermediate representations linked to multi-perspective info extraction and belief reasoning.

### 4. Attention Intervention

The top-$K$ attention heads displaying the highest sensitivity on the validation set are selected. Adjustments are introduced after multi-head attention calculation and prior to output mapping:

$$T_{l+1} = T_l + \sum_{h=1}^{H} \left(Attn_l^h(P_l^h T_l) + \alpha \sigma_l^h \theta_l^h \right) \cdot W_l^o$$

- $\sigma_l^h$: Standard deviation of the activation along the target direction.
- $\theta_l^h$: Target direction of the intervention, obtained from the probe's weight vector.
- $\alpha$: Intervention intensity parameter.

This approach is **entirely training-free**. It only requires training linear probes on a small set of samples to identify the target direction and shifts the activations along this direction during inference.

## Key Experimental Results

### Baseline Evaluation (GridToM First-Order Belief, Multimodal Setting)

| Model | TB(%) | FB(%) | Both(%) |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| Human | 99.9 | 99.9 | 99.8 |
| GPT-4o | 6.2 | 100.0 | 6.2 |
| Doubao-1.5-vision-pro | 16.8 | 100.0 | 16.8 |
| LLaVA-Next-Video-7B | 53.2 | 42.8 | 0.8 |
| Qwen2-VL-7B | 26.6 | 97.0 | 23.6 |

### Improvement After Intervention (Multimodal, First-Order Belief)

| Model | TB(%) | FB(%) | Both(%) |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| LLaVA-Next-Video +α | 63.8 (+10.6) | 51.6 (+8.8) | 22.0 (+21.2) |
| Qwen2-VL +α | 60.4 (+33.8) | 97.4 (+0.4) | 31.2 (+7.6) |

### Key Findings

1. **TB/FB Imbalance**: GPT-4o reaches 100% on FB but only 6.2% on TB. This suggests that the model heavily relies on FB patterns rather than genuinely understanding belief states.
2. **Extremely Low "Both" Metric**: All evaluated MLLMs perform poorly on "Both" (answering both TB and FB correctly), revealing the structural fragility of their reasoning capabilities.
3. **Text vs. Video**: Under text-only conditions, LLMs (e.g., Doubao-1.5-Pro-32k) achieve up to 100% accuracy, yet performance plummeting dramatically under multimodal/video settings.
4. **Second-Order Belief is Harder**: All examined models perform near random-guess levels (~50%) on second-order belief tasks.
5. **Effectiveness of Intervention**: The training-free attention intervention delivers notable gains of +7.6% to +21.2% on the "Both" metric.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Evaluating ToM through interpretability**: By inspecting internal representations rather than relying on raw QA outputs, this work successfully mitigates confounding factors like hallucinations.
2. **Discovery of linear separability**: The study empirically demonstrates that particular attention heads within MLLMs actively encode perspective-specific belief states in a linearly separable manner.
3. **Training-free intervention**: This lightweight method requires only minimal probing to locate direction vectors, enhancing ToM performance during inference with high efficiency.
4. **Slick dataset design**: The 2D grid world effectively isolates high-level semantic distractions, enabling precise perceptual control for cleaner evaluation.
5. **Introduction of the "Both" metric**: Measuring simultaneous correctness on both TB and FB tasks prevents falsely high evaluations caused by biased heuristic patterns.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. **Over-simplification of scenarios**: The 2D grid world remains far removed from real-world visually complex settings; whether these findings generalize to complex visual scenes warrants further validation.
2. **Limited model coverage**: The intervention experiments are verified only on LLaVA-Next-Video-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B, lacking internal analysis on larger closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o) due to engineering constraints.
3. **Hyperparameter dependence**: The optimal selection of $K$ and $\alpha$ relies on the validation set, requiring potential adjustments across different tasks and model architectures.
4. **Interventions limited to GridToM**: Due to dataset constraints in MMToM-QA, the intervention was only validated on GridToM, leaving open questions on real-world generalization.
5. **Probing constraints**: Linear probes might overlook non-linearly encoded belief information. Consequently, high probing accuracy does not fully validate that "the model truly understands ToM."

## Reproducibility Key Points

- The dataset is generated utilizing the open-source Multigrid/Minigrid libraries, with data accessible on the project page.
- Probes leverage standard logistic regression, which introduces minimal computational overhead.
- The intervention method is training-free, easily integrated into the forward pass by applying shifts.
- Generation temperature is set to 0. Inputs consist of 4 keyframes + 3 intermediate frames.
- The test set contains 500 samples, and the training/validation set has 148 samples (split 75%/25%).

## Personal Commentary

The core contribution of this work lies in shifting ToM evaluation from "guessing capabilities via black-box outputs" to "verifying capabilities through internal representations," providing a more rigorous methodology. Although the GridToM dataset uses simple environments, this simplicity guarantees precise control over variables, making it a solid experimental paradigm. The discovery of linear separability in attention heads is an insightful empirical finding that opens new avenues for inspecting cognitive structures in MLLMs.

Nonetheless, from a practical standpoint, ToM in a 2D grid world is vastly different from ToM in complex real-world social environments. Being able to distinguish perspectives in a grid world does not guarantee a model can do so in complex human videos. While the intervention method is lightweight and effective, the absolute performance gains remain modest (the "Both" metric tops out at 31.2%), indicating a substantial gap remains compared to human-level performance (99.8%). Moreover, an interpretative gap exists between "the model possessing internal belief representations" and "the model genuinely understanding beliefs" — linearly separable features might still be a byproduct of statistical correlation rather than causal, conceptual execution.
