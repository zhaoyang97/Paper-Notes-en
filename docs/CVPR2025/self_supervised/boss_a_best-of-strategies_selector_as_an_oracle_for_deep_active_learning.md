# BoSS: A Best-of-Strategies Selector as an Oracle for Deep Active Learning

**Conference**: CVPR 2025  
**arXiv**: [2603.13109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13109)  
**Code**: To be confirmed  
**Area**: Active Learning / Data Selection  
**Keywords**: Active Learning, Oracle Strategy, Strategy Ensemble, Batch Selection, Deep Learning

## TL;DR
Proposes BoSS—a scalable active learning oracle strategy that generates candidate batches by ensembling multiple selection strategies, evaluates performance gain by freezing the backbone and retraining only the last layer, and selects the optimal batch. It showcases oracle performance on large-scale datasets like ImageNet for the first time, revealing that SOTA active learning strategies still have significant room for improvement.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Active learning (AL) reduces annotation costs by iteratively selecting the most valuable samples for labeling. Existing selection strategies are categorized into uncertainty-based (Margin, BADGE) and representativeness-based (TypiClust) methods, with recent works combining both (FastBAIT, DropQuery). In the foundation model era, pre-trained features like DINOv2 make sample selection easier.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: (a) No single strategy performs optimally across all model architectures, annotation budgets, and datasets—some strategies excel in early stages (TypiClust) but degrade later, while others show the opposite trend. (b) Existing strategies are based on heuristics (uncertainty/representativeness) and do not directly optimize model performance. (c) There is a lack of a reliable oracle baseline to evaluate how far existing strategies are from the optimal performance.

**Key Challenge**: Theoretical oracle strategies (approximating optimal selection using ground-truth labels) can serve as evaluation baselines, but existing oracles (SAS, CDO) cannot scale to large-scale datasets and deep networks—SAS suffers from computational explosion due to excessive search steps, and CDO requires frequent model retraining due to its sample-by-sample greedy selection.

**Goal**
   - How to build an oracle strategy that scales to large-scale datasets like ImageNet?
   - How large is the actual performance gap between existing SOTA active learning strategies and the oracle?
   - What are the patterns of performance variation for different strategies at different AL stages?

**Key Insight**: Instead of searching for the optimal combination of individual samples (which suffers from combinatorial explosion), the approach searches for the optimal batch among a set of candidate batches generated by existing strategies—compressing the search space from $\binom{|\mathcal{U}|}{b}$ to $T$ candidate batches.

**Core Idea**: Use multi-strategy ensembles to generate a small set of high-quality candidate batches, freeze the backbone and retrain only the last layer to rapidly evaluate the performance gain of each batch, and select the optimal one.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
BoSS decomposes the optimal batch selection problem $\mathcal{B}^* = \arg\min_{\mathcal{B} \subset \mathcal{U}} \mathbb{E}[\ell(y, p(y|x, \mathcal{L}^+))]$ into three efficiently solvable sub-problems: Candidate Batch Generation $\rightarrow$ Fast Retraining $\rightarrow$ Performance Evaluation and Selection.

### Key Designs

1. **Candidate Batch Generation (Algorithm 1)**

    - **Function**: Generates $T$ candidate batches from an ensemble of multiple AL strategies.
    - **Mechanism**: Selects 10 complementary strategies (Random, Margin, CoreSets, BADGE, FastBAIT, TypiClust, etc.), with each strategy generating $T/|S|$ batches. Key trick: For each run, randomly sample a candidate pool $\mathcal{C}$ of size $k$ from $\mathcal{U}$ and run the strategy on $\mathcal{C}$. Here, $k$ varies randomly between $[b, k_{max}]$ to prevent certain strategies from being unstable on large pools.
    - **Design Motivation**: The probability of finding a good batch through purely random generation is extremely low ($\binom{1000}{10} \approx 2.63 \times 10^{23}$), but batches generated by existing AL strategies have significantly higher quality than random ones. Thus, a small number of candidate batches can successfully cover the high-quality regions of the solution space. Furthermore, different strategies cover the needs of different AL stages—representativeness is needed in the early stages (TypiClust), while uncertainty is required in the later stages (BADGE).

2. **Fast Retraining (Freezing Backbone)**

    - **Function**: Quickly evaluates the performance improvement of the model after adding each candidate batch.
    - **Mechanism**: Freezes the pre-trained backbone $h^\phi$ (e.g., DINOv2-ViT) and retrains only the classification head $g^\theta$ (a linear layer). For each candidate batch $\mathcal{B}_t$, it is added to the labeled set $\mathcal{L}^+$, the classification head is refit, and the accuracy is computed on the evaluation set $\mathcal{E}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: Retraining an entire DNN once can take hours, whereas training only a linear layer takes mere seconds. Freezing the backbone does not compromise the quality of feature extraction (as pre-trained features are already highly expressive), drastically reducing the total cost of $T$ retraining runs.

3. **Performance Evaluation and Selection**

    - **Function**: Selects the candidate batch among the $T$ choices that maximizes the accuracy improvement.
    - **Mechanism**: $\mathcal{B}^* = \arg\min_{\mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{B}_1, ..., \mathcal{B}_T\}} \sum_{(x,y) \in \mathcal{E}} \mathbb{1}[y \neq \arg\max_c p(c|x, \mathcal{L}^+)]$
    - **Note**: This is an oracle strategy that utilizes GT labels and the test set—which is unavailable in practical AL environments and is used solely as an evaluation benchmark.

### Loss & Training
- Outer loop: Selects $b$ samples (batch size) for labeling in each AL cycle (e.g., $b=10$ for CIFAR-10, $b=1000$ for ImageNet).
- Inner evaluation: Trains only the linear classification head and evaluates using 0-1 loss.
- Default setting uses $T=100$ candidate batches (10 from each of the 10 strategies).
- Sliding window AL with a fixed number of $A$ cycles.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results (BoSS vs SOTA AL strategies, DINOv2-ViT-S/14)

BoSS consistently outperforms all SOTA AL strategies across 8 image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, Snacks, Flowers102, Food101, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet).

Key Findings (using ImageNet as an example):
- The accuracy improvement of BoSS is approximately **twice** that of the best AL strategy.
- The gap is most pronounced on multi-class large-scale datasets (100+ classes).

### BoSS vs Other Oracle Strategies

| Oracle Method | CIFAR-10 Runtime | DTD Runtime | Scalability |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|---------|
| BoSS | 10:07 | 22:07 | Scalable to ImageNet |
| CDO (Aligned) | 10:26 | 24:56 | Degrades under large batch sizes |
| SAS (Aligned) | 10:20 | 22:14 | Requires extremely high search steps |
| CDO (Default) | 10:26 | 2:46:14 | - |
| SAS (Default) | 17:22:39 | 62:34:13 | - |

### Ablation Study

| Configuration | CIFAR-10 AULC | DTD AULC |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Randomly generated candidate batches | Inferior to strategy-generated batches | Inferior to strategy-generated batches |
| T=10 (1 per strategy) | 89.90 | 70.45 |
| T=50 (5 per strategy) | 90.45 | 71.55 |
| T=100 (10 per strategy) | **90.70** | **71.79** |
| T=200 (20 per strategy) | 90.83 | 71.91 |

### Key Findings
- **Significant gap between SOTA strategies and the oracle**: Especially on large-scale multi-class datasets (Food101, ImageNet), where the gap can reach up to 2x. This indicates substantial room for improving AL strategies.
- **No single strategy is optimal across all AL stages**: Analysis of strategy selection frequency in BoSS shows that representation-based strategies are preferred early on (TypiClust\*, DropQuery\*), while even Random is chosen in the later stages (suggesting specialized strategies become ineffective at that point).
- **Increasing the number of candidate batches $T$ consistently improves performance**, but with diminishing marginal returns (notable improvement from T=10 $\rightarrow$ 100, minimal improvement from 100 $\rightarrow$ 200).
- **Incorporating more strategies is always beneficial** and does not cause degradation even if weak strategies are introduced, because the batches from weak strategies are simply not selected.
- **Freezing the backbone has minimal impact on performance evaluation**: Demonstrating that the quality of pre-trained features is sufficient for reliable performance ranking.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Shift in perspective from "selecting optimal samples" to "selecting the optimal batch"**: Avoids the combinatorial explosion of sample-by-sample greedy selection while accounting for the collective effect of samples within a batch. This insight can be transferred to other scenarios requiring subset selection (e.g., data distillation, curriculum learning).
- **Meta-learning approach to strategy ensembles**: Instead of pre-judging which strategy is best, it lets the data speak—automatically choosing the optimal strategy at each cycle. This serves as a parameter-free meta-AL.
- **Revealing future directions for AL research**: Better exploration strategies are needed during early stages (current strategies struggle with cold-starts on multi-class datasets), whereas better exploitation strategies are required in the later stages. Ensemble-based AL is a direction highly worth investigation.
- **Scalability design**: The two techniques of freezing the backbone and candidate pool sampling enable BoSS to run on ImageNet for the first time, providing a solid benchmark for large-scale AL evaluation.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **The oracle strategy itself is impractical for deployment**: It relies on GT labels and the test set, serving only as an evaluation benchmark. Future work includes converting the ensemble concept of BoSS into a practically usable, label-free version (perhaps replacing the test set with a validation set?).
- **Only image classification tasks were evaluated**: Object detection, segmentation, and NLP tasks are not yet covered.
- **Assumption of freezing the backbone**: In scenarios with a large domain gap or poor pre-trained features, freezing the backbone might lead to inaccurate performance evaluations.
- **Directions for improvement**: (1) Transform the strategy selection frequency analysis of the oracle into adaptive weights to construct a practical ensemble AL strategy; (2) Use a small labeled validation set to replace the test set for an approximate oracle; (3) Extend the approach to non-classification tasks like detection and segmentation.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs CDO (Werner 2024)**: CDO performs sample-by-sample greedy selection and requires frequent retraining when the batch size is large, limiting its scalability. BoSS directly evaluates the performance of the entire batch, rendering its advantages more pronounced with larger batch sizes.
- **vs SAS (Zhou 2021)**: SAS uses simulated annealing to search for the optimal sequence of the entire labeled set. Its computational cost is proportional to the number of search steps, taking 62 hours on DTD under default settings. BoSS achieves superior results in only 22 minutes.
- **vs BADGE**: BADGE is one of the most frequently recommended AL strategies, yet the oracle performance revealed by BoSS shows that BADGE still exhibits a significant gap on large-scale datasets.
