# Persona-E2: A Human-Grounded Dataset for Personality-Shaped Emotional Responses to Textual Events

**Conference**: ACL 2026  
**arXiv**: [2604.09162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09162)  
**Code**: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CRIS-Yang/Persona-E2-Dataset)  
**Area**: Social Computing / Affective Computing  
**Keywords**: Personality Modeling, Affective Evaluation, Reader Perspective, MBTI, Big Five

## TL;DR
Constructed Persona-E2, the first large-scale dataset linking personality traits (MBTI + Big Five) with reader emotional responses, containing 112k annotations from 3111 events × 36 annotators. The study reveals a "personality illusion" problem in LLMs when simulating personalized emotional responses and finds that Big Five traits are more effective than MBTI in mitigating this issue.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: Research in affective computing primarily focuses on emotions expressed by authors within text, neglecting emotional evaluation from the reader's perspective. Most existing datasets aggregate annotations into a single label, thereby obscuring the emotional diversity arising from individual personality differences.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Role-playing LLMs attempt to simulate personalized responses by injecting personality traits into prompts, but they often exhibit a "personality illusion"—mimicking superficial linguistic styles rather than genuinely adopting personality-based cognitive appraisal patterns. Crucially, there is a lack of real human data to verify whether LLMs truly capture personality-driven emotional diversity.

**Key Challenge**: Cognitive appraisal theory suggests that emotion stems from an individualized appraisal process influenced by goals and personality traits. However, the NLP field lacks benchmark datasets that systematically link personality traits with emotional responses. Pseudo-labels generated by LLMs cannot substitute for real human data in validation.

**Goal**: To construct a reader emotional response dataset with real-world personality annotations to (1) analyze how personality affects emotional appraisal, (2) evaluate the ability of LLMs to simulate personalized emotions, and (3) explore whether LLMs can generate psychologically sound reasoning.

**Key Insight**: Recruit real annotators with measured personality traits (MBTI + Big Five) to perform emotional labeling on events from three domains: news, social media, and life narratives. Each event receives 36 annotations to ensure dense coverage of personality diversity.

**Core Idea**: By combining real personality assessments, dense annotation (36 people/event), and cross-domain event coverage, this work builds the first personality-event-emotion benchmark dataset to systematically evaluate the impact of personality on emotional appraisal and the simulation capabilities of LLMs.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
The construction of Persona-E2 is divided into three stages: (1) Event collection and filtering—collecting events from news, social media, and life narratives, followed by safety filtering, LLM multi-dimensional scoring, and expert review to select 3111 high-quality events from 77,000 candidates; (2) Personalized annotation—recruiting 36 annotators to complete MBTI and Big Five questionnaires, with each person labeling their real emotional responses to all 3111 events; (3) Experimental evaluation of three research questions—analyzing emotional disagreement patterns, LLM simulation capabilities, and cognitive soundness.

### Key Designs

1.  **Multi-dimensional Event Filtering Pipeline**:
    - **Function**: To screen high-quality stimuli from a large pool of raw events that can effectively trigger personality-differentiated emotional responses.
    - **Mechanism**: Three-stage filtering: (a) NSFW classifier to filter harmful content; (b) Using Qwen3-MAX to score each event across four dimensions: Personality Variability (V), Emotional Arousal (A), Emotional Implicitness (I), and Source Relevance (R), calculating a weighted $Score = 0.35V + 0.30A + 0.20R + 0.15I$; (c) Final review by a 5-person expert panel.
    - **Design Motivation**: Selecting only those events to which "people with different personalities would react differently" maximizes the discriminative value of the dataset.

2.  **Personality-Aware Annotation Protocol**:
    - **Function**: To obtain emotional annotation data anchored in real personality traits.
    - **Mechanism**: 36 annotators first complete standardized MBTI and Big Five scales, then label each event from a reader's perspective ("How would you feel when reading this event?") using Ekman’s six basic emotions + neutral. No role-playing is involved to ensure data reflects real personality. 36 annotations per event ensure dense personality coverage.
    - **Design Motivation**: Unlike previous methods where annotators simulate specific personalities, this approach directly utilizes the annotators' own real personalities to ensure psychological validity.

3.  **Personality Agreement Gap (PAG) Verification**:
    - **Function**: To verify that the annotation disagreements in the dataset are driven by personality rather than random noise.
    - **Mechanism**: K-means clustering ($k=6$) is performed on Big Five vectors, calculating the difference between intra-group agreement $Agr_{in}$ and inter-group agreement $Agr_{out}$, defined as PAG. Experiments show that PAG for all clusters is positive (+8.27% to +25.96%), proving that people with similar personalities have more consistent emotional responses to the same event.
    - **Design Motivation**: PAG serves as an intrinsic validation metric for data quality, proving that annotation divergence is a structured personality signal rather than noise.

### Loss & Training
This work is a dataset paper and does not involve model training. The experimental section uses existing LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Qwen2.5, etc.) for personalized individual emotion prediction evaluation under zero-shot and few-shot settings.

## Key Experimental Results

### Main Results
LLM performance in simulating personalized emotional prediction (Weighted F1):

| Model | News | Social Media | Life Narratives | Average |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| GPT-4o (zero-shot) | 0.42 | 0.31 | 0.38 | 0.37 |
| Claude 3.5 | 0.40 | 0.29 | 0.36 | 0.35 |
| Qwen2.5-72B | 0.39 | 0.28 | 0.35 | 0.34 |
| + BFI prompt | 0.45 | 0.34 | 0.41 | 0.40 |
| + MBTI prompt | 0.43 | 0.32 | 0.39 | 0.38 |

### Ablation Study
Impact of personality information on LLM emotional prediction:

| Configuration | Weighted F1 | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No personality info | 0.37 | Baseline |
| + MBTI Label | 0.38 | Providing only the 4-letter type |
| + BFI Vector | 0.40 | Providing continuous personality dimension scores |
| + BFI + Cognitive Expl. | 0.42 | Simultaneously requesting the model to explain the reasoning process |

### Key Findings
- LLMs perform worst in the social media domain (F1 only 0.28-0.31) because social media text is more ambiguous and relies more on personal interpretation.
- Big Five traits are significantly superior to MBTI for mitigating the "personality illusion," likely because BFI provides continuous dimensions rather than discrete types.
- Author-reader emotional disagreement is largest in life narratives and smallest in news, proving that first-person projection amplifies individual differences.
- PAG verification shows that the ESTP type has the highest personality agreement (+26.98%), while ISTJ is the lowest (+9.68%).

## Highlights & Insights
- The core insight of the dataset design is profound: "Annotation disagreement is not noise but a personality signal." The PAG verification method can be generalized to any annotation task involving subjective judgment.
- The scale of 36 people × 3111 events = 112k annotations is unprecedented, and every annotation is anchored in real, measured personality traits, providing a valuable benchmark for personalized AI research.
- Systematic verification of the "personality illusion" concept reveals that LLMs do not truly understand the impact of personality on cognitive appraisal but are merely mimicking stereotypes.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The number of annotators is limited to 36, resulting in limited personality coverage; specifically, some MBTI types have fewer than 3 people, preventing statistical analysis.
- Only 7 basic emotion categories are used, failing to capture finer emotional dimensions (e.g., mixed emotions, emotional intensity).
- Events are primarily from English sources, reflecting limited cultural diversity.
- Future work could expand to more annotators and cultural backgrounds, exploring the impact of dynamic changes in personality traits on emotional appraisal.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs GoodNewsEveryone**: GNE includes author + reader perspectives but lacks personality annotations; Persona-E2 introduces real personality measurement for the first time.
- **vs Big5-Chat**: Big5-Chat uses LLMs to generate personalized dialogue data, lacking real human validation; Persona-E2 is based on the actual responses of real annotators.
