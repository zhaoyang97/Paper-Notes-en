# Convergence of an actor-critic gradient flow for entropy regularised MDPs in general spaces

**Conference**: ICLR2026  
**OpenReview**: [https://openreview.net/forum?id=KUlPxDQF3T](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KUlPxDQF3T)  
**Code**: None (Purely theoretical)  
**Area**: Reinforcement Learning Theory  
**Keywords**: actor-critic, entropy-regularized MDP, policy mirror descent, Fisher–Rao gradient flow, two-timescale, convergence proof

## TL;DR
This paper establishes the **stability and global convergence** of a coupled actor-critic gradient flow—where the critic utilizes TD learning and the actor employs Policy Mirror Descent (PMD)—for infinite-horizon MDPs with continuous state/action spaces and entropy regularization. The core conclusion is that the system avoids finite-time blow-up and converges to the optimal regularized value function at an exponential rate, provided the critic updates at an **exponentially faster timescale**.

## Background & Motivation

**Background**: The actor-critic (AC) framework is a cornerstone of RL, where the critic performs policy evaluation (estimating advantage/Q-functions) and the actor performs policy improvement. Trust-region methods like TRPO/PPO have become prevalent, with their "softened" version—**Policy Mirror Descent (PMD)**—being theoretically favored due to its first-order nature and known sublinear or linear convergence rates for entropy-regularized problems with direct parameterization.

**Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing convergence analyses for AC mostly rely on the implicit assumption that the **action space is finite**. In finite spaces, for any reference measure $\mu$ with full support, the KL divergence is naturally bounded by $\log|A|$, which essentially provides stability for free. However, in continuous action spaces (common in robotics), the relative entropy term $\mathrm{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)\,\|\,\mu)$ is **unbounded and can reach $+\infty$**, resulting in a lack of a priori bounds for the value function.

**Key Challenge**: In general action spaces, the AC flow faces two entangled risks. First, the KL term might blow up in finite time, creating singularities in the dynamical system. Second, the convergence rate of mirror descent depends on a constant $\int_S \mathrm{KL}(\pi^*(\cdot|s)\,\|\,\pi_0(\cdot|s))\,\mathrm d\pi^*_\rho$. In the unregularized case, it is nearly impossible to choose an initial policy $\pi_0$ that ensures this term is finite, as unregularized optimal policies often reside on Dirac distributions.

**Goal**: To answer a specific research question under the $Q^\pi_\tau$-realizability (linear function approximation) assumption: *Is the actor-critic gradient flow for entropy-regularized MDPs in continuous action spaces stable, does it converge, and how fast?*

**Key Insight**: The authors idealize discrete "TD + Mirror Descent" as a **continuous-time coupled dynamical system** and introduce a **two-timescale** mechanism where the critic evolves much faster than the actor. Entropy regularization is used as a tool rather than a burden; it ensures the optimal policy $\pi^*$ has full support, making the KL constant finite as long as the initial policy $\pi_0$ also has full support.

**Core Idea**: By combining convex analysis on measure spaces, Euclidean convex analysis, and two-timescale analysis, the paper constructs a Lyapunov stability framework. It demonstrates that timescale separation is critical for both stability and convergence—the critic must update at an exponentially faster rate to suppress the "driving force" generated by the unbounded KL terms in continuous spaces.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The study analyzes a **widely used AC scheme** in its continuous-time limit. It considers an infinite-horizon MDP $(S, A, P, c, \gamma)$ where $S$ and $A$ are Polish spaces and $c$ is a bounded cost function. Given a reference measure $\mu\in\mathcal P(A)$ and regularization strength $\tau>0$, the regularized value function is defined as:

$$V^\pi_\tau(s) = \mathbb E^\pi_s\!\left[\sum_{n=0}^\infty \gamma^n\big(c(s_n,a_n) + \tau\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s_n)\,\|\,\mu)\big)\right].$$

The objective is to minimize $V^\pi_\tau$. Starting from initial critic parameters $\theta_0$ and initial policy $\pi_0\in\Pi_\mu$, the system generates a trajectory $(\theta_t, \pi_t)_{t\ge0}$. The analysis aims to prove that this trajectory (i) does not blow up and (ii) converges to $V^*_\tau$.

Specifically, the critic use linear approximation $Q(s,a;\theta)=\langle\theta,\phi(s,a)\rangle$ updated via semi-gradients of the Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE), while the actor follows PMD. The continuous-time limit results in a pair of coupled evolution equations:

$$\frac{\mathrm d\theta_t}{\mathrm dt} = -\eta_t\, g(\theta_t,\pi_t),\qquad \partial_t\pi_t(\mathrm da|s) = -A(s,a;\theta_t)\,\pi_t(\mathrm da|s),$$

where $g$ is the semi-gradient of MSBE, $A(\cdot;\theta)$ is the approximate soft advantage, and $\eta_t\ge1$ is the **timescale separation function** that accelerates the critic flow relative to the actor flow. The actor's equation is referred to as the **approximate Fisher–Rao gradient flow**.

### Key Designs

**1. Continuous-Time Two-Timescale Coupled Flow**
Direct analysis of discrete AC updates is complex. Following the logic of Konda–Tsitsiklis and Zhang, the authors use a continuous-time limit where the critic performs semi-gradient descent $\theta_{n+1}=\theta_n - h_n\,g(\theta_n,\pi_n)$ with a timescale separation ratio $\eta_n := h_n/\lambda_n > 1$. This simplifies the discrete combination of updates into a continuous dynamical system, allowing the use of differential inequalities and Lyapunov functions. In continuous spaces, $\eta_t$ must grow over time to counteract potential KL divergence growth.

**2. Mirror Descent Limit → Fisher–Rao Gradient Flow on Measure Space**
The actor update in PMD, when interpolated over time with step size $\lambda\to0$, recovers the **Fisher–Rao gradient flow**:

$$\partial_t \ln\frac{\mathrm d\pi_t}{\mathrm d\mu}(s,a) = -A^{\pi_t}_\tau(s,a),$$

utilizing the property $\int_A A^\pi_\tau(s,a)\pi(\mathrm da|s)=0$. This views the soft advantage as the **functional derivative** of the value function with respect to the policy $\pi$, characterizing policy improvement as a gradient flow on the space of probability kernels $\mathcal P(A|S)$.

**3. Forcing–Damping Lyapunov Stability Framework**
This is the most rigorous part of the proof. Defining $K_t := \sup_{s\in S}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_t(\cdot|s)\,\|\,\mu)$, Lemma 5.1 characterizes the coupled flow as a **forcing–damping system**:

$$\frac{1}{2\eta_t}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}|\theta_t|^2 \le -\frac{\Gamma}{2}|\theta_t|^2 + \frac{\tau^2\gamma^2 K_t^2}{\Gamma} + \frac{|c|_{B_b}^2}{\Gamma}.$$

The **damping term** $-\frac{\Gamma}{2}|\theta_t|^2$ stems from the strong convexity of the loss, while the **forcing term** $K_t$ originates from the policy update. By applying Grönwall-type inequalities, the authors prove that $K_t$ and $|\theta_t|$ **do not blow up in finite time**, provided the initial timescale separation $\eta_0 > \tau/\Gamma$.

**4. Three-Stage Convergence Proof**
First, Lemma 6.1 shows $\{V^{\pi_t}_\tau\}$ dissipates along the flow if the critic is exact. Second, Theorem 6.1 proves the AC flow **retains the exponential convergence rate** induced by $\tau$-regularization, plus a critic error term. Third, Theorem 6.2 proves this critic error decays exponentially. The main result, **Theorem 6.3**, confirms that if $\eta_t = \eta_0 e^{k_1 t}$ (critic accelerates exponentially), the entire flow converges to the optimal regularized value at an exponential rate for **all** $\gamma \in (0,1)$.

### Loss & Training
The critic minimizes MSBE $\mathrm{MSBE}(\theta,\pi)=\frac12\int(Q(s,a;\theta)-T^\pi Q(s,a;\theta))^2\,\mathrm d\pi_\beta$. Under $Q^\pi_\tau$-realizability (Assumption 4.1), MSBE is zero if and only if $Q(\cdot;\theta)=Q^\pi_\tau$. The actor follows the Fisher–Rao flow without an explicit loss function.

## Key Experimental Results

The paper is a **purely theoretical work without numerical experiments**. The core findings are encapsulated in the stability and convergence theorems.

| Conclusion | Form | Key Condition |
|------|------|----------|
| KL Stability (Cor. 5.1) | $\mathrm{KL}(\pi_t\|\mu)^2 \le a_1 e^{a_2 t}$ | Assump. 4.2/4.3, $\eta_0>\tau/\Gamma$ |
| Critic Stability (Cor. 5.2) | $|\theta_t|\le r_1 e^{r_2 t}$ | $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\eta_t\le\alpha\eta_t$ |
| **Exponential Convergence (Thm 6.3)** | $\min_r V^{\pi_r}_\tau-V^{\pi^*}_\tau \le C e^{-\tau t/2}$ | $\eta_t = \eta_0 e^{k_1 t}$ |

**Ablation Perspective**:
*   **Without Entropy Regularization**: The KL constant usually becomes infinite in continuous spaces, and the analysis fails.
*   **Without Timescale Separation**: The forcing term $K_t$ cannot be suppressed, making stability impossible to guarantee.

### Key Findings
- **Timescale separation is the bottleneck**: Stability requires $\eta_0 > \tau/\Gamma$, and exponential convergence requires $\eta_t$ to grow **exponentially** over time.
- **Entropy regularization as a facilitator**: It ensures the optimal policy has full support, allowing the KL term to remain finite starting from a full-support $\pi_0$.
- For MDPs with a small discount factor $\gamma$, the KL term is **uniformly bounded**, allowing for relaxed requirements on $\eta_t$.

## Highlights & Insights
- **Functional Derivative Perspective**: Treating soft advantage as a functional derivative allows policy improvement to be analyzed via measure space convex analysis, bypassing the cardinality restrictions of finite action spaces.
- **Forcing–Damping Decomposition**: Writing the coupled AC flow as a system where the loss provides damping and the policy update provides forcing is a powerful framework transferable to other bilevel optimization problems like GANs.
- **Quantifying Critic Acceleration**: The paper moves beyond qualitative "slow-fast" descriptions by specifying the required exponential growth rate $\eta_t = \eta_0 e^{k_1 t}$.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Idealized Continuous Time**: The gap between continuous-time analysis and discrete-time algorithmic implementation (with step-size errors) remains.
- **Linear Approximation**: The analysis is restricted to linear critic approximation; non-linear neural networks or Hilbert space approximations are left for future work.
- **Exact Integration**: The paper assumes integrals for semi-gradients are calculated exactly, ignoring Monte-Carlo sampling noise.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Kerimkulov et al. (2025a) / Lan (2023)**: These works assume exact advantages. This paper extends the analysis to the "real" AC setting where the advantage is estimated by a TD-based critic.
- **vs. Cayci et al. (2024a;b)**: Their stability results inherently rely on finite action spaces ($\log|A|$). This work removes that restriction.
- **vs. Zhang et al. (2021)**: This work achieves convergence to the **optimal** policy via entropy regularization, whereas prior two-timescale AC work often lacked such global optimality guarantees or required restarting mechanisms.
