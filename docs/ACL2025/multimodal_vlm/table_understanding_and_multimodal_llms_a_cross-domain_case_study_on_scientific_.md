# Table Understanding and (Multimodal) LLMs: A Cross-Domain Case Study on Scientific Tables

- **Conference**: ACL 2025
- **arXiv**: [2507.00152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00152)
- **Code**: [esborisova/TableEval-Study](https://github.com/esborisova/TableEval-Study)
- **Area**: Multimodal VLM / Table Understanding
- **Keywords**: Table Understanding, Multimodal Large Language Models, Cross-Domain Evaluation, Scientific Tables, Explainability Analysis

## TL;DR

This paper introduces the TableEval benchmark (3,017 tables, 5 formats) to systematically compare the performance of text LLMs and multimodal LLMs on scientific vs. non-scientific table understanding tasks. It reveals that while models remain robust to table modalities (image vs. text), their performance decreases significantly on scientific tables.

## Background & Motivation

Tables are one of the most common tools for structured data presentation, widely used in finance, medicine, education, and academic research. Although LLMs have demonstrated powerful capabilities across various NLP tasks, their ability to understand tabular data lacks systematic evaluation. Existing work suffers from several key limitations:

**Bias in Data Sources**: Most table understanding (TU) studies focus purely on non-scientific sources (e.g., Wikipedia, financial reports), while paying minimal attention to tables in scientific literature. Scientific tables contain extensive professional jargon, complex abbreviations, and dense numerical values, requiring domain knowledge and stronger arithmetic reasoning capabilities.

**Unknown Modality Impact**: Tables can be represented as images (PNG/JPEG) or in various textual formats (HTML, XML, LaTeX, Dict). However, the impact of different representation modalities on model performance remains unclear.

**Lack of Explainability**: Existing TU research rarely analyzes how models leverage contextual information in tables for decision-making from an interpretability perspective.

This work aims to address these three gaps by constructing a cross-domain, cross-modal table understanding benchmark, systematically evaluating the performance of LLMs/MLLMs, and conducting explainability analysis using gradient-based attribution methods.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The study is divided into three main phases:

1. **TableEval Dataset Construction**: Collect tables from existing scientific and non-scientific datasets and unify them into 5 formats (Image, Dict, HTML, XML, LaTeX).
2. **Model Evaluation**: Evaluate various LLMs and MLLMs on each subset of TableEval using different table representations.
3. **Explainability Analysis**: Apply gradient-based attribution methods to model outputs to generate token-level importance heatmaps, analyzing how models leverage the tabular content in their inputs.

### Key Designs

#### Key Design 1: TableEval Benchmark Construction

TableEval contains 3,017 tables and 11,312 evaluation instances, consisting of 6 subsets:

**Scientific Tables** (3 subsets):
- **ComTQA (PubTables-1M)**: VQA tasks from PubMed Central papers, with annotations generated by Gemini Pro.
- **numericNLG**: Focused on table-to-text generation with numerical reasoning, sourced from ACL Anthology.
- **SciGen**: Reasoning-aware text generation tasks, sourced from arXiv and ACL papers.

**Non-Scientific Tables** (3 subsets):
- **ComTQA (FinTabNet)**: VQA tasks based on S&P 500 annual corporate reports.
- **LogicNLG**: Logical reasoning table-to-text generation, sourced from Wikipedia.
- **Logic2Text**: Logical form-to-text generation, sourced from Wikipedia.

Each table is converted into 5 formats: PNG images are generated via PDFFigure2.0 or imgkit; HTML and XML are extracted from original sources or inter-converted via scripts; LaTeX is extracted from paper source code or generated from HTML; Dict represents serialized list-headers and cell values.

#### Key Design 2: Gradient-Based Explainability Analysis

The paper adopts the **Input × Gradient** feature attribution method from the Inseq toolkit. Its core formulation is:

$$\text{Attribution}(x_i) = x_i \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial x_i}$$

where $x_i$ is the embedding of the input token and $y$ is the output logit of the model. The gradient is multiplied by the input and averaged to obtain the contribution of each token to the output, visualized as a heatmap.

Additionally, the paper visualizes the **log-probability** of generated tokens as a proxy for model confidence. For instance, in an example from FinTabNet, Mistral-Nemo correctly answers with attention focused on the year column relevant to the question, whereas LLaMA-3 exhibits scattered attention and extremely low confidence on key numerical tokens.

#### Key Design 3: Cross-Model Evaluation Framework

The paper evaluates 9 model configurations (4 MLLMs + 5 LLMs):

- **MLLMs**: Gemini-2.0-Flash (closed-source baseline), LLaVA-NeXT-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, Idefics3-8B.
- **LLMs**: Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B/14B, Mistral-Nemo-12B.

All experiments are conducted in a zero-shot setting using a standardized prompt template, without annotating the document format type in the prompt or post-processing model outputs. Metrics include BertScore.F1, MoverScore, ROUGE-L.F1, and METEOR.

## Key Experimental Results

### Table 1: Performance Comparison of Image vs. Text Modalities (Averaged across Models and Datasets)

| Metric | Image | Dict | HTML | XML | LaTeX |
|------|-------|------|------|-----|-------|
| BertScore.F1 | ~0.72 | ~0.69 | ~0.68 | ~0.68 | ~0.69 |
| MoverScore | ~0.28 | ~0.25 | ~0.24 | ~0.24 | ~0.25 |
| ROUGE-L.F1 | ~0.25 | ~0.22 | ~0.21 | ~0.21 | ~0.22 |
| METEOR | ~0.30 | ~0.27 | ~0.27 | ~0.27 | ~0.27 |

Images lead text formats by approximately 1-13% across all metrics, while the differences among the 4 text formats are minimal (maximum of ~4%).

### Table 2: Performance Comparison of Scientific vs. Non-Scientific Tables (Averaged across Models and Formats)

| Metric | Scientific Tables | Non-Scientific Tables | Gap |
|------|---------|-----------|------|
| BertScore.F1 | ~0.62 | ~0.78 | +16% |
| MoverScore | ~0.17 | ~0.35 | +18% |
| ROUGE-L.F1 | ~0.13 | ~0.33 | +20% |
| METEOR | ~0.17 | ~0.40 | +23% |

Non-scientific tables significantly outperform scientific ones across all metrics, with the maximum gap reaching **34%**. Among them, LogicNLG achieves the highest scores, while SciGen yields the lowest.

### Table 3: Average Prediction Length of Models

| Model | Average Length | Min | Max |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Idefics3-8B | 139 | 0 | 4416 |
| Gemini-2.0-Flash (image) | 207 | 2 | 3097 |
| Qwen2.5-VL-7B | 292 | 4 | 3464 |
| Mistral-Nemo | 303 | 21 | 2941 |
| Llama-3.2-3B | 464 | 22 | 5626 |
| Qwen2.5-14B | 481 | 29 | 4154 |

Idefics3 generates the most concise responses, whereas Qwen2.5-14B and LLaMA-3 produce the longest. This variation in output length is a key factor causing discrepancies in n-gram overlap-based metrics (such as BLEU and ROUGE).

## Key Findings

1. **Modality Robustness**: Current LLMs/MLLMs are insensitive to the text format of table representations (the difference between HTML/XML/LaTeX/Dict is only about 4%). The image modality is slightly superior to text (by 1-13%), suggesting that models have been exposed thoroughly to these formats during pre-training.
2. **Scientific Tables as a Bottleneck**: Model performance drops significantly on scientific tables (by up to 34%). This is attributed to the higher complexity of scientific table tasks (which require multi-step reasoning and whole-table summarization) and the likely scarcity of scientific tables in pre-training data.
3. **Model Ranking**: Among MLLMs, Gemini-2.0-Flash and Idefics3 perform the best; among LLMs, Gemini-2.0-Flash and Mistral-Nemo are the top performers. Open-source models still lag behind closed-source Gemini. Model parameter size does not strictly correlate with accuracy, as Qwen2.5-3B performs comparably to Qwen2.5-14B.
4. **Interpretability Insights**: Input × Gradient attribution reveals that the correct model (Mistral-Nemo) focuses its attention on table areas relevant to the answer, while the incorrect model (LLaMA-3) exhibits scattered focus and low confidence on key tokens. Tokenization differences also impact performance—LLaMA-3's split of three-digit numbers makes handling year digits difficult.

## Highlights & Insights

- **Systematic Cross-Domain & Cross-Modal Evaluation**: This work is the first to simultaneously compare scientific vs. non-scientific tables alongside images vs. multiple text formats, filling several research gaps.
- **Practical Value of the TableEval Benchmark**: Comprising 3,017 tables × 5 formats × 2 task types, and publicly available on HuggingFace, it provides a standardized evaluation platform for future table understanding research.
- **Combining Explainability and Table Understanding**: Applying gradient attribution to TU tasks serves as a promising direction to reveal decision patterns of models on structured data.
- **Format Indifference Findings Provide Engineering Guidance**: In practical applications, there is little need to over-optimize which text format is used to input tables to LLMs—HTML, XML, LaTeX, and Dict deliver similar performance.

## Limitations & Future Work

1. All models use the same prompt without output post-processing, which may lead to lower scores; tailored prompts and structured outputs might improve results.
2. The evaluation relies solely on automatic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, etc.), the limitations of which are well-documented.
3. Explainability analysis is only applied to text inputs, leaving the modal contribution analysis in multimodal input scenarios unexplored.
4. The dataset is limited to English, leaving multi-lingual table understanding capabilities unevaluated.
5. Model sizes are limited to 3-14B parameters, without evaluations on larger models (e.g., 70B+ or GPT-4).

## Related Work & Insights

This work is built upon table understanding, LLM evaluation, and interpretability. Unlike prior studies focusing solely on Wikipedia tables (e.g., TabFact, ToTTo, WikiTables), this paper explicitly incorporates scientific tables into the evaluation scope. While Sui et al. (2024) and Singha et al. (2023) studied the impact of different formats on LLMs with inconsistent findings, this paper provides a robust "format matters little" conclusion through a larger-scale experiment. Deng et al. (2024) compared image vs. text inputs, and this work confirms similar findings across more models and datasets.

**Insight**: Scientific table understanding remains a highly valuable research direction. Incorporating structured data from scientific domains during pre-training or conducting domain-specific fine-tuning could bridge the performance gap between scientific and non-scientific table understanding.

## Rating

⭐⭐⭐

A standard benchmark and evaluation study. The TableEval benchmark itself is valuable, and the cross-domain, cross-modal evaluation offers broad coverage. However, no new techniques are proposed at the methodology level, making this essentially an empirical investigation. The qualitative analysis on explainability is confined to a few instances, lacking depth. It is suitable as a reference baseline and data resource in the field of table understanding.
