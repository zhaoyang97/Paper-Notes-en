# anyECG-chat: A Generalist ECG-MLLM for Flexible ECG Input and Multi-Task Understanding

**Conference**: AAAI 2026
**arXiv**: [2506.00942v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00942v2)  
**Code**: [https://github.com/CuCl-2/anyECG-chat](https://github.com/CuCl-2/anyECG-chat)  
**Area**: Multimodal VLM
**Keywords**: ECG-MLLM, electrocardiogram, multi-task understanding, dynamic input, curriculum learning

## TL;DR
This work constructs the anyECG dataset (covering three tasks: report generation, waveform localization, and multi-ECG comparison) and proposes the anyECG-chat model. Through a dynamic ECG input mechanism supporting variable-length, few-lead, and multi-ECG inputs, and a three-stage curriculum learning strategy, anyECG-chat comprehensively outperforms existing ECG-MLLMs in OOD generalization for report generation, second-level anomalous waveform localization, and multi-ECG comparative analysis.

## Background & Motivation
Existing ECG-MLLMs are primarily limited to: (1) single-task settings such as report generation or label classification; and (2) standard 12-lead, 10-second ECG inputs. These constraints fail to address two critical clinical needs: **home monitoring scenarios** requiring anomalous waveform localization from long-duration, few-lead ECGs, and **clinical practice** requiring longitudinal comparative analysis across multiple ECG recordings. Furthermore, existing ECG-QA datasets (single-task, single-ECG, short duration) are insufficiently challenging.

**Key Challenge**: While MLLMs are inherently capable of flexible multi-task processing, existing ECG-MLLMs have fallen far short of realizing this potential.

## Core Problem
**How to build a generalist ECG-MLLM that simultaneously supports report generation, second-level waveform localization, and multi-ECG comparison, while handling flexible inputs including variable-length, few-lead, and multi-ECG configurations?**

## Method

### Overall Architecture
anyECG-chat = ECG Encoder (ViT-base, contrastive pre-training) + MLP connector + LLaMA-3-8B (LoRA fine-tuning)
- **Input**: One or more ECG signals of arbitrary length/lead count + text query
- **Output**: Text response (report / localized time segments / comparative analysis)

### Key Designs

1. **Dynamic ECG Input Mechanism**:

    - **Variable-length ECG**: Signals shorter than 10s are zero-padded; signals longer than 10s are padded to an integer multiple of 10s, segmented into 10s clips, encoded independently, and then concatenated, with [CLS] tokens averaged.
    - **Few-lead ECG**: Missing leads are zero-padded. Lead embeddings enable the encoder to capture cross-lead relationships.
    - **Multi-ECG input**: Special tokens `<ECG_start>` and `<ECG_end>` mark the boundaries of each ECG's embeddings, allowing the LLM to distinguish between different ECG recordings.

2. **ECG Encoder Design**: ViT-base architecture with patch size (1, 200)—one patch per lead per 200 samples. A standard 12-lead, 10s ECG at 100 Hz yields 60 patches plus one [CLS] token. Lead embeddings are introduced as $E = E_{signal} + E_{pos} + E_{lead}$. The encoder is first pre-trained on MIMIC-ECG via ECG-report contrastive learning.

3. **anyECG Dataset**: Three subsets:

    - **ReportGen**: 773K QA pairs (MIMIC-ECG, 12-lead, 10s)
    - **Localization**: 150K QA pairs (European ST-T, MIT-BIH, etc., 2-lead, 10–60s, with second-level localization annotations)
    - **MultiECG**: 168K QA pairs (MIMIC + PTB-XL, 2–6 ECGs, open-ended QA generated by LLM)

### Loss & Training
Three-stage curriculum learning:
- **Stage 1 (Pre-training)**: Trains ECG encoder + connector on report generation; LLM is frozen.
- **Stage 2 (Fine-grained Pre-training)**: Adds localization task; trains ECG encoder + connector + LoRA.
- **Stage 3 (Instruction Tuning)**: Adds multi-ECG task; ECG encoder is frozen; trains connector + LoRA.

Each stage retains data from prior stages (experience replay to prevent forgetting). LoRA rank = 8, α = 16.

## Key Experimental Results

**Report Generation (OOD, 6 unseen datasets, macro-AUC):**

| Method | PTBXL-Super | PTBXL-Sub | PTBXL-Rhythm | CPSC | CSN | Type |
|--------|------------|-----------|-------------|------|-----|------|
| MERL | 74.20 | 75.70 | 78.50 | 82.80 | 74.40 | Discriminative zero-shot |
| PULSE | 66.61 | 61.32 | 73.91 | 66.91 | 66.38 | Generative zero-shot |
| MEIT | 62.34 | 57.91 | 70.45 | 62.38 | 62.73 | Generative zero-shot |
| anyECG-chat | **71.09** | **67.41** | **78.83** | **70.70** | **72.35** | Generative zero-shot |

**Waveform Localization (IoU)**: anyECG-chat surpasses the dedicated segmentation model U-Net on most datasets; other ECG-MLLMs (PULSE, MEIT) are entirely incapable of second-level localization (IoU = 0).

**Multi-ECG Comparison (MIMIC Multi-ECG QA, QwQ score 0–5)**: anyECG-chat achieves an average score of 3.02, vs. PULSE 1.53, MEIT 1.90, and LLaVa-Med 2.48; anyECG-chat attains the highest score on 816 out of 1,152 questions.

### Ablation Study
- **Contrastive pre-training**: Removing it reduces classification AUC from 71.09 to 68.24 and localization IoU from 56.10 to 53.69.
- **Lead embeddings**: Removing them causes a modest decline (70.10, 55.53), indicating a positive but non-critical contribution.
- **Dynamic input mechanism**: Removing it leads to a notable drop in localization and multi-ECG performance (52.66, 2.74), as the model can no longer handle long ECGs or multiple ECGs.
- **Curriculum learning vs. joint training**: Removing curriculum learning causes classification AUC to drop sharply from 71.09 to 61.21 and localization IoU from 56.10 to 50.62. Joint training on all tasks simultaneously causes the model to over-rely on language modeling capacity, increasing hallucinations.
- **Experience replay**: Removing it reduces classification AUC from 71.09 to 66.32, indicating severe catastrophic forgetting.

## Highlights & Insights
- **First truly multi-task ECG-MLLM**: Simultaneously supports report generation, second-level waveform localization, and multi-ECG longitudinal comparison, comprehensively covering clinical needs across three dimensions.
- **Concise and practical dynamic input mechanism**: The combination of segmentation, concatenation, and special tokens enables flexible input handling without any architectural modifications.
- **Dataset contribution**: The anyECG dataset fills a critical gap in ECG-MLLM training data, particularly for localization and multi-ECG comparison tasks.
- **Zero-shot single-lead capability**: Despite training only on 2-lead data, the model achieves reasonable zero-shot localization performance in certain single-lead scenarios.
- **Curriculum learning for hallucination mitigation**: The progressive training strategy effectively prevents the model from prematurely relying on language reasoning before sufficient ECG perceptual ability is acquired.

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Limited waveform localization precision**: IoU on MIT-BIH Arrhythmia falls short of the dedicated U-Net, with notable variance across datasets.
- **LLM-dependent multi-ECG QA evaluation**: Scoring via QwQ-32B, though partially validated by human annotators, does not constitute a gold standard.
- **Single-modality scope**: No integration of other medical signals (e.g., echocardiography, blood pressure waveforms) or imaging modalities.
- **Dated base LLM**: The use of LLaMA-3-8B leaves stronger foundation models unexplored.
- **Temporal resolution of localization**: Constrained by the 100 Hz sampling rate and patch size of 200, the minimum localization resolution is 2 seconds.

## Related Work & Insights

| Method | Supported Tasks | ECG Input | Key Differences from anyECG-chat |
|--------|----------------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| **MEIT** | Report generation only | 12-lead, 10s | anyECG-chat supports 3 tasks + flexible input |
| **PULSE** | Report + classification + rhythm | 12-lead, 10s (image) | Image-based rather than signal-based; incapable of second-level localization |
| **ECG-Chat** | Report + multi-turn dialogue | 12-lead, 10s | Incorporates RAG but still limited to single ECG with no localization capability |

anyECG-chat is the first ECG-MLLM to simultaneously address both task diversity and input flexibility.

The dynamic input mechanism is generalizable to MLLMs for other biosignals (e.g., EEG, EMG, PPG). The three-stage curriculum learning paradigm (coarse perception → fine-grained understanding → instruction following) provides a valuable reference for medical MLLM training. The formulation of waveform localization as text-described time intervals introduces a novel interaction paradigm for signal analysis.

## Rating
- **Novelty**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The extension in task scope and input flexibility constitutes a substantive contribution, though the overall architecture follows the LLaVA paradigm.
- **Experimental Thoroughness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Evaluation across three tasks is comprehensive, with OOD testing, zero-shot assessment, and full ablation analysis; comparisons with a broader set of MLLMs are lacking.
- **Writing Quality**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Dataset construction is described in detail and the methodology is clearly presented.
- **Value**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Offers direct applicability to the medical AI community, particularly for home ECG monitoring and clinical longitudinal analysis scenarios.
