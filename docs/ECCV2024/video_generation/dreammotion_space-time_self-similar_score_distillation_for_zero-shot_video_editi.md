# DreamMotion: Space-Time Self-Similar Score Distillation for Zero-Shot Video Editing

**Conference**: ECCV 2024  
**arXiv**: [2403.12002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12002)  
**Code**: Yes (Project Page: [https://hyeonho99.github.io/dreammotion](https://hyeonho99.github.io/dreammotion))  
**Area**: Diffusion Models / Video Editing  
**Keywords**: Score Distillation, Video Editing, Self-Similarity, Diffusion Models, Zero-Shot

## TL;DR

DreamMotion, a zero-shot video editing framework based on Score Distillation, is proposed. By employing space-time self-similarity regularization, it injects target appearances while preserving the structural and motion integrity of the original video, applicable to both cascaded and non-cascaded video diffusion models.

## Background & Motivation

Existing video editing methods based on diffusion models face a core challenge: how to establish and maintain real-world motion during the editing process. The main limitations include:

**Limitations of T2I-based methods**: Existing methods utilizing T2I diffusion models typically process multiple frames by inflating attention layers. However, this implicit motion preservation strategy is far from sufficient for achieving smooth and complete motion. Common complementary solutions (such as injecting self-attention maps or using structural guidance like ControlNet) increase system complexity.

**Limitations of T2V models**: Even when utilizing pre-trained T2V models, publicly available models lack rich enough temporal priors to accurately depict real-world motion (as shown in Fig. 2). Consequently, many methods require fine-tuning model weights on the input video (one-shot manner), which incurs extra overhead.

**Inherent difficulties of standard inverse diffusion process**: Whether using T2I or T2V, the traditional inverse diffusion process, starting from standard Gaussian noise or inverted latent representations, is naturally difficult to reprogram for complex real-world motion.

**Key Insight**: Instead of trying to reconstruct motion starting from noise (inverse diffusion), it is better to start from a video with natural motion and gradually modify the appearance—which is precisely the strength of Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). However, directly applying video score distillation leaks accumulated structural errors, leading to motion deviation; thus, self-similarity regularization is required as a constraint.

## Method

### Overall Architecture

The optimization strategy of DreamMotion consists of three components:
- Initialize the target video variable $\boldsymbol{x}_0^{1:N}(\theta)$ as the original video $\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{1:N}$
- Inject the appearance described by the target text via $\mathcal{L}_{\text{V-DDS}}$
- Maintain spatial structural consistency via $\mathcal{L}_{\text{S-SSM}}$
- Ensure temporal smoothness via $\mathcal{L}_{\text{T-SSM}}$

These three losses share the same noise $\epsilon$ and timestep $t$, and can be efficiently completed in a single diffusion forward and backward step.

### Key Designs

1. **Video Delta Denoising Score (V-DDS)**: Extends the image-level DDS mechanism to the video domain to inject target appearances.

   DDS eliminates noise directions in SDS by introducing reference text-image pairs:

    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{V-DDS}}(\theta;y) = \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\phi^w(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}(\theta), t, y) - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\phi^w(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_t^{1:N}, t, \hat{y})\|_2^2$

   Simultaneously, a binary mask $m^{1:N}$ is used to filter gradients ($\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{V-DDS}} \odot m^{1:N}$), ensuring that unedited areas remain unaffected to avoid blurring and oversaturation. The mask is obtained from bounding boxes generated by off-the-shelf object detection models.

   **Design Motivation**: While V-DDS effectively injects target appearances, it accumulates structural errors leading to motion deviation, which necessitates the following two self-similarity regularizations.

2. **Spatial Self-Similarity Matching (S-SSM)**: Preserves the structural integrity of each frame.

   **Core Idea**: Self-similarity descriptors focus on the **relative relationship** of object appearance with the surrounding environment rather than absolute appearance, thereby maintaining structural information after appearance changes.

   Concrete implementation: The same noise is added to the original and edited videos. Attention key features $K(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}), K(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_t^{1:N}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (H \times W) \times C}$ are extracted through the video diffusion U-Net, and the spatial self-similarity map of each frame is computed:

    $SS_{i,j}^n(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}) = \cos(K_i^n(x_t^{1:N}), K_j^n(x_t^{1:N}))$

    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{S-SSM}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|SS^n(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}) - SS^n(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_t^{1:N})\|_2^2$

   **Design Motivation**: Self-similarity is robust to local texture patterns while preserving the global layout and object shapes. This is the first work to apply self-similarity of deep diffusion features for structure preservation in video editing.

3. **Temporal Self-Similarity Matching (T-SSM)**: Eliminates inter-frame artifacts and ensures temporal smoothness.

   S-SSM optimizes each frame independently without considering inter-frame correlations, which may lead to local distortion and flickering. T-SSM compresses key features into a global descriptor through spatial marginal pooling:

    $M[K(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N})] = \frac{1}{H \cdot W}\sum_{i=1}^{H \cdot W}K_i(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C}$

   Then, self-similarity is computed in the temporal dimension:

    $TS_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}) = \cos(M_i[K(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N})], M_j[K(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N})])$$$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{T-SSM}} = \|TS(\boldsymbol{x}_t^{1:N}) - TS(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_t^{1:N})\|_2^2$

   **Design Motivation**: Spatial marginal pooling, as a first-order statistic, compresses spatial information while retaining critical spatial details, which has been shown to be an effective global descriptor.

### Loss & Training

- **Total Loss**: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{V-DDS}} + \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_{\text{S-SSM}} + \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_{\text{T-SSM}}$
- **Optimizer**: SGD, learning rate of 0.4, optimized for 200 steps
- **Efficiency**: About 2 minutes for an 8-frame video, 4 minutes for a 16-frame video (on a single A100 GPU)
- **Cascaded Extension**: For cascaded video diffusion models (e.g., Show-1), the optimization is applied only during the keyframe generation stage, and is then extended through temporal interpolation and spatial super-resolution modules.
- **Model-agnostic**: Applicable to both pixel-space (Show-1) and latent-space (ZeroScope) video diffusion models.

### Key Findings

- User study (36 participants) shows that the proposed method outperforms all baselines in editing accuracy, frame consistency, and structure and motion preservation.
- On the cascaded framework (Show-1), the structure and motion preservation (SM-Preserve) score is 4.30 vs 3.35 for VMC.
- Spatial self-similarity resolves structural drift, and temporal self-similarity eliminates artifacts caused by spatial constraints; the two collaborate and complement each other.

## Highlights & Insights

1. **Paradigm Innovation**: Bypassing the fundamental difficulty of "generating motion from noise", this method elegantly redefines video editing by "modifying appearance starting from existing motion".
2. **Clever Application of Self-Similarity**: Using self-similarity of diffusion features to simultaneously address both structure preservation and temporal smoothness.
3. **Model-Agnostic**: Applicable to both cascaded and non-cascaded frameworks without fine-tuning model weights—a true zero-shot method.
4. **Computationally Efficient**: The three losses share a single diffusion forward/backward computation, keeping computational cost highly manageable.

## Limitations & Future Work

- The framework is designed to maintain the original structure and is **not suitable for editing tasks requiring significant structural changes** (e.g., changing object poses).
- Relies on off-the-shelf target detectors to provide edited region masks.
- The computational complexity of self-similarity is $O((H \times W)^2)$, which may become a bottleneck for high-resolution videos.
- Future work could explore extending the method to more frames (currently supporting up to 16 frames).

## Related Work & Insights

- **DDS** (Delta Denoising Score): Resolves SDS noise direction issues through reference text-image pairs, acting as the core foundation of this work.
- **Tokenflow**: Achieves temporally consistent editing through diffusion internal feature consistency; represents the strongest baseline.
- **Self-Similarity**: From traditional visual pattern matching to DINO ViT features, this work is the first to introduce it into the diffusion feature space.
- Insight: The paradigm of Score Distillation + Structural Constraints might be applicable to other generative editing tasks that require structure preservation (e.g., 3D editing, audio editing).
