# What One Cannot, Two Can: Two-Layer Transformers Provably Represent Induction Heads on Any-Order Markov Chains

**Conference**: NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv**: [2508.07208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07208)  
**Code**: [GitHub](https://github.com/cekbote/two-layer-induction-heads)  
**Area**: LLM NLP / Transformer Theory / In-Context Learning
**Keywords**: induction heads, in-context learning, Markov chains, transformer depth, expressiveness

## TL;DR
This paper theoretically proves that a two-layer single-head Transformer suffices to represent the conditional $k$-gram model (i.e., $k$-th order induction head) for any $k$-th order Markov process, establishing the tightest known characterization of the relationship between Transformer depth and Markov order. The key insight is leveraging ReLU and LayerNorm nonlinearities in the MLP to compensate for the reduced number of layers.

## Background & Motivation
**Background**: In-context learning (ICL) is a core capability of Transformers, realized through induction head circuits in the input context that implement a "copy-and-match" mechanism. Theoretically, ICL can be modeled as learning conditional $k$-grams over Markov sequences.

**Limitations of Prior Work**:
- A single-layer single-head Transformer cannot efficiently represent a first-order induction head unless the hidden dimension is exponentially large [Sanford et al.].
- Two-layer single-head Transformers can handle the first-order case [Bietti, Edelman, Nichani], but higher orders require the number of heads to grow linearly with $k$ [Nichani].
- Best prior result: a three-layer single-head Transformer can handle arbitrary $k$-th order [Rajaraman et al.].
- **Open question**: Can a two-layer single-head Transformer handle arbitrary order $k$?

**Key Challenge**: The three-layer construction uses three attention layers to separately handle "current context," "shifted context," and "matching" — three distinct operations. Reducing to two layers forces a single attention layer to accomplish two operations simultaneously, which is informationally insufficient.

**Key Insight**: The observation that an MLP (with ReLU + LayerNorm) can recover missing information from the attention output in reverse — effectively letting the MLP assume the role of the second attention head.

**Core Idea**: Use the nonlinearities of the MLP (ReLU filtering + LayerNorm normalization) to disentangle key token embeddings from the first-layer attention output, replacing the second attention operation in the three-layer construction.

## Method

### Overall Architecture
The input is a sequence $s_{0:T}$ generated by a $k$-th order Markov chain, and the goal is to predict the next-token distribution (i.e., the conditional $k$-gram estimator). A 2-layer 1-head Transformer is constructed, where the first-layer attention + MLP computes two key representations $\mathbf{u}_n$ and $\mathbf{v}_n$, and the second-layer attention acts as the $k$-th order induction head to perform matching.

### Key Designs

1. **First-Layer Attention: Extracting Shifted Context $\mathbf{v}_n$**

    - **Function**: For position $n$, the attention focuses on $\{n-1, n-2, \ldots, n-k\}$ to compute the shifted context representation.
    - **Mechanism**: Attention weights $\operatorname{attn}_{n,i} = 3^i / \sum_{j=1}^k 3^j$, yielding $\mathbf{v}_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} 3^i \cdot e_{x_{n-i}}^S}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} 3^i}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: The exponential weighting by $3^i$ ensures that one-hot encodings at different positions can be uniquely separated (analogous to positional encoding in a number base system).

2. **MLP Nonlinear Reconstruction: Extracting Current Context $\mathbf{u}_n$**

    - **Function**: Separates $e_{x_{n-k}}^S$ (the token at the farthest position) from $\mathbf{v}_n$, then combines it with the current token $e_{x_n}^S$ from the skip connection to reconstruct the current context.
    - **Mechanism**:
        - Embeds the one-hot encodings of the full vocabulary into the MLP weight matrix.
        - ReLU with appropriate bias filters out all components except those aligned with $x_{n-k}$.
        - LayerNorm normalizes the result to obtain a clean $e_{x_{n-k}}^S$.
        - Linear combination: $\mathbf{u}_n = \frac{e_{x_n}^S}{\sum 3^i} + \frac{3 e_{x_{n-k}}^S \mathbf{v}_n}{\sum 3^i} - \frac{3^k e_{x_{n-k}}^S}{\sum 3^i}$.
    - **Design Motivation**: **This is the paper's central contribution** — the three-layer construction obtains $\mathbf{u}_n$ via a second attention head, while this work proves that the nonlinear components of the MLP suffice as a replacement, without any additional attention operation.

3. **Second-Layer Attention: $k$-th Order Induction Head**

    - **Function**: At position $T$, matches positions in history with identical context via cosine similarity $\langle \mathbf{u}_T, \mathbf{v}_n \rangle / (\|\mathbf{u}_T\| \|\mathbf{v}_n\|)$.
    - **Mechanism**: When $\mathbf{u}_T = \mathbf{v}_n$, the similarity equals 1 (indicating that the $k$ tokens preceding position $n$ exactly match the current context); as the softmax temperature tends to infinity, the output converges to the conditional $k$-gram.
    - **Design Motivation**: Identical to the third layer of the three-layer construction, differing only in the source of $\mathbf{u}_n$ and $\mathbf{v}_n$.

### Gradient Descent Analysis (Simplified First-Order Case)
- For a first-order Markov chain, training proceeds in two phases:
    - **Phase 1**: Train the positional encoding scalar $\mathbf{p}$ to learn the correct attention pattern.
    - **Phase 2**: Freeze the positional encoding and train the attention scaling scalar $a_2$ until the induction head converges.
- It is proved that the cross-entropy loss converges to near-optimal values after $T_1 + T_2$ steps.

## Key Experimental Results

### Attention Pattern Validation ($k=3$ Markov Chain)

| Layer | Observed Attention Pattern | Theoretical Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| Layer 1 | Attention weights increase monotonically along the first $k=3$ sub-diagonals, then drop to 0 | $\operatorname{attn} \propto 3^i$ for $i \leq k$ |
| Layer 2 | Attention concentrates on positions where the preceding $k$ tokens match the current context | Ideal pattern approximating the conditional $k$-gram |

### Parameter Efficiency Comparison

| Method | Layers | Heads/Layer | Parameter Scale |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| Nichani et al. | 2 | $k$ | $(k+(k+1)^2)(S+T)^2$ |
| Rajaraman et al. | 3 | 1 | $15(6S+3)^2 + (6S+3)(3T+2S+10)$ |
| **Ours** | **2** | **1** | $9(6S+3)^2 + (6S+3)(2T+2S+9)$ |

- The proposed construction is the most parameter-efficient when both $k$ and $T$ are large.
- Bit-precision requirement: $\Omega(\log T + k)$; approximation error: $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$.

### Key Findings
- **Single layer is impossible**: Experiments confirm that a single-layer Transformer with the same parameter count cannot solve the induction head task.
- **Nonlinearity is essential**: Removing either ReLU or LayerNorm causes the construction to fail, demonstrating that MLP nonlinearities are not merely auxiliary.
- **Robustness**: The two-layer model remains functional after adding noise to input sequences.

## Highlights & Insights
- **A new role for MLPs**: While MLPs in Transformers are traditionally viewed as feature transformers, this paper demonstrates that they can substitute for an entire attention layer — disentangling individual token embeddings from mixed representations via nonlinear operations.
- **Precise characterization of the depth-width trade-off**: The results for 2-layer 2-head, 3-layer 1-head, and 2-layer 1-head configurations form a complete Pareto frontier.
- **The $3^i$ positional encoding trick**: Exponential weighting enables unique decoding of mixtures of one-hot encodings, a technique transferable to other settings requiring fixed-dimensional encoding of variable-length sequential information.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The gradient descent analysis covers only the first-order case; learning dynamics for higher orders remain unresolved.
- The construction is an existential (constructive) proof, but weights learned through actual training do not necessarily adopt this specific construction.
- Validation is limited to Markov sequences; real natural language distributions are far more complex.
- The theory requires bit-precision $\Omega(\log T + k)$; whether standard floating-point precision suffices in practice warrants further analysis.

## Related Work & Insights
- **vs. Rajaraman et al. (3-layer 1-head)**: This work reduces the layer count from 3 to 2; the key contribution is proving that the MLP can compensate for one attention layer.
- **vs. Nichani et al. (2-layer $k$-head)**: This work reduces the head count from $k$ to 1, replacing multi-head parallel processing with MLP nonlinearities.
- **vs. Sanford et al. (1-layer lower bound)**: Combined with the single-layer impossibility result, this paper establishes that 2 layers is the minimum depth for representing induction heads.
- **Implications for model compression**: If attention heads can be replaced by MLPs, it may be possible to use fewer attention heads with stronger MLPs at inference time.

## Rating
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A tight result reducing depth from 3 to 2, with the novel insight that MLPs can substitute for attention heads.
- Experimental Thoroughness: ⭐⭐⭐ Primarily a theoretical work; experiments are verificational in nature and limited in scope.
